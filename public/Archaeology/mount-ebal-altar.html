Mount Ebal Structure (el-Burnat) and the Folded Lead Object

Late Bronze / Iron I stone installation on Mount Ebal, and a disputed folded lead object from its spoil heap

Overview

Adam Zertal of the University of Haifa found a stone installation on the second step of Mount Ebal in 1980 during his Manasseh Hill Country Survey and excavated it from 1982 to 1989. His preliminary report appeared in Tel Aviv 13-14 (1986-87), with a separate faunal report by L. K. Horwitz and a glyptic report by B. Brandl in the same issue. Zertal identified the large rectangular structure, with a ramp on its west side, as an altar, and the smaller round structure under its centre as an earlier altar; he connected the site with the altar Joshua built on Mount Ebal (Josh 8:30-35).

The bone assemblage, as summarised by Stripling et al. (2023), is 65% caprine, 21% bovine, 10% fallow deer and 4% other (snake, tortoise, etc.); they describe 96% as kosher. Ralph K. Hawkins's 2007 Andrews University dissertation (book 2012) tested Zertal's interpretation against domestic, military and cultic parallels and concluded the site 'appears to fit the criteria for a cultic site.'

In December 2019 Stripling's team wet-sifted about 30% of Zertal's discarded spoil. From the east dump, which held altar-area (Area A) material, Frankie Snyder recovered a folded lead object about 2 x 2 cm. It was announced at a press event in March 2022 and published in Heritage Science on 12 May 2023 by Stripling, Galil and a Czech X-ray tomography team, who read 48 proto-alphabetic letters inside it as a curse naming 'yhw'. Published responses from late 2023 and 2024 (Mazar; Maeir and Rollston; Yahalom-Mack; Haughwout) argue that the letters are not present and that the object may be a net sinker or other lead item.

What the text says (summary)

Cursed you [are] by the god YHW, cursed. / You will die, cursed; cursed, die, [you will] d[ie]. / [C]ursed you [are] by YHW, [c]ursed.Literal translation (this encyclopedia) of the transliteration published by Stripling et al., Heritage Science 11 (2023) 105, p. 7 ('th 'rwr l'l yhw 'rwr / tmt 'rwr 'rwr mt t[mt] / ['] rwr 'th lyhw [']rwr). Whether these letters exist at all is disputed (see questions).

What the evidence shows

  • Rectangular Stratum I structure 8.75-9.00 x 7.00 m with a ramp on the west side, over a 2 m round Stratum II structure at its centre (Stripling et al. 2023 p. 3, summarising Zertal).
  • Structures sit inside a 3.8-dunam foot-shaped enclosure (110 m long, c. 35 m wide) within a larger 14-dunam enclosure (same source).
  • Pottery: 97% Iron IA, 3% Late Bronze IIB (Zertal, per Stripling et al.); the 2019 sifting gave 268 diagnostic sherds: 95% Iron I, 4.75% Late Bronze, 0.25% Early Roman.
  • Bones: 65% caprine, 21% bovine, 10% fallow deer, 4% other including snake and tortoise (Stripling et al. 2023, citing the faunal study).
  • No radiocarbon dates: Zertal's organic samples are reported lost (Stripling et al. 2023 p. 3).
  • Two scarabs and a seal; Brandl (1986) assigned Scarab 1 to Ramesses II, Ben-Tor and van der Veen to Thutmose III or soon after.
  • Lead object found in December 2019 in a spoil heap, not in a stratified layer; 0.25% of the sifted sherds were Early Roman, so the dump held some post-Iron Age material.
  • Lead isotope analysis by N. Yahalom-Mack points to Lavrion (Greece) ore (Stripling et al. 2023 p. 3).
  • The object has not been unfolded; every claimed letter comes from X-ray tomography reconstructions made at the Czech Academy of Sciences, Telc.

Open questions

  • Altar or not? Zertal (Tel Aviv 1986-87; BAR 11/1, 1985) read the rectangular structure with its ramp, the bone assemblage and the enclosure as a cultic altar. Hawkins (dissertation 2007; book 2012) compared it with Iron I domestic and military buildings and religious structures and concluded it fits a cultic site better.
  • Are there letters inside the lead object? Stripling, Galil et al. (Heritage Science 2023) read 48 letters in three formulaic lines from the tomography slices, with the divine name YHW twice and 'rwr ('cursed') six times. Maeir and Rollston (IEJ 73/2, 2023, per Windle's summary) state that 'the putative 48 letters are simply not present' and that the images show striations, scratches, pitting and indentations in soft lead. Haughwout (Heritage Science 12:70, 2024) reached the same conclusion: there is 'insufficient epigraphic evidence to conclude that writing exists' on the object.
  • What is the object? A. Mazar (IEJ 73/2, 2023, per Windle; also cited by Haughwout as forthcoming in IEJ) identifies it as a Type L2.3b fishing-net sinker of an Aegean type. Windle (ABR/Bible Archaeology Report, Dec 2023) answers that the Ebal piece is cruder, thicker and heavier than such sinkers and has no rope grooves. Haughwout also lists a clasp on a string (Cargill, Barnea) or a Roman lead tag as possibilities.
  • Date of the object: Stripling et al. assign it to the Stratum II round altar because the east dump held only Area A material. Haughwout (2024) replies that a spoil heap cannot date an object, since topsoil and later material were also removed; the 0.25% Early Roman pottery in the sifted spoil shows later material was present. Yahalom-Mack's Lavrion lead isotopes fit several periods of mining and so do not decide the date either.

Corrected from the earlier edition of this page
  • Claims that the altar 'held bones of only kosher animals': the faunal breakdown given by Stripling et al. (2023) is 96% kosher, with 4% other including snake and tortoise.
  • Claims that the lead tablet was 'found in 2022' or 'published in 2022': it was recovered in December 2019, first announced at a press event in March 2022 (Rollston's critique is dated 26 March 2022, per Haughwout's references), and published on 12 May 2023.
  • Claims that the tablet was 'excavated from the altar': it came from Zertal's spoil heap during wet-sifting, not from a stratified layer.
  • Claims that the tablet is 'the earliest Hebrew inscription' as a settled fact: no letter is visible on the surface. The reading rests on tomography, and published analyses (IEJ 73/2, 2023; Heritage Science 12:70, 2024) deny that letters are present.
  • Citation seen in circulation, 'Stripling/Galil vs. Rollston/Mazar': the IEJ critical response is by Maeir and Rollston. Mazar wrote a separate article on the fishing-weight identification, and Yahalom-Mack wrote on the lead source.
  • Claims that the lead is local Canaanite: isotope analysis points to Lavrion in Greece (Stripling et al. 2023, reporting Yahalom-Mack).

The Find

  • DateStructure: Zertal's Strata II-I, c. mid-13th to 12th century BCE on pottery and scarabs. Lead object: undated in itself (found in a spoil heap); claimed Late Bronze II by its publishers.
  • How datedPottery (Zertal: 97% Iron IA, 3% Late Bronze IIB, as summarised by Stripling et al. 2023) and two scarabs (Brandl 1986; re-assessed by Ben-Tor and van der Veen as Thutmose III or soon after). Stripling et al. state that Zertal's organic samples are lost, so no radiocarbon dates exist. The lead object came from a dump pile and carries no stratigraphic date of its own; its publishers assign it to the Stratum II round altar because the east dump held only Area A (altar) material.
  • DiscoveredStructure found 1980 in survey, excavated 1982-1989; lead object recovered December 2019, Adam Zertal (University of Haifa, Manasseh Hill Country Survey); lead object found by Frankie Snyder in wet-sifting directed by Scott Stripling (Associates for Biblical Research), el-Burnat (A), second step of Mount Ebal, northeast of Tel Balata (Shechem)
  • Now heldStructure in situ on Mount Ebal, northern West Bank. Lead object: held by the Stripling team; no museum inventory number published.
  • MaterialStructure: fieldstone. Object: folded lead strip (lead isotopes point to Lavrion, Greece)
  • DimensionsRectangular structure (Stratum I) 8.75-9.00 x 7.00 m plus a ramp on the west side; round structure (Stratum II) 2 m diameter beneath its centre; inner enclosure 3.8 dunams (c. 110 x 35 m). Lead object c. 2 x 2 cm, 0.3 cm thick folded, strip c. 0.4 mm thick (all per Stripling et al. 2023, p. 3).
  • ScriptClaimed: proto-alphabetic, 48 letters (Stripling et al. 2023); disputed whether any letters exist
  • LanguageClaimed: early Hebrew (Stripling et al. 2023); disputed

Biblical Passages

  • Deut 11:29; 27:1-8Moses commands an altar of unhewn stones on Mount Ebal and the curses to be pronounced there. Mount Ebal and the altar command correspond to the site and structure. That this structure is that altar is Zertal's reading, not something written on the site.
  • Josh 8:30-35Joshua builds the altar on Mount Ebal and reads the blessings and curses. Joshua's name is not on anything found at el-Burnat.
  • Deut 27:15-26The Ebal ceremony is a series of twelve 'arur ('cursed') formulas. If the lead object's reading stood, its repeated 'rwr would echo this directly. Whether it does depends on whether the letters exist, which is disputed.
  • Exod 20:26Altars are to have no steps. The Ebal structure has a ramp. This agrees with the command but does not show that the builders knew it.
