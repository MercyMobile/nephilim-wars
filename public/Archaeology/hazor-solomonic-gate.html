Hazor Six-Chambered Gate

Iron Age gate and casemate wall, upper city of Tel Hazor · Stratum X

Overview

A city gate with three chambers on each side of the passage, set in a casemate wall around the western part of Hazor's upper city. Yigael Yadin exposed it in the 1957-1958 seasons of the James A. de Rothschild Expedition; the final report for those seasons is Hazor III-IV (1989).

Yadin assigned the gate and casemate wall to Stratum X and, noticing the same plan at Megiddo and (from Macalister's plan) Gezer, tied the three to the list in 1 Kings 9:15 (IEJ 8, 1958). That comparison is the origin of the 'Solomonic gates' label.

Whether Stratum X is 10th-century (Solomon) or 9th-century (Omrides) has been argued in print since 1999 between Finkelstein and the Hazor excavators Ben-Tor and Ben-Ami.

What the evidence shows

  • Six-chamber plan (three chambers each side of the passage) joined to a casemate wall (Yadin; summarised by Madain Project from the excavation reports).
  • Stratum X: the stratum Yadin gave the gate; Dever (JJAR 1, 2021, n. 33) cites Zarzecki-Peleg 1997 and Ben-Tor & Ben-Ami 1998 for its 10th-c. date.
  • No inscription or datable object naming a builder is associated with the gate in any source checked.

Open questions

  • Date of Stratum X: Ben-Tor and Ben-Ami ('Hazor and the Archaeology of the Tenth Century B.C.E.', IEJ 48, 1998) and Ben-Tor (BASOR 317, 2000; NEA 76.2, 2013) keep it in the 10th c.; Finkelstein ('Hazor and the North in the Iron Age: A Low Chronology Perspective', BASOR 314, 1999; Tel Aviv 27, 2000) lowers it to the 9th c. The ceramic arguments in these papers have not been reviewed for this entry; only titles, venues and the positions they take were verified.
  • Link to Megiddo: Finkelstein et al. (Tel Aviv 46, 2019) date Megiddo's six-chambered Gate 2156 to Strata VA-IVB in the Omride period; if the Hazor and Megiddo gates are one design, the Megiddo date bears directly on Hazor's.
  • Sameness of the three gates: Yadin (1958) described the Gezer gate as matching Hazor and Megiddo; Dever (2021) reports this as 'almost identical in plan'. A measured side-by-side comparison has not been located for this entry.

The Find

  • DateHazor Stratum X. Excavators (Yadin; Ben-Tor) date it to the 10th c. BCE; Finkelstein dates Stratum X to the 9th c. BCE (Omride). No radiocarbon date for the gate is cited in the sources here.
  • How datedStratigraphy and pottery, and comparison with Megiddo VA-IVB and Gezer VIII. Ben-Tor & Ben-Ami (IEJ 48, 1998) and Ben-Tor (BASOR 317, 2000; NEA 76, 2013) argue 10th c.; Finkelstein (BASOR 314, 1999; Tel Aviv 27, 2000) argues a low (9th c.) date.
  • Discovered1957-1958 (third and fourth seasons), Yigael Yadin, James A. de Rothschild Expedition (Hebrew University), Upper city (acropolis) of Tel Hazor (Tell el-Qedah), Upper Galilee, Israel
  • Now heldIn situ, Tel Hazor National Park, Israel
  • MaterialStone masonry

Biblical Passages

  • 1 Kgs 9:15Lists 'Hazor, and Megiddo, and Gezer' among Solomon's building works. The find is a fortification of the plan Yadin linked to that verse; its builder is not named on site. The Solomonic attribution depends on Stratum X being 10th-century.
  • 2 Kgs 15:29Records Tiglath-pileser III taking Hazor. Not directly tied to the gate in any source verified here.
