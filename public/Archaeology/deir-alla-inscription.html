Deir 'Alla Plaster Inscription (Balaam son of Beor)

Ink-on-plaster text naming 'Balaam son of Beor, a seer of the gods' · c. 800 BCE · Tell Deir 'Alla, Jordan

Overview

In 1967 the Dutch expedition under H. J. Franken found fragments of an ink text painted on the plastered wall of a building at Tell Deir 'Alla, in the Jordan Valley near the Jabbok. J. Hoftijzer and G. van der Kooij published the editio princeps in 1976 as Aramaic Texts from Deir 'Alla (Brill), with chapters on the conservation, the plaster and the script. The same editors later published The Balaam Text from Deir 'Alla Re-Evaluated (1991).

The fragments are grouped into 'combinations'. Combination I opens with a title naming 'Balaam son of Beor', 'a seer of the gods'. The gods come to him at night, and he sees a vision 'in accordance with El's utterance'. The vision reports a decree of the gods (the Shaddai-gods): the heavens are to be sewn shut with dense cloud, and there is to be darkness and no light. The goddess named with this decree is read Shagar-and-Ishtar in the Livius translation.

The text names Balaam son of Beor, El, and the Shaddai-gods. As translated on Livius, it does not name Israel, Moab, Balak, YHWH, or a donkey.

What the text says (summary)

The title line names 'the book of Balaam son of Beor', described as a seer of the gods. The next line says the gods came to him at night and he saw a vision according to the utterance of El.Paraphrase of Combination I, lines 1-2, after the published editions (Hoftijzer and van der Kooij 1976; Levine's translation via Livius). No public-domain translation exists, since the text was found in 1967.

What the evidence shows

  • Found in 1967 by the Dutch expedition under H. J. Franken; editio princeps Hoftijzer and van der Kooij 1976.
  • Written in ink on wall plaster and recovered as fragments grouped into combinations; two main combinations (I and II) plus smaller groups.
  • Combination I, line 1 names bl'm br b'r, 'Balaam son of Beor', and calls him a seer of the gods.
  • Deities named: El, the Shaddai-gods (šdyn), and a goddess read Shagar-and-Ishtar.
  • Content of the vision: the heavens closed with cloud, darkness instead of light.
  • Date c. 800 BCE on script and context.

Open questions

  • Language: the 1976 editio princeps presents the texts as Aramaic. The Livius summary calls it 'a language between Aramaic and Canaanite'. The 1991 re-evaluation volume has separate chapters by P. K. McCarter ('The Dialect of the Deir 'Alla Texts') and D. Pardee ('The Linguistic Classification of the Deir 'Alla Text Written on Plaster') on this question. Their specific arguments have not been reviewed for this entry, so their positions are not stated here.
  • Genre and meaning of Combination I: Hoftijzer's 1991 chapter 'What Did the Gods Say?' addresses the divine decree. Tsolin and Oriekhova (The World of the Orient, 2025) read the texts as an example of the prophetic genre massa'. The texts of both were not read.
  • Relation to Numbers 22-24: Deir 'Alla preserves a non-Israelite tradition about Balaam son of Beor as a seer receiving night visions, about 800 BCE, east of the Jordan. Whether Numbers draws on such a tradition, or the two share a common older figure, is interpretation. The plaster text itself gives no information on the direction of borrowing.

  • Claims that the inscription 'mentions Balak' or 'Moab' or 'Israel': the translated text names Balaam son of Beor, El, the Shaddai-gods and Shagar-and-Ishtar. Balak, Moab and Israel do not appear.

The Find

  • Datec. 800 BCE (script and archaeological context)
  • How datedScript type and the destruction of the building in which the plaster wall stood (Livius summary, citing Hoftijzer and van der Kooij 1976). No radiocarbon dates are confirmed in the sources cited here.
  • Discovered1967, Dutch expedition under H. J. Franken, Tell Deir 'Alla, east Jordan Valley, about 8 km east of the Jordan and 1 km north of the Jabbok (Zarqa)
  • Now heldAmman, Jordan. A Wikipedia pointer says the Jordan Archaeological Museum; this is not confirmed in a published museum record.
  • MaterialInk on wall plaster, recovered in many fragments
  • ScriptNorthwest Semitic alphabetic script, ink written; red ink used for emphasis (Wikipedia pointer, not confirmed)
  • LanguageDisputed: the editio princeps is titled 'Aramaic Texts'; later studies classify it differently (see questions)

Biblical Passages

  • Num 22:5; 24:3-4, 15-16Same name and patronymic, 'Balaam son of Beor'. Same role: a seer who receives visions from the divine, including at night (Num 22:8-9, 19-20: God comes to Balaam at night). Num 24:4, 16 also uses 'Shaddai' ('who sees the vision of Shaddai'), and the plaster text has the Shaddai-gods (šdyn).
  • Josh 13:22; Mic 6:5; Deut 23:4-5Other biblical mentions of Balaam son of Beor. The plaster text confirms that a Balaam tradition existed east of the Jordan around 800 BCE. It says nothing about Balak, Moab or Israel.
