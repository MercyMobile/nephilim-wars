Tel Shiqmona: purple-dye industry and seasonal production

Iron Age coastal industrial compound with purple dye, oil processing, weaving and livestock activity · new peer-reviewed microarchaeological analysis, 2026

Overview

Tel Shiqmona preserves an unusually well-documented Iron Age complex with evidence of purple-dye manufacture, olive pressing, textile work and livestock management.

A 2026 PLOS ONE study recovered detailed sedimentary traces of changing use of industrial space. Its proposed seasonal timetable is an interpretation of the archaeological record, not a calendar directly inscribed at the site.

What the evidence shows

  • Purple-stained vessel fragments, murex shells, olive-press installations and loom weights occur in the industrial sequence.
  • Microscopic sediment evidence includes dung-related spherulites and wood ash, documenting activities beyond purple dye.

Open questions

  • The exact season in which the purple-dye workshops operated has not been established directly.
  • Whether the site's political administration can be attributed to specific named kings is unresolved by these finds.
