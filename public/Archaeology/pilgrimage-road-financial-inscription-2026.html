Pilgrimage Road: Hebrew commercial inscription

Small inscribed limestone slab with names and numerical amounts from Jerusalem's Early Roman-period commercial district · discussed July 2026

Overview

A fragment of an inscribed chalkstone slab records portions of Hebrew personal names and amounts, likely a receipt or payment instruction.

Its discovery near Jerusalem's monumental stepped street illuminates commercial exchange in the Second Temple city. Because it came from a nineteenth-century excavation tunnel, it lacks its original depositional context.

What the evidence shows

  • Seven partly preserved lines contain Hebrew letters and numerical signs; a fragment of the name Shimon is legible.
  • Letter shapes and comparable inscriptions support an Early Roman-period date, despite the disturbed context.

Open questions

  • The transaction, full names, precise date and original location of use cannot be securely reconstructed.
  • There is no evidence identifying the named Shimon with a New Testament individual.
