Ai Excavations

Et-Tell's Early Bronze city, its long gap, and the search for Joshua's Ai

Overview

Et-Tell ('the ruin-heap') lies east of Beitin, which is identified with Bethel. It is the usual candidate for the Ai of Joshua 7-8 ('Ai, which is beside Bethaven, on the east side of Bethel', Josh 7:2). John Garstang made soundings there over a few days in 1928. Judith Marquet-Krause excavated it for the Rothschild expedition in 1933-1935, and Joseph Callaway led the Joint Archaeological Expedition in 1964-1972.

Both main excavators found a large walled Early Bronze city, with a citadel, a sanctuary and houses, which ended in the Early Bronze III period. The site then lay empty until a small unwalled village was built on the ruins in Iron Age I. Marquet-Krause wrote that the Middle and Late Bronze Ages were 'nonexistent' and that 'this city had therefore long since been destroyed by the time of Joshua's arrival.' Callaway, whose JBL article of 1968 was titled 'New Evidence on the Conquest of Ai', reached the same gap.

Two responses followed. The Associates for Biblical Research (B. G. Wood, S. Stripling) excavated Khirbet el-Maqatir, 1 km west of et-Tell. They found a small Late Bronze I fortress with a two-chambered gate, destroyed by a 'massive conflagration', and dated it c. 1400 BCE. Others have reopened et-Tell itself: in 2025 J. Kramer publicized Garstang's brief 1928 note of Late Bronze pottery and an unpublished 2019 sounding by T. Kennedy that reportedly yielded Late Bronze pottery and a burned layer.

What the evidence shows

  • Et-Tell: an Early Bronze city (EB IB to EB IIIB, c. 3300-2400 BCE) with walls, citadel and sanctuary, ending in destruction and abandonment (Marquet-Krause 1935; Callaway & Schoonover, BASOR 207, 1972; NEAEHL summary).
  • Marquet-Krause (1935, as quoted by Eames 2025): 'The Middle and Late Bronze Ages are nonexistent.'
  • Et-Tell Iron I: a small unwalled village on the acropolis, c. 1220-1050 BCE (NEAEHL summary).
  • Garstang's 1928 soundings were never fully published. A three-page report mentions Late Bronze material alongside Early Bronze (Eames 2025).
  • A 2019 sounding at et-Tell (T. Kennedy) reportedly produced Late Bronze pottery and a burned layer. As of Eames (June 2025) the dating of that layer was unpublished.
  • Khirbet el-Maqatir: a fortress of about 1 ha with a two-chambered gate (socket stones and an upper pivot stone), LB IB pottery throughout, and a 'massive conflagration' with calcined stones and ash, dated by the excavators to c. 1400 BCE (Wood 2012, ABR).
  • No inscription at either site gives the ancient name.

Open questions

  • Is et-Tell Ai? Marquet-Krause (Syria 16, 1935) and Callaway (JBL 87, 1968; BASOR 207, 1972) found no Middle or Late Bronze city at et-Tell. Taken with Josh 8, that yields either no conquest at et-Tell or a wrong identification; L.-H. Vincent (1937, as quoted by Eames 2025) put it as 'Either et-Tell… cannot be identified with Ai, or this identification goes directly against the Bible.' J. Kramer (Expedition Bible video, May 2025) argues that Garstang's note and T. Kennedy's 2019 soundings put Late Bronze material and a burn layer at et-Tell, and that the stone heap over the et-Tell gate fits Josh 8:29. C. Eames (Armstrong Institute, 2025) replies that the destruction layer's date is unpublished, that the gate was already blocked before the stones piled up, and that the stone pile covers a passage only about 1 m wide. The dispute is unresolved until Kennedy's material is published.
  • Is Khirbet el-Maqatir Ai? B. G. Wood and S. Stripling (ABR, 1995-2016) argue that its Late Bronze I fortress, burned c. 1400 BCE, fits Josh 8 on a 15th-century conquest date. The fortress's small size fits Josh 7:3 ('they are but few'), but Josh 8:25 counts 12,000 dead at Ai. Their case also depends on where Bethel lay relative to Maqatir. That question is argued in their publications and was not re-checked here. The Bronze Age volume of the final report has not been located for this entry; Volume 2 (2023) covers the later periods.

The Find

  • DateEt-Tell: Early Bronze IB-IIIB city (c. 3300-2400 BCE); abandoned; small unwalled Iron Age I village (c. 1220-1050 BCE). Khirbet el-Maqatir (1 km west): Late Bronze I fortress destroyed by fire, dated by its excavators to c. 1400 BCE.
  • How datedPottery stratigraphy from Marquet-Krause (1933-1935) and Callaway (1964-1972) at et-Tell. At Khirbet el-Maqatir, the excavators date the fortress by LB IB pottery.
  • Discovered1928 (Garstang soundings); 1933-1935 (Marquet-Krause); 1964-1972 (Callaway, Joint Archaeological Expedition to Ai); 1995-2001 and 2009-2016 (ABR at Khirbet el-Maqatir); 2019 (small et-Tell soundings, Kennedy, unpublished), John Garstang; Judith Marquet-Krause (Rothschild expedition); Joseph A. Callaway; Bryant G. Wood and Scott Stripling (Associates for Biblical Research); Titus Kennedy, Et-Tell, about 2 km east of Beitin (Bethel), West Bank; Khirbet el-Maqatir, about 1 km west of et-Tell
  • Now heldIn situ, et-Tell and Khirbet el-Maqatir, West Bank
  • MaterialEarly Bronze fortifications, citadel, sanctuary and houses at et-Tell; Iron I village houses; Late Bronze I fortress with a two-chambered gate at Khirbet el-Maqatir
  • DimensionsThe Early Bronze IC city at et-Tell measured about 27.5 acres (per the NEAEHL summary). The Maqatir fortress is about 1 ha.

Biblical Passages

  • Josh 7:2; 8:9, 12Ai lies 'beside Bethaven, on the east side of Bethel', with an ambush between Bethel and Ai on the west. Et-Tell lies east of Beitin, and Maqatir lies west of et-Tell. Which site fits depends on where Bethel was.
  • Josh 8:28'Joshua burnt Ai, and made it an heap (tel) for ever, even a desolation unto this day.' The Arabic name et-Tell means 'the heap'. The name corresponds to the text, but the date of the burning at et-Tell (Early Bronze) does not.
  • Josh 8:29The king of Ai is buried under 'a great heap of stones' at the entrance of the city gate. Marquet-Krause found a stone mound over et-Tell's gate. Whether that mound dates to any Late Bronze event has not been established in publication.
  • Josh 7:3; 8:25'They are but few' versus 12,000 dead. Maqatir's small fortress fits the first. Et-Tell's large Early Bronze city is bigger, but it was not occupied in the Late Bronze Age.
  • Gen 12:8; 13:3Abram camps between Bethel and Ai. Et-Tell's Early Bronze ruin would have been a landmark heap by any second-millennium date.
