Megiddo Stables

Pillared buildings with stone troughs, Stratum IV · date disputed (10th-8th c. BCE)

Overview

Two groups of long tripartite pillared buildings found on Tel Megiddo by the University of Chicago expedition: a southern compound of five units facing a 55 m courtyard with a central water tank, and a northern group. In each unit a central lime-plastered passage is flanked by rubble-floored aisles, separated by rows of stone pillars alternating with stone troughs.

Lamon and Shipton (Megiddo I, 1939) read them as stables for about 450 horses: the pillars had holes cut at the corners just above the troughs, which they took as tethering holes. They assigned them to Stratum IV (ca. 1000-800 BCE) and to Solomon, using the chariot figures in Kings and Chronicles to argue for three-horse chariot teams.

The identification as stables and the Solomonic date have both been questioned. Finkelstein's 2025 treatment frames Megiddo of the 8th century BCE as a 'horse industry city'.

What the evidence shows

  • Southern compound: five units facing a courtyard 55 m on a side, overall 64 x 85 m, with a water tank near the courtyard's centre (Lamon & Shipton 1939, p. 32).
  • Rows of stone pillars alternating with stone troughs c. 1.2 x 0.6 x 0.7 m; holes cut through pillar corners just above the troughs, 'almost invariably on the side toward the central passage' (Lamon & Shipton, pp. 32-44).
  • Central passages lime-plastered; side aisles floored with rubble (pp. 32-44).
  • Excavators' capacity estimate: about 450 horses for both groups; the counts per unit are the excavators' reconstruction (pp. 43-44).
  • No inscription or text found with the buildings.

Open questions

  • Function: Lamon & Shipton (1939) identify stables from troughs, tie-holes and rubble stall floors; Cantrell (The Horsemen of Israel, 2011, ch. 5 'Stables of Israel: The Case of Megiddo') and Finkelstein (NEA 88.3, 2025, 'Horse Industry City') keep the horse interpretation in their titles; Franklin ('Entering the Arena: The Megiddo Stables Reconsidered', 2017) and Davies ('Solomonic Stables at Megiddo After All?', PEQ 1988) re-examined the question. The texts of these later studies, and the storehouse alternative the old entry mentions, have not been reviewed for this entry, so their specific evidence is not reported here.
  • Date: Lamon & Shipton assign Stratum IV to ca. 1000-800 BCE and to Solomon on the strength of 1 Kings; Finkelstein (2025) places the horse complex in the 8th c. BCE. The stratigraphic arguments of the later studies have not been reviewed for this entry; only titles, venues and dates were verified.

The Find

  • DateMegiddo Stratum IV, labelled 'ca. 1000-800 B.C.' by the excavators (Lamon & Shipton 1939); later phased as IVA and dated by Finkelstein to the 8th c. BCE ('Horse Industry City: Eighth-Century BCE Megiddo', 2025).
  • How datedStratigraphy of the University of Chicago excavation; the 1939 attribution to Solomon rested on 1 Kings. Later dates rest on re-phasing of Megiddo's strata and radiocarbon from the renewed Tel Aviv University excavations.
  • DiscoveredUniversity of Chicago seasons 1925-1934 (as covered by Megiddo I), Oriental Institute of the University of Chicago Megiddo Expedition; published by Robert S. Lamon and Geoffrey M. Shipton (1939), who cite P. L. O. Guy's earlier report (OIC 9) for the stables, Tel Megiddo, Jezreel Valley, Israel: southern compound (Area A) and northern group (Area C)
  • Now heldIn situ, Tel Megiddo National Park, Israel
  • MaterialStone pillars and stone troughs; lime-plastered central passages, rubble-floored side aisles
  • DimensionsSouthern compound 64 x 85 m with a courtyard 55 m square; each unit a c. 3 m central passage flanked by aisles of similar width; pillars c. 0.5 m square; troughs c. 1.2 x 0.6 m, 0.7 m high (Lamon & Shipton 1939)

Biblical Passages

  • 1 Kgs 9:19Solomon built 'cities for his chariots, and cities for his horsemen'. The buildings are horse facilities on the excavators' reading, at a city named in 1 Kgs 9:15; the verse does not name Megiddo as a chariot city.
  • 1 Kgs 4:26; 2 Chr 9:25Kings gives 40,000 stalls, Chronicles 4,000. Lamon & Shipton (1939, p. 44) used the 4,000 figure and 1 Kgs 10:26-29 to argue for three-horse teams. This is their reading.
  • 1 Kgs 10:26-29Solomon's 1,400 chariots and 12,000 horsemen, and horse trade prices. Corresponds to large horse facilities only if the buildings are 10th-century stables, which is disputed.
