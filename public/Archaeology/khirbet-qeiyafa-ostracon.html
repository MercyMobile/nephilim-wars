Khirbet Qeiyafa Ostracon

Five-line ink inscription on a jar sherd · c. 1000 BCE

Overview

A sherd from a large storage jar with five lines of black-ink writing, found in 2008 in Building 2 next to the casemate wall north of the west gate at Khirbet Qeiyafa. At about 15 x 16.5 cm it carries more alphabetic text than any other inscription of its date from the region (Millard 2011).

The letters are irregular, some are abraded, the same letter appears in different stances, and the direction of reading was at first uncertain. Haggai Misgav published the editio princeps in Khirbet Qeiyafa Vol. 1 (2009) and read only some words; Ada Yardeni, Aaron Demsky and Shmuel Ahituv also offered partial readings.

Full readings followed from Gershon Galil (Ugarit-Forschungen 41, 2009) and Émile Puech (Revue Biblique 117, 2010), which differ from each other. Alan Millard (2011), following Ed Cook, argued the simplest reading is a list of personal names.

What the text says (summary)

Line 1 as transliterated by Millard: ʾ l t ʿ š / w ʿ b d ʾ ... Read as words: 'Do not do [it], and serve/make ...' (Misgav, Galil) or as names: 'Ellat-ʿash and ʿEbed-...' (Cook, Millard). Line 2 opens špṭ: 'judge' / 'he judged', or the name Shaphat.Literal rendering (this encyclopedia) of the transliteration printed in A. Millard, Tyndale Bulletin 62.1 (2011), pp. 8-10; not a published translation.

What the evidence shows

  • Ink on a jar sherd, max. 15 x 16.5 cm; lines possibly missing above line 1 and at the right edge (Millard 2011, p. 4).
  • Word divider of three vertical dots appears in line 1 only (Millard, p. 7).
  • Clearly legible sequences include špṭ at the start of line 2, nqm in the middle of line 4, and bdmlk at the end of line 4 (Millard, pp. 9-11).
  • Letter forms are inconsistent and 'not the work of an experienced scribe' (Millard, p. 4).
  • Found in a single-phase Iron Age town dated by 14C to c. 1000 BCE (Garfinkel et al. 2015).

Open questions

  • What it says: Misgav (Khirbet Qeiyafa 1, 2009, pp. 255-256) holds the verbs in line 1 are Hebrew and the text is a message with judicial or ethical commands; Galil (UF 41, 2009) reads a social statement ('Judge the slave and the widow...'); Puech (RB 117, 2010) reads a narrative about oppression and the establishing of a king; Demsky and Ahituv (2009 conference, per Millard n. 15) treat it as a writing exercise; Cook and Millard (Tyndale Bulletin 62, 2011) read a list of names (Ellat-ʿash, ʿEbed-..., Shaphat, Bodmilk), noting that nqm-names are Phoenician and ʿlmn ('widow') needs an unseen n.
  • Language: the reading ʾal taʿaś ('do not do') makes the text Hebrew because ʿśh is otherwise known only in Hebrew and Moabite; Millard (2011) shows the same five letters can be a personal name, which removes that argument.
  • Date: tied to the site's destruction, c. 1000 BCE (Garfinkel et al., Radiocarbon 57, 2015) or later 10th c. (Finkelstein & Piasetzky, Radiocarbon 57, 2015).

The Find

  • DateDestruction layer of the single Iron Age phase at Khirbet Qeiyafa: c. 1000 BCE by the excavators' 14C (Garfinkel et al. 2015); later 10th c. BCE by Finkelstein & Piasetzky's regional model (2015).
  • How datedFind context (Building 2, Iron Age level) and 14C of olive pits from the same destruction; the script, which Millard (2011) calls 'Canaanite' rather than Phoenician or Hebrew, fits the late 11th-10th c.
  • Discovered2008, Khirbet Qeiyafa excavation (Y. Garfinkel, S. Ganor); first published by Haggai Misgav, Room adjacent to a casemate in Building 2, north of the west gate, Khirbet Qeiyafa
  • Now heldStudied at the Institute of Archaeology, Hebrew University of Jerusalem (project site). Current display/inventory number Not confirmed in a published museum record.
  • MaterialPottery sherd from a large jar, black ink
  • DimensionsMax. 15 x 16.5 cm (Millard 2011)
  • ScriptEarly alphabetic ('Canaanite' letter forms per Millard; 'Proto-Canaanite/Early Alphabetic' per excavators)
  • LanguageDisputed: Hebrew (Misgav, Galil, Puech) or undetermined Canaanite (Millard: could be Hebrew, Canaanite, Phoenician or Moabite)

Biblical Passages

  • 1 Sam 17The sherd comes from the Elah Valley site the excavators connect with the David-Goliath setting; the text itself mentions no person or place known from the Bible.
  • Exod 22:21-24; Deut 24:17Galil's reading (judge the slave, widow, orphan, stranger) parallels this legal language; that parallel exists only on his restoration of damaged letters (e.g. ʾlmn).
