Kurkh Monolith (Stela of Shalmaneser III)

Assyrian royal stela naming 'Ahab the Israelite' at Qarqar · c. 852 BCE

Overview

A round-topped limestone stela showing Shalmaneser III standing before four divine emblems (winged disk of Aššur, star of Ištar, horned crown of Anu, crescent of Sîn), with an inscription covering his campaigns year by year from his accession to his sixth regnal year. It was found in 1861 at Kurkh on the upper Tigris by J. G. Taylor, together with a stela of Shalmaneser's father Ashurnasirpal II, and both went to the British Museum in 1863.

The last campaign recorded is the sixth-year march into Syria (853 BCE). Shalmaneser takes cities of Irḫulena of Hamath and destroys Qarqar, then lists the forces of a coalition of 'twelve kings' that met him there. The third entry is '2,000 chariots (and) 10,000 troops of Ahab (Aḫ-abbu) the Israelite (Sirʾalāyu)'. The king claims 14,000 enemy dead and a rout 'from Qarqar to Gilzau', and the text breaks off in the middle of the battle description.

The list is the earliest dated extra-biblical text that names a king of Israel. It is also the first cuneiform mention of 'Arabs' (Gindibu' with 1,000 camels).

In its own words

1,200 chariots, 1,200 cavalry, 20,000 soldiers, of Hadad-ezer, of Aram (? Damascus); 700 chariots, 700 cavalry, 10,000 soldiers of Irhulêni of Hamath, 2,000 chariots, 10,000 soldiers of Ahab, the Israelite ...D. D. Luckenbill, Ancient Records of Assyria and Babylonia I (Chicago, 1926), §611, p. 223

What the evidence shows

  • Line ii 91-92 transliterated: 2 LIM GIŠ.GIGIR.MEŠ 10 LIM ERIM.MEŠ šá ma-ḫa-ab-bu KUR.sir-ʾa-la-a-a: '2,000 chariots, 10,000 troops of Ahab of the land Sir'ala (Israel)' (RIMA 3 A.0.102.2).
  • Coalition as listed: Hadad-ezer of Damascus 1,200 chariots / 1,200 cavalry / 20,000 troops; Irḫulena of Hamath 700 / 700 / 10,000; Ahab 2,000 chariots / 10,000 troops; plus contingents of Byblos(?), Egypt, Irqanatu, Arwad, Usanatu, Si'annu, Gindibu' the Arab (1,000 camels), and Ba'sa of Bīt-Ruḫubi (Ammon).
  • The contingent attributed to 'Byblos' is written KUR.gu-<bal>-a-a in the RIAo edition: the sign 'bal' is an editorial restoration, not on the stone; Luckenbill (1926) printed 'Gueans'.
  • Numbers of two contingents are broken ([N],000 troops of Adunu-ba'al; [N] hundred of Ba'sa), so the coalition total cannot be computed from the stone.
  • The text says 'these twelve kings' but names eleven rulers/lands (the count depends on how the list is divided).
  • Claimed Assyrian result: 14,000 enemy killed, pursuit 'from Qarqar to Gilzau'; the text ends at line ii 102 describing captured chariots and horses.
  • Relief: king before emblems of Aššur, Ištar, Anu and Sîn; inscription runs across the relief surface.

Open questions

  • Did Ahab really field 2,000 chariots, more than any other ally? Nadav Na'aman (Tel Aviv 3, 1976, 'Two Notes on the Monolith Inscription of Shalmaneser III from Kurkh') argued the figure is a scribal error and that Ahab's force was 200 chariots, on grounds that 2,000 is out of scale with the other contingents (Damascus 1,200, Hamath 700) and that the stela was carved with other scribal errors (his argument as summarized in later discussions; the article itself is not summarized here). Against emendation: the stone as carved reads 2 LIM (2,000). Shigeo Yamada's collation of the monolith (The Construction of the Assyrian Empire, 2000, pp. 380-383) is the published check on the carved signs.
  • Was Qarqar an Assyrian victory? The stela claims a rout, but the Black Obelisk annals record Shalmaneser fighting 'Hadad-ezer of Damascus and twelve kings of the land Ḫatti' again in his 11th regnal year and 'twelve kings' again in his 14th (RIMA 3 A.0.102.14 ll. 87-92). The RIAo introduction to Shalmaneser III reads Qarqar as a battle he 'may in fact have been defeated [in] or ... may have been a draw'.
  • Why is Ahab called 'Sir'ala-ite' here while Jehu is 'of Bīt-Ḫumrî' twelve years later? Brad Kelle (JBL 121, 2002) examined the Neo-Assyrian names for the northern kingdom and what they imply for Israelite history; the stone itself gives only KUR.sir-ʾa-la-a-a.

The Find

  • DateText ends with the battle of Qarqar in Shalmaneser III's 6th regnal year (853 BCE); stela probably set up c. 852 BCE
  • How datedRegnal-year sequence in the inscription, whose narrative stops at the 6th-year campaign (Qarqar); Assyrian regnal years are fixed by the eponym list. The British Museum dates the stela c. 852 BC, reasoning that Shalmaneser set it up during his campaign in the Diyarbakır region the following year.
  • Discovered1861, John George Taylor, British consul in Diyarbakır (not Layard), Kurkh (modern Üçtepe Höyük), Bismil district, Diyarbakır province, south-eastern Turkey
  • Now heldBritish Museum, London, BM 118884 (registration 1863,0619.2); donated by Taylor in 1863. A companion stela of Ashurnasirpal II from the same find is BM 118883.
  • MaterialLimestone, round-topped stela
  • Dimensionsc. 221 cm high, 87 cm wide, 23 cm deep
  • ScriptNeo-Assyrian cuneiform, carved across the front (over the relief) and the back and sides
  • LanguageAkkadian

Biblical Passages

  • 1 Kgs 16:29-22:40Ahab king of Israel in Samaria. The stela names 'Ahab the Israelite' as a coalition member in 853 BCE; the Bible does not mention Qarqar.
  • 1 Kgs 20; 22:1Ahab fighting Ben-hadad of Aram, then 'three years without war between Aram and Israel'. On the stela, Ahab fights alongside Damascus (Hadad-ezer) against Assyria. Our reading: a temporary alliance against a common enemy; the text says nothing about relations before or after.
  • 1 Kgs 10:26-29; 22:34-35Israelite chariotry. The stela's 2,000-chariot figure (or 200 if Na'aman's emendation is right) is the only external number for Israel's chariot force.
