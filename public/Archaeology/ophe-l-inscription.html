Ophel Pithos Inscription

Letters incised on a storage jar before firing, Ophel, Jerusalem · Iron IIA context

Overview

In 2012 Eilat Mazar's Ophel excavation found fragments of seven large storage jars (pithoi) of the neckless Early Iron IIA type, used as fill beneath the second floor of Building II, about 80 m south of the Temple Mount's southern wall. One pithos shoulder carries seven surviving letters, incised before the jar was fired.

The editio princeps (Mazar, Ben-Shlomo and Ahituv, IEJ 63, 2013) read the letters in an early Canaanite script; the press release stated that the letter combination 'has no meaning in known west-Semitic languages' and that its meaning is unknown. It is the earliest alphabetic inscription so far found in Jerusalem.

Close to a dozen readings have followed. Galil read Hebrew 'yyn ḥlq' ('inferior wine'), followed by Petrovich (PEQ 2015); Vainstub (JJAR 4, 2023) reads Ancient South Arabian script in Sabaean: '[ ]šy ladanum 5'.

What the text says (summary)

]šy lḏn ḫ̇ - '[ ]šy ladanum 5' (Vainstub's reading, right-hand group); editio princeps letter sequence (as reported): m, q, p(?), ḥ, n, (possibly) l, n - meaning unknown.D. Vainstub, Jerusalem Journal of Archaeology 4 (2023): 45, fig. 3; editio princeps letters as summarised in the July 2013 press report of Mazar, Ben-Shlomo & Ahituv, IEJ 63 (2013).

What the evidence shows

  • Seven pithoi, neckless type, Early Iron IIA; one inscribed (Vainstub 2023, citing Mazar 2015: 467-468).
  • Incised before firing, so the writing was done where the jar was made; Vainstub states the vessel was locally made.
  • Seven letters survive (Vainstub 2023, p. 44); letter height c. 2.5 cm (press report).
  • Found in secondary use as floor fill, not in the jar's primary use context.
  • Editors of the editio princeps: script early Canaanite, meaning unknown (press report, 2013).

Open questions

  • Script and language: Mazar, Ben-Shlomo and Ahituv (IEJ 63, 2013) classify the script as early Canaanite and give no meaning; Galil and Petrovich (PEQ 147, 2015) read Hebrew 'yyn ḥlq' ('poor wine'), taking the double yod as southern Hebrew spelling; Vainstub (JJAR 4, 2023) reads Ancient South Arabian letters in Sabaean, '[ ]šy lḏn 5' ('ladanum, 5'), linking lḏn with Hebrew šəḥēlet of Exod 30:34.
  • Direction and letter count: Petrovich (2015) argues sinistrograde reading and restores yod-yod; Vainstub divides the text into a right-to-left group and a left-to-right group (fig. 3).
  • Date of the context: Mazar (2015) and Winderbaum (2022) place Building II in early Iron IIA (mid-10th c.); Finkelstein (2022) dissents (cited by Vainstub, n. 2); Wightman (PEQ 157, 2024) offers 'a significantly different reconstruction' of the Ophel's 11th-9th c. history; Kleiman (Eretz-Israel 35, 2024) wrote specifically on the inscription's date (text not read).

The Find

  • DateContext: early Iron Age IIA, which the excavator places in the mid-10th c. BCE; Winderbaum (2022) confirmed the phase; Finkelstein (2022, cited by Vainstub) dissented on the date.
  • How datedPottery typology of the pithoi (neckless type, Early Iron IIA) and stratigraphy of Building II; no radiocarbon date for the context was located.
  • Discovered2012 (announced July 2013), Eilat Mazar, Hebrew University Ophel excavations, Building II, c. 80 m south of the southern wall of the Temple Mount, Ophel, Jerusalem; sherds of seven pithoi reused as fill under the building's second floor
  • Now heldNot confirmed in a published museum record.
  • MaterialFired clay; inscription incised on the shoulder below the rim before firing
  • DimensionsLetters c. 2.5 cm high (press report of editio princeps)
  • ScriptDisputed: early Canaanite / proto-Canaanite (Mazar, Ben-Shlomo, Ahituv 2013) or Ancient South Arabian (Vainstub 2023)
  • LanguageDisputed: unknown West Semitic, Hebrew (Galil; Petrovich 2015), or Sabaean (Vainstub 2023)

Biblical Passages

  • 1 Kgs 10:1-13; 2 Chr 9:1-12The queen of Sheba brings spices to Solomon. Vainstub's Sabaean reading would put a South Arabian writer handling aromatics in 10th c. Jerusalem; that connection depends entirely on his script identification.
  • Exod 30:34šəḥēlet as an incense ingredient; Vainstub equates it with ladanum (lḏn). This is his lexical argument.
  • 2 Sam 5:7-9; 1 Kgs 3:1The Ophel lies between the City of David and the later Temple Mount. The find shows writing in a 10th c. (on the excavator's date) Jerusalem building; it says nothing on its own about David or Solomon.
