Baruch ben Neriah Bullae (Berekhyahu son of Neriyahu the Scribe)

Two unprovenanced clay seal impressions naming 'Berekhyahu son of Neriyahu the scribe' · authenticity contested

Overview

Two small clay bullae carry the same three-line seal impression, published as reading 'belonging to Berekhyahu, son of Neriyahu, the scribe'. Berekhyahu is the full, theophoric form of the name Baruch, and the Bible's Baruch son of Neriah is Jeremiah's scribe (Jer 36:4, 32; 45:1).

Nahman Avigad published the first in 1979 ('Baruch the Scribe and Jerahmeel the King's Son', Biblical Archaeologist 42). He wrote that 'two persons mentioned in the Bible are positively identified with the persons named on two recently discovered seal-impressions'. A second impression of the same seal, bearing a fingerprint, was announced by Hershel Shanks in BAR in 1996.

Neither object has an excavated context. In 2014 Yuval Goren and Eran Arie (BASOR 372) published laboratory analyses of both bullae, compared them with more than 180 bullae, most of them from Jerusalem, and concluded the results point to modern production of both.

What the text says (summary)

(Belonging) to Berekhyahu / son of Neriyahu / the scribeOur literal rendering of the reading usually given as lbrkyhw / bn nryhw / hspr. The names and title are confirmed by the titles of Avigad 1979 and Goren and Arie 2014; the Hebrew letter-by-letter reading is not confirmed from a primary publication.

What the evidence shows

  • Inscription as published: 'to Berekhyahu son of Neriyahu the scribe' (names and title as given in Goren and Arie's 2014 title; Avigad's 1979 title renders it 'Baruch the Scribe').
  • Two bullae impressed by the same seal design are known; the second carries a fingerprint (Shanks, BAR 1996).
  • No recorded findspot for either; both came through the antiquities market.
  • Goren and Arie (BASOR 372, 2014) analysed both bullae and compared them with more than 180 bullae, most from Jerusalem; their abstract states the results point 'to the production of the two Berekhyahu bullae in modern times'.
  • Avigad (BA 42, 1979) presented the first bulla together with a bulla of 'Jerahmeel the king's son' (cf. Jer 36:26) as positive identifications of biblical persons.

Open questions

  • Authentic or forged? For: Avigad (Biblical Archaeologist 42, 1979) presented the first bulla as genuine and identified the owner with Jeremiah's scribe; Shanks (BAR 1996) presented the fingerprinted second bulla as a further impression. Against: Goren and Arie (BASOR 372, 2014) found, by laboratory comparison with more than 180 bullae, that the two Berekhyahu bullae were produced in modern times. Andrew Vaughn and Christopher Rollston (Near Eastern Archaeology 68, 2005) had already set out the case that market inscriptions with sensational biblical names need special scrutiny for forgery. No excavated Berekhyahu bulla exists to settle it.
  • Is 'Berekhyahu son of Neriyahu the scribe' the biblical Baruch? If genuine, name, patronym and title all match Jer 36:4, 32. But a forger would copy exactly those three features from the Bible, so on an unprovenanced object the match cannot count as evidence of authenticity.

The Find

  • DateClaimed late 7th-early 6th century BCE by palaeography. No archaeological context. Laboratory analysis published in 2014 points to modern manufacture (see questions).
  • How datedNeither bulla comes from a recorded excavation; both appeared through the antiquities market. The proposed Iron Age date rests only on letter forms and on the match with the biblical Baruch son of Neriah. Goren and Arie (BASOR 372, 2014) compared the two bullae with more than 180 bullae, most of them from Jerusalem, and concluded their results point 'to the production of the two Berekhyahu bullae in modern times'.
  • DiscoveredFirst bulla: antiquities market, published by Nahman Avigad (BA 1979) as 'recently discovered'; second bulla: published by Hershel Shanks (BAR, March/April 1996), Antiquities market; no excavator, Unknown. No findspot is documented for either bulla.
  • Now heldNot confirmed in a published museum record. The first bulla is widely reported as being in the Israel Museum and the second as in a private collection; no museum record was located.
  • MaterialUnbaked clay seal impressions (bullae)
  • ScriptPalaeo-Hebrew
  • LanguageHebrew

Biblical Passages

  • Jer 36:4, 26, 32'Baruch the son of Neriah' writes Jeremiah's scroll; 'Baruch the scribe'. The bulla names Berekhyahu (full form of Baruch) son of Neriyahu (full form of Neriah), the scribe. Exact correspondence of name, father and title, on an object whose authenticity is contested.
  • Jer 32:12-16Jeremiah gives the sealed deed of purchase to 'Baruch the son of Neriah', who stores it in an earthen vessel. That text describes sealed documents of exactly the kind bullae closed.
  • Jer 45:1-5Oracle to Baruch son of Neriah. Named person only; no further correspondence.
  • Jer 51:59'Seraiah the son of Neriah', apparently Baruch's brother. Not on the bulla; noted for the family name only.
