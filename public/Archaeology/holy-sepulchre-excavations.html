Church of the Holy Sepulchre excavations (Sapienza University of Rome, 2022-2025)

Excavation beneath the church floor: quarry, cultivated soil, tombs, Roman and Constantinian phases · 2022-2025

Overview

From 2022 to 2025 a Sapienza University of Rome team under Francesca Romana Stasolla excavated beneath the floor of the Church of the Holy Sepulchre while the floor was being restored. The project was approved by the three main communities that hold the church. The first season (May-November 2022, IAA licence G-20/2022) opened five areas in the north aisle and the northwest of the Rotunda. The 2023 season worked in the southeast Rotunda, in front of the Aedicule, and in the northeast ambulatory. The 2024 season covered the entrance, the southeast ambulatory and the Franciscan area. The 2025 report states that the excavations inside the church are complete.

The lowest levels are an ancient limestone quarry. The 2024 report says it 'extends over the entire area of the current church' and that its use was 'prolonged in time'. Above the quarry the 2022 report records a cultivation phase with remains of 'cereals, grapes, figs and gramineous plants', and damaged funerary structures on the bedrock. Later levels include the Constantinian church's northern wall and triporticus, the apse of the early Christian church with a Roman street beneath it, 4th-century coins, an 11th-century mosaic, and Crusader pillar foundations.

Public reports in 2025 (Times of Israel, March; Latin Patriarchate, July) highlighted olive and grapevine evidence from pollen and plant remains, and linked it to John 19:41's 'garden'. Stasolla also said that radiocarbon dating of that evidence had not yet been done.

What the evidence shows

  • Quarrying traces are 'visible all along the exposed rock surfaces', with varied depths and unfinished stones (HA-ESI 2022 season report).
  • A cultivation phase above the quarry with remains of cereals, grapes, figs and grasses, and evidence of olive and grape cultivation (HA-ESI 2022 season report).
  • Damaged funerary structures on the bedrock, below the Christian phases (HA-ESI).
  • Constantinian phase: the northern enclosing wall and triporticus of the 4th-century church, water channels and cisterns, and coins of Constantius II (337-361 CE) and Valens (r. 364-378 CE) (HA-ESI).
  • 2023: the apse of the early Christian church, with a Roman street running beneath it (Liber Annuus 73 abstract).
  • 2024: the quarry underlies the entire church and was worked over a long period (Liber Annuus 74 abstract).
  • As of March 2025, the plant evidence had been identified by pollen and archaeobotanical analysis, but radiocarbon testing 'has not yet been carried out' (Stasolla, quoted by Times of Israel).

Open questions

  • Is the cultivated soil a 1st-century garden? Stasolla (Times of Israel, March 2025; Latin Patriarchate, July 2025) links the olive and grapevine evidence to John 19:41. The published season report (HA-ESI) lists a cultivation phase with cereals, grapes, figs and grasses, and, as of March 2025, no radiocarbon date. So the layer is shown to be later than the quarry and earlier than the Christian construction, and nothing tighter yet.
  • Inside or outside the city? Stasolla: 'at the time of Jesus, the area was not part of the city yet', but it was inside the city by Hadrian's time (Times of Israel, March 2025). This fits John 19:20 and Heb 13:12 only if the relevant wall line is fixed. The Holy Sepulchre reports read here do not themselves fix the course of the 1st-century wall.
  • Which tomb? The Latin Patriarchate (July 2025) reports several rock-cut tombs and says one is 'possibly' the tomb of Joseph of Arimathea. The HA-ESI report records damaged funerary structures. Nothing inscribed ties any tomb to a named person.

Corrected from the earlier edition of this page
  • Headlines saying the dig 'confirms the Gospel garden' outrun the data. The cultivation layer was not radiocarbon-dated as of March 2025 (Stasolla), and the season report lists cereals, grapes, figs and grasses without a 1st-century date.
  • An earlier draft note calls the quarry 'Iron Age ... later cultivated' as a single sequence. The 2024 report says quarrying under the church was 'prolonged in time', so the quarry cannot be treated as a single Iron Age event followed directly by the garden.
  • Popular claims that the tomb of Joseph of Arimathea has been identified overstate the Latin Patriarchate's own 'possibly'. No inscription or other find names a tomb's owner.

The Find

  • DateSequence from quarrying (Iron Age onward, exploitation 'prolonged in time') through cultivation, Roman, Constantinian (4th century CE) and later phases. The cultivation layer has not yet been radiocarbon-dated.
  • How datedStratigraphy and finds in the preliminary reports, including 4th-century coins of Constantius II and Valens in Constantinian construction contexts (HA-ESI). Stasolla said in March 2025 that radiocarbon testing of the cultivation evidence 'has not yet been carried out' (Times of Israel).
  • DiscoveredExcavation campaign May-November 2022 (IAA licence G-20/2022), continued 2023 and 2024, completed inside the church in 2025, Francesca Romana Stasolla, Sapienza University of Rome, as part of the floor-restoration project agreed by the Greek Orthodox Patriarchate, the Custody of the Holy Land and the Armenian Patriarchate, Beneath the floor of the Church of the Holy Sepulchre (Anastasis rotunda, aisles, ambulatories, entrance), Old City of Jerusalem
  • Now heldIn situ (Church of the Holy Sepulchre, Jerusalem)

Biblical Passages

  • John 19:41-42'In the place where he was crucified there was a garden, and in the garden a new tomb'. The excavation shows cultivation (vines, figs, cereals; olive per the 2025 statements) and rock-cut burials over an old quarry. Neither is yet dated closely enough to put it in the 1st century.
  • John 19:17, 20; Heb 13:12The crucifixion is outside the city gate but near the city. Stasolla says the area was outside the city in Jesus' time and inside it by Hadrian's time.
  • Matt 27:60; Mark 15:46A tomb 'hewn out in the rock'. Tombs cut into the old quarry face are attested beneath the church (Latin Patriarchate 2025; HA-ESI).
