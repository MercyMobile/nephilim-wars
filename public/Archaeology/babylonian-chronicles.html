Babylonian Chronicles

Year-by-year cuneiform chronicles, including Nebuchadnezzar's capture of Jerusalem on 2 Adar 597 BCE

Overview

The Babylonian Chronicles are clay tablets that record Babylonian and neighbouring history year by year in a terse, standard format: regnal year, month, what the king did. The tablets relevant to the Bible are in the British Museum. D. J. Wiseman published four of the Neo-Babylonian ones in Chronicles of Chaldaean Kings (1956), BM 21946 among them. A. K. Grayson's Assyrian and Babylonian Chronicles (1975) numbered the corpus ABC 1-25.

BM 21946 (ABC 5) covers Nebuchadnezzar's first eleven years. It records the defeat of the Egyptian army at Carchemish (605), Nebuchadnezzar's accession, the western campaigns, a drawn battle with Egypt (601), and in year 7 a march to the Hatti-land, a siege of 'the city of Judah', its capture on the 2nd of Adar, the capture of its king, and the installation of 'a king of his own choice'. BM 21901 (ABC 3) records the fall of Nineveh (612) and, in 609, Aššur-uballiṭ and 'a great army of Egypt' crossing the Euphrates against Harran. ABC 1 (BM 92502) records Shalmaneser V's accession and the destruction of a city read Šamara'in/Shabara'in.

The chronicles do not mention the 587/586 destruction of Jerusalem. BM 21946 stops after Nebuchadnezzar's 11th year (594/593), and the next Neo-Babylonian chronicle tablet in Wiseman's set (BM 25124) deals with Neriglissar's campaign of 557/556.

What the text says (summary)

He encamped against the city of Judah, and in the month of Adar, on the second day, he seized the city and captured the king. A king of his own choosing he appointed in it; its heavy tribute he took and brought into Babylon.Our literal translation of BM 21946 rev. 12-13 as transliterated by D. J. Wiseman, Chronicles of Chaldaean Kings (1956), p. 72 (ina muḫḫi āl Iaḫudu iddi-ma ina araḫ Addari ūmu 2-KAM āla iṣṣabat šarra iktašad / šarra ša libbišu ina libbi ipteqid bilassa kabittu ilqâm-ma ana Bābili ultērib)

What the evidence shows

  • BM 21946 rev. 11-13 (Wiseman's transliteration): 'Year 7, month Kislev: the king of Akkad mustered his troops, marched to the Hatti-land, encamped against the city of Judah (āl ia-a-ḫu-du), and on day 2 of Adar seized the city and captured the king; he appointed there a king of his own heart, took its heavy tribute and brought it to Babylon.'
  • The chronicle names neither Jerusalem nor Jehoiachin nor Zedekiah: 'city of Judah', 'the king', 'a king of his own choice'.
  • Wiseman (1956) converts 2 Adar of Nebuchadnezzar's year 7 to 15/16 March 597 BCE.
  • BM 21946 obv. 1-8: in Nabopolassar's 21st year (605), crown prince Nebuchadnezzar defeats the Egyptian army at Carchemish.
  • BM 21946 rev. 5-7: in year 4 (601) the king marches to Egypt; 'in open battle they smote the breast of each other and inflicted great havoc on each other'; the king of Akkad returns to Babylon.
  • BM 21901 l. 66-67: 'In the month of Tammuz Aššur-uballiṭ, king of Assyria, a great army of Egypt ... crossed the river [and] marched against the city of Harran to conquer it' (Wiseman 1956 translation, 609 BCE).
  • ABC 1 (BM 92502) i 27-28: 'On the twenty-fifth day of Tebet Shalmaneser took his seat on the throne in Assyria, and the city of Shabarain was destroyed' (Rogers 1912 translation).
  • BM 92502 is 'a copy made in the twenty-second year of Darius from an older and damaged text' (Wiseman 1956, p. 2).
  • Tablet provenance: Babylon on internal evidence; no recorded excavation context (Wiseman 1956, pp. 4-5).

Open questions

  • Seventh year or eighth? The chronicle dates the capture of the 'city of Judah' to Nebuchadnezzar's 7th year, Adar. Jeremiah 52:28 counts 3,023 Judeans deported 'in the seventh year'; 2 Kings 24:12 says Jehoiachin was taken 'in the eighth year of his reign'. One explanation is different year-counting (accession-year vs. non-accession-year reckoning, or a spring-versus-autumn new year); the texts themselves state only the numbers.
  • When were the chronicles written? Wiseman (1956) held that the script allows anything from near-contemporary to late Achaemenid. Yuval Levavi (JAOS 145, 2025) argues ABC 3-5 form a complete trilogy composed soon after the last events in ABC 5, around the late 590s BCE. A late-590s composition would make the Jerusalem entry close to contemporary.
  • Why no 587/586? The destruction of Jerusalem in Zedekiah's 11th year falls after the last year on BM 21946. Levavi (2025) argues ABC 3-5 are complete as they stand, which would make the gap the end of that composition. The Livius.org introduction to ABC 5 instead assumes a 'lost sequel of this chronicle'. Neither position has a surviving tablet behind it.

Since 2024

  • 2025-08Yuval Levavi (JAOS 145) argues that chronicles ABC 3-5 form a complete trilogy with no missing sections, with ABC 5 (the Jerusalem chronicle) as its centerpiece, composed around the late 590s BCE, shortly after the last events it records. [5]

The Find

  • DateEvents: ABC 1 covers c. 747-668 BCE; the Neo-Babylonian series (ABC 2-7) covers 626-539 BCE, with BM 21946 covering 605-594 BCE. Tablets: undated by script (Wiseman 1956); the ABC 1 copy (BM 92502) is dated by its colophon to the 22nd year of Darius (500/499 BCE if Darius I). Levavi (2025) argues ABC 3-5 were composed in the late 590s BCE.
  • How datedEach entry is dated by the Babylonian king's regnal year and month, e.g. 'the seventh year, the month Kislev ... the second day of the month Adar'. Converting to the Julian calendar (2 Adar, year 7 of Nebuchadnezzar = 15/16 March 597 BCE) follows Wiseman (1956). The tablets' own date of writing: Wiseman notes the script 'does not of itself allow any precise dating' and could fall anywhere from nearly contemporary with the events to the end of Achaemenid rule; BM 92502 has a Darius-year-22 colophon.
  • DiscoveredAcquired by the British Museum in the 19th century; BM 92502 published 1887 (Pinches; Winckler and Strassmaier); BM 21901 published 1923 (C. J. Gadd); BM 21946 and the other Nabopolassar-Nebuchadnezzar tablets published 1956 (D. J. Wiseman), British Museum collection (not excavated in a recorded context), Babylon 'so far as can be judged from the internal evidence' (Wiseman 1956); part of ABC 1 or its sources was copied at Sippar
  • Now heldBritish Museum, London: BM 21946 (Jerusalem/Nebuchadnezzar chronicle), BM 21901 (Fall of Nineveh), BM 22047, BM 25127, BM 25124, BM 92502 (ABC 1), BM 35382 (Nabonidus Chronicle)
  • MaterialClay tablets
  • ScriptLate/Neo-Babylonian cuneiform, small script
  • LanguageAkkadian (Babylonian)

Biblical Passages

  • 2 Kgs 24:10-17; 2 Chr 36:9-10Nebuchadnezzar besieges Jerusalem, takes King Jehoiachin, carries off the treasures, and makes Mattaniah/Zedekiah king. The chronicle: siege of 'the city of Judah', capture of 'the king', appointment of 'a king of his own choice', heavy tribute to Babylon. The events match; the chronicle supplies the day (2 Adar).
  • Jer 52:28'In the seventh year, three thousand Jews and three and twenty' deported. The chronicle also dates the capture to Nebuchadnezzar's 7th year.
  • Jer 46:2'The army of Pharaoh-necho ... by the river Euphrates in Carchemish, which Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon smote in the fourth year of Jehoiakim.' BM 21946 obv. 1-8 records Nebuchadnezzar's defeat of the Egyptian army at Carchemish in Nabopolassar's 21st year (605).
  • 2 Kgs 23:29; 2 Chr 35:20Necho goes up 'against/to the king of Assyria to the river Euphrates' and Josiah dies at Megiddo. BM 21901 has Aššur-uballiṭ with 'a great army of Egypt' crossing the river against Harran in 609. The chronicle does not mention Josiah.
  • 2 Kgs 24:1, 7Jehoiakim's three years of vassalage and the note that the king of Egypt 'came not again any more out of his land'. The chronicle's 601 clash with Egypt ('great havoc on each other') and Nebuchadnezzar's year at home rebuilding his chariotry are the Babylonian side of that period (our reading).
