Galilee Boat (Jesus Boat)

Wooden hull of a Lake Kinneret boat recovered from the shore mud · Early Roman period

Overview

In 1986, with the lake level low, the hull of an ancient boat was exposed in the mud of the Kinneret shore near Kibbutz Ginnosar. The Israel Department of Antiquities and Museums excavated it in a rescue operation directed by Shelley Wachsmann and Kurt Raveh; the excavation report is 'Atiqot (English Series) 19 (1990).

The team kept the hull intact rather than dismantling it, because the tenon joints and the corroded iron nails formed an 'iron-wood continuum' that dismantling would have broken. The boat was moved about 500 m north to Kibbutz Ginnosar and conserved in a pool with a two-step polyethylene glycol treatment (PEG 600, then PEG 4000). Eleven years passed from excavation to the end of conservation, and three more to build its exhibition hall (Cohen 2005).

It is a working boat with a long life: many repairs, timbers reused from older boats, some parts removed in antiquity, and finally abandoned on the shore (Cohen 2005; Werker 2005). Nothing on or in it names an owner or links it to any person.

What the evidence shows

  • Hull built of 12 plant genera; strakes 92% cedar, frames 75% unworked oak branches (Werker, 'Atiqot 50, 2005).
  • Cedar is the one wood not local to the lake; it was brought from further north (Werker 2005).
  • Several timbers are repairs or reused from older boats; some parts were removed in antiquity (Cohen 2005).
  • Iron nails and staples survive, corroded into the wood (Cohen & Roman 1990; Cohen 2005).
  • The boat was found abandoned on the shore, not wrecked in deep water (Cohen 2005).
  • No inscription, no personal objects tied to an owner are reported in the sources read.

Open questions

  • Exact date: the 1990 report contains a radiocarbon study (Carmi 1990); the individual C-14 determinations and any supporting pottery evidence are not accessible for this entry, so no narrower date is asserted here.
  • Original vs. repair timbers: Werker (2005) states it is 'difficult to decide, based on anatomical structure, which of the timbers differing from the main scheme of conifer/oak wood was originally used ... and which was added later as a replacement' - the boat's building history is only partly recoverable.
  • Link to the Gospels: Wachsmann (The Sea of Galilee Boat, 1995, ch. 'Galilean Seafaring in the Gospels') compares the hull with Gospel boat scenes; the boat itself carries no evidence connecting it to Jesus or the disciples. The 'Jesus Boat' name is a media label (Cohen 2005: 'dubbed the "Jesus Boat" by the media').

The Find

  • DateRoughly 2,000 years old (Cohen 2005); the report's radiocarbon range was not confirmed in the sources cited here
  • How datedThe excavation report includes a radiocarbon study (I. Carmi, 'Radiocarbon Dating of the Boat', in Wachsmann et al., 'Atiqot [ES] 19, 1990, pp. 127-128). Its figures, and any pottery or construction-typology arguments in that report, have not been reviewed for this entry and are not repeated here. The IAA conservation report states only that the boat was found 'after about 2000 years' (Cohen, 'Atiqot 50, 2005).
  • Discovered1986 (exposed by the drop in lake level; excavated the same year), Excavation directed by Shelley Wachsmann with Kurt Raveh for the Israel Department of Antiquities and Museums; conservation by Orna Cohen, NW shore of the Sea of Galilee, about 500 m south of Kibbutz Ginnosar
  • Now heldYigal Allon Centre ('Man in the Galilee Museum'), Kibbutz Ginnosar, in a purpose-built exhibition hall with environmental control (Cohen 2005)
  • MaterialWood of twelve plant genera; 105 of 113 strakes cedar (Cedrus), one pine; 45 of 60 frames unworked oak branches; also carob, redbud, hawthorn, laurel, Atlantic pistachio, plane, sycamore fig, willow, Christ-thorn; iron nails and staples; mortise-and-tenon joined planking

Biblical Passages

  • Mark 1:16-20; Matt 4:18-22Fishermen on this lake working from boats with hired men (Mark 1:20). The find shows what one lake boat of the period was built of and how it was repaired; it is a type-specimen, not a boat named in the text.
  • Mark 4:35-41A boat on the lake large enough for Jesus and the disciples, with a stern where Jesus slept 'on the cushion'. The hull size is what makes the comparison; the text gives no dimensions.
  • Luke 5:1-11; John 21:1-8Fishing from boats near the shore of the lake; same setting and vessel class, nothing more specific.
