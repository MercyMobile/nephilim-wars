Tall el-Hammam Destruction Layer

Middle Bronze Age city in the southern Jordan Valley ended by a burned destruction layer · c. 1650 BCE (radiocarbon); airburst paper retracted 2025

Overview

Tall el-Hammam is a large two-tiered tell in the southern Jordan Valley north-east of the Dead Sea, excavated since 2005 by a team led by Steven Collins. The excavators describe a fortified city occupied from the Chalcolithic through the Middle Bronze Age, ended by a thick burned destruction layer and followed by a long gap in regional settlement.

In 2021 T. Bunch and 20 co-authors argued in Scientific Reports that a Tunguska-size cosmic airburst caused the destruction, citing shocked quartz, melted pottery and mudbrick, spherules and metal grains, and linked it to the Sodom story. S. J. Jaret and R. S. Harris (Sci. Rep. 12, 2022) rebutted the mineralogy and geochemistry; M. Boslough and A. Bruno (Sci. Rep. 15, 2025) rebutted the Tunguska comparison. On 24 April 2025 the journal retracted the 2021 article.

What remains is the destruction layer itself - burned debris up to c. 1.5 m thick with high-temperature material - and the argument about what caused it and whether the city is Sodom. The retraction removes the published support for the airburst cause; it does not remove the destroyed city.

What the evidence shows

  • Destruction 'debris matrix' up to ~1.5 m thick across most of the tall, capped in places by a thin laminated 'blow-over layer' with plaster fragments, limestone spherules and charcoal (Bunch et al. 2021).
  • 26 radiocarbon dates from the layer; 20 combine to 1661 ± 21 BCE (Bunch et al. 2021, OxCal 4.4/IntCal20).
  • Site size claimed: 36 ha fortified + ~30 ha lower settlement, described as >4x Tall Nimrin and >5x Jericho (Bunch et al. 2021).
  • Materials reported: shocked-quartz candidates, pottery with melted glassy surfaces, melted mudbrick, Fe/Si-rich spherules, metal grains (Bunch et al. 2021) - each of these interpretations is what Jaret & Harris (2022) contest.
  • The 2021 article received Author Corrections (22 Feb 2022; 22 May 2023) and a Matters Arising (25 March 2022) before its retraction (Scientific Reports article page).
  • Retraction note (Sci. Rep. 15:14291, online 24 Apr 2025): 'the claims that an airburst event destroyed the Middle Bronze Age city of Tall el-Hammam appear to not be sufficiently supported by the data in the Article.'

Open questions

  • Cause of the destruction: Bunch et al. (Sci. Rep. 11:18632, 2021) read the melted materials and shocked-quartz candidates as proof of a >Tunguska airburst above 2000 °C. Jaret & Harris ('No mineralogic or geochemical evidence of impact at Tall el-Hammam', Sci. Rep. 12:5189, 2022) contest the mineralogical and geochemical identifications and methods. Boslough & Bruno (Sci. Rep. 15:13869, 2025) show the Tunguska comparison inherited overestimates of temperature, wind speed and blast. Eleven of the original authors (LeCompte, Burleigh, Hermes, Wolbach, Kletetschka, Witwer, Howard, Moore, Kennett, West, Silvia) disagree with the retraction (retraction note). Other causes (warfare, fire, earthquake) are not tested in the retracted paper; the layer's cause is presently unestablished in the peer-reviewed record.
  • Is it Sodom? Bunch et al. (2021) note the 'ongoing debate as to whether Tall el-Hammam could be the biblical city of Sodom (Silvia and references therein)' and set it aside. The identification rests on geography - Gen 13:10-12 places Sodom in 'the kikkar of the Jordan', which the excavators equate with the plain north-east of the Dead Sea. Rival proposals place the Cities of the Plain south of the Dead Sea; their publications are not reviewed here. No site has produced an inscription naming Sodom.
  • Date vs. biblical chronology: a c. 1650 BCE destruction is later than the c. 2000 BCE date for Abraham often derived from the Masoretic numbers (1 Kgs 6:1 + Gen/Exod figures). Anyone equating the two must either move Abraham or reject the equation; the stratigraphy itself says nothing about Abraham.

The Find

  • DateDestruction layer modelled at 1661 ± 21 BCE (1686-1632 BCE), rounded to c. 1650 BCE, Middle Bronze Age II
  • How datedBunch et al. (2021) report 26 radiocarbon dates from the destruction layer; OxCal 4.4 (IntCal20) 'Combine' found 20 of 26 statistically synchronous, giving 1661 ± 21 BCE. The radiocarbon section is part of the article later retracted; the retraction note criticises the mineralogy/geochemistry and the Tunguska comparison, not specifically the dates.
  • DiscoveredExcavations since 2005, Tall el-Hammam Excavation Project led by Steven Collins (Trinity Southwest University; Veritas International University), under the Department of Antiquities of Jordan (season reports by Collins, Byers and Kobs), Tall el-Hammam, southern Jordan Valley (the Middle Ghor / 'Kikkar of the Jordan'), north-east of the Dead Sea, Jordan
  • Now heldIn situ, Jordan
  • Dimensions36 ha within the fortifications plus c. 30 ha of lower settlement, per Bunch et al. 2021; destruction 'debris matrix' up to c. 1.5 m thick

Biblical Passages

  • Genesis 13:10-12The 'kikkar of the Jordan' (the Jordan disk/plain) is where Lot settles and where Sodom lies. Tall el-Hammam is the largest Bronze Age tell in the plain north-east of the Dead Sea (Bunch et al. 2021). This is a geographic correspondence, not an identification.
  • Genesis 19:24-28A city in that plain ended in a thick burned layer with high-temperature material and was followed by a long regional abandonment (per the excavators). The biblical account of fire from heaven and smoke 'like a furnace' matches a fiery destruction in general terms; the cosmic-airburst explanation that tied them tightly has been retracted.
