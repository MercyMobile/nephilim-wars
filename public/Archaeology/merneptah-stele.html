Merneptah Stele

Egyptian victory hymn naming Israel · Year 5 of Merneptah (c. 1208 BCE)

Overview

A large stone stela reused by Merneptah: the front carries a text and scene of Amenhotep III, which Merneptah's workmen took from Amenhotep's temple; Merneptah's own 28-line inscription is cut on the back. Petrie found it in 1896 in Merneptah's mortuary temple at western Thebes and recorded it as a single block of dark grey syenite over 10 feet tall. A fragmentary duplicate of Merneptah's text stood at Karnak.

Most of the text is a poetic celebration of Merneptah's Year-5 victory over the Libyans. The closing strophe (lines 26-28) turns to the Levant and lists defeated foreigners: Canaan, Ashkelon, Gezer, Yenoam, Israel, and Kharu (Hurru). Israel is the only name in that list written with the classifier for a people rather than the throw-stick-and-hill-country classifier used for Ashkelon, Gezer and Yenoam.

This is the earliest securely read occurrence of the name Israel outside the Bible. Breasted (1906) already noted that the Israel clause is short and built from stock phrases also applied to other defeated peoples.

In its own words

Carried off is Askalon; Seized upon is Gezer; Yenoam is made as a thing not existing. Israel is desolated, his seed is not; Palestine has become a widow for Egypt.J. H. Breasted, Ancient Records of Egypt III (1906) §617, pp. 263-264

What the evidence shows

  • Text dated Year 5 of Merneptah, 3rd month of Shemu, day 3 (Breasted ARE III §607).
  • Stela is a reused Amenhotep III monument; Merneptah's text is on the back (Petrie 1897 §24; Breasted ARE III §602 n.).
  • Petrie's measurement: 10 ft 3 in x 5 ft 4 in x 13 in, dark grey syenite with quartz veins, 'condition perfect' apart from one chip.
  • Line 27 spells the name y-s-r-i-a-r (Breasted: 'Israel (Y-s-r-'-r)').
  • Ashkelon, Gezer and Yenoam carry the foreign-land/city classifier; Israel carries the classifier for a people (man + woman + plural strokes).
  • The Israel clause 'his seed (pr.t) is not' is a formula also used of other defeated enemies (Breasted ARE III §603-604).
  • A second, fragmentary copy of the text exists at Karnak (Breasted ARE III §602 n., citing Dümichen and Erman).

Open questions

  • Where is Israel and what kind of group is it? M. G. Hasel (BASOR 296, 1994) reads the closing strophe as a structured ring in which Israel sits inside Canaan/Hurru and, from the people-classifier plus the meaning of pr.t, argues Israel was a sedentary/agricultural socio-ethnic group. Readers who stress the scribal inconsistency of classifiers in Ramesside texts treat the people-classifier as weaker evidence of social form; the stone itself proves only that Egyptian scribes classed Israel as a people, not a city-state.
  • Does Israel appear in pictures too? F. J. Yurco (JARCE 23, 1986) reattributed battle reliefs on the Cour de la Cachette wall at Karnak from Ramesses II to Merneptah and matched them scene-for-scene to Ashkelon, Gezer, Yenoam and Israel, identifying Israel with Canaanite-dressed chariot-less foes. D. B. Redford (IEJ 36, 1986) rejected the reattribution; A. F. Rainey (BAR 17/6, 1991) accepted the Merneptah date but argued Israel is the scene of Shasu-dressed captives. The reliefs carry no captions naming Israel, so every identification is a reading.
  • Merneptah's own Levantine campaign: some doubt Merneptah campaigned in Canaan at all and read the strophe as conventional boast; Breasted (1906, §605-606) pointed to the Year 3 border journal and the title 'binder of Gezer' as independent indications that he did.
  • What 'his seed is not' means: Breasted (1906) showed it is a stock phrase for devastation (grain/offspring), not a reference to killing Hebrew boys as some 1890s writers claimed.

The Find

  • DateYear 5 of Merneptah, 3rd month of Shemu, day 3 (c. 1208 BCE on the conventional Egyptian chronology)
  • How datedThe text is dated internally to Merneptah's regnal year 5 (Breasted, ARE III §607: 'Year 5, third month of the third season, third day'). The BCE figure depends on which absolute chronology is used for the 19th Dynasty; the stone gives only the regnal year.
  • Discovered1896, W. M. Flinders Petrie (text first edited by Wilhelm Spiegelberg), Ruins of Merneptah's mortuary temple, western Thebes
  • Now heldEgyptian Museum, Cairo (JE 31408)
  • MaterialDark grey syenite (granitic stone) 'infiltrated with quartz veins' (Petrie)
  • Dimensions10 ft 3 in high, 5 ft 4 in wide, 13 in thick (Petrie 1897, §24) = c. 3.12 x 1.63 x 0.33 m
  • ScriptEgyptian hieroglyphs
  • LanguageLate Middle / Late Egyptian (Ramesside literary hymn)

Biblical Passages

  • Judges 1-5; JoshuaThe stela places a people called Israel in the southern Levant by Merneptah's Year 5, alongside Canaanite towns (Ashkelon, Gezer) that the Bible also names. It says nothing about how or when Israel arrived.
  • Judges 1:29; 1 Kings 9:16Gezer is listed as 'seized'; the Bible reports Gezer remaining Canaanite until an Egyptian pharaoh took it for Solomon. The stela is independent evidence of Egyptian military interest in Gezer, not of the specific biblical event.
  • Exodus 1-15Reading, not finding: the stela shows Israel already in Canaan by c. 1208 BCE; anyone placing the Exodus must fit it before that date. The stone does not mention an exodus.
