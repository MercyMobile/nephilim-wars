Hazor Destruction Layer

Fiery end of the largest Late Bronze Age city in Canaan · 13th century BCE

Overview

Hazor is the largest Bronze Age tell in the southern Levant. It has a high upper city and a vast enclosed lower city. Yigael Yadin excavated it in 1955-1958 and 1968. Amnon Ben-Tor renewed the work in 1990 as the Selz Foundation Hazor Excavations in Memory of Yigael Yadin, which continues today, and new teams re-opened the lower city in 2023.

The last Canaanite city, of the 13th century BCE, ended in a fire. J. Ebeling describes it as 'so intense that it cracked the basalt architectural elements' and says it left ash up to about three feet deep. It destroyed the ceremonial palace (Building 7050) on the acropolis and the gate shrine. Statues of Canaanite and Egyptian rulers and deities were found deliberately mutilated.

Who burned Hazor is argued from those remains. Yadin and Ben-Tor argue for the Israelites of Joshua 11. S. Zuckerman argues for an internal social and ideological crisis. Egyptian and Sea Peoples scenarios have also been weighed. Bechar et al. (2021) combined stratigraphy, radiocarbon and imported objects and proposed two possible scenarios for the date and process of the end.

What the evidence shows

  • A destruction by fire at the end of the Late Bronze Age affected both the upper and lower cities (Selz excavation project; Ebeling 2003).
  • The fire was hot enough to crack basalt architectural elements, and ash layers reach about three feet (Ebeling 2003).
  • Statues of Canaanite and Egyptian rulers and deities were deliberately mutilated (Ebeling 2003, summarizing the Ben-Tor excavations).
  • The ceremonial palace (Building 7050) on the acropolis burned with its contents in place: bronze statues, ivory, jewellery, seals and a lion-headed rhyton (BAS summary of Ben-Tor 2013).
  • Radiocarbon from the renewed excavations, together with Aegean-type and Egyptian objects, did not produce a single agreed date. Bechar et al. (2021) present 'two different possible scenarios' for when and how the city ended.
  • The 2023-present renewed excavation of Area H in the northern lower city found much of that sector uninhabited in the Late Bronze Age. This contradicts the earlier view of a continuously occupied lower city (Shalvi & Bechar, Tel Aviv 52, 2025).
  • There is no inscription naming a destroyer. Cuneiform fragments from Hazor exist, but none describes the fall.

Open questions

  • Who destroyed Hazor? A. Ben-Tor (BAR 39:4, 2013; see Ebeling 2003) argues for the Israelites. The statues of Egyptian kings and Canaanite gods were mutilated, which he says neither Egyptians nor Canaanites would do to their own icons, and Sea Peoples material is absent at Hazor. S. Zuckerman (Journal of Mediterranean Archaeology 20/1, 2007) reads the same destruction as the product of 'social, political, cultural and ideological circumstances' inside a city already in decline, including termination rituals. In both cases the evidence is the pattern of burning and mutilation, and no text names the attacker.
  • Which century, and which stratum? I. Finkelstein re-interpreted the Late Bronze sequence. Ben-Tor & Zuckerman (BASOR 350, 2008) defended a scheme close to Yadin's against him, using Yadin's published data and the renewed excavations. Bechar et al. (2021) keep two scenarios open for the date and process. How Joshua 11 lines up in time with the burning depends on which scenario holds.
  • How big was the Late Bronze city? Shalvi & Bechar (2025) found much of the northern lower city empty in the LB, so the size of the city at the moment of destruction is being revised downward in at least that sector.

Corrected from the earlier edition of this page
  • The old entry described a city 'covering approximately 10 hectares'. Hazor's upper and lower cities together cover about 200 acres, roughly 80 ha (excavation project). It is the largest Bronze Age site in the country, not a 10 ha town.
  • The old entry gave the destruction as 'c. 1200 BCE'. The project and Ebeling place it in the 13th century BCE (Yadin c. 1230 BCE), and Bechar et al. (2021) leave two scenarios open. '1200' is not the excavators' date.
  • The old entry claimed Hazor is 'the only major Canaanite city in the region that exhibits evidence of such a systematic destruction'. No source consulted makes that claim, and it is not a finding of the excavations.
  • The old entry listed 'scattered weaponry' as destruction evidence. The reported evidence is intense fire, cracked basalt, deep ash and mutilated statues. No weapon scatter is reported in the sources consulted.
  • The old entry listed 'a sophisticated water system' among the features of the destroyed Canaanite city. None of the sources consulted describes a water system in the Late Bronze city. Hazor's well-known water shaft belongs to the Iron Age (Israelite) city built over it; that dating has not been re-checked for this entry.
  • The old entry said the evidence 'confirms violent conquest'. The violence is confirmed. The identity of the attacker is argued from indirect evidence (statue mutilation) and remains disputed (Ben-Tor vs. Zuckerman).
  • The old entry gave the dig dates as '1955-1958, Yadin' only. Yadin also dug in 1968, and the Selz excavations have run annually since 1990, with renewed lower-city work from 2023.

The Find

  • Date13th century BCE. The precise date within the century is disputed; Yadin put it c. 1230 BCE. Bechar et al. (2021) offer two radiocarbon-based scenarios.
  • How datedPottery and imported Aegean-type and Egyptian objects in the burned levels, and a radiocarbon series from the renewed excavations (Bechar, Ben-Tor, Wachtel, Ben-Tor et al., Ägypten und Levante 31, 2021). No text found at the site dates the destruction.
  • Discovered1955-1958 and 1968 (Yadin); 1990-present (Selz Foundation Hazor Excavations in Memory of Yigael Yadin); 2023-present (renewed work in the lower city), Yigael Yadin (Hebrew University); Amnon Ben-Tor, later Shlomit Bechar and Igor Kreimerman (Selz excavations); Univ. of Haifa lower-city team, Tel Hazor (Tell el-Qedah), north of the Sea of Galilee, Upper Galilee, Israel
  • Now heldIn situ, Tel Hazor National Park, Israel
  • MaterialBurned mudbrick and basalt architecture, ash layers, smashed and mutilated statuary
  • DimensionsUpper city (acropolis) plus lower city: about 200 acres (≈80 ha) in all, per the excavation project

Biblical Passages

  • Josh 11:10-11, 13'Hazor beforetime was the head of all those kingdoms… and he burnt Hazor with fire… save Hazor only; that did Joshua burn.' The text says Hazor was burned, and was singled out as burned, and the site has a massive fire ending the Late Bronze city. The text does not give a date, and the site does not name the attacker.
  • Deut 7:5; 12:3The command to hew down the graven images of the gods. Ben-Tor points to the mutilated statues as the behaviour of an iconoclastic attacker. The mutilation is the finding; its link to these commands is his reading.
  • Judg 4:2, 23-24'Jabin king of Canaan, that reigned in Hazor', destroyed by Israel. This is a second biblical account of a king of Hazor being overthrown. If both accounts refer to the same 13th-century destruction, only one burning is visible in the Late Bronze strata. How to reconcile Joshua 11 with Judges 4 is an interpretive question the stratigraphy does not answer.
  • 1 Kgs 9:15Solomon builds Hazor, Megiddo and Gezer. The Iron Age city with its six-chambered gate and casemate wall (attributed by the project to the 10th century BCE) is built over the burned Canaanite city.
  • 2 Kgs 15:29Tiglath-pileser III takes Hazor in 732 BCE. The project reports the destruction of the Iron Age city in that campaign.
