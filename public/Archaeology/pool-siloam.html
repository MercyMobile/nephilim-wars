Pool of Siloam

Reservoir at the south end of the City of David · dam c. 800 BCE; stepped pool of the late Second Temple period

Overview

The Gihon Spring, Jerusalem's only perennial source, lies in the Kidron Valley on the east side of the City of David. Its water was brought to the south end of the ridge, first through an open or semi-open channel (Channel II) and later through the rock-cut Siloam Tunnel. At the south end a reservoir formed behind a massive dam across the mouth of the Tyropoeon Valley. The name 'Siloam' (Hebrew Shiloah, 'sent') is attached to this whole system and to more than one pool built there over time.

In June 2004, during pipe repair work, Ronny Reich and Eli Shukron exposed stone steps of a large stepped pool south-east of the Byzantine church and pool long pointed out to pilgrims. They identified it as the Pool of Siloam of the late Second Temple period, the pool named in John 9. Most of it lay under an orchard; after 2022 the IAA began exposing more of the site.

In 2025 a radiocarbon study (Regev, Szanton, Vukosavovic, Berko, Shalev, Uziel, Mintz, Regev and Boaretto, PNAS) dated straw and twigs in the mortar of the dam to about 800 BCE, the reign of Jehoash or Amaziah of Judah, and argued that the Gihon Spring tower, Channel II and the reservoir formed one building project. The IAA's own 'Atiqot reports on the dam and pool area show that the dating is not settled: a radiocarbon date on dam plaster came out Persian-Hellenistic, against a stratigraphic date of the 7th century-586 BCE.

What the evidence shows

  • Stepped pool exposed June 2004 by Reich and Shukron during water-pipe repair south of the Temple Mount; trapezoidal, corners slightly over 90 degrees, one side c. 225 ft (BAS).
  • Dam across the Tyropoeon outlet: vertical and slanting sections, at least 19 m long, 11 m high, 10 m wide (PNAS 2025).
  • Dam mortar: 3 uncharred straw samples and 1 charred-twig sample, combined 805-795 BCE (68.3%) / 809-792 BCE (95.4%); passes chi-squared test (T = 7.5, 5% = 7.8) (PNAS 2025).
  • Earlier date estimates for the dam by various excavators ranged from the 8th to the 1st century BCE, including one radiocarbon date of the 4th-3rd century BCE (PNAS 2025 summary).
  • Plaster from the outer face of the dam wall was radiocarbon-dated to the Persian and Hellenistic periods, which the dating report calls 'difficult to explain' against the stratigraphic date of the 7th century-586 BCE ('Atiqot 101).
  • Siloam Tunnel floor plaster: a wood sample 810-780 BCE and a short-lived plant 770-550 BCE (Frumkin et al., as summarized in PNAS 2025).
  • A separate plastered pool just east of the Siloam Pool, along the outer dam wall, is dated by pottery and coins to the later 1st century CE, probably the last year of the Great Revolt ('Atiqot 101, Greenhut and Mazor).
  • Szanton ('Atiqot 113) identifies the Silwan Pool (the 2004 stepped pool) with the Siloam Pool and Birkat el-Hamra with 'Solomon's Pool' of Josephus, all Early Roman.

Open questions

  • When was the reservoir dam built? Regev et al. (PNAS 122/35, 2025) date it to c. 800 BCE from four short-lived plant samples in the mortar, arguing that fresh straw would not have been stored for long before use. E. Boaretto's report on the pool plaster ('Atiqot 101, 2025) got Persian-Hellenistic radiocarbon dates from plaster on the outer dam wall, while the excavators' pottery and stratigraphy pointed to the 7th century-586 BCE. The two may date different parts or repairs of the dam; neither report shows that yet.
  • Which pool is 'the' Pool of Siloam of Isaiah, Nehemiah and John? The reservoir behind the c. 800 BCE dam is a candidate for the Iron Age pool (Isa 22:9-11 'lower pool', 'reservoir between the two walls'). The 2004 stepped pool is Early Roman. Szanton ('Atiqot 113, 2024) argues from the excavated plan and the written sources that the Silwan Pool = Siloam Pool (for ritual immersion of pilgrims) and Birkat el-Hamra = Solomon's Pool (an open swimming pool). Y. Elitzur (PEQ 140, 2008) argued the Siloam Pool was itself the swimming pool known as 'Solomon's Pool'. The texts give names; the ground gives several pools.
  • Whether an Iron Age pool lies under the 2004 stepped pool is unknown; the later construction would have removed or buried it, and no excavation has yet reported one there.

The Find

  • DateTwo different things carry the name. (1) The monumental dam that created the reservoir: radiocarbon 805-795 BCE (68.3%), 809-792 BCE (95.4%) (Regev et al., PNAS 2025). (2) The stepped pool found in 2004: late Second Temple period (Reich and Shukron), destroyed around 70 CE
  • How datedDam: four radiocarbon dates on uncharred straw and charred twigs from the mortar of dam walls W001 and W114 (RTD 12351, 12352, 12353, 12948), statistically combined. Stepped pool: pottery, coins and architecture reported by the excavators; the detailed coin evidence for its construction date has not been checked for this entry.
  • DiscoveredJune 2004 (stepped pool); dam faces exposed in several excavations, dated 2025, Ronny Reich and Eli Shukron (Israel Antiquities Authority), during work to repair a water/sewer pipe; later IAA and Tel Aviv University excavations (N. Szanton, J. Uziel and others), Southern end of the City of David ridge, Silwan, Jerusalem, where the Tyropoeon Valley meets the Kidron
  • Now heldIn situ, City of David, Silwan, Jerusalem
  • MaterialStepped pool: dressed stone steps over an earlier plastered phase. Dam: stone walls bonded with mortar
  • DimensionsStepped pool: one side c. 225 ft (c. 69 m), trapezoidal, steps on at least three sides (BAS). Dam: at least 19 m long, 11 m high, 10 m wide (Regev et al. 2025)

Biblical Passages

  • John 9:7, 11Jesus tells the blind man 'Go, wash in the pool of Siloam'. The 2004 stepped pool is of the right period (late Second Temple) and place; its identification with John's pool rests on name and location, not on an inscription.
  • Neh 3:15'The wall of the pool of Siloah by the king's garden' in the 5th century BCE: a pool at the south end of the city near a royal garden. The orchard over the site was long called the King's Garden. Which structure Nehemiah meant is not known.
  • Isa 8:6; 22:9-11'The waters of Shiloah that go softly' (8:6) fits a gentle channel such as Channel II. 22:9-11 speaks of gathering the waters of the lower pool and making a reservoir between the two walls. A dam-built reservoir of c. 800 BCE predates Isaiah and would be the existing 'lower pool' in his day; that is our reading.
  • 2 Kgs 20:20; 2 Chr 32:30Hezekiah 'made a pool and a conduit' and brought the Gihon water down to the west side of the City of David. If the dam and reservoir date to c. 800 BCE, Hezekiah's work would be the tunnel and/or a new pool, not the first reservoir. The tunnel's own date is debated (plaster dates 810-780 and 770-550 BCE).
