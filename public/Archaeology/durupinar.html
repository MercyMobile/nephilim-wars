Durupınar Formation

Boat-shaped landform near Doğubayazıt, Ağrı, Turkey · proposed as Noah's Ark · first permitted deep core drilling 2026

Overview

The Durupınar formation is a pointed-oval, hull-shaped ridge of rock and mud lying in the Telçeker earthflow valley south of Mount Ararat in eastern Turkey. A Turkish army mapping captain named Durupınar spotted its outline on aerial survey photographs in September 1959. LIFE magazine printed a photo in its 5 September 1960 issue, pp. 112–114, as cited by Collins & Fasold 1996. Ron Wyatt took it up from 1977 on, and David Fasold, John Baumgardner (then at Los Alamos) and Atatürk University geologist Salih Bayraktutan surveyed it through the 1980s. Wyatt measured it as 515 ft (157 m), which he equated to 300 'Royal Egyptian cubits' of 20.6 in. On 17 September 1987 Turkey's Ministry of Culture and Tourism council (decision 3657) protected it as a natural site and open-air museum.

The claim that it is an ark rests on its shape and length, on metal-detector lines (13 longitudinal lines and 9 transverse lines, taken as walls), on iron-rich 'brackets', and on 'petrified wood' samples. Since 2014 it also rests on geophysics by Noah's Ark Scans (Andrew Jones et al.). Their 100 MHz GPR in 2019 showed right-angled and linear reflectors down to about 7 m, and ERT and LiDAR were added later. In 2024 they sampled soil at 22 points inside and outside the outline. A separate Turkish-American team (Istanbul Technical University, Ağrı İbrahim Çeçen University, Andrews University) reported first lab results on about 30 samples in October 2023.

Two bodies of field evidence stand against each other. On one side, rock evidence: In 1988 four core holes went down as far as 10 m and recovered no wood, petrified or otherwise (Baumgardner, 1988). They met a ridge of layered bedrock along the centreline, and Baumgardner said it matched a roadcut about 0.5 mi away. Collins's thin sections showed the 'iron bracket' is limonite granules in a clay–quartz–calcite matrix, not smelted iron, and the 'wood' samples are basalt and andesite. Accounts of the 1988 work add that the cores were compromised by the water used to cool the bit. In 2026 the first permitted deep coring programme (Sivas Cumhuriyet University, directed by Cenker Atila, with Noah's Ark Scans) cored to 18 m inside and outside the formation from June to September. On 23 Sep 2026 the team announced a drill bit broke on a hard layer at 4–5 m. They suggested petrified wood but have no lab result yet. Samples went to Ankara, and results are promised for winter 2026–27 into 2027.

On the other side, the 2026 season's own field observations, as the team reports them: core holes inside the outline and control holes outside; sequences of soil and sediment with intervals of organic-rich material; cavities, one of which kept refilling with water as drilling went on; a layer at 4-5 m inside the outline that stopped the bit ('harder than limestone'); an underground camera robot (GOPHER) that reached about 12 m; and no bedrock or solid limestone met in any interior hole. Preliminary soil results gave about 40 percent more carbon inside the outline than outside (Atila, 30 Aug 2026), and GPR showed anomalies at 3-7 m with regular edges and right-angle intersections. None of this has yet been independently confirmed; about 1,000 samples and the cores are in a laboratory in Ankara, and the project director says the carbon analyses of organic matter and any wood will decide, with results expected within months.

What the evidence shows

  • Outline: pointed-oval ridge; length measured 157 m (515 ft) by Wyatt and later workers; width given as 50 m maximum (district governorate; Avcı 2007); relief given as 4 m (Avcı 2007) to 13 m (governorate).
  • Metal-detector survey (Wyatt, Fasold, Baumgardner, 1980s): 13 longitudinal lines of iron response converging at the pointed end, 9 transverse lines; Fasold reported >5,000 buried iron targets.
  • The 'iron bracket' in thin section and SEM (Collins & Fasold 1996) is limonite granules shaped like ilmenitic-magnetite crystals, in a matrix of calcite, clay, quartz and anthophyllite. Five SEM spots gave variable composition with high, variable TiO2, which smelted iron would not show.
  • Collins made 12 thin sections of 'petrified wood' samples collected by Fasold along the length and width of the formation; all were basalt or andesite (Collins 2016).
  • 1988 coring (Bayraktutan & Baumgardner, 28 Jul–7 Aug 1988): hole 1 to 10 m, all mudflow material; holes 2–3 to about 6 m, changing abruptly at 5 m from pea-gravel mudflow to layered bedrock; hole 4 met bedrock at the surface. Baumgardner wrote in 1988 that there was 'no evidence for wood, petrified or otherwise'. Limonite nodules turned up at 3 points in the mudflow.
  • White fossiliferous limestone crops out at the formation's midpoint. Collins (2016) reports that drilling shows it continues through and beyond the formation rather than ending at a point. Atabey (2025) dates the region's fossiliferous limestones as Eocene, 55–33 million years old.
  • A stream channel 1.5 m wide and 1.5 m deep, filled with cobbles, cuts one dark sedimentary layer near the rounded end. The layers dip about 5° from the limestone toward the lower end (Collins 2016).
  • GPR, 2019 (Noah's Ark Scans / ToPa 3D, 100 MHz): right-angled and linear reflectors to about 7 m on the NW side. A 234-ft 'central corridor' is a later reinterpretation of the same data (as reported by Fox News, 2025). No raw radargrams have been peer-reviewed.
  • Soil chemistry, 2024 (Noah's Ark Scans, soil scientist William Crabtree): 22 points inside and outside the outline, collected in September and analysed over winter. Reported ratios vary by outlet: organic matter 2x, 2.72x ('carbon') or 3x inside vs outside; potassium about 38–40% higher inside; a pH difference. The lab is not named and no dataset has been published.
  • ITU / AİÇÜ / Andrews University samples (press release, Oct 2023): about 30 rock and soil samples tested at ITU. Reported contents are clayey material, marine material and 'sea products' (mollusc remains), stated to be '3500 to 5000 years' old. The method, the material dated and the lab IDs were not given.
  • Ceramic sherds exposed by road construction near the formation (reported Dec 2025 – Jan 2026) were assigned to the Chalcolithic, 5500–3000 BC, by Faruk Kaya (AİÇÜ). Typology is the implied basis. No excavation context has been published.
  • 2026 season (29 Jun – 11 Sep 2026, under Sivas Cumhuriyet University with Ministry of Environment, Urbanization and Climate Change approval): about 20 researchers from 5 countries. Work included GPS/3D modelling, GPR, cores to 18 m inside and outside the formation, and about 1,000 soil samples from 100–150 points. Arkeonews (23 Sep 2026) reported carbon about 40% higher inside, and a drill bit broke at 4–5 m inside the outline.
  • 2026 cores, as reported by the team (23-26 Sep 2026): holes inside the outline and control holes beyond it; soil and sediment sequences, organic-rich intervals, cavities (one refilling with water); a bit-breaking layer at 4-5 m inside; no bedrock or solid limestone in the interior holes. Not yet independently confirmed.
  • GOPHER underground camera robot: first run (25 Sep 2026) stalled in loose sediment; second run (26 Sep) reached about 12 m (team timeline).
  • GPR 2026: anomalies at about 3-7 m with relatively regular edges and right-angle intersections (Arkeonews, 30 Aug 2026).
  • Soil carbon 2026 (preliminary): 'The amount of carbon coming from the interior of the ship is 40 percent higher than what was found outside' (Atila, 30 Aug 2026); about 1,000 samples from 100-150 points, in lab at Ankara.
  • Pottery fragments the team suggests may predate the flood period were recovered in 2026; not yet dated (Atila, 27 Sep 2026).

Open questions

  • What is the formation made of? Collins & Fasold (J. Geoscience Education 44, 1996) read it as a doubly plunging syncline, from converging limonite lines at the pointed end and fossiliferous limestone cutting the fold. Collins later withdrew the syncline reading in a postscript to his reprint. Collins (PSCF 68:4, 2016) instead reads flat-lying flood-plain sediments shaped by landslide debris flowing around a resistant basalt mass at the pointed end. Avcı (Bull. Eng. Geol. Environ. 66, 2007) reads a slumped, joint-bounded block of Miocene limestone creeping on Eocene clays, noting an axial displacement of about 20° from the valley trend. Güner (Jeomorfoloji Dergisi 14, 1986; summarised by Avcı) read a residual hill of ophiolitic bedrock. Atabey (MTA geologist, ResearchGate upload June 2025, after a site visit on 30 May 2025) returns to a syncline shaped by landslide. The naturalistic readings disagree with each other on mechanism, even though all agree the rock is bedrock. The 2026 interior cores, as the team reports them, met no bedrock or solid limestone at all, while the 1988 holes 2-3 met layered bedrock at 5 m: either the two campaigns sampled different parts of the formation, or one set of logs is wrong. The 2026 hard layer at 4-5 m sits at the same depth as the 1988 bedrock contact. The Ankara laboratory work (mineralogy, microscopy, organic content, radiocarbon) is the test that can settle it.
  • Wood or rock? Wyatt (1994 video; Wyatt Archaeological Research) identified black layered rock as petrified wood and deck beams, citing Galbraith Laboratory chemistry (Fe, Mg, Al, Ca, Ti). Collins (2016) answers that those element ratios match basalt, not silicified wood, which is nearly pure SiO2, and his 12 thin sections are all volcanic rock. The 1988 cores found no wood (Baumgardner). The 2026 claim of a hard layer at 4–5 m that broke a bit (Jones, press release 23 Sep 2026) is untested until the core is sectioned; basalt and limestone were both already logged in this formation. Against that, the team reports organic-rich intervals in the 2026 cores and reads them as the decayed upper timbers of a vessel; buried soil horizons or organic sediment within a mudflow are the alternative reading. The sectioned cores decide.
  • Do the GPR 'rooms' show structure or bedding? Noah's Ark Scans (Jones 2023, 7th Int. Noah's Ark Symposium; 2019 ToPa 3D survey) read 100 MHz right-angle reflectors at about 7 m as walls and corridors. Collins (2020, 'Discussion of the Subsurface Images Project') reads the same patterns as joint sets, alteration of feldspar to clay along fractures, and dense basalt masses. He notes that the joint system on the surface is where the metal-detector 'wall' ribbons were laid. Neither side has published radargrams with ground truth from cores; the 2026 cores are the first test able to settle it.
  • Is the inside/outside soil difference meaningful? Crabtree (Noah's Ark Scans, 2024–25) reads higher organic matter, potassium and carbon inside the outline as decayed wood. No lab, method or data table has been published, and the reported ratios differ between outlets (2x, 2.72x, 3x). The ridge holds a different substrate from the surrounding mudflow (bedrock near the surface vs colluvium, per the 1988 cores), which is itself a possible cause of different soil and vegetation. That reading is ours, and it is untested.
  • What was dated in 2023? The Hürriyet report (26 Oct 2023) quotes Kaya: samples were found in the dating study to be '3500 ile 5000 yıl öncesine ait' (3,500–5,000 years old). The same report says there was human activity 'milattan önce 5500 ve 3000 yılları arasındaki' (5500–3000 BC). Material, method and lab report are all unstated, and no publication was found. Kaya himself said (Hürriyet 2023; Christian Daily, 8 Jan 2026) that the dating cannot show the ark was there and that 'no satisfactory information or evidence has been reached.'

The Find

  • DateUndated as an object. No radiometric date on the formation itself has been published. Geological readings assign its rocks to Eocene–Miocene strata (Avcı 2007) or to flood-plain sediments and volcanics shaped by later landslides (Collins 2016); advocates assign it to the Genesis flood.
  • How datedThe '3500–5000 years' figure circulating since Oct 2023 comes from a press statement (Hürriyet, 26 Oct 2023) about ~30 rock/soil samples tested at Istanbul Technical University; no dating method, lab number, or material dated was stated, and no paper has been located. The same statement also gives 'human activity from the Chalcolithic, 5500–3000 BC'. The two ranges do not match: 3,500–5,000 years before present is c. 3000–1500 BCE. The 2026 cores and ~1,000 soil samples are in lab (Ankara); the project director expects carbon results within 2-3 months (Atila, 27 Sep 2026); none released as of 27 Sep 2026.
  • Discovered11 September 1959 (aerial photo review); site visit 1960, Turkish Army mapping captain Durupınar (named 'İhsan' on the Doğubayazıt district governorate page; 'İlhan' in most English sources); 1960 visit by George Vandeman and photogrammetrist Arthur Brandenberger per secondary accounts (not checked against the 1960 LIFE article itself), Telçeker/Üzengili area, c. 27–30 km south(-west) of the summit of Greater Ararat, c. 3.5 km from the Turkey–Iran transit road, Doğubayazıt district, Ağrı province
  • Now heldIn situ. Collins (2016) gives Google Earth centre 39°26'26"N, 44°14'0.5"E, elevation 6,625 ft (c. 2,020 m); Collins & Fasold (1996) said c. 7,000 ft. Protected by decision no. 3657 (17 Sep 1987) of Turkey's High Council for Immovable Cultural and Natural Property as a natural site and open-air museum; visitor centre on the adjacent slope.
  • MaterialDisputed by drill core and thin section: 1988 cores reached mudflow material and, at c. 5 m, layered bedrock (Baumgardner); Collins's 12 thin sections were basalt/andesite; Avcı (2007) reads Miocene limestone on Eocene clays; the 1988 team leader reported the drilled bedrock as ophiolite
  • DimensionsLength 157 m / 515 ft (Wyatt; Collins 2016; Noah's Ark Scans); 538 ft in an earlier Wyatt measurement; 165 m × 50 m × 13 m (Doğubayazıt district governorate); 150 m × 50 m × 4 m high (Avcı 2007)

Biblical Passages

  • Gen 6:15The text gives 300 × 50 × 30 cubits (KJV: 'The length of the ark shall be three hundred cubits, the breadth of it fifty cubits, and the height of it thirty cubits'). At an 18-in (0.457 m) cubit that is 137 × 23 × 13.7 m. At the 20.6-in (0.5236 m) 'Egyptian royal' cubit chosen by Wyatt and Fasold it is 157 × 26 × 15.7 m. The formation's length of 157 m matches only if the 20.6-in cubit is chosen. Its maximum width of 50 m (governorate, Avcı) is about double 50 cubits; Fasold argued the *average* width matches. Its relief of 4 m (Avcı) to 13 m (governorate) is a landform height, not a hull depth. The cubit choice is an interpretive step, not something the text gives.
  • Gen 8:4'The ark rested ... upon the mountains of Ararat' (plural). The text names a mountain region, not a peak. The site lies in the Ararat range about 27–30 km from Greater Ararat's summit, so it falls within the text's wording, as do other candidate locations in the same region; the verse does not single out this spot.
  • Gen 6:14'Gopher wood' and pitch: the 1988 cores and Collins's 12 thin sections found no wood. The 2026 cores recovered organic-rich intervals whose nature is untested; the team reads them as decayed timber, and the Ankara laboratory will say.
