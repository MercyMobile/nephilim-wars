Timna Copper Mines

Copper mining and smelting district in the southern Arabah · Chalcolithic to Islamic, with a major smelting peak in the 11th-9th centuries BCE

Overview

The Timna Valley in the southern Arabah holds thousands of ancient mine shafts and several large smelting camps. Nelson Glueck surveyed it in 1934. Beno Rothenberg's Arabah Expedition surveyed from 1959 and in 1969 excavated a shrine of the Egyptian goddess Hathor (Site 200); from that find the main mining and smelting was assigned to the Egyptian New Kingdom (the 'Egyptian paradigm').

Since 2012 the Central Timna Valley Project of Tel Aviv University, directed by Erez Ben-Yosef, has re-excavated the smelting camps with radiocarbon dating of short-lived seeds. Site 30 is now dated to the early Iron Age (11th-9th century BCE) (Ben-Yosef et al. 2012). At Site 34, the 'Slaves' Hill', a 1 m section through Slag Mound 19 contains 32 layers representing about 150 years of smelting, and eleven AMS dates from it fall in the 10th century BCE. Extraordinary dryness preserved seeds, textiles (wool, goat hair and linen), rope and cordage.

OSL dating of mine fills showed open-pit mining at Merkavot 1 in the Chalcolithic and again at the end of the 2nd millennium BCE, and a shaft at Merkavot 2 in the Chalcolithic-Early Bronze Age, overturning the assumption that simpler mining techniques are always older. The Iron Age industry relied on local white broom and acacia charcoal, and its end has been linked to fuel exhaustion. The Hebrew Bible never names Timna; the tie to Solomon is a modern label.

What the evidence shows

  • Site 30 radiocarbon-dated to the early Iron Age, 11th-9th century BCE, replacing the Late Bronze/New Kingdom date (Ben-Yosef et al. 2012, as summarized by the CTV Project).
  • Site 34, Slag Mound 19, Square 19A: deposit slightly over 1 m thick, 32 layers, c. 150 years of operation; eleven AMS radiocarbon dates on short-lived seeds, all 10th century BCE (CTV 2013 season report).
  • The slag's high copper content and a two-piece tuyere at Site 34 fit Late Bronze / early Iron Age smelting technology (CTV 2013 season report).
  • The Hathor shrine (Site 200) was excavated by the Arabah Expedition in 1969; on that basis Type C shaft-and-gallery mines (cut with metal chisels) were dated to the Egyptian New Kingdom (CTV mines page).
  • OSL: Merkavot 1 Area A open-pit mine is Chalcolithic; Merkavot 1 Area B was mined at the end of the 2nd millennium BCE; the Merkavot 2 Area C shaft is Chalcolithic-Early Bronze Age (CTV mines page).
  • Late Bronze Age copper production is also attested at Site 3 (Yagel, Ben-Yosef and Craddock, Levant 48, 2016).
  • About 100 textile and cordage fragments from the 2013-2014 seasons at Site 34: wool, goat hair and linen, some with coloured designs (CTV textile page).
  • Charcoal from two stratified waste heaps of the 11th-9th century industry is mostly local white broom (Retama raetam) and acacia; later layers include less suitable taxa, read by the authors as a widening search for fuel (Cavanagh, Ben-Yosef and Langgut 2022).
  • In Timna the smelting ores were almost exclusively the Amir/Avrona type in the Late Bronze-Early Iron cycle (Bode et al. 2024).
  • Heavy-metal enrichment of soil at two Timna smelting camps is confined to the production areas (Yagel et al. 2024).

Open questions

  • Who ran the Iron Age industry? Ben-Yosef (Vetus Testamentum 69, 2019) argues that a complex society of largely nomadic people ran the Arabah copper centers, and that archaeologists of all schools have missed such polities because they rely on stone architecture ('architectural bias'). Eshel et al. (PLoS One 2025) call it 'the Arabah copper polity' and propose Faynan and Timna were 'possibly controlled by a joint polity'. Whether this polity is biblical Edom, a Judahite/Israelite enterprise, or something else is not settled by any inscription from the sites; it is an inference from location (1 Kings 9:26 places Ezion-geber 'in the land of Edom') and date.
  • Egyptian or local? Rothenberg's Arabah Expedition dated the main mining and smelting to the Egyptian New Kingdom from the Hathor shrine (Site 200). Ben-Yosef et al. (BASOR 367, 2012) show from radiocarbon that the largest camps (Site 30, Site 34) operated in the 11th-9th centuries BCE, after Egyptian control of Canaan ended. Both phases are real; the dispute is over which phase the thousands of shafts belong to, which the CTV Project calls 'now insecure'.
  • Link to Solomon. The radiocarbon peak at Site 34 overlaps the 10th century BCE that the biblical chronology gives Solomon. No object, inscription or trade record found at Timna links its copper to Jerusalem; 1 Kings 7:46 places the casting of the temple bronze in the Jordan plain, not the Arabah.

The Find

  • DateMining from the Chalcolithic period onward; Egyptian New Kingdom (Late Bronze Age) activity at the Hathor shrine (Site 200); the main smelting camps Site 30 and Site 34 radiocarbon-dated to the early Iron Age (11th-9th century BCE), Site 34's Slag Mound 19 to the 10th century BCE
  • How datedHigh-resolution AMS radiocarbon dating of short-lived seeds from stratified slag mounds (Ben-Yosef et al., BASOR 367, 2012 for Site 30; eleven AMS dates from Slag Mound 19 at Site 34, all 10th century BCE, per the Central Timna Valley Project); OSL dating of sediments filling mines (Merkavot 1 and 2); earlier Egyptian New Kingdom dating from the Hathor shrine (Site 200) excavated by Rothenberg's Arabah Expedition in 1969.
  • DiscoveredSurveyed 1934 (Glueck); Arabah Expedition surveys 1959-1961 and excavations from the 1960s (Hathor shrine 1969); Central Timna Valley Project from 2012 (field seasons from 2013, latest listed 2025), Nelson Glueck (survey); Beno Rothenberg and the Arabah Expedition; Erez Ben-Yosef, Central Timna Valley (CTV) Project, Tel Aviv University, Timna Valley, southern Arabah (Aravah), southern Israel; the copper ore district measures c. 10 x 12 km
  • Now heldIn situ, Timna Valley (Timna Park), southern Arabah, Israel
  • MaterialCopper ore-bearing sandstone and conglomerate; mines (open pits and shaft-and-gallery workings); smelting camps with slag mounds, furnace remains, tuyeres, charcoal, textiles and organic refuse

Biblical Passages

  • Deuteronomy 8:9'a land whose stones are iron, and out of whose hills thou mayest dig brass' (KJV; 'brass' = copper/bronze). General statement about the land; does not name Timna.
  • 1 Kings 7:13-47Hiram casts the temple bronzes; 'in the plain of Jordan did the king cast them... between Succoth and Zarthan' and the weight of the brass 'was not found out'. The text says where casting happened, not where the copper came from. Radiocarbon dates for Site 34 fall in the same century as the traditional Solomonic date; no direct link is attested.
  • 1 Kings 9:26-28; 2 Chronicles 8:17-18Solomon's fleet at Ezion-geber 'beside Eloth, on the shore of the Red sea, in the land of Edom'. This places Solomonic activity near the Gulf of Aqaba, c. the southern end of the Arabah where Timna lies; the texts mention gold from Ophir, not copper.
  • 2 Samuel 8:13-14David puts garrisons in Edom. If the 10th-century Arabah copper producers were Edomite, this is the biblical claim of Israelite control over their territory (inference; no Timna find names Edom or Israel).
  • Genesis 36:31'kings that reigned in the land of Edom, before there reigned any king over the children of Israel.' Ben-Yosef's case for a complex nomadic polity in the Arabah bears on whether an early Edomite kingdom is plausible (our reading).
