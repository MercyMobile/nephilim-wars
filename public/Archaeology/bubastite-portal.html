Bubastite Portal (Shoshenq I Campaign Relief, Karnak)

Egyptian triumph relief and town list of Shoshenq I (biblical Shishak) · c. 925 BCE · Karnak

Overview

Shoshenq I, founder of Egypt's 22nd Dynasty, added a gateway and the great First Court to the Amun temple at Karnak. On the outer south wall beside that gate (the 'Bubastite Portal') is a triumph scene. Amun and the goddess of Thebes lead rows of bound captives to the king, and each captive's body is a crenellated oval holding a place name. Breasted (1906) counted 156 ovals; about 75 names were still legible, and the fourth and tenth rows were almost entirely lost.

The legible names include towns in the Jezreel and Beth-Shean valleys and the central hills: Taanach, Shunem, Beth-Shean, Rehob, Hapharaim, Mahanaim, Gibeon, Beth-Horon, Aijalon, Megiddo. There is also a long series of Negev names, many of them compounds built with 'field' and similar words. Breasted read Nos. 71-72 as 'the Field of Abram'. Jerusalem does not appear among the surviving names. Breasted wrote that it 'must have been lost in one of the lacunae'.

A fragment of a large Shoshenq I stela was picked out of Schumacher's spoil at Megiddo by the Oriental Institute team in the 1920s. Breasted, in Fisher's 1929 report, took it as evidence that Shoshenq set up a victory monument in one of the towns on his list.

In its own words

Thou wentest forth in victory, and thou hast returned in might; thou hast united [...]; I have [...] for thee the countries that knew not Egypt, that had begun to invade [thy] boundaries, in order to cut off their heads.Amun's speech to Shoshenq I, J. H. Breasted, Ancient Records of Egypt IV (Chicago, 1906) §720, p. 356

What the evidence shows

  • Triumph scene: 156 captive-ovals in ten rows (65 led by Amun, 91 by the goddess of Thebes); about 75 names legible in 1906 (Breasted ARE IV §718).
  • Legible names include Taanach (14), Shunem (15), Beth-Shean (16), Rehob (17), Hapharaim (18), Mahanaim (22), Gibeon (23), Beth-Horon (24), Aijalon (26) and Megiddo (27) (Breasted §712-713).
  • Nos. 71-72 read by Breasted as p3 hqr 'brm, 'the Field of Abram' (§715).
  • No. 29, Yw-d-h-m-rw-k, had been rendered 'the king or kingdom of Judah'; Breasted (§713) says it 'is, of course, not to be rendered' so.
  • Jerusalem is not among the preserved names (Breasted §717; Breasted in Fisher 1929 p. xii: 'Shishak's list ... does not mention it').
  • The accompanying inscriptions are 'made up of stereotyped phrases drawn from earlier monuments' (Breasted §709).
  • A fragment of a round-topped Shoshenq I stela, about 20 inches thick and restorable to about 5 ft wide, was recovered from Schumacher's dump at Megiddo (Breasted foreword to Fisher, OIC 4, 1929).

Open questions

  • Where is Jerusalem? 1 Kgs 14:25-26 says Shishak 'came up against Jerusalem' and took the temple and palace treasures. Breasted (ARE IV §717, 1906) held that the name 'must have been lost in one of the lacunae', since 31 names in rows 4 and 10 and 12 others are destroyed. In Fisher 1929 he states simply that the Karnak list 'does not mention it'. The wall itself shows only that Jerusalem is not among the surviving names; whether it was ever carved cannot be read from the damaged rows.
  • Is the list a route or a composite? Breasted (1906, §711-712) read the list by geographic clusters and noted that it seems to mix towns of Israel and Judah (e.g., No. 19 possibly Adoraim). K. A. Wilson's monograph The Campaign of Pharaoh Shoshenq I into Palestine (FAT II/9, 2005; reviewed by Hoffmeier, BASOR 349, and Kitchen, JSS 54) and A. Dodson's 'The Palestinian Campaign(s) of Shoshenq I' (2023) take up how far the relief records one historical route. Their arguments have not been reviewed for this entry.
  • Does No. 29 name Judah? Nineteenth-century readers rendered Yw-d-h-m-rw-k as 'the king or kingdom of Judah'. Breasted (1906 §713) rejected this, calling it the 'much-discussed' name. The wall supports only the consonantal sequence; what the name means was not settled from a primary source cited here.

Corrected from the earlier edition of this page
  • Claims that the relief 'names Jerusalem' or 'records the sack of Jerusalem': Jerusalem is not among the surviving names (Breasted 1906 §717; 1929).
  • Claims that the relief 'names Rehoboam' or the 'kingdom of Judah': no king of Judah is named. The old reading of No. 29 as 'kingdom of Judah' was rejected by Breasted in 1906.
  • Claims of 'some 150 towns': there are 156 ovals, but by 1906 only about 75 names survived as legible Palestinian towns (Breasted §718).
  • Claims that the Megiddo stela fragment was 'excavated in situ': it was noticed on a block thrown out by Schumacher's workmen that had lain for about twenty years on the mound (Breasted in Fisher 1929).

The Find

  • DateCarved in or after Shoshenq I's year 21 (Breasted: about 924 BCE); the campaign it commemorates came earlier (Breasted: about 926 BCE, Rehoboam's 5th year)
  • How datedBreasted (ARE IV §709) dates the building of the Bubastite gate and first court to Shoshenq's year 21 from the Silsila quarry inscription (ARE IV §§701-708). The BCE figures depend on synchronising Shoshenq with Rehoboam's 5th year (1 Kgs 14:25); the relief itself carries no regnal date for the campaign.
  • DiscoveredAlways visible; published in the 19th century, Published by Champollion, Rosellini, Lepsius and Mariette (Breasted ARE IV §709 n.); full facsimile by the Epigraphic Survey (Oriental Institute, Chicago) 1954, South wall of the Great Temple of Amun, Karnak, beside the Bubastite gate (south entrance of the First Court), between the Second Pylon and the temple of Ramesses III
  • Now heldIn situ, Karnak Temple, Luxor, Egypt. Four name rings (Nos. 105-108) are in Berlin (Breasted, ARE IV §709 n.)
  • MaterialSandstone temple wall, sunk relief
  • ScriptEgyptian hieroglyphs
  • LanguageEgyptian, with Semitic place names

Biblical Passages

  • 1 Kgs 14:25-26; 2 Chr 12:2-9'Shishak king of Egypt' attacks in Rehoboam's 5th year. The Karnak relief is the Egyptian record of a Shoshenq I campaign against towns in the same land. The equation Shishak = Shoshenq is by name and period. The relief does not name Rehoboam, Judah (see No. 29) or Jerusalem.
  • 2 Chr 12:4The Chronicler says Shishak 'took the fortified cities which belonged to Judah'. The relief's Negev and southern names fit a southern operation; Breasted identifies Arad and possibly Sharuhen among them.
  • Gen 12-25Breasted's reading 'the Field of Abram' (Nos. 71-72) would be a 10th-century place name containing the name Abram. It is a toponym, not a mention of the patriarch.
