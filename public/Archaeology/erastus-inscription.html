Erastus Inscription, Corinth

Latin paving inscription of an aedile named Erastus · Roman Corinth

Overview

Three fragments of two inscribed paving slabs, originally one of three adjoining slabs, from the paved square east of the stage building of Corinth's theatre. The Latin letters were bronze inlays set into cuttings; the bronze is gone and only the cuttings and two punctuation marks remain.

The text reads: [praenomen nomen] Erastus pro aedilit[at]e s(ua) p(ecunia) stravit - '[...] Erastus, in return for his aedileship, laid (this pavement) at his own expense.' The left slab carrying the first part of his name is lost; about seven letters stood before 'Erastus'.

The first published report (Shear, AJA 1929) called Erastus a procurator because the text was incomplete; the full reading as aedile came only after the 1947 fragment. Whether this aedile is the Erastus 'oikonomos of the city' of Romans 16:23 is the live question.

What the evidence shows

  • Language is Latin, not Greek; office named is aedile (aedilitas), not treasurer.
  • Letters were bronze inlay; only the cuttings survive, 0.18 m high.
  • Name preserved: the cognomen Erastus only; praenomen and nomen lost on the missing left slab.
  • Found in three campaigns: 1928, 1929, 1947; studies before 1947 lacked the full text (Gill 1989, n. 1).
  • The inscription carries no date formula.
  • Romans 16:23 calls Erastus ὁ οἰκονόμος τῆς πόλεως; the stone's title is aedile. The equation of the two titles is itself disputed.

Open questions

  • Same man as Rom 16:23? J. H. Kent (Corinth VIII.3, 1966, p. 99) held the identification 'still seems sound' because he dated the pavement to the mid-1st century, the cognomen Erastus was uncommon at Corinth, and oikonomos could render aedile (as summarized by Gill 1989). H. J. Cadbury (JBL 50, 1931) and A. G. Roos (Mnemosyne 1930) objected that a Christian holding a pagan-linked magistracy was unlikely (Gill 1989, n. 29).
  • Title equivalence: J. K. Goodrich (NTS 56, 2010, 90-115), relying on a new inscription from the Achaean colony of Patras, argues oikonomos tes poleos = quaestor, a post held by the economic elite, and rebuts the arcarius (slave treasurer) and aedile equations.
  • Date and publication record: S. J. Friesen ('The Wrong Erastus', in Corinth in Context, Brill 2010, 231-256) states 'the inscription was incorrectly published and the identification of the two Erastus references is wrong'; per Goodrich (NTS 57, 2011, 583-593) Friesen argues the Romans Erastus was an unbelieving public slave. Goodrich 2011 replies, and also answers A. Weiss's claim that Corinth had no municipal quaestorship. Friesen's specific re-dating evidence is not accessible for this entry.

The Find

  • DateDisputed: mid-1st century CE (Kent 1966) vs. later (Friesen 2010, who says the inscription was 'incorrectly published')
  • How datedNo date appears in the text. Kent (Corinth VIII.3, 1966, pp. 99-100) held that it 'probably dated to the middle of the first century AD' (as reported by Gill 1989, p. 299); the grounds Kent gave have not been reviewed for this entry. Friesen (2010) says the pavement 'was incorrectly published' and rejects the identification with Romans' Erastus; his dating evidence was not accessible.
  • Discovered1928 (left part of second slab, east parados of the theatre); 1929 (complete slab in situ); 1947 (remaining fragment, in a late vaulted building SW of the theatre), American School of Classical Studies at Athens excavations; the 1929 find published by T. Leslie Shear, Paved square (c. 19 x 19 m) east of the stage building of the theatre, Corinth, at its entrance from the street along the theatre's east side
  • Now heldThe complete slab was found in situ in the square east of the theatre at ancient Corinth (Gill 1989); current storage of the 1928/1947 fragments Not confirmed in a published museum record
  • MaterialGrey Acrocorinthian limestone paving slabs; letters originally inlaid bronze, now only the cuttings survive
  • DimensionsEach slab c. 0.66 x 2.27 x 0.15 m; three adjoining slabs, total width 6.81 m; letters 0.18 m high (Gill 1989, after Kent)
  • ScriptLatin capitals (letter cuttings for bronze inlay)
  • LanguageLatin

Biblical Passages

  • Rom 16:23'Erastus the oikonomos of the city greets you' - written from Corinth. Same cognomen, same city; the stone says aedile, the letter says oikonomos. Identity is a reading, not a statement of either text.
  • Acts 19:22An Erastus sent by Paul with Timothy into Macedonia; no office given.
  • 2 Tim 4:20'Erastus remained at Corinth'; no office given.
