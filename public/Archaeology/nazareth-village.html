Early Roman Nazareth (houses and excavations)

Excavated Early Roman remains of Jesus' home village · 1st century BCE – 1st century CE

Overview

Three separate things are mixed together under this name. The first is Nazareth Village, an open-air museum that 'reconstructs and reenacts village life in the Galilee in the time of Jesus' (its own description). Its research is credited to Stephen Pfann's team, with Ross Voss as project archaeologist. It is a modern reconstruction on farmland, not the ancient village centre.

The second is the Sisters of Nazareth convent in the town centre. Found by accident in 1884 and dug informally for decades, it was re-examined by Ken Dark's Nazareth Archaeological Project in 2006–10. Dark identifies a partly rock-cut first-century domestic building, perhaps a courtyard house, as the first surface-built Early Roman house from Nazareth to be published. He notes the area was later used for burial, and that a Byzantine church above it is probably the 'Church of the Nutrition'. A 7th-century pilgrim text says that church stood over the house where Jesus was raised.

The third is an IAA excavation by Yardenna Alexandre next to the Church of the Annunciation compound. It found three strata: Iron IIA–B (10th to early 8th century BCE), a late Hellenistic to Early Roman dwelling (late 2nd century BCE to the first third of the 2nd century CE), and Crusader–Mamluk. The Roman-period house had a three-level complex of underground pits or silos full of discarded potsherds. Alexandre reads this as possibly the Jewish practice of breaking ritually impure pottery. The final report appeared in 'Atiqot 98.

What the evidence shows

  • A late Hellenistic to Early Roman dwelling next to the Church of the Annunciation compound, with a three-level complex of subterranean pits or silos (Alexandre, 'Atiqot 98).
  • Many potsherds dumped in those pits, similar to deposits at other Early Roman Jewish villages in Galilee (Alexandre, 'Atiqot 98).
  • An Iron IIA–B stratum (10th to early 8th century BCE) under the same spot (Alexandre, 'Atiqot 98).
  • At the Sisters of Nazareth convent, a partly rock-cut structure that Dark interprets as a 1st-century CE domestic building, later superseded by burials (Dark, Antiquaries Journal 92, 2012).
  • A vaulted Byzantine church above the Sisters of Nazareth remains, which Dark identifies as probably the Church of the Nutrition (Dark 2023).
  • Excavations at Mary's Well found remains from the late Hellenistic to the Ottoman periods, mostly a Crusader fountain house and Mamluk alterations (Alexandre 2012).

Open questions

  • Is the Sisters of Nazareth house the house the Byzantines venerated as Jesus' home? Dark (OUP 2023, chs. 3–6) argues the Byzantine church over it is the Church of the Nutrition, which a 7th-century pilgrim says covered the house where Jesus grew up. Bagatti's older view, which Dark says prevailed from the 1930s, treated the convent site as unimportant. Dark's identification of the church rests on the match with the pilgrim text. The first-century house itself carries no inscription or name.
  • Did Jewish-Christians keep Nazareth's holy places before Constantine? Bagatti and Testa said they did. Taylor (Christians and the Holy Places, OUP 1993, ch. 11) examines that hypothesis and argues it was assembled from scattered literary and archaeological pieces. Her chapter is the counter-case to their reconstruction.

Since 2024

  • 2025-01Alexandre's final report on the Iron Age and Early Roman dwelling beside the Church of the Annunciation is published online in 'Atiqot 98. It proposes that the potsherd-filled pits reflect the Jewish practice of destroying ritually impure vessels. [1]

The Find

  • DateLate Hellenistic to Early Roman occupation (late 2nd century BCE to the first third of the 2nd century CE) next to the Church of the Annunciation, following an Iron Age IIA–B stratum
  • How datedStratigraphy and pottery in the IAA excavation by Y. Alexandre ('Atiqot 98); stratified sequence at the Sisters of Nazareth convent, Nazareth Archaeological Project 2006–10 (Dark 2012).
  • DiscoveredSisters of Nazareth site first uncovered 1884 and re-examined 2006–10; Mary's Well excavated by the IAA (published 2012); house next to the Church of the Annunciation excavated by the IAA (published in 'Atiqot 98), Ken Dark (Nazareth Archaeological Project); Yardenna Alexandre (Israel Antiquities Authority), Central Nazareth, Lower Galilee
  • Now heldIn situ, Nazareth, Israel (the Sisters of Nazareth convent; the area next to the Franciscan Church of the Annunciation compound; Mary's Well). 'Nazareth Village' is a separate open-air museum on the edge of town.

Biblical Passages

  • Luke 1:26; 2:39, 51; Matt 2:23The Gospels make Nazareth in Galilee the home of Mary and Joseph and the town where Jesus grew up. The excavations show a settlement there in the late Hellenistic to Early Roman period, the right date for that.
  • Luke 4:16, 29Nazareth 'where he had been brought up', built on a hill with a brow. The finds show a village; they do not identify the synagogue or the cliff.
  • John 1:46'Can anything good come out of Nazareth?' Nathanael is scorning the town; he is not calling it Jesus' hometown. The finds show a modest Jewish village with ordinary domestic installations.
