Egyptian Execration Texts

Middle Kingdom ritual curse lists naming foreign rulers and places, written on pots and prisoner figurines

Overview

The Execration Texts are ritual curse lists. Egyptian priests wrote the names of hostile rulers, peoples and places - Nubian, Libyan, Asiatic and even Egyptian - on pots or on clay figurines of bound prisoners, which were then broken or buried to disable the enemies named. Two big groups are known: the bowls published by Kurt Sethe in 1926 (Berlin), and the figurines published by Georges Posener in 1940 (Brussels and Cairo).

The Asiatic section names Levantine rulers with their towns, in a fixed formula: 'The ruler of [place], (called) [name].' Posener's list includes ꜣwšꜣmm (E 45), which Sethe had identified with Jerusalem, and Skmimi (Posener p. 68), which Posener equates with the Skmm of the Sebek-khu stela and 'the biblical Shechem'. ꜣskn (E 2) is identified with Ashkelon.

The texts are magic, not administration. They name the places the Egyptians feared or claimed; they say nothing about tribute.

What the text says (summary)

The ruler of Skmimi, (called) Ibsh-Hadad. ... The ruler of Iskn, (called) Muri.Literal translation (this encyclopedia) of Posener's transliterations and French renderings, Princes et pays d'Asie et de Nubie (1940), p. 68 (Shechem) and E 2 (Ashkelon). No pre-1930 English translation of these texts exists.

What the evidence shows

  • Asiatic princes list formula: 'Le prince de [pays] (appelé) [nom]' (Posener 1940, section E).
  • E 45: ꜣwšꜣmm, with two princes in the Berlin texts (Sethe e 27-28, f 18); Sethe identified it with Jerusalem (Posener 1940).
  • Shechem: Skmimi / Skmmi, ruler 'Ibš-Hadad', a name compounded with Hadad (Posener 1940, p. 68).
  • E 2: ꜣskn, ruler Mwri; the Berlin texts spell it with -nw (Sethe e 23-25, f 15); identified with Ashkelon (Posener 1940).
  • Saqqara figurines found in 1922 in a mudbrick chapel with five private stelae north of Teti's temple enclosure; rediscovered by Posener in a Saqqara storeroom in spring 1938 and moved to Cairo (Posener 1940, §2).
  • Brussels figurines: unbaked clay, c. 30 cm high, bound captives, hieratic texts; bought in Paris in summer 1938 (Capart's foreword to Posener 1940).

Open questions

  • Is ꜣwšꜣmm Jerusalem? Sethe (1926) proposed it and Posener (1940, E 45) reports it without objection. The equation rests on the consonants (Egyptian ꜣ can render l/r: ꜣ-w-š-ꜣ-m-m ~ Rushalimum). No text states the place's location.
  • Two Jerusalem rulers vs one: the Berlin bowls give ꜣwšꜣmm two princes; the figurine list gives one (name mostly effaced). Whether this reflects a change of ruler across one generation, as Posener argued for the Nubian princes, is his inference from the two series.

The Find

  • DateMiddle Kingdom. Berlin bowls: earlier group; Brussels/Saqqara figurines: end of the 12th Dynasty (possibly early 13th), about one generation after the bowls (Posener 1940)
  • How datedPosener (1940, §6) dated the figurines by palaeography (later than the Illahun papyri of Senusret III-Amenemhat III, earlier than Papyrus Boulaq 18) and by Egyptian personal names on the companion figurines built on 12th-Dynasty royal names, including Senusret III, giving a terminus post quem. He found cases where a Nubian prince's father appears on the bowls and the son on the figurines, putting a generation between the two groups.
  • DiscoveredBerlin bowls: published by Kurt Sethe in 1926. Saqqara figurines: found 1922; Brussels figurines: bought 1938, Sethe (Berlin); C. M. Firth's excavation (Saqqara, 1921-1922 season); Jean Capart / Musées Royaux, Brussels (purchase); published by Georges Posener (1940), Saqqara group: at the entrance of the 'Street of Tombs', along the north enclosure wall of the pyramid temple of Teti, in a small mudbrick chapel with five stelae. Brussels group: bought from a dealer; Posener judged it to share an origin with the Saqqara finds.
  • Now heldÄgyptisches Museum, Berlin (bowls); Egyptian Museum, Cairo (Saqqara figurines, moved there in 1938); Musées Royaux d'Art et d'Histoire, Brussels (figurines)
  • MaterialPottery bowls/sherds (Berlin); unbaked clay figurines of bound captives about 30 cm high (Brussels/Cairo); five alabaster figurines in Cairo
  • ScriptHieratic
  • LanguageMiddle Egyptian, with foreign (Semitic and Nubian) names in Egyptian writing

Biblical Passages

  • Genesis 12:6; 33:18-20; 34Shechem is attested as a ruled town in Middle Kingdom Egypt's list of Asiatic enemies, i.e. it existed as a political centre in the Middle Bronze Age, the setting Genesis gives Abram and Jacob there. The text says nothing about its people beyond the ruler's name.
  • Genesis 14:18; Joshua 10:1; 2 Samuel 5:6-9If ꜣwšꜣmm is Jerusalem (Sethe's reading), the city had rulers by the late 12th Dynasty - the earliest mention of the name outside the Bible. The Execration Texts do not mention Salem or Melchizedek.
  • Judges 1:18; 14:19Ashkelon (ꜣskn) appears as a ruled city in the same lists.
