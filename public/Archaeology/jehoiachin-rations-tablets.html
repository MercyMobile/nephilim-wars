Jehoiachin Ration Tablets

Oil-ration lists from Nebuchadnezzar's palace at Babylon naming the exiled king of Judah · c. 595-570 BCE

Overview

Administrative tablets from the storerooms of Nebuchadnezzar II's palace at Babylon record monthly issues of sesame oil and other staples to people held or maintained at court. Four of them list rations for 'Ya'u-kinu (also written Ya'ukin), king of the land of Yahudu' - Jehoiachin, king of Judah, deported in 597 BCE - together with his five sons and other Judahites.

Koldewey found the tablets in the vaulted substructure at the north-east corner of the Southern Palace, noting in 1914 that 'the large number of tablets found in the stairway chamber on the south side' related to food supplies, which led him to read the building as a storehouse. Ernst Weidner recognised Jehoiachin's name in the Berlin tablets and published the key texts in 1939. In 2025 Olof Pedersén published the whole archive of 527 texts.

The same lists issue oil to craftsmen and dignitaries from many lands (secondary transcriptions mention carpenters from Arvad, woodworkers from Byblos and Greek craftsmen). Pedersén's summary notes that the largest single quantity of oil in the archive went to Jehoiachin.

What the text says (summary)

One tablet lists 10 sila of oil for 'Ya'ukin, king of Yahudu', 2 1/2 sila for 'the sons of the king of Yahudu' (another tablet: 'the 5 sons of the king of Yahudu'), and 4 sila for eight men of Yahudu.Summary of the published readings (Weidner 1939, as reproduced in secondary sources; full edition Pedersén 2025); no public-domain translation exists

What the evidence shows

  • About three hundred ration texts dated between Nebuchadnezzar's 10th and 35th years (CoJS); Pedersén (2025) publishes 527 texts and fragments from the palace archives.
  • Four texts name Jehoiachin with the title 'king of the land of Yahudu'; one is dated year 13 of Nebuchadnezzar (592/591 BCE) (CoJS).
  • Five sons of the king of Judah receive oil alongside him (secondary transcriptions of Weidner's texts).
  • The archive records barley, dates and sesame oil shipped mostly by boat, stored in silos and issued to foreigners and guests; the largest single oil allocation goes to Jehoiachin (Pedersén 2025 abstract).
  • Findspot: the Vaulted Building of the Southern Palace; Koldewey (1914, p. 100) records many tablets in its southern stairway chamber and reads the building as a storehouse (he also proposed it as the Hanging Gardens, an identification CoJS reports as since abandoned).

Open questions

  • Prisoner or pensioner? 2 Kgs 25:27-29 says Evil-Merodach 'did lift up the head of Jehoiachin ... out of prison' in the 37th year of the exile (c. 561 BCE). The tablets show him drawing large rations with his household in 592/591 BCE. CoJS reads the size of the ration as evidence that he was not imprisoned for the whole exile; another reading (ours) is that a period of confinement came later than 592. The tablets themselves say nothing about prison.
  • Five sons in 592 BCE: 1 Chr 3:17-18 lists seven sons of Jeconiah (Shealtiel, Malchiram, Pedaiah, Shenazzar, Jecamiah, Hoshama, Nedabiah). The tablet count (five) is a snapshot of one year and does not contradict a later total; it does bear on Jer 22:30 ('write ye this man childless'), which the text itself explains in the same verse as 'no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David'.
  • Weidner's 1939 first edition ('Jojachin, König von Juda, in babylonischen Keilschrifttexten', Mélanges syriens offerts à M. René Dussaud II) is the standard reference in secondary literature; it was not available for this entry, so it is not listed as a verified source.

The Find

  • DateDated archive texts run from Nebuchadnezzar II's 10th to 35th years (c. 595-570 BCE); the one Jehoiachin tablet with its date preserved is from year 13 (592/591 BCE)
  • How datedRegnal-year dates written on the tablets (Babylonian dating by year of Nebuchadnezzar); converted with the standard Neo-Babylonian chronology.
  • DiscoveredExcavated 1902-1903 (Koldewey's work on the north-east corner of the Southern Citadel, Dec 1902 - Jan 1904); the Jehoiachin entries were identified in Berlin by E. F. Weidner and published in 1939, Robert Koldewey (Deutsche Orient-Gesellschaft); read by Ernst F. Weidner, The 'Vaulted Building' at the north-east corner of the Southern Palace (Kasr), Babylon - tablets from its stairway chamber
  • Now heldVorderasiatisches Museum, Staatliche Museen zu Berlin (secondary listings cite VAT 16378 and excavation numbers Babylon 28122, 28178, 28186; not checked against a museum record). The museum's South Wing has been closed since 23 Oct 2023.
  • MaterialClay tablets
  • DimensionsThe dated year-13 tablet is about three inches tall (CoJS)
  • ScriptNeo-Babylonian cuneiform
  • LanguageAkkadian (Babylonian)

Biblical Passages

  • 2 Kgs 24:12-15Jehoiachin surrenders to Nebuchadnezzar and is carried to Babylon with his mother, wives and officials - the tablets show him in Babylon, at the royal storehouse's expense, by 592 BCE.
  • 2 Kgs 25:27-30; Jer 52:31-34'His allowance was a continual allowance given him of the king, a daily rate for every day' - a royal food allowance of exactly the kind these lists record, though these tablets date some 30 years before the release described there.
  • 1 Chr 3:17-18Sons of Jeconiah the captive; the tablets record five unnamed sons of the king of Judah receiving oil.
