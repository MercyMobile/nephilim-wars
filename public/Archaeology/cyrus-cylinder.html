Cyrus Cylinder

Babylonian foundation cylinder of Cyrus II · after 539 BCE

Overview

A barrel-shaped clay foundation cylinder found in several pieces by Hormuzd Rassam at Babylon in 1879. The text, written in Babylonian script and language, first describes (in the third person) how Nabonidus, the last Neo-Babylonian king, disrupted the cult of Marduk and burdened Babylon's population with labour-service; how Marduk searched for a righteous ruler, called Cyrus king of Anshan by name, and let him enter Babylon without battle. From line 20 the text switches to Cyrus's own voice: 'I am Cyrus, king of the world...'.

Cyrus claims to have ended the forced labour at Babylon, received tribute from kings 'from the Upper to the Lower sea', returned the gods of a named list of cities (Ashur, Susa, Agade, Eshnunna, Zamban, Me-Turnu, Der, the land of the Gutians, the cities across the Tigris) to their shrines, and gathered their inhabitants back to their dwellings. The gods Nabonidus had brought into Babylon were returned 'by command of Marduk'. The text closes with food offerings and the rebuilding of Babylon's wall Imgur-Enlil, during which an older foundation inscription of Ashurbanipal was found.

The cylinder does not mention Judah, Jerusalem, the Jews, or YHWH. Its connection to the biblical return from exile is one of policy and phrasing (a king returning deported peoples and their cult objects on a god's command), not a direct reference. A fragment in Yale (NBC 2504) restoring lines 36-45 was identified and joined in the 1970s, and in 2009-2010 two fragments of a clay tablet carrying a copy of the same text were identified in the British Museum by W. G. Lambert and Irving Finkel, showing the text also circulated outside the foundation deposit.

In its own words

(32) the gods, who dwelt in them, I brought them back to their places, and caused them to dwell in a habitation for all time. All their inhabitants I collected and restored them to their dwelling places.R. W. Rogers, Cuneiform Parallels to the Old Testament (New York, 1912), p. 383, line 32

What the evidence shows

  • Museum record: fired clay cylinder, length 21.9-22.8 cm, BM 90920 / 1880,0617.1941, excavated by Hormuzd Rassam at Babylon (Amran).
  • The cylinder broke into several pieces in antiquity; it was refired in 1961; plaster fills were removed in 2007.
  • Lines 1-19 are in the third person (Marduk chooses Cyrus); lines 20 onward are in the first person of Cyrus.
  • Line 12 (Rogers 1912): Marduk 'sought a righteous prince... Cyrus, king of Anshan, he called by name'.
  • Line 17: Babylon entered 'without battle and conflict'; Nabonidus delivered into Cyrus's hand.
  • Lines 30-32 list the places whose gods and people were returned: all are in Mesopotamia and the lands east of the Tigris; no western (Syro-Palestinian) place is named.
  • Line 33: the gods of Sumer and Akkad that Nabonidus had brought into Babylon were returned to their shrines 'by command of Marduk'.
  • Closing lines (with the Yale fragment NBC 2504, lines 36-45) describe rebuilding the wall Imgur-Enlil and finding an inscription of Ashurbanipal.
  • Two fragments of a clay tablet carrying a copy of the cylinder's text were identified in the British Museum's collections in 2009-2010 (W. G. Lambert, I. Finkel); one clarifies a passage illegible on the cylinder, the other supplies part of its missing text.

Open questions

  • Scope of the 'repatriation': the text itself names only Mesopotamian and trans-Tigridian cult centres (lines 30-32). A. Kuhrt (JSOT 25, 1983) reads the cylinder as a Babylonian building inscription in the Mesopotamian royal tradition, praising Cyrus in terms used of earlier kings, and argues it cannot be taken as evidence of an empire-wide policy of freeing deportees. Readers who connect it with Ezra 1 point to the same clause: the pattern 'return the gods, gather the people' is the same kind of act Ezra 1:2-4 and 6:3-5 attribute to Cyrus for Jerusalem. The cylinder is silent on Judah; any link is our inference from the parallel.
  • Genre: the British Museum curatorial note records the suggestion (Harmatta 1971; see BM bibliography) that the author used Ashurbanipal's foundation inscription found in the wall as a literary model. The 2009-2010 tablet copies show the text also circulated on tablets; whether that means it was 'distributed throughout the empire' (British Museum statement reported in The Art Newspaper, Feb 2010) or only copied within Babylonia is not settled by two fragments of unknown findspot.
  • 'Human rights charter': the text contains no statement of rights; the British Museum record says the concept 'would have been quite alien to Cyrus's contemporaries'. UNESCO's General Conference in November 2025 nevertheless adopted a resolution calling it 'an early charter of human rights and cultural diversity'. That is a modern political reading, not something the cuneiform says.

The Find

  • DateAfter the capture of Babylon by Cyrus in 539 BCE (British Museum record: production date '539 BC (after)')
  • How datedInternal: the text narrates Cyrus's entry into Babylon and the handing over of Nabonidus, then speaks in Cyrus's voice as king of Babylon; the object was a foundation deposit for the rebuilding of Babylon's wall Imgur-Enlil described at the end of the text. No regnal-year date is preserved.
  • Discovered1879, Hormuzd Rassam (British Museum excavations), Babylon (Amran mound), Iraq; findspot evidence discussed by C. B. F. Walker 1972
  • Now heldBritish Museum, London, BM 90920 (reg. no. 1880,0617.1941), Room 52; joined fragment NBC 2504 on loan from the Yale Babylonian Collection
  • MaterialFired clay (coarse core with large stone inclusions, finer surface slip), wheel-made
  • DimensionsLength 21.9-22.8 cm; diameter 7.8-8.2 cm (end A), 7.9 cm (end B), 10 cm maximum
  • ScriptNeo-Babylonian cuneiform
  • LanguageAkkadian (Babylonian)

Biblical Passages

  • Ezra 1:1-4; 2 Chr 36:22-23Both texts present Cyrus as acting on a god's command to return a deported people and restore a temple. The cylinder does the same for Mesopotamian cults under Marduk. The type of act corresponds; the named people and god do not.
  • Ezra 1:7-11; 6:5Return of the Jerusalem temple vessels corresponds in kind to line 32-33's return of cult images to their shrines (Israel had no cult image; the vessels are the parallel object).
  • Isa 44:28; 45:1-4YHWH 'calls Cyrus by name' and takes him by the right hand; the cylinder line 12 has Marduk calling Cyrus by name and taking his hand. Same royal-election language, different god. This is a verbal parallel, stated as our observation.
