Shiloh (Khirbet Seilun / Tel Shiloh) excavations

Multi-period hilltop site north of Jerusalem · excavated 1926-1963, 1981-1984, 2017-2026

Overview

Tel Shiloh has been dug by three expeditions: a Danish team (1926, 1929, 1932, 1963), a Tel Aviv University team led by Israel Finkelstein (1981-1984, preliminary report in Tel Aviv 12, 1985; monograph 1993), and since 2017 the Associates for Biblical Research (ABR) under Scott Stripling, which finished its eighth season in June 2026.

ABR reports worship-related objects from the area of a large building that runs east-west: three altar horns (2019), a ceramic pomegranate (2018, published in Judea and Samaria Research Studies 28, 2019), murex shells, and a Thutmose III scarab. In 2022 it found the piers and socket stones of a gate in the northern wall. In 2025 it worked on this gate complex. In 2026 it reported three things: more walls showing a bent-axis gate with several rooms; the southern wall of the east-west building; and three Middle Bronze Age storage jars in Area D, two holding charred olives and one holding wheat.

Most of the ABR results so far are season announcements. The only peer-reviewed ABR article located for this entry is the pomegranate study. The building's full dimensions, its stratigraphy, and radiocarbon dates have not yet been published.

What the evidence shows

  • Three restorable Middle Bronze Age pithoi in Area D (the favissa area). Two held charred olives and one held wheat. Radiocarbon testing of the contents is planned (ABR, 22 June 2026).
  • The southern wall of a 'monumental building' oriented east-west was located in 2026. ABR says this now lets it reconstruct the full dimensions, but it has not yet published them (ABR, 22 June 2026).
  • Gate complex in the northern fortification: 2022 exposed the gate's piers and socket stones where the glacis against the northern wall ends, with a terraced slope leading up to it; 2026 exposed more walls showing a bent-axis gate with several rooms (ABR 2022; 2026).
  • Altar horns (2019): horn 1 was 38 x 23.5 cm and was built into an Early Roman wall; horn 2 was 18 x 12.5 cm and lay about 3 m to the southwest; horn 3 was 38 x 20 cm and came from a 'destruction matrix' about 1 m lower. All came from the general area of the east-west building (ABR, 12 Aug 2019).
  • A ceramic pomegranate from the same area (2018), published by Lopez, Stripling and Ben-Shlomo in JSRS 28 (2019).
  • Arrowheads that look medieval, together with Islamic pottery and Islamic and Crusader coins, are read as evidence of a possible Frankish raid on the medieval village of Seilun in the 13th century (Seevers, Barber and Campbell, JSRS 34/2, 2025).
  • A rock-cut pool about 13 x 18 m and 2.5 m deep, about 300 m south of the tell, was built in the Byzantine-Umayyad period and used until the Ottoman period (IAA salvage digs in 2013 and 2022; HA-ESI 138, 2026).

Open questions

  • Is the east-west building the tabernacle precinct? ABR (22 June 2026) says the structure 'exhibits proportions similar to those described for the biblical tabernacle (Ex. 26:15-25; 27:13)' and lists altar horns, pomegranates and murex shells found 'in and around' it. Against this, from the published data: the dimensions have not been published, so the proportion claim cannot yet be checked; horn 1 was reused in an Early Roman wall, so its original context is lost; and the only date given (1177-980 BC, ABR 2019) comes without stratigraphic or radiocarbon support. Until a report appears, this stays an announced claim.
  • Is the northern gate the one where Eli died? ABR (2022) says it 'may have been the gate where Eli died' (1 Sam 4:18). The posts read here give no phase date for the gate, so the tie to Eli rests on the biblical narrative plus the gate's position, not on a dated stratum.
  • How does ABR's sequence fit the earlier Tel Aviv University stratigraphy (Finkelstein et al., Tel Aviv 12, 1985; monograph 1993)? Neither report has been reviewed in full for this entry. Any agreement or conflict between the two sequences has not been checked here.

Corrected from the earlier edition of this page
  • Claims that the tabernacle's building has been 'found' at Shiloh go beyond the evidence. ABR's own June 2026 wording is that the east-west structure 'exhibits proportions similar to' the Exodus tabernacle, and its dimensions are not yet published.
  • Claims that the altar horns come from 'the tabernacle altar' ignore their contexts. Horn 1 was built into an Early Roman wall, and the three horns lay at two elevations about 1 m apart (ABR 2019).
  • Calling the northern gate 'the gate where Eli died' overstates ABR's own 'may have been' (2022). No phase date for the gate appears in the posts read.
  • An earlier draft note mentioned 'bone deposits'. No ABR post cited here describes bone deposits, so that claim is not repeated here.
  • The 2026 pithoi are Middle Bronze Age (Canaanite), which ABR itself places before any Israelite presence. They are not evidence for the tabernacle period.

The Find

  • DateOccupation from the Middle Bronze Age through the Iron Age and later periods; ABR's 2026 finds include Middle Bronze Age storage jars, and ABR dates its monumental east-west building to 1177-980 BC
  • How datedThe MB date of the 2026 jars is ABR's ceramic typology; ABR says radiocarbon tests on their charred contents are planned, not yet done. The 1177-980 BC date for the monumental building is stated in ABR's 2019 news post without published stratigraphic or radiocarbon data; the peer-reviewed article announced then has not been located for this entry.
  • DiscoveredDanish excavations 1926, 1929, 1932 and 1963; Tel Aviv University 1981-1984; Associates for Biblical Research (ABR) from 2017, eighth season in 2026, Danish expedition (H. Kjaer and successors); Israel Finkelstein with S. Bunimovitz, Z. Lederman and others; ABR under director Scott Stripling, Khirbet Seilun, north of Jerusalem
  • Now heldIn situ (site). Finds from the ABR seasons: repository not stated in the sources read.

Biblical Passages

  • Josh 18:1The congregation sets up the tent of meeting at Shiloh. ABR's east-west building is its candidate for the tabernacle area (their reading; dimensions not yet published).
  • 1 Sam 1:9; 3:3The Shiloh sanctuary has a doorpost and is called hekhal ('temple'), which suggests a built structure there by Eli's time. A monumental building would fit that wording, but no excavated feature has been tied to it by inscription.
  • 1 Sam 4:12-18Eli falls 'by the side of the gate' and dies. ABR's northern gate is its candidate (their 'may have been').
  • Exod 26:15-25; 27:13The tabernacle's board counts and the court width that ABR cites for its proportion comparison.
  • Jer 7:12-14; 26:6; Ps 78:60Shiloh's ruin is remembered as a warning. The published data read here do not establish which destruction layer, if any, corresponds to it.
