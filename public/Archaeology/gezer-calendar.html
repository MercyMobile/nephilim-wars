Gezer Calendar

Inscribed limestone tablet listing the farming year · Gezer · Iron Age (date by script only)

Overview

A small limestone tablet found in 1908 during R. A. S. Macalister's excavation of Gezer for the Palestine Exploration Fund. According to Macalister's final report, it came from the northern end of trench 8, in debris of his 'Fourth Semitic' period, with little datable material around it. The inscription is scratched into the stone. It has seven lines of writing, plus the remains of an eighth line written vertically and cut off at the break. The back is plain, and the left edge carries a diagonal fret pattern. A peg-hole suggests it was fixed to a wall.

The text lists farming tasks in order, each tied to one month or two: ingathering, sowing, late sowing, the flax harvest, the barley harvest, the harvest of all the rest (or 'harvest and measuring'), pruning or vine-tending, and summer fruit. Read with the 'two months' forms, the list covers twelve months. The vertical line at the edge is usually read as a personal name beginning 'Abi-', and some restore it as 'Abiyah'.

Right after the find, Lidzbarski, Gray, Pilcher, Ronzevalle, Dalman, Vincent, Marti and Cook each published competing readings in the Palestine Exploration Fund Quarterly Statement and the Revue Biblique. Macalister collected them in 1912. The main disagreement was whether a repeated sign was the conjunction 'and' or a dual ending meaning 'two months'. Later work split over whether the script and language are Hebrew or Phoenician.

In its own words

(1) Month of the fruit-harvest—Month of (2) the sowing—Month of the after-grass— (3) Month of the flax-harvest— (4) Month of the barley-harvest— (5) Month of the harvest of all [the rest]— (6) Month of the pruning of vine-plants— (7) Month of the fig-harvest—M. Lidzbarski's rendering as printed in R. A. S. Macalister, The Excavation of Gezer 1902-1905 and 1907-1909 (London: PEF / John Murray, 1912), vol. II p. 25. On pp. 25-26 Macalister also prints Vincent's alternative: '(1) Two months, late crops—Two months, (2) sowing—Two months, spring crops— (3) One month, cutting of flax— (4) One month, harvest of barley— (5) One month, all the harvest— (6) Two months, fruits [vines]— (7) One month, summer fruits'.

What the evidence shows

  • Material: limestone tablet with the text scratched in. It is not an ostracon.
  • Seven horizontal lines survive, plus the start of a vertical eighth line interrupted by the fracture (Macalister 1912, II p. 24).
  • The lower part of the tablet is lost along an oblique break through a square peg-hole. A second hole on the back connects to it.
  • The back is plain except for one or two tool marks. The left edge has a diagonal fret of about five or six lines to the inch.
  • Findspot: trench 8, north end, 'Fourth Semitic debris', with scanty associated pottery (Macalister 1912, II p. 24). This is not a closed, well-stratified context.
  • Farming vocabulary on the tablet: ingathering (ʾsp), sowing (zrʿ), late sowing (lqš), flax (pšt), barley (śʿrm), harvest (qṣr), pruning (zmr), summer fruit (qṣ).
  • Macalister reports that within a year of the find, opinion on its date was split between the 8th century and earlier (Lidzbarski, Gray and Ronzevalle for the 8th; Vincent and Marti otherwise). Macalister himself put it in the 6th century BCE.

Open questions

  • Script and language. W. F. Albright (1943, as quoted by Rollston) called it 'perfect classical Hebrew'. C. Rollston (Biblical Archaeology Review, May/June 2012) argues that 'there is no lexeme or linguistic feature in the Gezer Calendar that can be considered distinctively Hebrew' and that the script is Phoenician, citing J. Naveh's judgment that 'no specifically Hebrew characters can be distinguished.' A. Koller (JNES 72, 2013) works from the Hebrew lexicon, reading mʿṣd / ʿṣd as the Hebrew term for the flax-harvesting tool and task. D. Sivan (IEJ 48, 1998) sets out the Northwest Semitic linguistic features. The disputed evidence is a handful of word forms and letter shapes. No single feature settles it.
  • 'And' or 'two months'. Lidzbarski and Gray (PEFQS 1909) read a recurring sign after 'month' as the conjunction w- ('a month and ingathering'). Vincent (Revue Biblique 1909) read it as a dual or suffix ending, giving 'two months'. His reading turns eight items into twelve months. Ronzevalle reached twelve months another way and argued the text is a rotation of work, not a calendar (all summarized by Macalister 1912, II pp. 25-26).
  • Date. Macalister (1912) dated it to the 6th century BCE from associated pottery. The palaeographers he reports put it about two centuries earlier. Today's late 10th or early 9th century BCE date rests entirely on letter shapes, because the findspot does not fix it. Whether it belongs to Solomon's Gezer (1 Kgs 9:15-17) therefore depends on the palaeographic sequence, not on stratigraphy.
  • Purpose. Scholars have proposed a school exercise, a tax or administrative schedule (Ronzevalle, via Macalister 1912), or a farmer's mnemonic. The tablet itself says nothing about its purpose. The peg-hole suggests it was displayed or hung up, and that is the only physical clue.

The Find

  • DateIron Age; late 10th or early 9th century BCE is the usual palaeographic date. Macalister, the excavator, dated it to the 6th century BCE.
  • How datedLetter shapes only. Macalister (1912, II p. 24) found it in 'Fourth Semitic debris' in trench 8 with associated pottery he calls 'unfortunately scanty'; he dated it by that pottery to the 6th century BCE, while the palaeographers he cites put it two centuries earlier. No independent stratigraphic or radiocarbon date exists for the tablet itself.
  • Discovered1908, R. A. Stewart Macalister (Palestine Exploration Fund excavation), Tell Gezer, trench 8, northern end, in 'Fourth Semitic debris'
  • Now heldIstanbul Archaeology Museums (Gezer lay in Ottoman territory in 1908)
  • MaterialLimestone tablet (not a potsherd), lower part lost along an oblique break through a square peg-hole
  • DimensionsMacalister gives the height as 4¼ in (about 11 cm); his width and thickness figures do not come through legibly in the scan used
  • ScriptEarly alphabetic linear script: Phoenician or early Hebrew, which is disputed (see questions)
  • LanguageNorthwest Semitic: Hebrew or Phoenician, which is disputed

Biblical Passages

  • Exod 23:16; 34:22The first item is ʾsp, 'ingathering'. The same root names the Feast of Ingathering (ḥag hāʾāsip), which the text places 'at the end of the year' or 'at the turn of the year'. The two sources share the word and a year that turns at the autumn harvest.
  • Ruth 1:22; 2:23; 2 Sam 21:9The tablet's 'barley harvest' (qṣr śʿrm) uses the same two words as 'the beginning of barley harvest' in these verses. On the tablet it comes before the harvest of everything else, which matches Ruth's barley-then-wheat order.
  • Josh 2:6Rahab hides the spies under 'stalks of flax' (pištê hāʿēṣ) laid on the roof. The tablet has a month for flax (pšt), which confirms flax was a scheduled crop in the region's farming year.
  • 1 Kgs 9:15-17Gezer is named as a city Solomon rebuilt. The tablet comes from Gezer, but its tie to Solomon's reign is only as good as the palaeographic date, because its findspot does not date it.
