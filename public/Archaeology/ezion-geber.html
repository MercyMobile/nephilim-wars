Ezion-geber and Tell el-Kheleifeh

Iron Age casemate fortress at the head of the Gulf of Aqaba, identified with Ezion-geber · 8th–6th centuries BCE

Overview

Tell el-Kheleifeh is a low mudbrick mound about 500 m inland from the northern tip of the Gulf of Aqaba, about halfway between Eilat and Aqaba. Fritz Frank surveyed it in 1933 and identified it with biblical Ezion-geber. Nelson Glueck accepted that identification and excavated three seasons in 1938–1940. He announced it as Solomon's port and a great copper smelter.

Glueck never published a technical final report. Gary Pratico went back to his records and pottery (BASOR 259, 1985). He found two main building phases, a casemate fortress and then a fortified settlement, and pottery from the 8th to 6th centuries BCE with a later postscript. On that evidence nothing at the site belongs to the 10th century.

Glueck returned to the site's interpretation in 'Ezion-Geber' (Biblical Archaeologist 28, 1965); that article's text is not quoted here. In January 2026 Ben-Ami, Weiss, Erickson-Gini and Boaretto (Levant 58) paired Kheleifeh with the 'En Hazeva fortress at the Arabah's northern outlet. They dated 'En Hazeva by radiocarbon to the first half of the 8th century BCE, before Assyrian dominance, and attributed both forts to the northern Kingdom of Israel.

What the evidence shows

  • A casemate fortress, later expanded into a fortified settlement (Pratico 1985).
  • Pottery horizons from the 8th to the 6th centuries BCE; no securely 10th-century assemblage (Pratico 1985).
  • The site stands about 500 m from the present shoreline. No harbour installations are reported in the published abstracts.
  • Glueck's 'smelter' reading of the main building is not supported by Pratico's 1985 reappraisal of the excavation records, which assigns the pottery to the 8th–6th centuries BCE.
  • 'En Hazeva, the matching fortress at the Arabah's other outlet, is radiocarbon-dated to the first half of the 8th century BCE (Ben-Ami et al. 2026).

Open questions

  • Is Tell el-Kheleifeh Ezion-geber at all? Frank (1933) and Glueck (BASOR 71, 1938; BASOR 75, 1939; BASOR 79, 1940) identified it as Ezion-geber, and Glueck's dating followed from that identification. Pratico (1985) says the identification 'is both an archaeological and an historical problem'. His pottery gives 8th–6th centuries BCE, while 1 Kings 9:26 places Solomon's fleet at Ezion-geber in the 10th. Either Solomon's Ezion-geber lies elsewhere, or it left no remains at this mound.
  • Who built the fortress? Ben-Ami et al. (Levant 2026) argue the 8th-century date of 'En Hazeva and the scale of both forts point to the northern Kingdom of Israel, before Assyrian hegemony. An 8th-century date also overlaps Judah's Uzziah/Azariah, who 'built Elath and restored it to Judah' (2 Kgs 14:22). That reading of the text is mine; the Levant abstract does not discuss it.
  • Glueck's 5 periods from the 10th to 5th century (BASOR 1938–40) against Pratico's two phases from the 8th to 6th century (BASOR 1985). The difference comes from Glueck fitting the pottery to the biblical date, which Pratico sets aside.

  • 2026-01Ben-Ami, Weiss, Erickson-Gini and Boaretto (Levant 58) date the 'En Hazeva fortress by radiocarbon to the first half of the 8th century BCE. They pair it with Tell el-Kheleifeh as twin forts at the two outlets of the Arabah and attribute both to the northern Kingdom of Israel. [4]

The Find

  • Date8th to 6th centuries BCE, with a later postscript of uncertain length (Pratico 1985). Glueck had dated five periods from the 10th to the 5th century BCE.
  • How datedPottery horizons re-studied by Pratico (BASOR 259, 1985) from Glueck's unpublished excavation records. Glueck's 10th-century start rested on the assumed biblical identification, not on the pottery. Ben-Ami et al. (Levant 58, 2026) date the twin fortress at 'En Hazeva to the first half of the 8th century BCE by radiocarbon and argue Tell el-Kheleifeh was built alongside it.
  • DiscoveredSurveyed 1933 (Fritz Frank); excavated 1938–1940, Fritz Frank (identification); Nelson Glueck (three seasons, American Schools of Oriental Research), Low mudbrick mound c. 500 m north of the Gulf of Aqaba shore, between modern Eilat and Aqaba (Jordanian side)
  • Now heldIn situ, Aqaba region, Jordan
  • MaterialMudbrick (casemate walls)

Biblical Passages

  • 1 Kgs 9:26–28'King Solomon made a fleet of ships in Ezion-geber, which is beside Eloth, on the shore of the Red Sea, in the land of Edom.' Pratico's re-study of Kheleifeh found no 10th-century occupation, so the mound does not show Solomon's port.
  • 1 Kgs 22:48; 2 Chr 20:35–37Jehoshaphat's ships 'were broken at Ezion-geber' (9th century BCE). Pratico's 8th-century start is also later than this.
  • 2 Kgs 14:22; 16:6Azariah builds Elath (8th century), and later Edom takes it. This is the period in which the Kheleifeh fortress was actually standing (Pratico; Ben-Ami et al.).
  • Num 33:35–36; Deut 2:8Ezion-geber as a wilderness station. The mound has no remains of that period.
