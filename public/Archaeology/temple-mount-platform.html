Temple Mount Platform (Herodian enclosure)

Herod's enlarged temple esplanade in Jerusalem · begun c. 20/19 BCE, work continuing to the 60s CE

Overview

The Temple Mount platform is the huge artificial esplanade Herod the Great built around the Jerusalem temple. His builders ran retaining walls down the slopes of the eastern ridge, filled and vaulted the space behind them, and levelled the top. The modern Haram al-Sharif keeps this outline on the west, south and east.

The Survey of Western Palestine's Jerusalem volume (1884, p. 119) measured the enclosure: south wall 922 ft, west wall 1,601 ft, east wall 1,530 ft, north wall 1,042 ft, about 35 acres. It is an irregular four-sided figure, not a square. Warren's shaft 90 ft east of the south-west angle exposed 24 courses, 87 ft 6 in of masonry, with the foundation course bedded on rock (SWP Jerusalem, p. 169–170).

Josephus describes the project (Whiston's translation, Ant. 15.11.3). Herod 'built a wall below, beginning at the bottom… and at the south side he laid rocks together, and bound them one to another with lead… and made it a level on the external upper surface'. He gives the circuit as four furlongs, 'each angle containing in length a furlong'. The excavated walls are longer than a furlong on the west and east. That is the difference between his figure and the measured outline. (Josephus quotations: W. Whiston, 1737, public domain.)

What the evidence shows

  • Irregular quadrilateral: W 1,601 ft, E 1,530 ft, S 922 ft, N 1,042 ft; about 35 acres (SWP Jerusalem 1884, p. 119 and in the section on Arab measurements).
  • At the south wall, 24 courses of 3 ft 6 in to 3 ft 9 in masonry, 87 ft 6 in in all, run down to bedrock 90 ft east of the south-west corner (SWP Jerusalem, pp. 169–170).
  • Buried courses have drafted margins with rough, unfinished bosses that were never exposed to weather. The joints are so tight a knife blade can scarcely go in (SWP Jerusalem, p. 170).
  • A pavement and drainage channel of the 1st century CE were found west of Warren's Gate, probably linked to a north-western Herodian gate (Onn & Weksler-Bdolah, 'Atiqot 106, 2025).
  • Plastered channels cut only in the Herodian courses of the western and southern walls are dated before 70 CE and read as runoff collectors feeding water installations (Szanton & Weksler-Bdolah, 'Atiqot 119, 2025).
  • In the Western Wall Tunnels, a Herodian dam wall (latter half of the 1st century BCE) was later topped by a paved triclinium building. A coin of Tiberius (17–25 CE) sealed under its fountain floor gives a terminus post quem of 17 CE for it ('Atiqot 119, 2025).

Open questions

  • When was the enclosure finished? Josephus dates the start to Herod's 18th year (Ant. 15.380) and says the work was 'finished' only under Albinus, with 18,000 workmen then laid off (Ant. 20.219). Regev, Uziel et al. (PLOS ONE 2020) date Wilson's Arch as begun under Herod and enlarged under the Roman procurators, including Pilate, within about 70 years. Levy ('Atiqot 119, 2025) moves the triclinium building beside the wall from Herod's reign to after 17 CE. Together these show construction going on around the platform through the first half of the 1st century CE.
  • The north edge: the SWP (1884, p. 119) records a 30 ft rock scarp forming 350 ft of the north boundary at the north-west. How far north the Herodian platform ran, and how it met the Antonia fortress, is not settled by the sources cited here. Patrich (The Jerusalem Temple and the Temple Mount, Mohr Siebeck 2024) re-evaluates the literary and archaeological evidence for the enclosure's development. Only the publisher's abstract is cited here, so his conclusions are not stated.

Corrected from the earlier edition of this page
  • Old dimensions 'approximately 150 meters by 50 meters' / '150 m long, 50 m high' are wrong. The enclosure's sides are about 488, 466, 281 and 318 m (SWP 1884, p. 119), covering about 35 acres.
  • Old claim that the platform 'has been under continuous construction and expansion for centuries' mixes periods. The Herodian enclosure was begun c. 20/19 BCE and, per Josephus, finished in the 60s CE. Later Islamic and other work on top is a separate matter.
  • Old 'discovery of the Temple Mount Platform' framing is wrong. It is a standing monument. What has been 'discovered' is its buried courses, gates, streets and dating evidence through excavation (Warren 1867–70; Mazar 1968–78; IAA work to the present).
  • Old 'Mark 13 (Jesus on the Mount of Olives)' and 'Luke 21 (signs of the end)' are loose links. The relevant verses are Mark 13:1–2 / Luke 21:5–6, where the disciples admire the stones. John 2:20 (46 years in building) is the verse that bears directly on the construction and was missing.
  • Old 'Massive platform Jesus walked on' is a reasonable inference from the Gospels, which place Jesus teaching in the temple courts (e.g., John 10:23). But the platform's full extent was still under construction in his day (see questions).

The Find

  • DateBegun in Herod's 18th regnal year, about 20/19 BCE (Josephus, Ant. 15.380). Josephus says the temple works were 'finished' under the procurator Albinus, about 62–64 CE (Ant. 20.219). Excavation shows building and alterations around the enclosure well into the 1st century CE.
  • How datedJosephus' regnal-year dating; coins and radiocarbon from excavations along the walls (e.g., Western Wall Tunnels 2018–2020, Levy and Weksler-Bdolah, 'Atiqot 119, 2025; Regev et al., PLOS ONE 2020 for Wilson's Arch).
  • DiscoveredStanding monument; measured by the Palestine Exploration Fund survey (Warren's shafts 1867–70); excavated along the south and south-west walls 1968–1978 and in many later campaigns, Charles Warren (PEF); Benjamin Mazar (1968–78); Israel Antiquities Authority teams (Reich, Baruch, Weksler-Bdolah, Uziel and others), Eastern ridge of the Old City of Jerusalem
  • Now heldIn situ, Jerusalem
  • MaterialLimestone ashlar retaining walls with drafted margins, earth and vault fill
  • DimensionsPEF survey (1884): west wall 1,601 ft (c. 488 m); east wall 1,530 ft (c. 466 m); south wall 922 ft (c. 281 m); north wall 1,042 ft (c. 318 m); c. 35 acres

Biblical Passages

  • John 2:20'Forty-six years was this temple in building.' Counted from Herod's 18th year (c. 20/19 BCE), 46 years lands about 27/28 CE. The excavated evidence for work continuing through the 1st century CE fits a project still under way then.
  • Mark 13:1–2; Matt 24:1–2; Luke 21:5–6'What manner of stones and what buildings!' The disciples point out the great stones. The platform walls are built of those very large drafted ashlars (Josephus Ant. 15.392 gives stones of 25 × 8 × 12 cubits).
  • Matt 21:12–13; Mark 11:15–17; John 2:14–16The cleansing of the temple took place in the outer courts that stood on this platform. The platform is the ground of the event; nothing excavated identifies the spot.
  • John 10:23; Acts 3:11'Solomon's Porch/Colonnade' on the east. Josephus (Ant. 20.221) describes the eastern cloisters in the deep valley, built of stones twenty cubits long.
