Gallio Inscription, Delphi

Fragmentary Greek letter of the emperor Claudius naming the proconsul Gallio · 52 CE

Overview

A letter of the emperor Claudius to Delphi (or, on another reading, to Gallio's successor), cut on limestone and set up at the sanctuary of Apollo. It survives as a set of fragments; Plassart's 1970 edition (Fouilles de Delphes III.4, no. 286) combines nine of them, and Oliver (Hesperia 1971) printed a revised text.

The prescript gives Claudius' titles including his 26th acclamation as imperator. The body refers to what 'L. Junius Gallio, my friend and proconsul' reported, and, on Plassart's reading of line 5, concerns Delphi's depopulation: Claudius wants new, well-born settlers invited to keep up the city.

Gallio is the Roman governor before whom the Jews of Corinth brought Paul (Acts 18:12-17). Because Achaia's proconsuls normally served one year, the letter's date pins Gallio's term to 51/52 or 52/53 and gives the one external fixed point in the chronology of Paul's Corinthian stay.

What the text says (summary)

Tiberius Claudius Caesar Augustus Germanicus ... acclaimed imperator for the 26th time, father of the fatherland ... as Lucius Junius Gallio, my friend and proconsul [of Achaia, wrote] ...Literal translation (this encyclopedia) of the Greek of lines 1-2 and 5-6 as printed by A. Deissmann, St. Paul (Eng. tr. 1912), Appendix I p. 246 (names, 'my friend' and 'proconsul' partly restored; 'of Achaia' wholly restored)

What the evidence shows

  • The numeral 26 for Claudius' imperial acclamations is legible; Deissmann calls it 'above all doubt' (1912, p. 248).
  • Gallio's name survives as Γαλλίων with [Ἰού]νιος partly restored; φ[ίλος] and [ἀνθύπ]ατος are partly restored; the province name 'Achaia' is a restoration.
  • The addressee's name in the prescript is lost.
  • Four 'old' fragments (Delphi 3883, 2178, 2271, 4001) plus further fragments; Plassart's 1970 edition uses nine.
  • Letter height 18-20 mm; limestone from the local Hagios Elias quarries.
  • Seneca, Ep. Mor. 104.1: Gallio, taking a fever in Achaia, took ship at once, saying the illness was of the place, not the body (quoted in a footnote by Deissmann 1912, App. I).

Open questions

  • Who received the letter: A. Plassart (FD III.4, 1970) divides line 17 so it contains a second-person singular pronoun and concludes the recipient was an individual, probably Gallio's successor as proconsul. J. H. Oliver (Hesperia 40, 1971, 239-240) rejects this because the letters can be the end of a verb and because Gallio is still called proconsul in the text; he reads the recipient as a community, Delphi or the Amphictyonic Council.
  • Imperial titles in line 1: Deissmann (1912) restored 'pontifex maximus' (ἀρχιερεὺς μέγιστος), making a 71-letter line; Plassart (1970) omitted it; Oliver (1971) accepts the omission, citing a Claudian letter from Miletus (Milet III no. 156) that also lacks the title.
  • Start of Gallio's year: Deissmann (1912, pp. 252-255) argues proconsuls took office in early summer and puts Gallio's entry around mid-summer 51, giving Paul's arrival at Corinth in early 50; this rests on the one-year-term rule and the acclamation range, not on a date stated in the inscription. If the letter was written after Gallio's successor arrived (Plassart's reading), the term shifts; the inscription alone does not decide between 51/52 and 52/53.
  • Whether the 'new' fragments belong to the same letter: Pomtow (in Deissmann 1912, p. 243) held they were cut by the same mason but belonged to another rescript, on line spacing and letter height; Deissmann left it open; Plassart (1970) included them.

The Find

  • DateBetween the end of 51 / beginning of 52 CE and 1 August 52 CE (Claudius' 26th imperial acclamation)
  • How datedLine 2 gives Claudius as acclaimed imperator for the 26th time (numeral KS). He is already 'imperator XXVII' on the Aqua Claudia arch dedicated 1 August 52 (CIL VI 1256), so the letter falls before that; Deissmann (1912, App. I pp. 248-250), using data compiled by H. Dessau, puts it 'between (the end of 51 or more probably) the beginning of 52 and 1 August 52'. The tribunician-power numeral (12th) is restored, not read.
  • DiscoveredFragments recovered in the French excavations at Delphi before 1905; first published by Émile Bourguet in 1905; more fragments identified later (Deissmann 1912 reports three 'new' fragments, Delphi nos. 728, 500 and 2311), École française d'Athènes excavations; edited by É. Bourguet (1905), A. Plassart (1970), Sanctuary of Apollo, Delphi; H. Pomtow held that it was set on an outer wall of the south side of the temple of Apollo
  • Now heldDelphi Archaeological Museum; the four 'old' fragments bear Delphi collection numbers 3883, 2178, 2271 (old no. 59), 4001 (Deissmann 1912, App. I p. 244)
  • MaterialWhitish-grey limestone from the Hagios Elias quarries near Delphi
  • DimensionsLetter height 18-20 mm; original line length estimated at c. 1.40 m (Deissmann 1912)
  • ScriptGreek alphabetic, monumental
  • LanguageKoine Greek

Biblical Passages

  • Acts 18:12-17'When Gallio was proconsul (ἀνθυπατεύοντος) of Achaia' the Jews brought Paul before the tribunal at Corinth. The inscription attests a Gallio holding the proconsulship in Claudius' 26th acclamation. It says nothing about Paul, Corinth or the Jews.
  • Acts 18:11Paul stayed 18 months in Corinth; combining this with Gallio's dated term is the basis of the 50-51/52 CE reconstruction. That combination is an inference, not a statement in the stone.
  • Acts 18:2Aquila and Priscilla came from Italy 'because Claudius had commanded all Jews to depart from Rome' - the same emperor who issued this letter.
