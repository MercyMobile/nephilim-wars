Bullae of Jeremiah's officials: Jehucal son of Shelemiah, Gedaliah son of Pashhur, Gemariah son of Shaphan

Three excavated clay seal impressions from the City of David naming men who appear in Jeremiah 36-38 · late 7th to early 6th century BCE

Overview

Three bullae (clay lumps stamped by a seal to close a document) from the City of David carry names that also appear in Jeremiah 36-38. The Gemariah bulla came from the hoard of 51 bullae in the 'House of the Bullae' (Locus 967) in Area G of Yigal Shiloh's excavations. Eilat Mazar announced the Jehucal bulla on 4 August 2005 and the Gedaliah bulla on 31 July 2008, both from her excavation at the summit of the City of David.

The readings, as transcribed by Mykytiuk (2009), are lyhwkl bn shlmyhw bn shby, 'belonging to Yehukal son of Shelemyahu son of Shobi'; lgdlyhw bn pshhwr, 'belonging to Gedalyahu son of [P]ashhur'; and lgmryhw [b]n shpn, 'belonging to Gemaryahu son of Shaphan'. The Bible names Jehucal son of Shelemiah and Gedaliah son of Pashhur together among the officials who heard Jeremiah and had him put in the cistern (Jer 37:3; 38:1-6). It places Gemariah son of Shaphan the scribe in a chamber of the temple under Jehoiakim (Jer 36:10-12, 25).

Mykytiuk grades the Jehucal and Gedaliah matches 'grade 2 or higher' (reasonable, with a third identifying mark lacking) and the Gemariah match 'grade 3' (reliable). He notes that the stratigraphic context of Mazar's two bullae is disputed and that her final publication was still awaited in 2009.

What the text says (summary)

(1) Belonging to Yehukal, son of Shelemyahu, son of Shobi. (2) Belonging to Gedalyahu, son of [P]ashhur. (3) Belonging to Gemaryahu, [so]n of Shaphan.Literal translation (this encyclopedia) of the transliterations lyhwkl b[n] shlmyhw bn shby; lgdlyhw. bn [p]shhwr.; lgmryhw [b]n shpn, as published in Mykytiuk, Maarav 16/1 (2009): 86, 92, 110. No public-domain translation exists.

What the evidence shows

  • Jehucal bulla: a horizontal oval from a nearly circular seal. There is no border line, and three lines hold five words without dividers. A chip removes the top of the first letter of line 2, the nun of bn (Mykytiuk 2009: 86).
  • Jehucal bulla: found by Area A supervisor Yoav Farhi 'between two large stones' in the Large Stone Structure; discovery announced 4 Aug 2005 (Mykytiuk 2009: 87).
  • Jehucal's patronyms: Mykytiuk reads shin, Shelemyahu and Shobi, because those spellings match known biblical names (2 Sam 17:27 has Shobi), rather than sin (Mykytiuk 2009: 86-87).
  • Gedaliah bulla: aniconic horizontal ellipse. A single line surrounds the face and a double line divides two registers. Diagonal scratches cross the centre, and the pe of Pashhur is partly scraped off, so it is 'very uncertain at first sight' (Mykytiuk 2009: 92-93).
  • Gedaliah bulla: discovery announced 31 July 2008. Photos are in Mazar, Palace of King David (2009): 66, 68, and the findspot is mapped on p. 69 (Mykytiuk 2009: 92-93).
  • Gemariah bulla: from Locus 967 ('House of the Bullae'), Area G, Shiloh's excavations, in a hoard of 51 bullae. Its first nun (lower register), documented in an early photograph before it broke off, has parallel shafts and a perpendicular crossbar, a late-7th-century form (Mykytiuk 2009: 90, 110-111).

Open questions

  • Same men or namesakes? Mykytiuk (Maarav 2009) accepts all three matches. Jehucal and Gedaliah are grade 2 or higher: the name and patronym match, and the date and setting fit. Gemariah is grade 3. He notes that another father-and-son pair Shelemyahu/Yehukal could have lived in Jerusalem, and that a royal-palace findspot would be a third identifying mark if the Large Stone Structure could be shown to be royal.
  • Stratigraphic context. Eilat Mazar (Palace of King David, 2009) takes the Large Stone Structure to be a royal building used until 586 BCE. Finkelstein, Herzog, Singer-Avitz and Ussishkin ('Has King David's Palace in Jerusalem Been Found?', Tel Aviv 34, 2007) dispute her dating of the building's elements. Mykytiuk (2009: 94) states that the Gedaliah and Jehucal findspots 'lie in an archaeological context whose date is disputed' and relies on palaeography.
  • The first letter of Gedaliah's patronym. Mykytiuk argues for pe, ruling out kaph and mem on letter shape and space; nun 'perhaps cannot be ruled out completely'. The identification with Pashhur depends on this reading.

Corrected from the earlier edition of this page
  • A claim circulating with this find (including in the sister project's catalogue) says the Jehucal and Gedaliah bullae were found 'in the 586 BCE destruction layer'. Mykytiuk (2009: 94) states that their findspots lie in a context whose date is disputed and that stratigraphy may not match the bullae's date, so they are dated by palaeography.
  • Calling the bullae 'Jeremiah's captors' own seals' is a step too far. They are clay impressions of seals, and the identification with the biblical officials rests on matching name plus patronym plus date, graded 'grade 2 or higher', not certain, for Jehucal and Gedaliah (Mykytiuk 2009).
  • The sister project cited the madainproject.com list for these bullae. That is not a primary source; the primary publications are Shiloh and Tarler (1986), Mazar (2009), and the analysis in Mykytiuk (2009).
  • Gedaliah's patronym is sometimes printed as certain. The first letter of Pashhur is damaged and uncertain (Mykytiuk 2009: 92-93).

The Find

  • DateLate 7th to early 6th century BCE (palaeographic dating)
  • How datedLetter forms. Mykytiuk (Maarav 16/1, 2009) dates the Gedaliah bulla by the form of its he and the Gemariah bulla by its first nun to the late 7th to early 6th century. The findspots of the Jehucal and Gedaliah bullae lie in contexts whose date is disputed, so he relies on palaeography rather than stratigraphy.
  • DiscoveredGemariah: Yigal Shiloh's City of David excavations (1978-1985). Jehucal: announced 4 Aug 2005. Gedaliah: announced 31 July 2008., Gemariah: Yigal Shiloh. Jehucal and Gedaliah: Eilat Mazar; the Jehucal bulla was found by Area A supervisor Yoav Farhi., Gemariah: Locus 967, the 'House of the Bullae', Area G. Jehucal: between two large stones in the Large Stone Structure, Mazar's Area A. Gedaliah: Mazar's excavation at the summit of the City of David (findspot map in Mazar 2009: 69).
  • Now heldCurrent repository not stated in the sources cited here
  • MaterialUnfired clay seal impressions (bullae)
  • DimensionsJehucal: about 0.4 inch (1 cm) wide per the BAR caption; Gedaliah: about 1 cm long per news reports (both as reported by Mykytiuk 2009)
  • ScriptPaleo-Hebrew
  • LanguageHebrew

Biblical Passages

  • Jer 37:3Zedekiah sends 'Jehucal the son of Shelemiah' to Jeremiah. The bulla reads Yehukal son of Shelemyahu (and adds a grandfather, Shobi).
  • Jer 38:1-6'Gedaliah the son of Pashhur' and 'Jucal the son of Shelemiah' hear Jeremiah and urge his death, and he is put into the cistern. Both names appear on bullae from the same excavation.
  • Jer 36:10-12, 25Baruch reads the scroll 'in the chamber of Gemariah the son of Shaphan the scribe'; Gemariah later pleads with Jehoiakim not to burn it. The Area G bulla reads Gemaryahu son of Shaphan.
  • 2 Kgs 22:3-14Shaphan the scribe under Josiah, father of Ahikam, Gemariah and others. The bulla names Gemariah's father Shaphan, but gives no title for him.
