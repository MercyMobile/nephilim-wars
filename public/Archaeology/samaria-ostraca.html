Samaria Ostraca

Hebrew ink dockets for wine and oil sent to Samaria · early 8th century BCE (disputed)

Overview

In 1910 the Harvard expedition found inscribed potsherds in rooms on the east side of the courtyard of the Israelite palace at Samaria. D. G. Lyon published them in the 1924 report. They are short ink notes, each opening with a regnal year (9, 10 or 15), and they record deliveries of 'a jar of old wine' or 'a jar of fine (washed) oil' from named places or clan districts to named men.

Seventeen names follow the preposition 'from'. Lyon pointed out that six of them (Abiezer, Helek, Shechem, Shemida, Noah and Hoglah) are the clan divisions of Manasseh listed in Joshua 17:2-3 and Numbers 26:30-33. The personal names include Yahweh-names ending in -yau (Gaddiyau, Shemaryau, Yeda'yau, Abedyau) and Baal-names (Ba'ala, Meriba'al, Abiba'al, Ba'alzamar).

A 2020 algorithmic handwriting study of 31 of the texts, using Harvard's original negatives, concluded they were most likely written by two scribes working in Samaria, not by many writers out in the villages.

In its own words

"In the 10th year. To Shemaryau. From Be'er-yam. Jar[s ?] of old [wine]. Rage' son of Elisha' ... 2; 'Uzza son of ---- 1; ... Ba'ala son of Elisha' [1] ..."D. G. Lyon in G. A. Reisner, C. S. Fisher and D. G. Lyon, Harvard Excavations at Samaria 1908-1910, vol. I (1924), p. 228, Ostracon No. 1

What the evidence shows

  • Findspot: 'In the eastern part of the courtyard in rooms 401, 416, 417, 419, 470-474, in the lowest part of the debris of occupation' (Lyon 1924, p. 227).
  • Every text begins with a regnal year: years 9 and 10 (the 'old wine' and 'fine oil' dockets) and year 15 (dockets naming clan districts such as Shemida, Helek, Abiezer, Hoglah, Noah).
  • Formula types, per Lyon: 'In the 10th year. Wine of Kerm ha-Tell for a jar of fine oil' (Nos. 43, 54, 55?); 'A jar of old wine' (Nos. 4-10, 12, 14, 15, year 9); 'A jar of fine oil' (Nos. 16-19, year 10).
  • Six of the seventeen 'from' names match Manasseh clan names in Josh 17:2-3 / Num 26:30-33: Abiezer, Helek, Shechem, Shemida, Noah, Hoglah.
  • Theophoric names: Yahwistic -yau names (Gaddiyau, Shemaryau, Yeda'yau, Marnayau, Abedyau) alongside Baal names (Ba'ala, Meriba'al, Abiba'al, Ba'alzamar).
  • Lyon judged the letter forms would place them in 'the 8th or 9th century B.C.' on epigraphic grounds alone.
  • The ostraca are written on already-broken sherds, some shaped to fit and written across the wheel marks, so they are office notes and not jar labels (except Nos. 62-63).
  • Faigenbaum-Golovin et al. 2020: 31 texts analysed; most likely two hands.

Open questions

  • Which king's years 9, 10 and 15? Anson Rainey (BASOR 272, 1988, 'Toward a Precise Date for the Samaria Ostraca') argued for a specific early-8th-century placement. Faigenbaum-Golovin et al. (PLOS ONE 2020) list the only Israelite kings with 15+ years (Ahab, Jehu, Jehoahaz, Joash, Jeroboam II) and exclude the 9th-century kings because proto-Canaanite script was still in use then, leaving Joash or Jeroboam II. Lyon (1924) only bracketed the texts to the life of the palace, 'about 865 to 722 B.C.'
  • Floor or fill? Lyon (1924) put them 'in the lowest part of the debris of occupation' on the courtyard floor. Rainey (1988), as cited by Faigenbaum-Golovin et al., treats them as coming from a fill laid down before a building went up. Ron E. Tappy's re-study of the excavation records (The Archaeology of the Ostraca House at Israelite Samaria, ASOR Annual 70, 2016) takes a different view of that context. The difference affects whether the building dates the ostraca or the ostraca date the building.
  • Were the men after 'to' (l-) the recipients or the owners? Yigael Yadin (IEJ 9, 1959, 'Recipients or Owners?') took on that question. Hermann Michael Niemann (Tel Aviv 35, 2008) reread the texts as evidence of a king-clan relationship, not a simple tax system.
  • Where were they written? The 2020 algorithmic study found about two hands, which points to scribes at Samaria rather than writing spread across the villages. The result depends on the image-based letter reconstructions, which the authors published on figshare.

The Find

  • DateProbably first half of the 8th century BCE. The texts are dated only by regnal years 9, 10 and 15 of an unnamed king.
  • How datedThe ostraca give 'in the 9th year', 'in the 10th year' and 'in the 15th year' but never name the king (Lyon 1924, p. 227). The absolute date rests on letter forms, on the stratigraphy of the find spot, and on which Israelite kings reigned 15 years or more. Faigenbaum-Golovin et al. (2020) narrow this to Joash or Jeroboam II, since proto-Canaanite script still appears in the 9th century.
  • Discovered1910, Harvard Expedition to Samaria (G. A. Reisner, field director; C. S. Fisher; texts published by D. G. Lyon), Samaria, royal acropolis: rooms 401, 416, 417, 419 and 470-474 on the east side of the palace courtyard, in the building the excavators called the 'Ostraca House'
  • Now heldNot confirmed in a published museum record. The excavation was carried out under an Ottoman permit issued on the recommendation of the Imperial Ottoman Museum, Constantinople (Harvard report, 1924). The Harvard Semitic Museum holds the expedition's photographic negatives, which were used for the 2020 handwriting study.
  • MaterialPotsherds (ostraca) from jars, jugs, pans and bowls, written in carbon ink
  • DimensionsSmall sherds; Harvard numbered 63 texts (Nos. 1-63), two of them true jar labels (Nos. 62-63); the full assemblage is c. 100 short administrative texts
  • ScriptPaleo-Hebrew (Old Hebrew) script, ink
  • LanguageHebrew (northern / Israelite dialect)

Biblical Passages

  • Josh 17:2-3; Num 26:30-33Abiezer, Helek, Shechem, Shemida (sons of Gilead) and Noah, Hoglah (daughters of Zelophehad) are Manassite clan names in these genealogies; the same six names appear on the ostraca as districts sending wine and oil to Samaria.
  • 1 Kgs 16:24Samaria as the royal capital of Israel. The ostraca are palace deliveries at Samaria.
  • Hos 2:8, 2:16-17Hosea's complaint about Baal in Israel. The ostraca carry both Yahweh-names (-yau) and Baal-names among the same people; our reading is that this is onomastic evidence for both cults, not proof of either practice.
  • Amos 6:6'Drink wine in bowls and anoint themselves with the finest oils.' The ostraca record deliveries of 'old wine' and 'fine/washed oil' to the capital; a direct match of commodities, not of events.
