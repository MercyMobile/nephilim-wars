Late 7th-Century Egyptian and East Greek Pottery at Megiddo (Level X-3)

Egyptian-made and East Greek pottery from Level X-3 at Tel Megiddo · late 7th century BCE · published 2025, disputed 2026

Overview

The Megiddo Expedition has published several layers from after the Assyrian conquest of 732 BCE from a small area (Area X), with a mudbrick wall and two successive buildings. Level X-3, which they date to the late 7th century BCE, produced what they describe as an exceptional number of Egyptian-made pottery vessels and a significant amount of East Greek pottery. They read this as an Egyptian (26th Dynasty, Saite) presence at Megiddo, with Greek mercenaries in Egyptian service.

Finkelstein, Adams, Fantalkin and Kleiman (Scandinavian Journal of the Old Testament, January 2025) present this as the archaeological background to the meeting of Josiah and Pharaoh Necho at Megiddo in 609 BCE (2 Kgs 23:29).

In August 2026 Shalvi, Gimatzidis, Kahn, Wodzinska, Ownby and Gilboa (Levant) re-examined the stratigraphy and the Phoenician, Greek, Egyptian and Assyrianizing pottery. They argue that Megiddo was abandoned c. 640 BCE, before Assyria collapsed, and not significantly resettled in the Iron Age, so that there was no Saite-period stronghold at Megiddo in 609.

What the evidence shows

  • Area X exposed a mudbrick wall and two successive buildings with layers of the 8th-6th centuries BCE (BAS summary of the SJOT paper).
  • Level X-3 contains 'an exceptional number of Egyptian-made pottery vessels' and 'a significant number of East Greek pottery' (SJOT 2025 abstract).
  • BAS reports the excavators' claim that no other site in the region has as much Egyptian pottery, and no inland site as much Greek pottery.
  • The Levant 2026 response counters that Strata III and II are both phases of Neo-Assyrian Magiddu, with no archaeologically attested Neo-Babylonian occupation and abandonment c. 640 BCE.
  • No inscription naming Necho or Josiah has been reported from these layers.

Open questions

  • Was Megiddo an Egyptian stronghold in 609 BCE? Finkelstein et al. (SJOT 39/1, 2025) date Level X-3 to the late 7th century and read its Egyptian-made vessels and East Greek wares as a Saite garrison with Greek mercenaries. Shalvi et al. (Levant, 2026) re-examine the stratigraphy and the same four ceramic groups and date the end of occupation to c. 640 BCE, before any Saite control, with no significant later Iron Age resettlement. The dispute is over the dating of the Greek and Egyptian pottery types and how the strata connect, not over whether the pottery exists.
  • Josiah's death: 2 Kgs 23:29 says only that Necho killed Josiah at Megiddo 'when he saw him'. 2 Chr 35:20-24 describes a battle in the valley of Megiddo. BAS notes that the difference between the two accounts is still argued. The pottery could bear on whether an Egyptian post stood at Megiddo; it cannot show which account describes the encounter.

The Find

  • DateLate 7th century BCE (Level X-3, per the Megiddo Expedition)
  • How datedPottery typology and stratigraphy of post-732 BCE layers in Area X (Finkelstein et al., SJOT 2025). Shalvi et al. (Levant 2026) re-date the same ceramic categories and argue the site was abandoned c. 640 BCE.
  • DiscoveredRecent Megiddo Expedition seasons; published January 2025, Megiddo Expedition (Tel Aviv University; I. Finkelstein, M. J. Adams, A. Fantalkin, A. Kleiman), Area X, Tel Megiddo, Jezreel Valley
  • Now heldIn situ at Tel Megiddo; finds held by the Megiddo Expedition (inventory numbers not published in the sources seen)

Biblical Passages

  • 2 Kgs 23:29-30Necho goes up to the king of Assyria at the Euphrates; Josiah goes to meet him, and Necho kills him at Megiddo. If Finkelstein et al. are right, Megiddo then held an Egyptian presence. If Shalvi et al. are right, the site was largely deserted and the encounter took place at or near a ruin.
  • 2 Chr 35:20-24The Chronicler describes a battle in the valley of Megiddo with archers and a chariot. The pottery evidence does not address the form of the encounter.
  • Jer 46:2Necho's army at the Euphrates at Carchemish, 605 BCE. This is the same Egyptian push north that the Megiddo excavators use to explain their garrison.
