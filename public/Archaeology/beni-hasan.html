Beni Hasan: the Aamu Procession in the Tomb of Khnumhotep II

Painted procession of 37 Asiatics in a Middle Kingdom rock-cut tomb · Year 6 of Senusret II

Overview

Beni Hasan is a Middle Kingdom cemetery of rock-cut tombs of the governors of the Oryx nome. Tomb No. 3 belongs to Khnumhotep II, 'Administrator of the Eastern Desert'. The north wall of its main chamber is painted in six rows showing produce and people being brought to him. Row 3 is a procession of foreigners.

The procession is introduced by the royal scribe Neferhotep, holding a document dated Year 6 of Senusret II that counts '37 Aamu of Shu' brought on account of mesdemet (black eye-paint/kohl). The leader is labelled hqꜣ ḫꜣst Ibshꜣ, 'ruler of the desert/foreign land, Abesha'. A line over the group reads 'arrival bringing kohl, which 37 Aamu bring to him.'

The tomb was known and copied long before photography; Newberry (1893) records that the Great Inscription was vandalised in 1888 or 1889 and that the colours had faded since the 1828 copies.

In its own words

The arrival, bringing eye-paint, which 37 Asiatics bring to him. ... Sheik of the highlands, Ibshe.J. H. Breasted, Ancient Records of Egypt I (1906) §620 note d, p. 281 (citing Newberry, Beni Hasan I, pl. XXX); cf. Newberry (1893) p. 69: 'arrival bringing kohl which 37 Aamu bring to him'

What the evidence shows

  • Number: 37 Aamu, written twice (the scribe's document and the caption over the group) (Newberry 1893 p. 69).
  • Group label: 'Aamu of Shu' (ꜥꜣmw n Šw); Breasted renders 'Asiatics of the desert' (ARE I §620 n.).
  • Leader's title and name: hqꜣ ḫꜣst Ibshꜣ - Newberry 'hak-prince of the desert, Abesha'; Breasted 'Sheik of the highlands, Ibshe'.
  • Stated purpose: they come 'on account of mesdemet' (eye-paint, stibium or kohl) (Newberry p. 69).
  • Dated document: Year 6 of Usertsen (Senusret) II, carried by the royal scribe Neferhotep (Newberry p. 69, pl. XXXVIII).
  • Scene position: Tomb No. 3, north wall, row 3, pls. XXVIII, XXX, XXXI (Newberry 1893).

Open questions

  • Who were the Aamu of Shu? J. Kamrin (JAEI 1/3, 2009) treats the group in detail; Breasted (1906, §620 n.) called the leader's name 'a good Hebrew name' while rejecting the old link to Abraham as having 'nothing to do' with it. The captions give only a group label, a place-word (Šw) and a personal name; nothing in the tomb states their homeland beyond that.
  • The leader's title hqꜣ ḫꜣst is the same phrase later used for the Hyksos rulers ('rulers of foreign lands'). Whether that is just the ordinary Egyptian title for any foreign chief in the 12th Dynasty, or an early instance of the same group, is an inference either way; the tomb only shows the title in use by c. Year 6 of Senusret II.

The Find

  • DateYear 6 of Senusret II (Usertsen II), 12th Dynasty; early 19th century BCE on conventional chronologies
  • How datedThe scene itself carries a date: the royal scribe Neferhotep holds a document headed 'Year 6 under the majesty of ... Usertsen II' (Newberry, Beni Hasan I, p. 69, pl. XXXVIII). Breasted notes this is the latest date in the tomb (ARE I §620 n.). The BCE figure depends on the 12th-Dynasty chronology chosen.
  • DiscoveredTomb described by Jomard in 1798; copied by Burton (1825), Champollion, Rosellini and Hay (1828), Lepsius (1842); full survey 1890-1891, Survey published by Percy E. Newberry for the Egypt Exploration Fund's Archaeological Survey of Egypt (1893), Tomb No. 3 (northern group), rock-cut cemetery of Beni Hasan, east bank of the Nile, Middle Egypt
  • Now heldIn situ, Tomb No. 3 (BH 3), Beni Hasan, Egypt; north wall of the main chamber, row 3
  • MaterialPaint on plaster over rock-cut tomb walls
  • ScriptEgyptian hieroglyphs (captions)
  • LanguageMiddle Egyptian

Biblical Passages

  • Genesis 12:10; 42-47The tomb shows a party of 37 West Asians, with a named chief, being received and registered by an Egyptian official in the 12th Dynasty. That matches the biblical picture of small groups of Asiatics coming down to Egypt and being handled by officials. It does not show famine, and the group is not named as any biblical family.
  • Genesis 37:25-28Old entry claimed an exact match. The Ishmaelite caravan carries gum, balm and myrrh and buys Joseph; the Beni Hasan group brings eye-paint and is registered as a party of 37 visitors. The correspondence is general (Asiatics trading goods into Egypt), not specific.
