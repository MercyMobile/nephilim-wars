Sergius Paulus Inscriptions (Soloi, Rome, Pisidian Antioch)

Three separate stones bearing a Paul(l)us linked to the proconsul of Acts 13 · 1st century CE

Overview

Acts 13:7–12 names 'the proconsul Sergius Paulus' at Paphos on Cyprus. No stone yet found names 'Sergius Paulus, proconsul of Cyprus' in one text. Three inscriptions are used to build the case, each carrying part of it.

Soloi, Cyprus. A Greek funerary text in which one Apollonius honours his father and his mother Artemidora and lists his civic offices. Lines added later say he also revised the city council 'in the time of the proconsul Paulus' (Hogarth 1889). Cesnola found it in the 1870s. Hogarth recopied it in 1888, built into the threshold of a grain store at Karavostasi where the owner dug it out for paying visitors, and warned it was being worn away.

Rome. A boundary cippus of the Tiber commissioners (curatores riparum et alvei Tiberis) under Claudius names 'L(ucius) Sergius Paullus' among five commissioners. Pisidian Antioch: Ramsay and Anderson copied in 1912 an honorific base for 'L. Sergius Paullus, son of Lucius, the younger', listing early posts of a senatorial career. Ramsay read him as the son of the proconsul of Acts.

In its own words

'Apollonius to his father … and to his mother Artemidora … consecrated the enclosure and this monument … having filled the offices of clerk of the market, prefect, town-clerk, high priest … He also revised the senate by means of assessors in the time of the proconsul Paulus.'D. G. Hogarth, Devia Cypria (London 1889) p. 115 (Soloi inscription, his no. 36)

What the evidence shows

  • Soloi: the proconsul is named only as 'Paulus'. No praenomen or 'Sergius' survives (Hogarth 1889, 114–115; Cesnola 1877, 425).
  • Soloi: Hogarth reports that the last two and a half lines, including the Paulus clause, are a later addition in a different letter form. The main text is dated to 'year 13', with no era given.
  • Rome (CIL VI 31545 = ILS 5926 = EDR127293): 'Paullus Fabius Persicus, C. Eggius Marullus, L. Sergius Paullus, C. Obellius Rufus, L. Scribonius Libo, curatores riparum et alvei Tiberis, ex auctoritate Ti. Claudi Caesaris Aug. Germanici …' marked the bank 'a Trigario ad pontem Agrippae'.
  • Rome: EDR notes that the verb terminaverunt lacks Claudius' extra letter, which may narrow the date to 41–47 CE, before Claudius' censorship.
  • Antioch: 'L. Sergio L. f. Paullo filio, IIIIvir. v. c., trib. mil. leg. VI Ferr., quaest. …' (Ramsay 1915, 151). The word filio ('the younger') marks a son of the same name.

Open questions

  • Is the Soloi 'Paulus' the Sergius Paulus of Acts? Cesnola's editor D. Pierides (1877, 425) and Hogarth (1889) both say 'may be'. Nothing on the stone gives his nomen, and the local 'year 13' is not tied to an emperor. Any date for this Paulus depends on assumptions the text does not supply.
  • Is the Tiber commissioner the proconsul? A Tiber commission and a provincial proconsulship both belong to a senatorial career, and the cippus falls under Claudius, the same reign in which Acts places Paul's visit to Cyprus. The stone gives only the name and the commission. It does not say he ever held Cyprus.
  • Ramsay (1915, 150–172) reads the Antioch Paullus as the proconsul's son and ties the family's estates near Antioch to Paul's route from Cyprus to Pisidian Antioch (Acts 13:13–14). That link is Ramsay's inference; the stone does not name the father's office.

The Find

  • DateRome cippus: reign of Claudius (41–54 CE; possibly 41–47). Soloi epitaph: undated Roman-period text; the 'Paulus' lines are a later addition. Antioch honorific: lettering of c. 60–100 CE (Ramsay).
  • How datedRome: acting under the authority of Ti. Claudius Caesar Augustus Germanicus (EDR). Soloi: the text has a local 'year 13' date, but no era or emperor is named. Antioch: letter forms only (Ramsay 1915, 151).
  • DiscoveredSoloi: 1870s (Cesnola); Rome: 1887; Pisidian Antioch: 1912, Soloi: L. P. di Cesnola (text by D. Pierides), re-read by D. G. Hogarth in 1888; Rome: found on the Tiber's left bank; Antioch: W. M. Ramsay and J. G. C. Anderson, Soloi (north-west Cyprus), Rome (near S. Biagio della Pagnotta), Salir quarter of Pisidian Antioch
  • Now heldRome cippus: Museo Nazionale Romano, chiostro di Michelangelo, inv. 242. Soloi stone: Hogarth saw it built into a shop threshold at Karavostasi, Cyprus; its present whereabouts are not stated in any source cited here. Antioch stone: not established in the sources cited here.
  • MaterialRome: travertine (lapis tiburtinus) cippus 160 × 80 × 24 cm, letters 3.5–5 cm (EDR). Soloi: marble slab/pedestal (Cesnola; Hogarth).
  • ScriptSoloi: Greek; Rome and Antioch: Latin
  • LanguageGreek; Latin

Biblical Passages

  • Acts 13:7'the proconsul (anthypatos) Sergius Paulus, a man of intelligence'. The Soloi stone uses the same title (anthypatos) with the name Paulus, and the Rome cippus gives a Claudian L. Sergius Paullus.
  • Acts 13:12The proconsul 'believed'. None of the inscriptions bears on his belief. Ramsay's argument about the family's religion is his own interpretation.
  • Acts 13:13–14Paul goes from Paphos to Pisidian Antioch. The Antioch stone shows a L. Sergius Paullus honoured in that colony.
