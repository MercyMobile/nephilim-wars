Capernaum Synagogue

White limestone synagogue over earlier basalt remains · Late Roman / Byzantine

Overview

The standing white-limestone synagogue has a rectangular prayer hall, its facade facing south toward Jerusalem, with a U-shaped stylobate dividing the nave from east, west and north aisles, stone benches along the east and west aisles, a trapezoidal eastern courtyard with porticoes, a southern porch and a basalt side room at the NW corner. Two small platforms (aediculae/bemas) flanked the central entrance in the first phase, later replaced by one platform across the nave.

Stratigraphic excavation since 1969 dated the prayer hall to the late 4th century CE on the basis of more than 30,000 Late Roman coins and pottery; the east court was finished after the mid-5th century and the building was abandoned in the 7th. Earlier scholars (Wilson, Orfali) had thought it the 1st-century synagogue itself, and Watzinger's school dated it to the late 2nd-early 3rd century by style.

Beneath the nave the excavators found a large basalt-stone pavement they date to the 1st century CE, over a layer with Hellenistic pottery and coins, and a 'basalt stone wall' under the limestone walls that the white synagogue reused as footing. Corbo identified pavement and wall as the 1st-century synagogue; Loffreda identifies the pavement as the 1st-century synagogue and considers the wall possibly an intermediate phase.

What the evidence shows

  • Prayer hall interior 23 x 17.28 m, facade to the south (Loffreda).
  • Over 30,000 Late Roman coins plus pottery from sealed contexts under the white synagogue (Loffreda).
  • Basalt pavement under the central nave, dated 1st century CE by the excavators; beneath it Hellenistic pottery and coins.
  • Basalt wall under the peripheral walls, continuous under the outer walls but broken or missing under the stylobates; axis shifted relative to the limestone walls; tops levelled with pebbles.
  • East-court foundations are separate, better-built, and abut the basalt wall.
  • Private houses (pavements, walls, fireplaces) under the aisles, porch and east court.
  • Nothing found names the 1st-century builder; the pavement is uninscribed.

Open questions

  • Date of the white synagogue: S. Loffreda and V. Corbo (SBF excavations from 1969) date the prayer hall to the late 4th century from 30,000+ coins and pottery beneath it. J. Magness ('Capernaum', in Ancient Synagogues in Palestine, British Academy 2024) evaluates 'a recent claim' that the white synagogue was built in the 3rd century and rebuilt after the earthquake of 363 CE and served Jews called minim, and gives a 'fine-grained analysis' of the dating evidence for the synagogue, the domus ecclesia and the octagonal church; her detailed conclusions are not accessible for this entry.
  • What lies beneath: Corbo reads both the 1st-century basalt pavement and the basalt wall as the 1st-century synagogue; Loffreda agrees on the pavement but suggests the wall may be an intermediate building between the 1st-century synagogue and the late-4th-century one. The argument for 'synagogue' is inferential: the pavement is too large for a house, sacred buildings are often rebuilt on the same spot, and pilgrims such as Egeria placed the synagogue there (Loffreda).
  • Why a lavish synagogue beside a Christian shrine in the 4th-5th century: W. V. Cirafesi, Capernaum: Jews and Christians in the Ancient Village (Fortress 2024) treats the building in chapters on Jewish Christ-followers before the 4th century and 'Architecturalizing Jesus in the Fourth Century'; S. Tarkhanova (2020) re-dates the 'peopled scroll' friezes by stylistic method. Their specific arguments have not been reviewed for this entry.

The Find

  • DatePrayer hall built in the late 4th century CE; east court completed after the mid-5th century; abandoned in the 7th century (Loffreda). Earlier basalt pavement beneath the nave dated by the excavators to the 1st century CE.
  • How datedStratified excavation by the Studium Biblicum Franciscanum since 1969: 'more than 30,000 Late Roman coins' and pottery sealed in the fills under the floors (Loffreda). The 1st-century date of the basalt pavement under the nave rests on pottery and coins in and beneath it (Loffreda reports Hellenistic pottery and coins in the layer below it). The older late-2nd/early-3rd-century date (following Watzinger) was based on architectural style.
  • DiscoveredRuins described in the 19th century (E. Robinson); partly restored by G. Orfali 1922-1925; stratigraphic excavation by V. Corbo and S. Loffreda from 1969, Studium Biblicum Franciscanum (Franciscan Custody of the Holy Land), Capernaum (Kfar Nahum), NW shore of the Sea of Galilee
  • Now heldIn situ, Capernaum
  • MaterialWhite limestone superstructure on foundations/walls of black basalt; basalt side room
  • DimensionsPrayer hall interior 23 m N-S x 17.28 m E-W (Loffreda)

Biblical Passages

  • Mark 1:21-28; Luke 4:31-37Jesus teaches and exorcises in the synagogue at Capernaum on the Sabbath. The standing limestone building is 300+ years later; only the basalt pavement under it is of 1st-century date, and its identification as that synagogue is the excavators' inference.
  • Luke 7:1-5Jewish elders say the centurion 'built us our synagogue'. Loffreda explicitly links the 1st-century pavement to 'the long-looked for synagogue built by the Roman centurion'; the pavement carries no builder's name.
  • John 6:59The bread-of-life discourse is set 'in the synagogue, as he taught at Capernaum'.
  • Matt 4:13; 9:1Capernaum as Jesus' base ('his own city'); the site has 1st-century houses under the later synagogue.
