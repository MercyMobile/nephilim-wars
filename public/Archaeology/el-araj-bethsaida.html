El-Araj (Bethsaida-Julias candidate)

Roman-period village and Byzantine basilica on the NE shore of the Sea of Galilee · 1st c. BCE - 3rd c. CE; basilica 5th/6th c. CE

Overview

El-Araj is a low site on the northern shore of the Sea of Galilee that R. Steven Notley and Mordechai Aviam have excavated over ten seasons (to 2026) as the most likely location of Bethsaida, the fishing village the Gospels name as home of Peter, Andrew and Philip, which Josephus says Herod Philip raised to a city called Julias. Their excavation has exposed Roman-period houses with finds running from the 1st century BCE to the 3rd century CE, and, above them after a gap of about two centuries, a Byzantine basilica found in 2021.

Three Greek inscriptions were set in the basilica's mosaic floor. One names 'the chief of the apostles and the keeper of the keys of the heavenly spheres', titles the editors (Di Segni, Ashkenazi, Aviam and Notley 2023) take as referring to Peter. They identify the building with the church that the pilgrim Willibald saw in 725 CE at 'Bethsaida, whence came Peter and Andrew', built where their house had stood, and argue that the Byzantine church therefore marks where Byzantine Christians placed Bethsaida.

In July 2025 a wildfire burned across the site for three days and cleared the vegetation, exposing Roman-period architectural pieces (column drums, Corinthian and Doric capitals, cornices). The tenth season (July-August 2026) worked the Roman level (2nd-3rd century CE) in Areas D, F and G and reported coin hoards (150+ coins), Herodian lamps, chalk/stone vessels, fishing weights, frescoed walls, fine glass and a half Doric capital. The rival candidate, et-Tell about 2 km away, has been excavated as Bethsaida since 1987 by Rami Arav.

What the text says (summary)

The basilica's mosaic floor carried three Greek inscriptions. The one reported in the editors' abstract names 'the chief of the apostles and the keeper of the keys of the heavenly spheres' (titles of Peter). It does not name Peter or Bethsaida. The other two inscriptions' contents were not seen for this entry.English phrase from the abstract of Di Segni, Ashkenazi, Aviam and Notley, Liber Annuus 73 (2023) 365-384; no public-domain translation exists (found 2021).

What the evidence shows

  • Josephus, Ant. 18.2.1 (18.28), Whiston trans.: Philip 'advanced the village Bethsaida, situate at the lake of Gennesareth, unto the dignity of a city ... and called it by the name of Julias, the same name with Caesar's daughter.'
  • Willibald, Hodoeporicon ch. XIV (725 CE), Brownlow trans., PPTS 3 p. 16: 'And thence they proceeded to Bethsaida, whence came Peter and Andrew. There is now a church where their house was formerly. They stayed there one night.'
  • Byzantine basilica with mosaic floor found at el-Araj in 2021 (Di Segni et al. 2023 abstract).
  • Three Greek inscriptions in the mosaic; one mentions 'The chief of the apostles and the keeper of the keys of the heavenly spheres' (Di Segni et al. 2023 abstract). The project's 2026 post quotes it as 'the chief of the Apostles and the keeper of the keys of heaven'.
  • The inscription does not contain the name Bethsaida or Peter; the link to Peter rests on the titles.
  • Roman-period remains beneath the church and elsewhere span the 1st century BCE to the 3rd century CE, followed by an occupation gap of about two centuries before the church (Di Segni et al. 2023 abstract).
  • The July 2025 wildfire exposed column drums, two Corinthian and two Doric capitals, cornices and house foundations (Times of Israel, 30 Aug 2025).
  • Season 10 (2026) finds: 150+ coins in several hoards, three intact Herodian lamps, a discus lamp (2nd-3rd c. CE), chalk/stone vessels, fishing weights, frescoed walls, fine glassware, a half Doric capital (project season reports, 1 and 8 Aug 2026).
  • No inscription found at el-Araj names the settlement.

Open questions

  • Which site is Bethsaida. Notley (Near Eastern Archaeology 70:4, 2007: 220-230) argued in 'Et-Tell Is Not Bethsaida'; Arav (Near Eastern Archaeology 74:2, 2011: 92-100) answered in 'Bethsaida - A Response to Steven Notley'. The specific arguments in those two papers were not read for this entry (titles and pagination confirmed via Crossref only). What the el-Araj team puts forward (Di Segni et al. 2023): Roman-period occupation 1st c. BCE - 3rd c. CE at el-Araj plus a Byzantine church with Petrine titles matching Willibald's church. What stands against a simple equation on their own evidence: the two-century gap between the Roman and Byzantine phases, so the church shows where Byzantine Christians placed Bethsaida, not directly where the 1st-century village was.
  • Is the Roman town Julias as well as Bethsaida? Josephus (Ant. 18.28) says Philip made the village a city named Julias. The excavators read the architectural pieces (Corinthian/Doric capitals, cornices) and site size as fitting a polis and the stone vessels as marking a Jewish population (season 10 reports, 2026). No inscription or civic building naming Julias has been reported.
  • Date of the basilica: Notley (Novum Testamentum 64:4, 2022) calls it a 6th-century basilica; the Times of Israel report (2025) calls it fifth-century. The published sources seen do not state the dating evidence.
  • Capernaum vs el-Araj as the house of Peter: Notley (NovT 2022) argues the el-Araj finds challenge the 1921 identification (by Orfali) of the octagonal church at Capernaum as built over Peter's house. The Gospels place Peter's house at Capernaum (Mark 1:21, 29) while John 1:44 calls Bethsaida his town; both traditions sit in the text.

The Find

  • DateRoman-period occupation 1st century BCE - 3rd century CE; after a gap of about two centuries, a Byzantine basilica (dated 5th century by the Times of Israel report, 6th century by Notley 2022)
  • How datedExcavators' pottery, coins and lamp typology (including Herodian lamps they describe as made only in Jerusalem before 70 CE) and the stratigraphy beneath the church floor (Di Segni et al., Liber Annuus 73, 2023; project season reports 2025-2026). The date of the basilica differs between the published abstracts and press reports (5th vs 6th century); no radiocarbon dates were seen for this entry.
  • DiscoveredMulti-season excavation; the tenth season ran July-August 2026; basilica found 2021, El-Araj Excavation Project (Bethsaida-Julias), archaeological director Mordechai Aviam (Kinneret College), academic director and co-director R. Steven Notley (Pillar College, New Jersey), El-Araj, on the northern shore of the Sea of Galilee, about 2 km from et-Tell
  • Now heldIn situ (northern shore of the Sea of Galilee)

Biblical Passages

  • John 1:44; 12:21Bethsaida named as the town of Philip, Andrew and Peter. The el-Araj church inscription uses Petrine titles, and Willibald located Peter and Andrew's house at Bethsaida.
  • Mark 8:22-26Healing of a blind man at Bethsaida, which Mark calls a village (kome). The excavators hold that the Roman-period remains are the village later made into Julias (their reading).
  • Luke 9:10Jesus withdraws to 'a city called Bethsaida' before the feeding of the five thousand.
  • Matthew 11:21; Luke 10:13Woe pronounced on Chorazin and Bethsaida. Nothing in the finds bears on this beyond the location question.
