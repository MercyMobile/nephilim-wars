Papyrus Brooklyn 35.1446

Late Middle Kingdom hieratic register; the verso lists a Theban household's servants, many labelled 'Asiatic' · 13th Dynasty

Overview

Papyrus Brooklyn 35.1446 is a late Middle Kingdom administrative papyrus written on both sides, published by W. C. Hayes in 1955 and recorded as coming from Thebes. It is not the only 'Brooklyn papyrus': the museum holds other famous papyri (e.g. the medical/snake papyrus), so the accession number matters.

The recto is an administrative register (Hayes 1955 pp. 19-66, pls. I-VII) with inserted documents: a letter dated Year 36 (assigned to Amenemhat III) and a text naming the vizier Ankhu. The verso, dated Year 1 of Sobekhotep III, is a legal list of dependents (Hayes pp. 87-125, pls. VIII-XIV). Each person is listed with a status word, often a work designation, and one or two names.

A large share of the verso's entries are labelled ꜥꜣm (Asiatic man) or ꜥꜣmt (Asiatic woman). Many carry a foreign (Northwest Semitic) name followed by an Egyptian name, e.g. 'ꜥꜣmt ... špr / snb-ḥnwt⸗s'. The list shows Asiatic men, women and children as household servants and textile workers in a Theban household of the 13th Dynasty.

What the text says (summary)

ꜥꜣmt, sḫt ḥꜣtjw, mnḥmj - 'Asiatic woman, weaver (?), Menahemi'; ꜥꜣmt, sḫt ḥꜣtjw, špr / snb-ḥnwt⸗s - 'Asiatic woman, weaver (?), Shipra, [Egyptian name] Seneb-henutes'Literal rendering (this encyclopedia) of entries 11 and 21 of the verso as transliterated in the Mainz PNM online edition (Ilin-Tomich), which follows Hayes 1955 pls. VIII-XIV. The work-title sḫt ḥꜣtjw is a textile-worker designation; its exact meaning is uncertain.

What the evidence shows

  • Verso date: Year 1 of Sobekhotep III (PNM record, citing Hayes 1955 pp. 87-125, pls. VIII-XIV).
  • Provenance: Thebes (PNM record).
  • Recto: administrative text, late 12th-early 13th Dynasty (Hayes 1955 pp. 19-66, pls. I-VII); inserted letter dated Year 36 (Amenemhat III) and a text naming the vizier Ankhu (PNM records 17754, 3892).
  • In the PNM edition, at least 36 preserved verso entries carry the label ꜥꜣm or ꜥꜣmt (Asiatic); many other entries are broken at that point.
  • Foreign names in the list include ꜥpr-ršpw (a name built on the god Reshef), mnḥmj, špr, jšr, ꜥqbj, ꜥqbtw, sk-r-tw, šmštw, bꜥꜣtwjj and hjjbjrw (PNM transliterations).
  • Many Asiatics carry double names: foreign name / Egyptian name (e.g. špr / snb-ḥnwt⸗s; ꜥpr-ršpw / [broken]).
  • Status words beside the Asiatic entries are household and textile-work designations (e.g. sḫt ḥꜣtjw, ḏꜣ šsr, ḥrj-pr 'house-servant/domestic'); none is a building or quarry designation.
  • Children are recorded with their mothers, e.g. an Asiatic woman's son named ꜥnḫw / pꜣ-ꜥꜣm ('the Asiatic') (PNM entries 56-58).

Open questions

  • How to read the names: W. F. Albright ('Northwest-Semitic Names in a List of Egyptian Slaves from the Eighteenth Century B.C.', JAOS 74, 1954) treated the foreign names as Northwest Semitic; S. H. Horn reviewed Hayes's edition in JNES 16 (1957). Individual equations with Hebrew names (e.g. špr = Shiphrah, mnḥmj = Menahem, ꜥqb- = the root of Jacob) are readings of consonants, not proven identities; I have not read Albright's article to state which equations he made.
  • Date in BCE: the text fixes a regnal year of Sobekhotep III; 13th-Dynasty absolute dates are poorly fixed, so any BCE figure (often given as 18th century BCE, as in Albright's title) is an estimate outside the document.

The Find

  • DateVerso (the servant list): Year 1 of Sobekhotep III, 13th Dynasty. Recto and inserted texts: late 12th to early 13th Dynasty (an inserted letter is dated Year 36, assigned to Amenemhat III; another names the vizier Ankhu).
  • How datedThe verso is dated in its text to Year 1 of Sobekhotep III (Sekhemre-Sewadjtawy), as recorded in the Mainz 'Persons and Names of the Middle Kingdom' edition (citing Hayes 1955). A BCE figure depends on the 13th-Dynasty chronology, which is loose; the document gives only the regnal year.
  • DiscoveredAcquired in Egypt before entering the Brooklyn Museum; published 1955, Published by William C. Hayes, Unexcavated (bought, not dug); provenance recorded as Thebes
  • Now heldBrooklyn Museum, New York, 35.1446
  • MaterialPapyrus, inscribed on both sides
  • ScriptHieratic
  • LanguageMiddle Egyptian; the verso records many Northwest Semitic personal names in Egyptian writing

Biblical Passages

  • Genesis 39:1; 46-47; Exodus 1:1-14The papyrus shows people labelled Asiatic, with Northwest Semitic names, serving in an Egyptian household at Thebes in the 13th Dynasty, some given Egyptian names, and bearing children there. That matches the biblical picture of Semites living and serving in Egypt (compare Joseph's renaming, Gen 41:45). It does not name any Israelite, and the verso is not a list of construction labour.
  • Exodus 1:15One Asiatic woman is named špr, the same consonants as Shiphrah, one of the two Hebrew midwives. That is a correspondence of name form only (our observation), in a document from a different setting.
