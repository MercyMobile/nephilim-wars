Western Wall and Robinson's Arch

Herodian western retaining wall of the Temple Mount, with its great arches · late 1st century BCE – 1st century CE

Overview

The Western Wall is the western retaining wall of Herod's Temple Mount enclosure, 1,601 ft (about 488 m) long by the Palestine Exploration Fund survey (1884). The open-air prayer area is only a short stretch of it; most of its length runs behind and under later buildings and is reached through the Western Wall Tunnels.

In 1838 Edward Robinson noticed three courses of huge curved stones jutting from the wall near its south-west corner. He wrote (Biblical Researches in Palestine I, 1841, p. 425): 'their external surface is hewn to a regular curve; and being fitted one upon another, they form the commencement or foot of an immense arch, which once sprung out from this western wall in a direction towards Mount Zion, across the Valley of the Tyropoeon.' He took it for the bridge Josephus mentions between the temple and the Xystus. Further north, Wilson's Arch still stands and carried a causeway across the valley.

Recent work has dated these structures directly. Radiocarbon from building fills shows Wilson's Arch was begun under Herod and enlarged under the procurators, Pilate among them (Regev et al. 2020). The 2018–2020 Western Wall Tunnels excavation found a Herodian dam wall across the valley, later topped by a paved triclinium building after 17 CE ('Atiqot 119, 2025).

What the evidence shows

  • Robinson's Arch: three surviving courses of curved voussoirs, one 5 ft 4 in thick; a chord of 12 ft 6 in on the surviving arc; 350 ft from the arch to the rock scarp opposite (Robinson 1841, p. 425).
  • Buried courses near the south-west corner keep fine drafted margins and rough projecting bosses, never weathered. Four courses match 'those at the Wailing Place' (SWP Jerusalem 1884, p. 170).
  • Wilson's Arch: charred material in the construction fills dates the first phase to Herod and an enlargement to the procurators; a theatre-like structure beneath is Hadrianic and was left unfinished before 132–136 CE (Regev et al. 2020).
  • Western Wall Tunnels 2018–2020: a Herodian dam wall with drainage channels, a later paved triclinium building with a fountain (after 17 CE by a sealed Tiberius coin), and a miqweh of the time of the First Revolt (67/68 CE) (Weksler-Bdolah; Levy, 'Atiqot 119, 2025).
  • Plastered horizontal channels cut only in the Herodian courses, dated before 70 CE (Szanton & Weksler-Bdolah, 'Atiqot 119, 2025).

Open questions

  • What did Robinson's Arch carry? Robinson (1841, p. 425) identified it with the bridge Josephus names between the temple and the Xystus, measuring 350 ft across the valley to the opposite rock. A different reading, that the arch topped a monumental stair running down south to the street below, is attributed to B. Mazar's 1968–78 excavations at its foot. Their final reports (Qedem series) were not available for this entry, so the stair reading is flagged here as unverified, not stated as fact.
  • How long did the building take? Josephus (Ant. 20.219) says the temple works were 'finished' under Albinus (c. 62–64 CE). Radiocarbon at Wilson's Arch (Regev et al. 2020) and coins in the Western Wall Tunnels (Levy 2025) show construction around the western wall continuing well after Herod's death in 4 BCE. How much of the western wall's visible masonry is from after Herod is not settled in the sources cited here.

The Find

  • DateHerodian and later 1st-century CE construction. Wilson's Arch was begun under Herod and enlarged under the Roman procurators within about 70 years (radiocarbon, Regev et al. 2020).
  • How datedJosephus' account of Herod's work (Ant. 15.11); radiocarbon on charred material in building fills beneath Wilson's Arch (Regev et al., PLOS ONE 2020); coins and stratigraphy in the Western Wall Tunnels ('Atiqot 119, 2025) and along the Western Wall foundations (Baruch, Reich, Hagbi & Uziel 2023).
  • DiscoveredStanding wall; Robinson identified the arch springer in 1838 (published 1841); Warren's shafts 1867–70; excavations 1968–78 and continuing, Edward Robinson; Charles Warren; Benjamin Mazar; IAA teams (Reich & Baruch; Hagbi & Uziel; Weksler-Bdolah; Uziel, Solomon & Lieberman), West side of the Temple Mount enclosure, Jerusalem
  • Now heldIn situ, Jerusalem
  • MaterialLimestone ashlars with drafted margins
  • DimensionsWhole west wall of the enclosure 1,601 ft (c. 488 m) (SWP 1884). Robinson's Arch springer: 51 ft along the wall, its south side 39 ft from the south-west corner; stones up to 24½ ft long (Robinson 1841).

Biblical Passages

  • Mark 13:1–2; Matt 24:1–2; Luke 21:5–6Jesus says of the temple buildings, 'There shall not be left one stone upon another.' The Western Wall is a retaining wall of the platform, not the temple building. The temple itself was destroyed in 70 CE, and these lower walls still stand.
  • John 2:20'Forty-six years was this temple in building.' The dated construction phases at Wilson's Arch and the tunnels show the complex was still being built through the early 1st century CE.
