Tell es-Safi / Gath: the late 9th-century siege and destruction

Philistine city with a siege trench, wall breach and destruction layer attributed to Hazael of Aram · late 9th century BCE

Overview

Tell es-Safi, identified with Philistine Gath, was excavated by a Bar-Ilan University team under Aren Maeir from 1996. The final-report series (Ägypten und Altes Testament 69, 105, 122) says the last large-scale season was in July 2021.

Aerial photographs showed a feature encircling the tell on three sides. Excavation showed it is a man-made trench over 2 km long, 5-6 m deep and more than 8 m wide, cut through bedrock, with the spoil dumped on one side as a berm. It was dug in Iron Age IIA and is read as part of a siege system (Ackermann, Bruins and Maeir 2005). A destruction level of the late 9th century BCE covers the Iron IIA city. Maeir (Vetus Testamentum 54, 2004) takes the siege system and destruction as the physical trace of Hazael of Aram's capture of Gath (2 Kgs 12:17), after which the site was of little importance until the end of the Iron Age. In 2021 the team reported a breach of about 30 feet in the city wall, read as the point where the attackers broke in.

The site's earlier Iron Age finds include a late Iron I/early Iron II inscription on a bowl sherd with two names, read 'lwt and wlt[...] (Maeir, Wimmer, Zukerman and Demsky, BASOR 351, 2008), and a 9th-century horned altar.

What the evidence shows

  • A trench over 2 km long, 5-6 m deep and more than 8 m wide, cut through bedrock around three sides of the tell, with the spoil heaped as a berm on one side (Ackermann, Bruins and Maeir 2005, abstract).
  • The trench fill has two phases, Iron Age IIA and Byzantine, separated by a soil-formation horizon. The digging is dated to Iron IIA (ca. 1000-800 BCE) (same).
  • A destruction level and siege system dated to the late 9th century BCE, after which the site is 'of little significance until the end of the Iron Age' (Maeir 2004, abstract).
  • A 9th-century BCE destruction layer studied with combined macro- and micro-archaeological methods (Namdar, Zukerman, Maeir, Katz and Cabanes 2011).
  • A breach of nearly 30 feet in the fortification, found in the 2021 season (BAS, 12 Aug 2021, reporting Maeir).
  • An inscribed sherd from a red-slipped, hand-burnished bowl in a clear late Iron I/early Iron II context, read as two personal names, 'lwt and wlt[...], judged by the editors to be Philistine names of Greek or Anatolian origin (Maeir et al. 2008, abstract).

Open questions

  • Was it Hazael? The dates fit: a late-9th-century destruction (Maeir 2004; Namdar et al. 2011), and 2 Kgs 12:17 records Hazael taking Gath in the reign of Jehoash of Judah. No inscription at the site names the attacker. The attribution rests on that date match plus the biblical notice.
  • Trench date: broad or narrow? The geoarchaeological study (Ackermann et al. 2005) dates the trench to Iron IIA, 'ca. 1000-800 B.C.E.', and calls it 'apparently' part of a siege system. Maeir (2004) ties the siege system specifically to the late-9th-century destruction. The two fit together, but the trench fill alone gives the broader range.
  • Amos 6:2. Maeir (VT 54, 2004) argues that Amos's 'go down to Gath of the Philistines' refers to cities destroyed by Hazael, and so dates the oracle soon after Gath's destruction, while it was fresh in memory. This is a reading of the biblical text in light of the dig, not something the stratigraphy itself shows.
  • The 'lwt / wlt inscription and Goliath. The editors (BASOR 351, 2008) discuss how these two names relate to the biblical Philistine names and do not claim either is Goliath. Popular 'Goliath inscription' headlines go beyond the article's own framing.
  • The horned altar: two horns or four? Eitam (Academia Letters, 2022) takes up the question (title only seen).

The Find

  • DateDestruction late 9th century BCE (c. 830 BCE in the excavators' popular accounts); siege trench cut in Iron Age IIA
  • How datedStratigraphy and pottery of the Iron IIA destruction level. Radiocarbon and ceramics for the trench fill (Ackermann, Bruins and Maeir, Geoarchaeology 20, 2005: 'Iron Age IIA (ca. 1000-800 B.C.E.)'). Namdar et al. (J. Archaeological Science 38, 2011) studied the 9th-century destruction layer. The link to Hazael is historical: 2 Kgs 12:17 is the only recorded conqueror of Gath in that period.
  • DiscoveredBar-Ilan University excavations 1996 to July 2021 (the last large-scale season, per the final-report series), Aren M. Maeir (director), Tell es-Safi/Gath Archaeological Project, Bar-Ilan University, Tell es-Safi (Tel Zafit), at the edge of the Shephelah and the coastal plain, central Israel
  • Now heldIn situ (site); finds with the project and the Israel Antiquities Authority (repository details not stated in the sources read)

Biblical Passages

  • 2 Kgs 12:17 (Heb. 12:18)'Hazael king of Syria went up and fought against Gath and took it'. The late-9th-century siege works and destruction are the excavators' match to this verse.
  • Amos 6:2'Then go down to Gath of the Philistines'. Maeir (2004) dates the oracle close to the city's destruction.
  • 1 Sam 17:4; 2 Sam 21:19-22Goliath and the giants 'of Gath'. The site is Gath, but no find names Goliath. The Iron I/IIA 'lwt and wlt names are Philistine names that the editors discuss alongside the biblical onomasticon, not the same name.
  • 2 Chr 26:6Uzziah 'broke down the wall of Gath'. This comes after the Hazael destruction, when Maeir (2004) describes the site as of little significance.
  • Mic 1:10'Tell it not in Gath'. Gath still serves as a name to conjure with in the late 8th century.
