Ophel: monumental Second Temple ritual-bath complex

Three large mikva’ot, a plastered pool, paved plaza and water system near the Temple Mount · announced September 2026

Overview

Hebrew University excavations led by Uzi Leibner and Orit Peleg-Barkat exposed a large purification complex immediately south of the Temple Mount.

Three substantial ritual baths, a central plastered pool, a plaza and water installations indicate infrastructure suited to ritual washing. Use by pilgrims is a strongly supported site interpretation, not the identity of particular biblical visitors.

What the evidence shows

  • One bath survives unusually high, including a stone-vaulted ceiling.
  • Architectural and coin evidence associates the complex with the final centuries of Second Temple Jerusalem.

Open questions

  • The precise function of the central pool and traffic patterns through the complex require continued analysis.
  • Architectural interpretation does not identify individual people or confirm a specific gospel episode.
