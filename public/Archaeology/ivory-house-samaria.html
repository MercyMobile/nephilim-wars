Samaria Ivories (the 'Ivory House' finds)

Carved ivory inlays from the royal quarter of Samaria · Iron Age II (9th-8th c. BCE range)

Overview

Samaria, the northern kingdom's capital, has produced a large body of carved ivory: plaques, panels and fragments that were once inlays on furniture and wall panelling. A few came from the Harvard excavations of 1908-10. The main body came from the Joint Expedition of 1931-35, published by J. W. and G. M. Crowfoot in PEQ (1933) and in Early Ivories from Samaria (Samaria-Sebaste II, 1938). About 500 pieces are counted, and only a small part could be restored.

The motifs are those of the Levantine ivory workshops known from Nimrud and Arslan Tash. One group is Egyptianizing: the child Horus (Harpocrates) on a lotus, Isis and Nephthys, Heh, Horus and Ma'at. The other group has the 'woman at the window', winged sphinxes, winged human figures, lion-and-bull combat and the stylized palmette 'tree'.

'Ivory House' is a modern label taken from 1 Kings 22:39. The Harvard report (1924) already called the second Israelite palace building the 'ivory palace' of Ahab, before the main ivory finds, on the strength of its fine ashlar masonry and its place in the building sequence.

What the evidence shows

  • About 500 carved ivory pieces from the royal quarter; only a small fraction restorable (Pienaar 2008, citing SS II and Kenyon 1960).
  • Findspots spread across several areas of the summit (Harvard S2, S4, S7; Joint Expedition Qc, Qk, Qd, Qh, Z, B), not a single sealed room.
  • Egyptianizing iconography (Harpocrates on lotus, Isis/Nephthys, Heh, Horus with Ma'at) alongside Levantine motifs (woman at the window, winged sphinx, lion and bull).
  • Same motif repertoire as ivories from Nimrud and Arslan Tash, e.g. the 'woman at the window'.
  • Reisner-Fisher-Lyon (1924): the second palace was 'naturally identified with the "ivory palace" of Ahab, and the smoothly dressed surfaces ... are sufficient justification for this name' - an identification based on masonry and sequence, not on ivory finds.
  • No ivory from Samaria carries a royal name; nothing on the objects names Ahab.

Open questions

  • When were the ivories made and deposited? The Crowfoots (PEQ 1933; Early Ivories from Samaria, 1938) presented them in the context of the Israelite palace and the biblical 'ivory house' of Ahab. Ron E. Tappy (The Archaeology of Israelite Samaria II: The Eighth Century BCE, 2001, ch. 'The Archaeo-Historical Context of the Samaria Ivories', pp. 443-503) re-examined the excavation records and treats the ivories' contexts within the eighth-century material. The objects themselves are not stratigraphically dated; the question turns on which fills and floors they came from.
  • Whose workshop? Irene J. Winter (Iraq 43, 1981) separated a 'South Syrian' carving style from the Phoenician and North Syrian styles found at Nimrud, which bears on whether the Samaria pieces are imports or local work. D. Barag (Anatolian Studies 33, 1983) proposed using glass inlays to classify and date ninth-eighth century ivories.
  • Ahab's 'house of ivory' (1 Kgs 22:39) or the later 'houses of ivory' Amos condemns (Amos 3:15; 6:4)? Because the ivories are undated, they fit either text; no find ties them to one king.

The Find

  • DateIron Age II; the range usually given is 9th-8th century BCE. No object carries a date, and the dating is itself disputed (see questions).
  • How datedNot dated by inscription. Dating rests on (a) the find contexts in and around the Israelite palace/acropolis at Samaria, and (b) stylistic comparison with the ivory hoards from Nimrud and Arslan Tash. The 1908-10 Harvard excavators assigned the second palace phase to Ahab by building sequence ('it is the earliest of the three structures' for Omri, the next for Ahab), not by any inscription.
  • DiscoveredA few pieces 1908-1910 (Harvard Expedition, G. A. Reisner, C. S. Fisher, D. G. Lyon); the main body 1931-1935 (Joint Expedition to Samaria led by J. W. Crowfoot, with K. M. Kenyon and E. L. Sukenik; ivories published by J. W. and G. M. Crowfoot), Harvard Expedition; then the Joint Expedition (PEF, British Academy, British School of Archaeology in Jerusalem, Hebrew University, Harvard), Summit (royal acropolis) of Samaria/Sebaste; Harvard pieces in areas S2, S4, S7; Joint Expedition pieces in areas Qc, Qk, Qd, Qh, Z and B (per the 1938 publication's plan, as cited by Pienaar 2008)
  • Now heldNot confirmed in a published museum record. The pieces were split between the two expeditions and are not in one collection; no museum inventory numbers were located.
  • MaterialElephant ivory, carved in relief and openwork; some pieces set with coloured glass/stone inlay
  • DimensionsMostly small plaques and fragments; about 500 pieces in all (Pienaar 2008, citing Crowfoot & Crowfoot 1938 and Barkay 1992)

Biblical Passages

  • 1 Kgs 22:39'The ivory house which he [Ahab] built.' Samaria has produced a large body of ivory inlay from its royal quarter; nothing ties any piece to Ahab by name or by sealed date.
  • Amos 3:15; 6:4'Houses of ivory' and 'beds of ivory' among Samaria's elite. The ivories are furniture and wall inlays of exactly that kind; Amos is set in the 8th century, within the date range proposed for the ivories.
  • Ps 45:8'Palaces of ivory' as a royal luxury; a general parallel only.
