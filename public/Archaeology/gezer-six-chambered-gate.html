Gezer Six-Chambered Gate

Iron Age city gate, Field III, Tel Gezer · Stratum 8/VIII

Overview

A monumental city gate on the south side of Tel Gezer with three chambers on each side of a central passage, tied into a casemate wall. Macalister exposed half of it in 1902-1909 and published it as the 'Maccabaean castle' (Excavation of Gezer I, plan p. 217). In 1958 Yigael Yadin, reading Macalister's plan without seeing the buried remains, argued that it was half of an Iron Age gate matching gates he knew at Hazor (Stratum X) and Megiddo (Stratum VA/IVB), and tied all three to 1 Kings 9:15.

The Hebrew Union College / Harvard Semitic Museum excavations under William G. Dever cleared the structure in 1967-1971 and confirmed the gate. Dever later excavated 'Palace 10,000', a large administrative complex immediately west of the gate and in use with it.

Renewed work by the Tandy Institute (Ortiz and Wolff, 2006-2017) produced the first 14C series for the Iron Age city. The gate's own stones were not sampled; the dates come from the adjacent administrative building of the same stratum.

What the evidence shows

  • Plan: central passage flanked by three chambers per side (a 'four-entryway' gate in Dever's wording), joined to a casemate wall.
  • Macalister's volume I lists a 'Plan of the Maccabaean castle' and 'Eastern jamb of the gate of the Maccabaean castle' (figs. at pp. 217-218): he dug the structure and misdated it.
  • Dever's stratigraphic grounds (JJAR 1, 2021, p. 107): Stratum VIII lies above a late Philistine Decorated Ware stage and a destruction; hand burnish only, no wheel burnish; a heavy destruction after the third street level of VIII; 9th-8th c. administrative structures overlie it.
  • Webster et al. 2023: Stratum 8 built 998-957 BCE (68.3% hpd) and destroyed 969-940 BCE (68.3%); the authors state the model 'rule[s] out a 9th century BC date' for the stratum.
  • The dated samples are from the administrative building next to the gate, not from the gate foundations.

Open questions

  • Date of the gate: Dever (JJAR 1, 2021) assigns it to the 10th c. BCE on stratigraphy (Stratum VIII above late Philistine ware, hand-burnish-only ceramics, destruction he attributes to Shoshenq I) and cites the new 14C dates; Webster, Wolff and Ortiz (PLOS ONE 2023; JEMAHS 12.4, 2024) model Stratum 8 construction at 998-957 BCE. Finkelstein and Piasetzky ('The Gezer Discrepancy', JEMAHS 12.4, 2024) object that the contexts' stratigraphic attribution is not secure and that the Gezer dates disagree with other Shephelah 14C sequences.
  • Whether the gate belongs to the same plan as Hazor and Megiddo: Yadin (IEJ 8, 1958) argued one royal design; at Megiddo, Finkelstein et al. (Tel Aviv 46, 2019) place six-chambered Gate 2156 in Strata VA-IVB, which they date to the Omride 9th century, so the three gates would not be contemporary on their reading. At Gezer the 14C model (Webster et al. 2023) puts the stratum in the early 10th century.
  • Sampling gap: no sample comes from the gate's construction fill; the link rests on the gate and Palace 10,000 being in the same stratum (Dever 2021; Webster et al. 2023).

Since 2024

  • 2024-12JEMAHS 12.4 published a forum on the Gezer 14C dates: summary by Webster, Wolff and Ortiz, a critique by Finkelstein & Piasetzky ('The Gezer Discrepancy'), and responses by Dever, A. Mazar, Ben-Yosef, Garfinkel, Maeir, Levy and Ben-Dor Evian, with the authors' closing remarks. [5]

The Find

  • DateBuilt with Gezer Stratum 8 (Tandy numbering) / VIII (HUC numbering); 14C model for Stratum 8 construction: 998-957 BCE (68.3%), 1023-942 BCE (95.4%). The gate itself has not been radiocarbon-dated.
  • How datedStratigraphy and ceramics (Dever's HUC excavations 1967-1971, 1984, 1990) plus a Bayesian 14C model on short-lived samples from the administrative building adjoining the gate (Webster et al. 2023). Finkelstein & Piasetzky (2024) contest the reliability of that dataset.
  • DiscoveredFirst exposed 1902-1909 (misidentified); recognised as a gate 1958 (on paper); cleared 1967-1971, R. A. S. Macalister (Palestine Exploration Fund) exposed half of it and called it a 'Maccabaean castle'; Yigael Yadin identified it from Macalister's plan (1958); William G. Dever and the HUC-JIR/Harvard Semitic Museum expedition excavated it, South side of Tel Gezer (Field III), Israel
  • Now heldIn situ, Tel Gezer National Park, Israel
  • MaterialStone masonry; ashlar tower recorded beside it (Dever 1985 plan)

Biblical Passages

  • 1 Kgs 9:15-17The text names Hazor, Megiddo and Gezer among Solomon's building works and says Pharaoh had taken and burned Gezer and given it to his daughter, Solomon's wife, after which Solomon rebuilt it. The find is a gate in the stratum the excavators date to the early 10th c.; no inscription names a builder. Reading the gate as Solomon's is an inference from date and plan.
  • 1 Kgs 9:16Dever (2021) links the destruction beneath Stratum VIII to an Egyptian campaign (he names Siamun); the Tandy team's 14C work is framed against the same verse. This is the excavators' reading, not a written record at the site.
