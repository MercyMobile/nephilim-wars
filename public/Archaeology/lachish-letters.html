Lachish Letters

Hebrew military letters on potsherds from the gate of Lachish · c. 588-586 BCE

Overview

Twenty-one inscribed potsherds found in the burnt gate complex of Lachish, most of them in 1935 by J. L. Starkey's expedition. Most are letters from a subordinate named Hoshayahu to his superior Ya'ush, written in ink in Hebrew during the Babylonian invasion that ended Judah's kingdom. They were first published by Harry Torczyner (later Tur-Sinai) in 1938.

Letter 3 reports that 'the commander of the army, Coniah son of Elnathan, has gone down to go to Egypt' and mentions a letter passed on through 'the prophet'. Letter 4 ends with the writer saying that they are watching for the fire-signals of Lachish according to the signs his lord gave, 'for we cannot see Azekah'.

Jeremiah 34:7 names Lachish and Azekah as the last fortified cities of Judah still holding out against Babylon. Letter 4 is read by many as a snapshot of that moment; what it literally says is that the writer's post could not see Azekah.

What the text says (summary)

Letter 4 (closing lines): the writer tells his lord that they are watching for the signal-fires of Lachish according to all the signs his lord has given, because they cannot see Azekah.Paraphrase of Lachish Letter 4 (Torczyner 1938 editio princeps; no public-domain English translation exists)

What the evidence shows

  • 21 ostraca, ink on pottery, Old Hebrew cursive; found in the burnt gate complex of Lachish (Windle 2025; CoJS).
  • Most are addressed to Ya'ush by his servant Hoshayahu (CoJS).
  • Letter 3 names 'Coniah son of Elnathan', commander of the army, who has 'gone down to go to Egypt', and refers to 'the prophet'.
  • Letter 4 closes: watching for the signals of Lachish; 'we cannot see Azekah'.
  • The word 'prophet' in Letter 3 line 20 is spelled without a yod (point made by Rollston in the Isaiah-bulla debate).
  • Found in the destruction layer of the gate complex, i.e. they were lying there when the city burned.

Open questions

  • Where were the letters written, and what are they? The long-standing reading (Torczyner 1938 and most later work, as summarised by CoJS) is that Hoshayahu commanded an outpost between Lachish and Jerusalem and sent these letters to Ya'ush at Lachish. Yigael Yadin argued instead that Hoshayahu was at Lachish itself and that the sherds are drafts or copies of letters sent (on papyrus) to Ya'ush in Jerusalem. Anson Rainey ('Watching Out for the Signal Fires of Lachish', PEQ 119, 1987) defended the outpost reading (as summarised by Windle 2025; Rainey's article itself not read).
  • Does 'we cannot see Azekah' mean Azekah had already fallen? That is a common inference tied to Jer 34:7. The letter itself only says the post could not see Azekah; it could also mean the line of sight was blocked or the signal was not lit. The text does not say Azekah fell.
  • Are the letters full texts? Person (Jerusalem Journal of Archaeology 7, 2024) argues that Iron Age Judahite written texts, including the Lachish letters, were metonymic stand-ins for fuller oral messages, so the ostraca 'did not necessarily contain the full' message.
  • Who is 'the prophet' in Letter 3? The name is not given. Identifications with Jeremiah or Uriah (Jer 26:20-23) are readings, not text.

The Find

  • DateFinal years of Judah, just before Lachish fell to Nebuchadnezzar II (c. 588-586 BCE)
  • How datedFound in the burnt destruction debris of the city gate complex of Lachish (Level II), attributed to the Babylonian campaign that ended with Jerusalem's fall in 586 BCE; the letters themselves carry no year.
  • DiscoveredJanuary-February 1935 (most of the letters); a few more in 1938, James Leslie Starkey's Wellcome-Marston expedition to Tell ed-Duweir (Lachish), Burnt remains of the gate complex (gate chamber), Tel Lachish
  • Now heldMost in the British Museum, London; a smaller group, including Letter 6, in Jerusalem (Rockefeller Museum / Israel Antiquities Authority). Individual museum numbers circulate in secondary listings but were not checked against museum records.
  • MaterialPottery sherds (ostraca) written in ink
  • ScriptOld Hebrew (Paleo-Hebrew) cursive
  • LanguageHebrew

Biblical Passages

  • Jer 34:7'The king of Babylon's army fought against Jerusalem, and against all the cities of Judah that were left, against Lachish, and against Azekah: for these defenced cities remained.' Letter 4 names exactly Lachish and Azekah in a siege-signalling context.
  • Jer 26:20-23Elnathan son of Achbor is sent to Egypt to fetch the prophet Uriah; Letter 3 has a 'Coniah son of Elnathan' going down to Egypt. Same patronym and destination; whether the same family is our inference.
  • 2 Kgs 25:1-4; Jer 39:1The Babylonian siege of Judah's cities under Zedekiah, the setting of the destruction layer the letters were found in.
