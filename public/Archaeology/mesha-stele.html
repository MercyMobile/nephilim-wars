Mesha Stele

Victory and building inscription of Mesha king of Moab · 9th c. BCE

Overview

A round-topped basalt stele, 125 cm high, inscribed in 34 lines in the name of Mesha son of Kemosh-[yat] king of Moab, 'the Dibonite'. The Louvre record says it was engraved shortly after 842 BCE for a sanctuary of the god Kemosh.

Klein saw the complete stone at Dhiban in 1868. A paper squeeze was taken for Clermont-Ganneau; the stone was then broken up by local people, and Clermont-Ganneau recovered fragments that were reassembled with the squeeze as guide. The Louvre bought it from him in 1873.

Mesha says Omri king of Israel oppressed Moab 'many days' because Kemosh was angry with his land, that Omri's son said he too would oppress Moab, and that Kemosh restored the land in Mesha's day. He lists taking Ataroth from the men of Gad, taking Nebo and carrying off 'the vessels of Yahweh' to Kemosh, driving the king of Israel out of Jahaz, and building works at Qarhoh, Aroer, the Arnon road and other towns. The closing lines, damaged, speak of Horonaim.

In its own words

Omri, king of Israel - he oppressed Moab many days, because Chemosh was angry with his land. And his son succeeded him, and he also said I will oppress Moab. ... And I took from there the altar-hearths of Yahweh (Jehovah), and I dragged them before Chemosh.George A. Barton, Archaeology and the Bible (Philadelphia: American Sunday-School Union, 1916), pp. 363-364 (translated from Smend and Socin 1886).

What the evidence shows

  • Basalt, H 125 x W 69 x D 37 cm; incomplete, fragmented, reassembled with restoration (Louvre AO 5066).
  • 34 lines of text in Phoenician-type letters, Moabite language (Louvre record).
  • Names Omri king of Israel and his son; names Yahweh (the vessels/altar-hearths taken from Nebo); names Kemosh repeatedly; names 'the men of Gad' as dwelling in Ataroth 'from of old'.
  • Place names in the text include Medeba, Baal-meon, Kiriathaim, Ataroth, Nebo, Jahaz, Dibon, Aroer, Arnon, Beth-bamoth, Bezer, Beth-diblathan, Beth-baal-meon and Horonaim (Barton 1916 translation).
  • Line 31 is damaged; Barton's 1916 translation leaves the passage before Horonaim blank. Its reading is the subject of the 'House of David' dispute.

Open questions

  • Line 31 'House of David': Lemaire proposed bt[d]wd; Langlois (Semitica 61, 2019) applied RTI to the stone and the 1868 squeeze and judged it 'hypothetical but ... the most probable reading', pointing to a word divider after the group; Lemaire and Delorme (BAR 48, Nov. 2022) state that new photographs 'clearly establish the presence of the first dalet and confirm the last dalet, while only the letter taw remains somewhat unclear', and conclude: 'we believe the reading btdwd is confirmed once and for all'. Richelle and Burlingame (BAR Spring 2023; BAS, 25 Aug 2023) answer that where the taw should stand 'there are no traces of scribal strokes belonging to a taw, only striations and small depressions', that the dalet on the squeeze depends on choosing which shadows count, and that the reading remains a hypothesis, not a finding. Finkelstein, Na'aman and Römer (Tel Aviv 46, 2019) examined new photographs of the stone and squeeze and concluded the ruler's name had three consonants starting with b, proposing Balak (Num 22-24).
  • Timing against 2 Kings: the stele says Omri held Medeba 'his days and half the days of his son, forty years' and that Israel oppressed Moab in the son's days; 2 Kgs 1:1 and 3:5 place Moab's rebellion after Ahab's death. Barton (1916, p. 364) notes that the Bible says nothing of Mesha's revolt as he tells it and Mesha says nothing of the campaign in 2 Kgs 3. How to reconcile 'son' and 'forty years' with the regnal lengths in Kings is an open reading.
  • Line 12 'rʾl dwdh' (Barton: 'altar-hearth of Duda'): whether dwdh is a divine name, a title, or a personal name is not settled by the text itself.

Since 2024

  • 2022-11Lemaire and Delorme (BAR 48) publish the 2015 West Semitic Research Project RTI photographs and call btdwd 'confirmed once and for all'; press coverage in January 2023. [6]

The Find

  • Date9th century BCE. The Louvre record: 'IXe s. av. J.-C. (Epoque de Mesha vers 830-805)' and 'Gravé peu après 842'.
  • How datedNo regnal year in the text. Mesha names Omri king of Israel and Omri's son; 2 Kgs 3:4-5 places Mesha's revolt after Ahab's death. Letter forms fit the 9th c.
  • DiscoveredAugust 1868, Seen by the missionary F. A. Klein; squeeze taken for Charles Clermont-Ganneau before local people broke the stone; fragments recovered and reassembled by Clermont-Ganneau, Dhiban (ancient Dibon), Jordan
  • Now heldMusée du Louvre, Paris, Department of Near Eastern Antiquities, AO 5066 (also MNB 752); Sully wing, room 303 per the collections record
  • MaterialBasalt
  • DimensionsH. 125 cm, W. 69 cm, D. 37 cm (Louvre); round-topped; broken and reassembled with restorations
  • ScriptAlphabetic, Phoenician-type letters ('34 lines in Phoenician-type characters' - Louvre)
  • LanguageMoabite

Biblical Passages

  • 2 Kgs 3:4-27Mesha king of Moab, a sheep-breeder (nōqēd), paid tribute to the king of Israel and rebelled after Ahab died. The stele names Mesha, Omri and Omri's son, and Mesha's revolt; it does not describe the Israel-Judah-Edom campaign of 2 Kgs 3.
  • 2 Kgs 1:1'Moab rebelled against Israel after the death of Ahab.' The stele places the recovery 'in my day' and during the son's reign; see questions.
  • 1 Kgs 16:21-28Omri as king of Israel: the stele names 'Omri king of Israel' (ʿmry mlk yśrʾl).
  • Num 32:34-38; Josh 13:15-23Gad and Reuben building/holding Ataroth, Dibon, Aroer, Nebo, Baal-meon, Kiriathaim, Jahaz, Beth-baal-meon. The stele says 'the men of Gad had dwelt in the land of Ataroth from of old'.
  • Num 22-24Balak king of Moab: the Finkelstein-Na'aman-Römer restoration of line 31.
