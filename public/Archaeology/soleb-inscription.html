Soleb 'Land of the Shasu of Yhw' Inscription

Prisoner-name cartouche in Amenhotep III's temple of Amun-Ra at Soleb, Sudan · c. 1400-1385 BCE

Overview

Amenhotep III's temple of Amun-Ra at Soleb in Nubia has columns carved with bound foreign prisoners, each over a fortified name-ring naming a subject people or land. Column IV N4 in the hypostyle hall carries four names of Shasu (Egyptian šꜣsw, 'nomads') groups. The second, N4 a2, reads tꜣ šꜣsw yhwꜣ, 'the land of the Shasu of Yhwꜣ'.

The same phrase appears on a fragmentary wall block (SB 69) from the temple's second court, and again in a later list at Amara West (Ramesside, early 13th century BCE), which appears to copy part of the Soleb list. The column is still standing at Soleb; the drawing is published in the excavation volume Soleb V (1998).

The name is spelled y-h-w-ꜣ. Kennedy (2019), photographing the column, reported that the last sign is the vulture/aleph (G1), not the quail-chick w (G43) printed in the excavation report. No text accompanies the name beyond the list formula: it says nothing about who these Shasu worshipped or where exactly they lived.

What the text says (summary)

tꜣ šꜣsw yhwꜣ - 'land of the Shasu (nomads) of Yhwꜣ'Literal translation (this encyclopedia) of the transliteration published by T. Kennedy, Dotawo 6 (2019) 184 (column IV N4 a2)

What the evidence shows

  • Column IV N4 names four Shasu groups: twr byl, yhwꜣ, smt, bt ꜥn[...] (Kennedy 2019, citing Leclant, Les Fouilles de Soleb, p. 214).
  • Sign sequence of N4 a2: N16 (land) + šꜣ-sw (nomads) + M17-O4-V4-G1 (y-h-wꜣ-ꜣ) (Kennedy 2019, p. 184).
  • The name carries no city/foreign-land classifier and no 'god' sign (Kennedy 2019).
  • A second Soleb occurrence is on wall block SB 69, 2nd court, R38 (Soleb III pp. 179-180; Soleb V pls. 206-207, as cited by Kennedy).
  • The prisoner figure on N4 is headless; the ethnic dress of this particular captive is not preserved (Kennedy 2019).
  • Amenhotep III's only recorded military campaign is the Year 5 Kush campaign; the Asiatic names in the list are not tied to a recorded campaign (Kennedy 2019, citing Urk. IV 1665-1666).

Open questions

  • Is yhwꜣ the divine name YHWH? T. Kennedy (Dotawo 6, 2019) says yes: the name is unique, is not a known place-name, lacks a land classifier, and sits beside other Shasu names he reads as divine (twr byl = 'Bull Baal'; bt ꜥnt = 'house of Anat'); he concludes these Shasu 'could be identified with the early Israelites'. F. Adrom and M. Müller (in The Origins of Yahwism, de Gruyter 2017, 93-114) hold that the sequence cannot be shown to be a tribe, a place or a deity, and caution against tying it to Israelite or proto-Israelite groups. The inscription itself settles the spelling, not the referent.
  • Where were these Shasu? Egyptian texts of Ramesses II-III place Shasu in the Seir/Edom region; D. B. Redford (Egypt, Canaan, and Israel in Ancient Times, 1992, pp. 269-273, as cited by Kennedy) uses this to locate the Yhw Shasu in the south, in or near Edom. Kennedy widens the field to Sinai, Edom, Moab, Transjordan or Canaan. The Soleb column gives no geography of its own beyond grouping these names as Shasu.
  • Last sign: the excavation report (Soleb III pp. 122-123) transcribed the final bird as w (G43); Kennedy (2019), from new photographs, reads aleph (G1), matching the Amara West copy. Adrom & Müller (2017) follow the older reading, per Kennedy n. 30.
  • Date: Kennedy dates the lists 1409-1385 BCE on a high chronology; on the lower chronologies common in Egyptological handbooks Amenhotep III's jubilee falls in the 1360s BCE. The absolute date moves with the chronology chosen, not with anything on the column.

The Find

  • DateReign of Amenhotep III, 18th Dynasty; carved no later than his Year 30 jubilee (c. 1385 BCE on Kennedy's high chronology; c. 1360s on lower chronologies)
  • How datedThe temple was built and decorated for Amenhotep III and carries his Year-30 Heb-Sed scenes, so the prisoner lists were in place by the first jubilee. Kennedy (2019) brackets the lists to c. 1409-1385 BCE using a high 18th-Dynasty chronology; the stone gives only the king, not a regnal year for the list itself.
  • DiscoveredTemple known since Burckhardt (1813); the Yhw column and wall block uncovered in the 1957-1963 excavations, Mission of Michela Schiff Giorgini (with Clément Robichon, Jean Leclant), Hypostyle hall (Sector IV), column IV N4; and wall block SB 69 from the 2nd court (Sector III), temple of Amun-Ra, Soleb, west bank of the Nile south of the Third Cataract, Sudan
  • Now heldColumn IV N4: in situ at Soleb. Wall block SB 69: in storage in Sudan (antiquities department), per Kennedy 2019
  • MaterialSandstone temple column (relief-carved)
  • DimensionsName-ring (cartouche) 42 cm x 15 cm, measured on site by Kennedy (2019)
  • ScriptEgyptian hieroglyphs
  • LanguageEgyptian (rendering a foreign, West Semitic name)

Biblical Passages

  • Exodus 3:15; 6:3If yhwꜣ is the divine name, this is the earliest written occurrence of it, attached to a nomadic group east of Egypt, not to a city. That is a reading of the name, not a statement in the text.
  • Deuteronomy 33:2; Judges 5:4; Habakkuk 3:3These poems have YHWH coming from Seir/Edom/Teman. Egyptian texts put Shasu in Seir; the Soleb list groups a 'land of the Shasu of Yhw' among Shasu. The correspondence is regional, and depends on the Seir location of these particular Shasu, which the Soleb column does not state.
