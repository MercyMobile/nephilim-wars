Solomon's Wall (Ophel fortification)

Iron Age city wall, gatehouse and tower on the Ophel, south of the Temple Mount · date disputed (10th vs 8th-7th century BCE)

Overview

A stretch of city wall with an inner gatehouse, an adjoining 'royal structure' and towers on the Ophel, the saddle between the City of David ridge and the Temple Mount. Charles Warren traced the outline of the large tower in an underground survey in 1867 without assigning it a date. Benjamin and Eilat Mazar excavated in the area in the 1980s (final report: Qedem 29, 1989). Eilat Mazar re-excavated it and on 22 February 2010 the Hebrew University announced it as a city wall of the 10th century BCE, which she connected with Solomon's building of 'the wall of Jerusalem round about' (1 Kings 3:1).

The 2010 release lists a 70 m wall section 6 m high, a four-room gatehouse, a 24 x 18 m tower, storage jars 1.15 m tall burned in a fire, cult figurines, seal impressions reading 'to the king', Hebrew bullae, and a jar with a partial Hebrew inscription that Mazar read as naming a high government official. The date rests on sherds in the fill of the lowest floor of the building beside the gate and on comparison with gates at Megiddo, Beersheba and Ashdod.

The date is disputed. Finkelstein, Herzog, Singer-Avitz and Ussishkin (Tel Aviv 34, 2007) argued, from Mazar's own earlier publication of this 'Solomonic city-wall', that the dated evidence placed its use in the 8th-7th centuries BCE and that the 10th-century attribution came from the biblical text rather than the finds.

What the evidence shows

  • Wall section c. 70 m long and 6 m high; eastern section 35 m long and 5 m high (Hebrew University release, 22 Feb 2010).
  • Inner gatehouse 6 m high with a symmetrical four-room plan; tower beside it 24 x 18 m; corner tower 8 m long, 6 m high overlooking the Kidron valley (same release).
  • Storage jars 1.15 m high were found burned; one bears a partial Hebrew inscription that the excavator read as belonging to a high government official (same release).
  • Finds also include cult figurines, seal impressions reading 'to the king', and Hebrew bullae; the release does not give the stratum of each.
  • The dating evidence given is: pottery sherds 'within the fill of the lowest floor of the royal building near the gatehouse', plus architectural comparison with gates at Megiddo, Beersheba and Ashdod.
  • Charles Warren recorded the outline of the large tower in 1867 without attributing it to Solomon (same release).
  • The Ophel area was excavated in the 1980s by B. and E. Mazar; their report (Qedem 29, 1989) includes Iron Age II figurines, Hebrew inscriptions and seal impressions, and a dendrochronology study from the 1986 season.
  • No radiocarbon dates specific to this wall were found in the sources checked.

Open questions

  • Date of construction. Eilat Mazar (Hebrew University release, 22 Feb 2010) dates the wall to the late 10th century BCE on the basis of sherds in the fill of the lowest floor of the adjoining building and on gate plans like Megiddo, Beersheba and Ashdod. Finkelstein, Herzog, Singer-Avitz and Ussishkin (Tel Aviv 34, 2007: 159) respond, concerning Mazar's 2006 chapter 'The Solomonic Wall in Jerusalem': 'As she admits, the chronological data recovered in her excavations indicate that the sole Iron Age fortification system extending in this area was in use during the 8th-7th centuries BCE,' and that it was dated to Solomon because 'the Solomonic city-wall must have passed here' according to the biblical sources. Evidence to test: sherds in a fill give only the earliest possible date of the floor above them; four-room gates are known in more than one Iron Age phase (our reading). The 2010 finds post-date Finkelstein's critique, and no published response to them was located for this entry.
  • Whether 'to the king' seal impressions, bullae and burned jars belong to the wall's construction phase or to its later use and destruction: the 2010 release lists them together and does not separate them by stratum.
  • Reading of the jar inscription: the 2010 release gives only the excavator's reading ('belonged to a high-level government official'); the full published reading was not located for this entry.

The Find

  • DateDisputed. Eilat Mazar (2010): late 10th century BCE. Finkelstein, Herzog, Singer-Avitz and Ussishkin (2007), writing about Mazar's earlier (2006) publication of the same fortification: in use in the 8th-7th centuries BCE.
  • How datedNo inscription dates the wall. Mazar's 10th-century date rests on (1) pottery sherds 'within the fill of the lowest floor of the royal building near the gatehouse' and (2) architectural comparison with Iron Age gates at Megiddo, Beersheba and Ashdod (Hebrew University release, 22 Feb 2010). Finkelstein et al. (Tel Aviv 34, 2007: 159) state that the chronological data Mazar had published then showed the fortification 'was in use during the 8th-7th centuries BCE'. No radiocarbon dates for the Ophel wall were located for this entry.
  • DiscoveredTower outline recorded 1867; excavated 1986 (B. and E. Mazar); re-excavated and announced February 2010, Charles Warren (1867 underground survey); Benjamin Mazar and Eilat Mazar (1980s, Hebrew University); Eilat Mazar (2009-2010 season, Hebrew University Institute of Archaeology), The Ophel, between the City of David and the southern wall of the Temple Mount, Jerusalem
  • Now heldIn situ, Ophel, south of the Temple Mount, Jerusalem (outside the Old City walls; not in the Jewish Quarter)
  • MaterialStone masonry (wall, gatehouse, towers)
  • DimensionsAs announced 22 Feb 2010: wall section c. 70 m long and 6 m high; inner gatehouse 6 m high, symmetrical four-room plan; adjacent tower 24 x 18 m; corner tower 8 m long, 6 m high; an eastern wall section 35 m long, 5 m high

Biblical Passages

  • 1 Kings 3:1Solomon 'had made an end of building his own house, and the house of the LORD, and the wall of Jerusalem round about.' The 2010 announcement ties the Ophel wall to this verse. The verse names a wall around Jerusalem; it does not describe its route, gate plan or towers.
  • 1 Kings 9:15, 24; 11:27Solomon builds 'the wall of Jerusalem' and the Millo with levied labour. Same caution: the text gives no location on the Ophel.
  • 2 Chronicles 27:3; 33:14Jotham: 'on the wall of Ophel he built much' (KJV); Manasseh encircles the Ophel with a very high wall. These are the only verses naming building on the Ophel wall, and they are 8th-7th century kings, the period Finkelstein et al. assign to the fortification's use (our observation).
  • Nehemiah 3:26-27The 'wall of Ophel' and 'the great tower that lieth out' in the Persian-period repair list; a tower on the Ophel is part of the city's later description.
