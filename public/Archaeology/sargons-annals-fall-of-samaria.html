Sargon's Annals: the Fall of Samaria

Sargon II's claims to have captured Samaria and deported 27,290 people · 722-720 BCE

Overview

Several of Sargon II's inscriptions claim that he conquered Samaria, capital of the kingdom of Israel, deported its population, took chariots into his army, resettled the city with people from elsewhere and made it an Assyrian province. The fullest versions are the Display Inscription from Khorsabad, the Khorsabad Annals (Room II) and the Nimrud Prism. Shorter titularies on bull colossi and pavements call him 'conqueror of Samaria and all of Bīt-Ḫumria (Israel)'.

The numbers are not uniform. Exemplars of the Display Inscription give 27,290, 27,280 and apparently 24,280 deportees with 50 chariots. The Nimrud Prism has '[20?]+27,280', read tentatively as 47,280, with 200 chariots. In the Annals of Room II the whole passage, number included, is restored from those other texts.

2 Kings 17:3-6 and 18:9-10 name Shalmaneser as the king who besieged Samaria for three years, and say 'they took it'. The Babylonian Chronicle credits Shalmaneser V with destroying a city whose name is usually read Šamara'in. Sargon, who came to the throne within weeks of Shalmaneser's death, claims the conquest for himself.

In its own words

(23) From the beginning of my rule (722 B.C.) to the fifteenth (707 B.C.) of my years of reign I accomplished the defeat of Khumbanigash of Elam in the environs (?) of Dur-ilu; I besieged and captured Samaria; I carried away (24) 27,290 of its inhabitants, I collected there 50 chariots; the remainder of them I permitted to retain their goods (?), put my governors over them, and upon them the tribute of former kings (25) I laid.R. W. Rogers, Cuneiform Parallels to the Old Testament (New York, 1912), p. 331 (Sargon's 'General' = Display Inscription ll. 23-25)

What the evidence shows

  • Display Inscription (RINAP 2 no. 7) l. 23b-25: URU.sa-me-ri-na al-me ak-šud ... 27 LIM 2 ME 90 UN.MEŠ a-šib ŠÀ-šú áš-lu-la 50 GIŠ.GIGIR.MEŠ ... 'I surrounded (and) conquered the city Samaria. I carried off as booty 27,290 of its inhabitants, conscripted fifty chariots from among them...'
  • RINAP 2 note: 'Various exemplars of text no. 7 line 24 have 27,290, 27,280, and apparently 24,280.'
  • Khorsabad Annals, Room II (RINAP 2 no. 1) ll. 11-17: only fragments survive ('LÚ.URU.sa-me]-ri-na-a-a', 'people of Samaria'); the conquest and the '27,280/27,290' figure are restored from nos. 7 and 74. RINAP 2 describes these lines as 'heavily restored from the following texts'.
  • Nimrud Prism (RINAP 2 no. 74) iv 25-41: the Samarians had allied with a king hostile to Sargon; '[20?]+27,280 people, together with their chariots and the gods who helped them' taken as booty; 200 chariots conscripted; 'I restored the city Samaria and made (it) greater than before. I brought there people from the lands that I had conquered'.
  • Frame (RINAP 2) on the Prism figure: 'For the proposal that the number in the Nimrud Prism was 47,280, see Na'aman, NABU 2000 ... but note that his reference to the Khorsabad Annals ... is misleading since the number there is totally restored.'
  • Annals and Display Inscription l. 33: in Sargon's 2nd year Ilu-bi'dī of Hamath 'incited the cities Arpad, Ṣimirra, Damascus, (and) Samaria to rebel against me'.
  • Room XIV Display Inscription (no. 8) l. 15: 'I plundered (ašlul) the city Šinuḫtu, the city Samaria, and all of the land Bīt-Ḫumria'. Frame notes that ašlul can also mean 'I carried off as booty/captives'.
  • Babylonian Chronicle (BM 92502) i 27-28, as translated by Rogers 1912: 'On the twenty-fifth day of Tebet Shalmaneser took his seat on the throne in Assyria, and the city of Shabarain was destroyed.' The copy was made in the 22nd year of Darius (Wiseman 1956: 500/499 BCE if Darius I).

Open questions

  • Who took Samaria, Shalmaneser V or Sargon II? For Shalmaneser: 2 Kings 17:3-6 and 18:9-10, and the Babylonian Chronicle, which RINAP 1 summarizes as recording that Shalmaneser 'conquered or devastated Samaria'. For Sargon: his inscriptions repeatedly claim the conquest, the deportation and the resettlement. Hayim Tadmor (JCS 12, 1958) credited the 722 capture to Shalmaneser and put Sargon's own action against Samaria in 720, with the Hamath revolt. Bob Becking (The Fall of Samaria, 1992) argued for two captures, one by each king. Eckart Frahm (The Last Days of the Kingdom of Israel, 2018, pp. 55-86) re-examined what Sargon's inscriptions attest. (These positions are summarized from the literature; the three works are cited but not summarized here.) RINAP 2 (Frame 2021) leaves the question open: 'Exactly when Samaria fell ... [has] been [a matter] of intense scholarly discussion.'
  • Is 'Shabarain/Šamara'in' Samaria? Rogers (1912) printed the Chronicle's city name as 'Shabarain'. The later RINAP 1 overview reads the same line as Shalmaneser having 'conquered or devastated Samaria'. The Chronicle names no region, so the identification rests on the reading of the name and on the biblical parallel.
  • How many were deported: 27,290, 27,280, 24,280 or 47,280? The Display Inscription exemplars disagree with one another, the Prism's first digit is broken, and the Annals' figure is a modern restoration. Nadav Na'aman (NABU 2000) proposed 47,280 for the Prism. The texts themselves do not settle it.

The Find

  • DateEvents: 'at the beginning of my reign' (Sargon's accession year, 722 BCE) and/or his 2nd regnal year (720 BCE). Texts written c. 720s-706 BCE.
  • How datedSargon's own texts place the Samaria action 'at the beginning of my reign' (Annals, text no. 1 l. 12b, restored) and group Samaria with the Hamath-led revolt of his 2nd regnal year (720). The Babylonian Chronicle (BM 92502) records that Shalmaneser V died in Ṭebētu of his 5th year and Sargon took the throne on 12 Ṭebētu (722 BCE), and that under Shalmaneser a city read Šamara'in (older reading Shabara'in) was destroyed.
  • DiscoveredKhorsabad texts 1843-1854 (Botta, Place); Nimrud Prism fragments from the British excavations at Nimrud, published by C. J. Gadd in Iraq 16 (1954), P.-É. Botta and V. Place (Khorsabad); British excavations at Nimrud (prism), Khorsabad (Dūr-Šarrukīn) palace slabs and pavements; Nimrud (Kalḫu), clay prism fragments
  • Now heldSeveral objects. The Khorsabad Annals of Room II survive mostly in Botta's copies (originals lost in the Tigris in 1855 or reburied); the Display Inscription is known from five exemplars; the Nimrud Prism fragments were excavated at Nimrud (museum numbers Not confirmed in a published museum record).
  • MaterialStone wall slabs and pavement slabs (Khorsabad); clay prism (Nimrud)
  • ScriptNeo-Assyrian cuneiform
  • LanguageAkkadian

Biblical Passages

  • 2 Kgs 17:3-6Shalmaneser besieges Samaria three years; 'in the ninth year of Hoshea the king of Assyria took Samaria, and carried Israel away into Assyria, and placed them in Halah and in Habor by the river of Gozan, and in the cities of the Medes'. Sargon's texts record the deportation but name no destinations for the Samarians; they name Sargon, not Shalmaneser, as conqueror.
  • 2 Kgs 18:9-11Repeats the three-year siege by Shalmaneser and 'at the end of three years they took it'. The plural 'they' leaves the capturing king unnamed; the Babylonian Chronicle's Shalmaneser line and Sargon's claims are the two external witnesses.
  • 2 Kgs 17:24'The king of Assyria brought men from Babylon, and from Cuthah, and from Ava, and from Hamath, and from Sepharvaim, and placed them in the cities of Samaria.' Sargon: 'I brought there people from the lands that I had conquered.' A direct correspondence in policy; Sargon's text does not list the origins.
