Tiglath-pileser III Records

Assyrian royal inscriptions naming Menahem, Pekah, Hoshea and Ahaz · 744-727 BCE

Overview

Tiglath-pileser III (745-727 BCE) turned Assyria from a kingdom exacting tribute into an empire of annexed provinces. His inscriptions come in two forms: annals carved in sequence across slabs of his palace at Kalḫu (Nimrud), and 'summary' inscriptions that list conquests geographically. Many slabs were shifted or broken in antiquity and in the 19th century, and several key texts survive only in fragments or in early copies. RINAP 1 (Tadmor and Yamada, 2011) is the current edition.

Four Israelite and Judahite kings appear. 'Menahem of the city Samaria' is on the tribute list of 738 (annals and the Iran stele). Summary Inscription 4 says of Bīt-Ḫumrî (Israel) that Peqah (Pekah) was killed and Hoshea set up as king, paying 10 talents of gold. The summary tablet K 3751 lists 'Jehoahaz of the land Judah', the full form of the biblical name Ahaz, among the western tributaries. The texts also record the annexation of Damascus and of Gilead and Abil-šiṭṭi 'on the border of Bīt-Ḫumrî'.

In its own words

816. The land of Bit-Humria ...... all of its people, together with their goods I carried off to Assyria. Pakaha, their king they deposed and I placed Ausi' (Hoshea) over them as king. 10 talents of gold, x talents of silver, as their tribute I received from them and to Assyria I carried them.D. D. Luckenbill, Ancient Records of Assyria and Babylonia I (Chicago, 1926), §816, p. 293

What the evidence shows

  • Annals (RINAP 1 no. 14 l. 10, slab BM 124961 (+) Detroit 50.32): ... mme-ni-ḫi-im-me URU.sa-me-ri-na-a-a, 'Menahem of the city Samaria', in a list of western rulers paying tribute alongside Rezin (Raḫiānu) of Damascus and Hiram of Tyre.
  • Iran stele (RINAP 1 no. 35 iii 5, IMJ 74.049.0096a+b): the same tribute list, with 'Menahem of the land Samaria'.
  • Summary Inscription 4 (RINAP 1 no. 42 ll. 15'b-19'a): 'As for the land Bīt-Ḫumrî (Israel), I brought to Assyria [...] (and) all of its people ... [I/they] killed Peqah, their king, and I placed Hoshea [as king o]ver them. I received from them ten talents of gold, ... talents of silver'.
  • RINAP 1 note to no. 42 l. 17': the verb before Peqah is damaged: '[i]-du-ku-ma' ('they killed') or '[a]-du-uk-ma' ('I killed') are both possible restorations, and G. Smith's draft shows space for more signs than either. Luckenbill (1926) rendered 'they deposed'.
  • Summary tablet ND 4301+4305 (RINAP 1 no. 49 rev. 9-11): '[I conquered the land Bīt-Ḫumr]î (Israel) in [its] entirety' (restored), '[I placed Hoshea as] king over them', and tribute brought to the city Sarrabānu.
  • Summary tablet K 3751 (RINAP 1 no. 47 rev. 11'): mia-ú-ḫa-zi KUR.ia-ú-da-a-a, 'Jehoahaz of the land Judah', among the western tributaries (with Mitinti of Ashkelon, Qauš-malaka of Edom, Hanūnu of Gaza).
  • No. 42 l. 5'b-6' and no. 49 rev. 3-4: annexation of '[...]nite, Gil[ead, and] Abil-šiṭṭi, which are (on) the border of the land Bīt-Ḫumrî', and of 'the extensive [land of Bīt-Ḫazā-i]l (Damascus)'. Where Luckenbill (1926, §815) printed 'the wide land of Naphtali', RINAP 1 restores 'the extensive land of Bīt-Ḫazā'il (Damascus)'.
  • RINAP 1 campaign table: 738 (year 7), tribute of the western kings including Rezin and Menahem; 733-732, siege and conquest of Damascus, campaigns to Gilead and Galilee (with the editors' cross-references to 2 Kgs 15:29; 16:5-9; Isa 7:1).

Open questions

  • Who killed Pekah? 2 Kings 15:30 says Hoshea son of Elah conspired against Pekah and killed him. The Assyrian verb is broken and can be restored as 'they killed' or 'I killed' (RINAP 1 no. 42 n. 13); Luckenbill (1926) read 'they deposed'. The text is too damaged to say whether Tiglath-pileser claimed the killing himself.
  • 'Naphtali' or 'Bīt-Ḫazā'ili'? The older reading (Luckenbill 1926) had Tiglath-pileser annexing 'the wide land of Naphtali', which matches 2 Kgs 15:29 ('all the land of Naphtali'). RINAP 1 restores the damaged passage as the land of Damascus (Bīt-Ḫazā'ili). The 2 Kgs 15:29 parallel for Galilee now rests on the Gilead/Abil-šiṭṭi and Galilee entries, not on the word 'Naphtali'.
  • Is 'Pul' (2 Kgs 15:19) Tiglath-pileser? 2 Kgs 15:19-20 has Menahem pay 1,000 talents of silver to 'Pul king of Assyria'; 1 Chr 5:26 names 'Pul king of Assyria' and 'Tilgath-pilneser king of Assyria' together. The Assyrian texts call the king only Tiglath-pileser and give no amount for Menahem's payment. Identifying Pul with Tiglath-pileser is our reading here; no Assyrian royal inscription cited here uses the name Pul.

The Find

  • DateReign of Tiglath-pileser III, 745-727 BCE (RINAP 1 dates his inscriptions 744-727). Menahem's payment falls in 738 BCE; the Damascus-Israel campaigns in 734-732 BCE.
  • How datedThe annals are arranged by regnal year (palû), anchored to the Assyrian Eponym Chronicle, which says he became king in Ayyāru of 745 after a revolt in Kalḫu in 746. RINAP 1's campaign table puts the western tribute list with 'Menahem of Samaria' in year 7 (738) and the siege and annexation of Damascus, with Gilead and Galilee, in 733-732. Summary inscriptions are undated retrospectives, so their events are dated by the annals and eponym entries.
  • Discovered1845-1847 (Layard's excavations at Nimrud); further inscriptions in the 1950s British excavations at Nimrud (published by D. J. Wiseman in Iraq 13, 1951; 18, 1956; 26, 1964); the stele with provenience given as Luristan (Iran), one fragment in a private collection, Austen Henry Layard (Nimrud); later British excavations at Nimrud under Max Mallowan, Nimrud (ancient Kalḫu), Central Palace slabs (many later reused or displaced); a stele reportedly from Luristan, western Iran
  • Now heldDispersed. Examples: Annals slab BM 124961 (+) Detroit Institute of Arts 50.32 (RINAP 1 no. 14); Summary Inscription tablet ND 4301+4305 (no. 49); tablet K 3751 (no. 47); Iran stele IMJ 74.049.0096a (+) 0096b, Israel Museum, plus a fragment in a private collection (no. 35).
  • MaterialStone wall slabs with reliefs (annals and summary texts), a stone stele, clay tablets (summary inscriptions)
  • ScriptNeo-Assyrian cuneiform
  • LanguageAkkadian

Biblical Passages

  • 2 Kgs 15:19-20Menahem pays tribute to 'Pul king of Assyria'. Assyrian annals and the Iran stele list 'Menahem of Samaria' among tributaries in 738 BCE. The Bible gives 1,000 talents of silver; the Assyrian texts give no figure for Menahem.
  • 2 Kgs 15:29In Pekah's days Tiglath-pileser took Ijon, Abel-beth-maacah, Janoah, Kedesh, Hazor, Gilead, Galilee and Naphtali and deported the people. The Assyrian summaries record annexing Gilead and Abil-šiṭṭi on Israel's border and carrying Bīt-Ḫumrî's people to Assyria.
  • 2 Kgs 15:30Hoshea kills Pekah and reigns. Assyria: Peqah killed (subject damaged) and 'I placed Hoshea as king over them'. Both put Hoshea on the throne in place of Pekah; the Assyrian text claims the appointment.
  • 2 Kgs 16:5-10; Isa 7:1-9Rezin of Damascus and Pekah attack Ahaz; Ahaz sends tribute to Tiglath-pileser, who takes Damascus. Assyrian texts list 'Jehoahaz (Ahaz) of Judah' as a tributary, and record the siege and annexation of Damascus (733-732).
