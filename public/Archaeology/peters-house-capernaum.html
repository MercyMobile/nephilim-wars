House of Peter, Capernaum

Late Hellenistic house under a 5th-century octagonal church · venerated as Peter's

Overview

At Capernaum, south of the white limestone synagogue, the Franciscans excavated a Byzantine octagonal church from 1968. Beneath it they found a cluster of simple basalt houses around courtyards. One room in that cluster was singled out, plastered and repeatedly rebuilt as a place of gathering.

The Custody's summary of the results: the house was 'built in the Late Hellenistic period'. 'In the late first century A.D. it was changed into a domus-ecclesia', which in the 4th century was enlarged and enclosed by a wall. 'In the second half of the fifth century A.D. an octagonal church was built upon the house of St. Peter' and remained in use until the 7th century. The walls produced many fragments of painted plaster and graffiti with Christian symbols and invocations.

No inscription in the house names Peter as owner. The identification rests on the room's long special treatment, on the pilgrim graffiti (published by E. Testa, Cafarnao IV, 1972), and on the Gospel statement that Jesus stayed at Simon's house in Capernaum.

What the evidence shows

  • A residential quarter of basalt houses under the octagon; one room was repeatedly replastered and set apart (Custody summary; Corbo's publications).
  • A 4th-century enclosure wall around the venerated house (Custody summary).
  • A 5th-century octagonal church built directly over the venerated room, centred on it (Custody summary).
  • Painted plaster fragments and pilgrims' graffiti with Christian symbols and invocations on the walls (Custody summary; Testa 1972).
  • Excavation seasons: 19 seasons 1968–1986 (Corbo, Loffreda) and 4 campaigns 2000–2003 (Loffreda).

Open questions

  • When did Christian use of the room begin? The excavators (Corbo; Custody summary) put its conversion into a domus-ecclesia in the late 1st century CE, pointing to the replastering and the graffiti. Taylor (Christians and the Holy Places, OUP 1993, ch. 12, pp. 268–294) directly tests the excavators' claim that the octagon was built on a Jewish-Christian house-church, in her wider critique of the Bagatti–Testa hypothesis. Cirafesi (Capernaum, Fortress 2024) returns to the question with a chapter on 'Jewish Christ-Followers in Capernaum before the Fourth Century'.
  • The graffiti readings: Testa (Cafarnao IV, 1972) published readings that include names and invocations. How many of them can be securely read, and whether any name Peter, is disputed in later discussion (Strange & Shanks, BAR 1982, discuss Testa's volume). The full texts were not available for this entry.

Corrected from the earlier edition of this page
  • Old subtitle '1st century CE house' is too narrow. The Custody's excavation summary says the house was built in the Late Hellenistic period.
  • Old claim that the find 'provides tangible evidence of the existence of Peter' goes beyond what was found. No inscription names the owner. The finds show a room given special treatment and later marked by Christian graffiti and churches. The link to Peter is the tradition carried by that treatment.
  • Old claim it 'aligns precisely with the biblical description of Peter's residence'. The Gospels say only that Simon and Andrew's house was in Capernaum and was entered after the synagogue (Mark 1:29). They give no location or description to match 'precisely'.
  • Old dimension 'Approximately 10 meters square' and material 'Stone and mudbrick' are unsourced. The houses are basalt-built (Custody). No measurement was verified, so the size is dropped.
  • Old phrase 'testament to the enduring legacy of ancient Jerusalem' is wrong. Capernaum is in Galilee.
  • Old 'venerated as Peter's since 1st century' states the excavators' interpretation as fact. The late-1st-century date for the domus-ecclesia is their reading and is disputed (Taylor 1993).

The Find

  • DateHouse built in the Late Hellenistic period; converted to a place of assembly (excavators: late 1st century CE); enclosed and enlarged in the 4th century; octagonal church second half of the 5th century CE, used to the 7th century
  • How datedStratified excavation by the Franciscan Custody (Corbo and Loffreda) 1968–1986 and 2000–2003; phase dates as published by the Custody. The 1st-century conversion date is the excavators' reading.
  • Discovered1968 (excavation of the house under the octagon), Virgilio Corbo and Stanislao Loffreda, Studium Biblicum Franciscanum, Franciscan property at Capernaum (Kfar Nahum), north shore of the Sea of Galilee, south of the limestone synagogue
  • Now heldIn situ, Capernaum; since 1990 under a modern memorial church (architect Ildo Avetta) with a glass floor

Biblical Passages

  • Mark 1:21, 29–31After the Capernaum synagogue, Jesus goes 'into the house of Simon and Andrew' and heals Simon's mother-in-law. The excavated house lies south of the synagogue on the same Franciscan property. It is in the right town, but the text does not give the house's position.
  • Matt 8:14–15; Luke 4:38–39Parallel accounts of Peter's house and his mother-in-law.
  • Mark 2:1–4Jesus 'at home' in Capernaum, and the roof dug through to lower a paralysed man. The excavated houses are single-storey basalt structures with light roofs. The dig shows that type of house; it does not identify this episode.
  • Matt 4:13Jesus settles in Capernaum.
