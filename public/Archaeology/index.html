Primeval History (Genesis 1–11)

  • Durupınar Formation

    Boat-shaped landform near Doğubayazıt, Ağrı, Turkey · proposed as Noah's Ark · first permitted deep core drilling 2026
    Passages: Gen 6:15, Gen 8:4, Gen 6:14
    Now held: In situ. Collins (2016) gives Google Earth centre 39°26'26"N, 44°14'0.5"E, elevation 6,625 ft (c. 2,020 m); Collins & Fasold (1996) said c. 7,000 ft. Protected by decision no. 3657 (17 Sep 1987) of Turkey's High Council for Immovable Cultural and Natural Property as a natural site and open-air museum; visitor centre on the adjacent slope.

    Outline: pointed-oval ridge; length measured 157 m (515 ft) by Wyatt and later workers; width given as 50 m maximum (district governorate; Avcı 2007); relief given as 4 m (Avcı 2007) to 13 m (governorate).

Patriarchal Period (Genesis)

  • Beni Hasan: the Aamu Procession in the Tomb of Khnumhotep II

    Painted procession of 37 Asiatics in a Middle Kingdom rock-cut tomb · Year 6 of Senusret II
    Passages: Genesis 12:10; 42-47, Genesis 37:25-28
    Now held: In situ, Tomb No. 3 (BH 3), Beni Hasan, Egypt; north wall of the main chamber, row 3

    Number: 37 Aamu, written twice (the scribe's document and the caption over the group) (Newberry 1893 p. 69).

  • Ebla Tablets (Royal Archives of Palace G)

    Third-millennium cuneiform archive from the royal palace at Tell Mardikh, Syria
    Passages: Genesis 10-11; 14
    Now held: Syria (Syrian national collections). The specific museums holding the tablets are not confirmed from a primary source.

    Find-spot: Palace G on the acropolis of Tell Mardikh; the archives' finding and arrangement are the subject of Matthiae, Studia Eblaitica 11 (2025) 1-20.

  • Egyptian Execration Texts

    Middle Kingdom ritual curse lists naming foreign rulers and places, written on pots and prisoner figurines
    Passages: Genesis 12:6; 33:18-20; 34, Genesis 14:18; Joshua 10:1; 2 Samuel 5:6-9, Judges 1:18; 14:19
    Now held: Ägyptisches Museum, Berlin (bowls); Egyptian Museum, Cairo (Saqqara figurines, moved there in 1938); Musées Royaux d'Art et d'Histoire, Brussels (figurines)

    Asiatic princes list formula: 'Le prince de [pays] (appelé) [nom]' (Posener 1940, section E).

  • Mari Tablets (Royal Archives of Mari)

    Old Babylonian palace archive from Tell Hariri on the Middle Euphrates, Syria · early 18th century BCE (middle chronology)
    Passages: 1 Samuel 9-10; 2 Samuel 7; Jeremiah; Ezekiel, Joshua 11:1-13; Judges 4:2, Genesis 11-31
    Now held: Syria and France (Louvre) hold Mari material; the present repository of the archive tablets is not confirmed from a primary source

    Excavation began with Parrot's first campaign, winter 1933-34 (Parrot, Syria 16, 1935, 1-28 and 117-140); second campaign winter 1934-35 (Syria 17, 1936, 1-31).

  • Nuzi Tablets

    Private and official cuneiform records from a Hurrian town near Kirkuk, Iraq · 15th-14th century BCE
    Passages: Genesis 16:1-4; 30:1-13, Genesis 15:2-4, Genesis 31:19-35
    Now held: Divided between the Iraq Museum and the American partner institutions of the joint expeditions; the present repositories of specific tablets are not confirmed in a published museum record

    HSS V 67 (Speiser text 2): an adoption tied to a marriage. If the wife Gilimninu is childless she must provide her husband a Lullu woman; the concubine's children may not be expelled; if Gilimninu has children the husband may not take another wife (Speiser 1930).

  • Papyrus Brooklyn 35.1446

    Late Middle Kingdom hieratic register; the verso lists a Theban household's servants, many labelled 'Asiatic' · 13th Dynasty
    Passages: Genesis 39:1; 46-47; Exodus 1:1-14, Exodus 1:15
    Now held: Brooklyn Museum, New York, 35.1446

    Verso date: Year 1 of Sobekhotep III (PNM record, citing Hayes 1955 pp. 87-125, pls. VIII-XIV).

  • Tall el-Hammam Destruction Layer

    Middle Bronze Age city in the southern Jordan Valley ended by a burned destruction layer · c. 1650 BCE (radiocarbon); airburst paper retracted 2025
    Passages: Genesis 13:10-12, Genesis 19:24-28
    Now held: In situ, Jordan

    Destruction 'debris matrix' up to ~1.5 m thick across most of the tall, capped in places by a thin laminated 'blow-over layer' with plaster fragments, limestone spherules and charcoal (Bunch et al. 2021).

  • Tel Shimron cultic megastructure and favissa

    Middle Bronze Age monumental complex on the acropolis of a Jezreel Valley city · c. 1750-1650 BCE
    Passages: Joshua 11:1, Joshua 12:20, Joshua 19:15
    Now held: In situ (Tel Shimron, Jezreel Valley); finds with the excavation / Israel Antiquities Authority

    Grid 33 (eastern acropolis): 'walls built of huge boulders supported a multi-story structure'; large mudbrick walls parallel to the slope; 'tens of thousands of bones and pottery fragments' between them (Master and Martin, HA 2021).

Exodus & Conquest Period

  • Ai Excavations

    Et-Tell's Early Bronze city, its long gap, and the search for Joshua's Ai
    Passages: Josh 7:2; 8:9, 12, Josh 8:28, Josh 8:29, Josh 7:3; 8:25
    Now held: In situ, et-Tell and Khirbet el-Maqatir, West Bank

    Et-Tell: an Early Bronze city (EB IB to EB IIIB, c. 3300-2400 BCE) with walls, citadel and sanctuary, ending in destruction and abandonment (Marquet-Krause 1935; Callaway & Schoonover, BASOR 207, 1972; NEAEHL summary).

  • Amarna Letters

    Cuneiform diplomatic archive of Amenhotep III and Akhenaten, with letters from Jerusalem's ruler · 14th century BCE
    Passages: Josh 10:1-5; 15:63; Judg 1:21, Josh 10:12; 15:44; 1 Sam 23:1-13, Gen 14:13; Exod 1-3 ('ibri / Hebrews), Judg 9 (Shechem); Josh 24
    Now held: Dispersed. Abdi-Heba's Jerusalem letters EA 285-290 are in the Vorderasiatisches Museum, Berlin (EA 285 = VAT 1601; EA 286 = VAT 1642; EA 287 = VAT 1644; EA 288 = VAT 1643; EA 289 = VAT 1645 + VAT 2709; EA 290 = VAT 1646). Others are in the British Museum (e.g., EA 252 = BM 29844), Cairo and elsewhere.

    Found 1887-88 at Tell el-Amarna; Barton (1916, p. 35) gives 'nearly four hundred' tablets 'inscribed in the Babylonian language and characters'.

  • Berlin Pedestal Relief

    Granite statue-base fragment with three bound Asiatic captives and name rings · Egyptian, date disputed
    Passages: Merenptah Stele (context); Judg 1:1-2:5, 1 Kgs 6:1, Josh 13:3; Judg 1:18
    Now held: Ägyptisches Museum und Papyrussammlung, Staatliche Museen zu Berlin, inv. ÄM 21687

    Grey granite slab, 46 × 39.5 cm, sawn to 7.5 cm thick, bearing three bound Asiatic captives with name rings (van der Veen et al. 2010, p. 15).

  • Deir 'Alla Plaster Inscription (Balaam son of Beor)

    Ink-on-plaster text naming 'Balaam son of Beor, a seer of the gods' · c. 800 BCE · Tell Deir 'Alla, Jordan
    Passages: Num 22:5; 24:3-4, 15-16, Josh 13:22; Mic 6:5; Deut 23:4-5
    Now held: Amman, Jordan. A Wikipedia pointer says the Jordan Archaeological Museum; this is not confirmed in a published museum record.

    Found in 1967 by the Dutch expedition under H. J. Franken; editio princeps Hoftijzer and van der Kooij 1976.

  • Gezer Calendar

    Inscribed limestone tablet listing the farming year · Gezer · Iron Age (date by script only)
    Passages: Exod 23:16; 34:22, Ruth 1:22; 2:23; 2 Sam 21:9, Josh 2:6, 1 Kgs 9:15-17
    Now held: Istanbul Archaeology Museums (Gezer lay in Ottoman territory in 1908)

    Material: limestone tablet with the text scratched in. It is not an ostracon.

  • Hazor Destruction Layer

    Fiery end of the largest Late Bronze Age city in Canaan · 13th century BCE
    Passages: Josh 11:10-11, 13, Deut 7:5; 12:3, Judg 4:2, 23-24, 1 Kgs 9:15
    Now held: In situ, Tel Hazor National Park, Israel

    A destruction by fire at the end of the Late Bronze Age affected both the upper and lower cities (Selz excavation project; Ebeling 2003).

  • Ipuwer Papyrus

    Egyptian lament of a land turned upside down · New Kingdom copy of an older poem
    Passages: Exod 7:17-21, Exod 9:3-6, Exod 12:35-36, Ps 78:44; 105:29
    Now held: Rijksmuseum van Oudheden, Leiden, Netherlands: Papyrus Leiden I 344 (recto = Ipuwer; verso = hymns to a sun god)

    Object: Papyrus Leiden I 344, 378 cm × 18 cm, inscribed on both sides. The recto has 17 pages of about 14 lines each (Gardiner 1909, Introduction §1).

  • Jericho Excavations

    Tell es-Sultan · the burned Bronze Age city and the dispute over its date
    Passages: Josh 6:20, 24, Josh 2:1-6, 15, Josh 6:17-18, 24, Josh 6:26; 1 Kgs 16:34
    Now held: In situ, Tell es-Sultan, Jericho. The site was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List on 17 September 2023 (Nigro & Yasine 2024).

    The last MB city (Sultan IVc / City IV) was destroyed by fire. Walls and floors were burned and mudbrick collapsed against the stone revetment (Garstang; Kenyon; Nigro & Yasine 2024 §§5-6).

  • Jerubba'al inscription, Khirbet al-Ra'i

    Proto-Canaanite ink inscription on a small jug · late 12th - early 11th century BCE
    Passages: Judges 6:31-32, Judges 7:1; 8:29, 35; 9:1-57, 2 Samuel 11:21
    Now held: Israel Antiquities Authority (photographed by Dafna Gazit, IAA); no museum or inventory number located

    Found in Silo A310, Area A, Khirbet al-Ra'i: two sherds on the last day of the 2019 season, a third in 2020 when the whole silo was sifted (Rollston et al. 2021: 3-4).

  • Merneptah Stele

    Egyptian victory hymn naming Israel · Year 5 of Merneptah (c. 1208 BCE)
    Passages: Judges 1-5; Joshua, Judges 1:29; 1 Kings 9:16, Exodus 1-15
    Now held: Egyptian Museum, Cairo (JE 31408)

    Text dated Year 5 of Merneptah, 3rd month of Shemu, day 3 (Breasted ARE III §607).

  • Mount Ebal Structure (el-Burnat) and the Folded Lead Object

    Late Bronze / Iron I stone installation on Mount Ebal, and a disputed folded lead object from its spoil heap
    Passages: Deut 11:29; 27:1-8, Josh 8:30-35, Deut 27:15-26, Exod 20:26
    Now held: Structure in situ on Mount Ebal, northern West Bank. Lead object: held by the Stripling team; no museum inventory number published.

    Rectangular Stratum I structure 8.75-9.00 x 7.00 m with a ramp on the west side, over a 2 m round Stratum II structure at its centre (Stripling et al. 2023 p. 3, summarising Zertal).

  • Shiloh (Khirbet Seilun / Tel Shiloh) excavations

    Multi-period hilltop site north of Jerusalem · excavated 1926-1963, 1981-1984, 2017-2026
    Passages: Josh 18:1, 1 Sam 1:9; 3:3, 1 Sam 4:12-18, Exod 26:15-25; 27:13
    Now held: In situ (site). Finds from the ABR seasons: repository not stated in the sources read.

    Three restorable Middle Bronze Age pithoi in Area D (the favissa area). Two held charred olives and one held wheat. Radiocarbon testing of the contents is planned (ABR, 22 June 2026).

  • Soleb 'Land of the Shasu of Yhw' Inscription

    Prisoner-name cartouche in Amenhotep III's temple of Amun-Ra at Soleb, Sudan · c. 1400-1385 BCE
    Passages: Exodus 3:15; 6:3, Deuteronomy 33:2; Judges 5:4; Habakkuk 3:3
    Now held: Column IV N4: in situ at Soleb. Wall block SB 69: in storage in Sudan (antiquities department), per Kennedy 2019

    Column IV N4 names four Shasu groups: twr byl, yhwꜣ, smt, bt ꜥn[...] (Kennedy 2019, citing Leclant, Les Fouilles de Soleb, p. 214).

United Monarchy Period

  • Ezion-geber and Tell el-Kheleifeh

    Iron Age casemate fortress at the head of the Gulf of Aqaba, identified with Ezion-geber · 8th–6th centuries BCE
    Passages: 1 Kgs 9:26–28, 1 Kgs 22:48; 2 Chr 20:35–37, 2 Kgs 14:22; 16:6, Num 33:35–36; Deut 2:8
    Now held: In situ, Aqaba region, Jordan

    A casemate fortress, later expanded into a fortified settlement (Pratico 1985).

  • Gezer Six-Chambered Gate

    Iron Age city gate, Field III, Tel Gezer · Stratum 8/VIII
    Passages: 1 Kgs 9:15-17, 1 Kgs 9:16
    Now held: In situ, Tel Gezer National Park, Israel

    Plan: central passage flanked by three chambers per side (a 'four-entryway' gate in Dever's wording), joined to a casemate wall.

  • Gezer Water System

    Rock-cut stepped tunnel to a spring cave · Middle Bronze Age
    Passages: 1 Kgs 9:15-17, Josh 10:12-13
    Now held: In situ, Tel Gezer National Park, Israel

    Keyhole-shaped entrance pit, 34 ft 6 in long, 7 ft 5 in wide at north end and 14 ft 6 in at south; steps cut in its floor (Macalister I, p. 258).

  • Hazor Six-Chambered Gate

    Iron Age gate and casemate wall, upper city of Tel Hazor · Stratum X
    Passages: 1 Kgs 9:15, 2 Kgs 15:29
    Now held: In situ, Tel Hazor National Park, Israel

    Six-chamber plan (three chambers each side of the passage) joined to a casemate wall (Yadin; summarised by Madain Project from the excavation reports).

  • Khirbet Qeiyafa Fortifications

    Casemate wall and two four-chambered gates · late 11th-early 10th c. BCE
    Passages: 1 Sam 17:1-2, 52, Josh 15:36
    Now held: In situ, Khirbet Qeiyafa, Elah Valley, Israel

    Casemate wall with megalithic stones; small-stone Hellenistic additions at the top (Millard 2011, p. 4).

  • Khirbet Qeiyafa Ostracon

    Five-line ink inscription on a jar sherd · c. 1000 BCE
    Passages: 1 Sam 17, Exod 22:21-24; Deut 24:17
    Now held: Studied at the Institute of Archaeology, Hebrew University of Jerusalem (project site). Current display/inventory number Not confirmed in a published museum record.

    Ink on a jar sherd, max. 15 x 16.5 cm; lines possibly missing above line 1 and at the right edge (Millard 2011, p. 4).

  • Large Stone Structure

    Massive walls at the summit of the City of David, Jerusalem · date disputed (Iron I to Hellenistic)
    Passages: 2 Sam 5:11, 2 Sam 5:17, Jer 37:3; 38:1
    Now held: In situ, City of David, Jerusalem

    Walls 6-8 ft wide of large stones (Mazar, BAR 32.1, 2006).

  • Megiddo Stables

    Pillared buildings with stone troughs, Stratum IV · date disputed (10th-8th c. BCE)
    Passages: 1 Kgs 9:19, 1 Kgs 4:26; 2 Chr 9:25, 1 Kgs 10:26-29
    Now held: In situ, Tel Megiddo National Park, Israel

    Southern compound: five units facing a courtyard 55 m on a side, overall 64 x 85 m, with a water tank near the courtyard's centre (Lamon & Shipton 1939, p. 32).

  • Mesha Stele

    Victory and building inscription of Mesha king of Moab · 9th c. BCE
    Passages: 2 Kgs 3:4-27, 2 Kgs 1:1, 1 Kgs 16:21-28, Num 32:34-38; Josh 13:15-23
    Now held: Musée du Louvre, Paris, Department of Near Eastern Antiquities, AO 5066 (also MNB 752); Sully wing, room 303 per the collections record

    Basalt, H 125 x W 69 x D 37 cm; incomplete, fragmented, reassembled with restoration (Louvre AO 5066).

  • Ophel Pithos Inscription

    Letters incised on a storage jar before firing, Ophel, Jerusalem · Iron IIA context
    Passages: 1 Kgs 10:1-13; 2 Chr 9:1-12, Exod 30:34, 2 Sam 5:7-9; 1 Kgs 3:1
    Now held: Not confirmed in a published museum record.

    Seven pithoi, neckless type, Early Iron IIA; one inscribed (Vainstub 2023, citing Mazar 2015: 467-468).

  • Radiocarbon Chronology of Iron Age Jerusalem (Regev et al., PNAS 2024)

    103 radiocarbon dates from the City of David and the Giv'ati Parking Lot · published April 2024
    Passages: 2 Sam 5:6-9; 1 Kgs 3-11, Amos 1:1; Zech 14:5, 2 Kgs 15-16; 2 Chr 26:9, 2 Kgs 25:8-10
    Now held: Data and supplementary appendix published with PNAS 121(19) e2321024121; the excavation areas are in situ in Jerusalem

    103 archaeological radiocarbon measurements plus 100 single-ring measurements from dated Irish oak (PNAS 2024).

  • Solomon's Wall (Ophel fortification)

    Iron Age city wall, gatehouse and tower on the Ophel, south of the Temple Mount · date disputed (10th vs 8th-7th century BCE)
    Passages: 1 Kings 3:1, 1 Kings 9:15, 24; 11:27, 2 Chronicles 27:3; 33:14, Nehemiah 3:26-27
    Now held: In situ, Ophel, south of the Temple Mount, Jerusalem (outside the Old City walls; not in the Jewish Quarter)

    Wall section c. 70 m long and 6 m high; eastern section 35 m long and 5 m high (Hebrew University release, 22 Feb 2010).

  • Stepped Stone Structure

    Stone revetment on the eastern slope of the City of David, Jerusalem · date disputed (Iron I / Iron IIA / later phases)
    Passages: 2 Samuel 5:7-9; 1 Chronicles 11:5-8, 1 Kings 9:15, 24; 11:27, 2 Kings 12:20, 2 Chronicles 32:5
    Now held: In situ, City of David archaeological park, Jerusalem

    Macalister and Duncan (1926) exposed the upper edge and labelled it the 'Jebusite Ramp' (their Pl. V), with a southern 'Great Tower' and northern 'Maccabean Tower' beside it (Finkelstein et al. 2007: 143).

  • Tel Dan Stele

    Old Aramaic victory stele fragments naming the "House of David" · 9th century BCE
    Passages: 2 Kings 8:28-29; 9:14-28, 2 Kings 8:7-15, 2 Kings 10:32-33, 1 Kings 15:20
    Now held: The Israel Museum, Jerusalem (permanent display; Israel Antiquities Authority numbers 1993-3162 and 1996-125). Lent to the Jewish Museum, New York, 5 Dec 2024 - 5 Jan 2025, and to the Museum of the Bible, Washington DC, 30 Aug - 3 Nov 2025.

    Fragment A was found 21 July 1993 in the eastern part of the flagstone pavement before the outer gate in Area A; the pavement had been exposed from 1992 and the fragment's tip shows in earlier photographs (Langlois 2024: 60, citing Biran and Naveh 1993: 81).

  • Timna Copper Mines

    Copper mining and smelting district in the southern Arabah · Chalcolithic to Islamic, with a major smelting peak in the 11th-9th centuries BCE
    Passages: Deuteronomy 8:9, 1 Kings 7:13-47, 1 Kings 9:26-28; 2 Chronicles 8:17-18, 2 Samuel 8:13-14
    Now held: In situ, Timna Valley (Timna Park), southern Arabah, Israel

    Site 30 radiocarbon-dated to the early Iron Age, 11th-9th century BCE, replacing the Late Bronze/New Kingdom date (Ben-Yosef et al. 2012, as summarized by the CTV Project).

Divided Kingdom - Israel (North)

  • Black Obelisk of Shalmaneser III

    Assyrian four-sided tribute monument from Nimrud · erected c. 825 BCE
    Passages: 2 Kgs 9-10, 2 Kgs 10:32-33, 2 Kgs 8:7-15
    Now held: British Museum, London, BM 118885 (registration 1848,1104.1)

    Epigraph over register 2, transliterated: ma-da-tu šá mia-ú-a DUMU mḫu-um-ri-i ... am-ḫur-šu, literally 'payment of Iāūa son (DUMU) of Ḫumrî ... I received from him' (RIMA 3 A.0.102.88).

  • Kuntillet 'Ajrud (Horvat Teman) inscriptions

    Iron Age way-station in northeastern Sinai with ink and plaster inscriptions naming YHWH of Samaria and YHWH of Teman · c. 800 BCE
    Passages: Deut 16:21-22, 1 Kgs 16:33; 2 Kgs 13:6; 21:7; 23:4-7, Hab 3:3; Deut 33:2; Judg 5:4-5, Num 6:24-26
    Now held: Site in northeastern Sinai. The present repository of the pithoi and plaster fragments is not stated in the sources cited here.

    KA 3.1, red ink on Pithos A: '... I have [b]lessed you to YHWH of Shomron (Samaria) and to his 'asherah' (Ahituv et al. 2012: 87, as quoted by Smoak and Schniedewind 2019).

  • Kurkh Monolith (Stela of Shalmaneser III)

    Assyrian royal stela naming 'Ahab the Israelite' at Qarqar · c. 852 BCE
    Passages: 1 Kgs 16:29-22:40, 1 Kgs 20; 22:1, 1 Kgs 10:26-29; 22:34-35
    Now held: British Museum, London, BM 118884 (registration 1863,0619.2); donated by Taylor in 1863. A companion stela of Ashurnasirpal II from the same find is BM 118883.

    Line ii 91-92 transliterated: 2 LIM GIŠ.GIGIR.MEŠ 10 LIM ERIM.MEŠ šá ma-ḫa-ab-bu KUR.sir-ʾa-la-a-a: '2,000 chariots, 10,000 troops of Ahab of the land Sir'ala (Israel)' (RIMA 3 A.0.102.2).

  • Samaria Ivories (the 'Ivory House' finds)

    Carved ivory inlays from the royal quarter of Samaria · Iron Age II (9th-8th c. BCE range)
    Passages: 1 Kgs 22:39, Amos 3:15; 6:4, Ps 45:8
    Now held: Not confirmed in a published museum record. The pieces were split between the two expeditions and are not in one collection; no museum inventory numbers were located.

    About 500 carved ivory pieces from the royal quarter; only a small fraction restorable (Pienaar 2008, citing SS II and Kenyon 1960).

  • Samaria Ostraca

    Hebrew ink dockets for wine and oil sent to Samaria · early 8th century BCE (disputed)
    Passages: Josh 17:2-3; Num 26:30-33, 1 Kgs 16:24, Hos 2:8, 2:16-17, Amos 6:6
    Now held: Not confirmed in a published museum record. The excavation was carried out under an Ottoman permit issued on the recommendation of the Imperial Ottoman Museum, Constantinople (Harvard report, 1924). The Harvard Semitic Museum holds the expedition's photographic negatives, which were used for the 2020 handwriting study.

    Findspot: 'In the eastern part of the courtyard in rooms 401, 416, 417, 419, 470-474, in the lowest part of the debris of occupation' (Lyon 1924, p. 227).

  • Sargon II Palace Inscriptions (Khorsabad)

    Royal inscriptions from Sargon II's palace at Dūr-Šarrukīn · c. 710-706 BCE
    Passages: Isa 20:1-6, 2 Kgs 17:6, 24; 18:9-11, Isa 10:9
    Now held: Dispersed: Louvre (Botta's and Place's shipments), British Museum, Oriental Institute Chicago, Iraq Museum and elsewhere. Many inscribed slabs were lost in the Tigris in 1855 or reburied at the site and survive only in Botta's copies (Monument de Ninive, 1849-50).

    Display Inscription (RINAP 2 no. 7) ll. 90-109: Azūri of Ashdod stops tribute and is replaced by his brother Aḫī-Mīti; the people put up Iāmānī; Sargon marches 'with (only) my warriors who never leave my side'; Iāmānī flees 'to the (far) edge of Egypt, on the border with the land Meluḫḫa'; Ashdod, Gath and Ashdod-Yam are conquered and resettled under an Assyrian governor.

  • Sargon's Annals: the Fall of Samaria

    Sargon II's claims to have captured Samaria and deported 27,290 people · 722-720 BCE
    Passages: 2 Kgs 17:3-6, 2 Kgs 18:9-11, 2 Kgs 17:24
    Now held: Several objects. The Khorsabad Annals of Room II survive mostly in Botta's copies (originals lost in the Tigris in 1855 or reburied); the Display Inscription is known from five exemplars; the Nimrud Prism fragments were excavated at Nimrud (museum numbers Not confirmed in a published museum record).

    Display Inscription (RINAP 2 no. 7) l. 23b-25: URU.sa-me-ri-na al-me ak-šud ... 27 LIM 2 ME 90 UN.MEŠ a-šib ŠÀ-šú áš-lu-la 50 GIŠ.GIGIR.MEŠ ... 'I surrounded (and) conquered the city Samaria. I carried off as booty 27,290 of its inhabitants, conscripted fifty chariots from among them...'

  • Three previously unknown Assyrian kings (Edmonds and Frahm 2026)

    Kings erased from the Assyrian King List, reconstructed from a grant, a ritual text and a recut stela · 10th-8th century BCE
    Passages: 2 Kings 15:19-20, 29; 16:7-10; 1 Chronicles 5:26, 2 Kings 17:3; 18:9, 2 Kings 14:23-29; 15:8-16
    Now held: Tell Abta stela: Istanbul Archaeological Museums, Ist 01326 (ORACC RIAo). Rm 75: British Museum (Yale News). VAT 10136: Vorderasiatisches Museum, Berlin (by the VAT prefix; no museum record seen).

    Tell Abta stela, Ist 01326, line 9: 'Bel-Harran-belu-usur, the palace herald of Tiglath-pileser, king of Assyria'; the signs of the royal name are marked damaged in the ORACC transliteration, and the king's name is cut over an erased Shalmaneser (ORACC RIAo Shalmaneser IV 2; Luckenbill 1926 §824).

  • Tiglath-pileser III Records

    Assyrian royal inscriptions naming Menahem, Pekah, Hoshea and Ahaz · 744-727 BCE
    Passages: 2 Kgs 15:19-20, 2 Kgs 15:29, 2 Kgs 15:30, 2 Kgs 16:5-10; Isa 7:1-9
    Now held: Dispersed. Examples: Annals slab BM 124961 (+) Detroit Institute of Arts 50.32 (RINAP 1 no. 14); Summary Inscription tablet ND 4301+4305 (no. 49); tablet K 3751 (no. 47); Iran stele IMJ 74.049.0096a (+) 0096b, Israel Museum, plus a fragment in a private collection (no. 35).

    Annals (RINAP 1 no. 14 l. 10, slab BM 124961 (+) Detroit 50.32): ... mme-ni-ḫi-im-me URU.sa-me-ri-na-a-a, 'Menahem of the city Samaria', in a list of western rulers paying tribute alongside Rezin (Raḫiānu) of Damascus and Hiram of Tyre.

  • Politarch Inscription (Vardar Gate, Thessalonica)

    Greek list of Thessalonica's civic officials, headed by six politarchs · Roman period, undated
    Passages: Acts 17:6, Acts 17:8, Acts 17:5
    Now held: British Museum, London, reg. no. 1877,0511.1

    The first word is the participle ΠΟΛΕΙΤΑΡΧΟΥΝΤΩΝ, 'being politarchs', spelled with ΕΙ (Burton 1898, 600).

  • Qumran and the Dead Sea Scrolls

    Manuscripts from eleven caves near Khirbet Qumran · 3rd century BCE – 1st century CE
    Passages: Isaiah 1–66, Daniel 8–11, Ecclesiastes
    Now held: Mostly Israel Antiquities Authority (Dead Sea Scrolls lab, Jerusalem) and the Israel Museum's Shrine of the Book (the Cave 1 scrolls including 1QIsaᵃ). Other pieces are held elsewhere; the Copper Scroll (3Q15) is in Amman.

    Cave 1 yielded seven large scrolls in 1947, including 1QIsaᵃ. Caves 4 and 11 yielded thousands of fragments.

  • Sergius Paulus Inscriptions (Soloi, Rome, Pisidian Antioch)

    Three separate stones bearing a Paul(l)us linked to the proconsul of Acts 13 · 1st century CE
    Passages: Acts 13:7, Acts 13:12, Acts 13:13–14
    Now held: Rome cippus: Museo Nazionale Romano, chiostro di Michelangelo, inv. 242. Soloi stone: Hogarth saw it built into a shop threshold at Karavostasi, Cyprus; its present whereabouts are not stated in any source cited here. Antioch stone: not established in the sources cited here.

    Soloi: the proconsul is named only as 'Paulus'. No praenomen or 'Sergius' survives (Hogarth 1889, 114–115; Cesnola 1877, 425).

  • Temple Mount Platform (Herodian enclosure)

    Herod's enlarged temple esplanade in Jerusalem · begun c. 20/19 BCE, work continuing to the 60s CE
    Passages: John 2:20, Mark 13:1–2; Matt 24:1–2; Luke 21:5–6, Matt 21:12–13; Mark 11:15–17; John 2:14–16, John 10:23; Acts 3:11
    Now held: In situ, Jerusalem

    Irregular quadrilateral: W 1,601 ft, E 1,530 ft, S 922 ft, N 1,042 ft; about 35 acres (SWP Jerusalem 1884, p. 119 and in the section on Arab measurements).

  • Western Wall and Robinson's Arch

    Herodian western retaining wall of the Temple Mount, with its great arches · late 1st century BCE – 1st century CE
    Passages: Mark 13:1–2; Matt 24:1–2; Luke 21:5–6, John 2:20
    Now held: In situ, Jerusalem

    Robinson's Arch: three surviving courses of curved voussoirs, one 5 ft 4 in thick; a chord of 12 ft 6 in on the surviving arc; 350 ft from the arch to the rock scarp opposite (Robinson 1841, p. 425).
