Bethesda Pool

Twin rock-cut pools beside St. Anne's Church, Jerusalem · Second Temple period and later

Overview

After the Crimean War the Ottoman Sultan presented the ruined church of St. Anne (then the 'Salahieh' school) near St. Stephen's Gate to the Emperor Napoleon. While the French-appointed monks cleared the grounds they found a deep rock-cut 'cistern' with steps. Conrad Schick planned and published it in the Palestine Exploration Fund's Quarterly Statement for July 1888.

Schick's report describes a pool cut 30 ft into rock, 55 by 12.5 ft, with a flight of 24 steps down from the east, lying beneath a later apsed building (read as a church) whose floor was carried on vaults. The PEF editors added that Schick had found 'a second pool to the west of this, forming what is called a twin pool'.

The editors then argued from John 5:2: 'the only possible way for a pool to have five porticoes (unless it is a pentagon) is to be a double or twin pool, so that there may be one portico along each side and one for the wall of separation.' That is an argument from the text to the plan; the 1888 report does not describe standing porticoes.

What the evidence shows

  • Rock-cut pool 55 ft E-W x 12.5 ft N-S, 30 ft deep, 24 steps from the east (Schick, PEF QS 1888).
  • North wall of masonry with four piers on rock bases carrying arches, spaces later filled in (PEF QS 1888).
  • A second pool west of the first ('twin pool') reported by Schick in 1888.
  • A later apsed building (c. 70 x 25 ft) built over the pool after c. 10 ft of earth had accumulated (PEF QS 1888).
  • Pilgrim testimony forms 'a continuous chain of evidence from the 4th to the 12th century' for the site, per the PEF editors citing Sir Charles Wilson (1888).
  • Before 1888 the name 'Bethesda' had been attached to the Birket Israil pool north of the Haram (PEF QS 1888).

Open questions

  • What the five porticoes were: the PEF editors (1888) proposed one portico on each of four sides of a double pool and a fifth on the dividing wall. U. C. von Wahlde ('The Puzzling Pool of Bethesda', BAR Sept/Oct 2011, as summarized by BAS) describes 'a rectangular pool with two basins separated by a wall' and reads the southern basin, with 'broad steps with landings', as a mikveh and the northern as its reservoir (otzar). The mikveh function is a reading of the stepped design, not an inscription.
  • Healing cult: whether the site was also, or later, a healing sanctuary (a reading in some later literature) was not documented in the sources cited here and is left open.
  • Exact dates of construction of each basin and of the dividing wall are not available from a primary excavation report cited here; they stay open here rather than being filled with secondary figures.

The Find

  • DateRock-cut pools in use in the Second Temple period; later Roman and Byzantine/Crusader structures built over them. The precise construction dates are not confirmed from the excavation report.
  • How datedThe 1888 report dates only the church above the pool (built after about 10 ft of earth had accumulated over the rock). Dating of the pools themselves rests on the later White Fathers / École Biblique excavations, which have not been reviewed for this entry.
  • DiscoveredCistern exposed during clearing of the St. Anne's property after the Crimean War and opened to visitors by 1888; published by Conrad Schick in the PEF Quarterly Statement, July 1888, with a second, western pool reported the same year, The 'Algerian monks' (White Fathers) at St. Anne's; the architect 'Mons. Maux' (as spelled in the report); recorded by Conrad Schick, Grounds of St. Anne's Church, north of the Temple Mount (Haram), near St. Stephen's (Lions') Gate, Jerusalem
  • Now heldIn situ, St. Anne's (Sainte-Anne) compound, Old City of Jerusalem
  • MaterialRock-cut basins with masonry; later vaults and church remains above
  • Dimensions1888 measurement of the first-found pool: 55 ft E-W x 12.5 ft N-S, cut 30 ft deep, reached by a flight of 24 steps from the east scarp; the building above it c. 70 x 25 ft with an eastern apse (PEF QS 1888)

Biblical Passages

  • John 5:2'There is at Jerusalem by the sheep market a pool, which is called in the Hebrew tongue Bethesda, having five porches' (KJV). The site is north of the Temple Mount near the traditional Sheep Gate area; a double pool gives a plan that could carry five porticoes, as the 1888 editors argued.
  • John 5:3-9Many sick lie in the porticoes; Jesus heals a man who had been ill 38 years, telling him to take up his bed and walk. The man was lame/infirm, not blind. The find shows the pools; it carries no evidence of the event.
  • Neh 3:1, 32; 12:39The Sheep Gate of Jerusalem, north of the Temple, which John's 'by the sheep [gate/market]' echoes. Topographic correspondence only.
