Large Stone Structure

Massive walls at the summit of the City of David, Jerusalem · date disputed (Iron I to Hellenistic)

Overview

In 2005 Eilat Mazar opened an area of over 3,000 square feet at the northern summit of the City of David and exposed a set of very wide walls built of large stones. She treated them as one building, named it the Large Stone Structure, and argued it was part of one complex with the Stepped Stone Structure on the slope below.

Mazar dated construction to the 10th c. BCE: worn Iron I (12th-11th c.) pottery lay beneath the walls, and Iron IIA pottery, including a Cypriot Black-on-Red juglet, came from later phases. She proposed that it was the palace built for David by Hiram's craftsmen (2 Sam 5:11). Among later finds she reported a bulla of Yehuchal son of Shelemiyahu, a name that occurs in Jer 37:3.

Three published readings compete: Mazar's 10th-century palace; Finkelstein, Herzog, Singer-Avitz and Ussishkin (2007), who say the walls do not form one building and date the most elaborate ones to the Hellenistic period; and Faust (2010), who dates the structure to Iron I, before David.

What the evidence shows

  • Walls 6-8 ft wide of large stones (Mazar, BAR 32.1, 2006).
  • Iron I pottery (12th-11th c. BCE) beneath the structure; Iron IIA pottery (10th-9th c.) in later phases; a Cypriot Black-on-Red juglet (Mazar 2006).
  • Seal impression reading 'Yehuchal son of Shelemiyahu son of Shovi' from the site; the building was still in use in the late Iron Age (Mazar 2006).
  • Finkelstein et al. (2007): the walls 'do not belong to a single building'; the Stepped Stone Structure has at least two phases (a possible 9th c. BCE phase and a Hellenistic one).
  • Regev et al. (PNAS 2024) found that 18 of their dates fall in the 12th-10th c. BCE in other City of David areas ('widespread occupation of yet undetermined character'); the LSS was not sampled.

Open questions

  • Date and unity: Mazar (BAR 2006; The Palace of King David, 2009) dates a single building to the mid-10th c. from Iron I sherds sealed beneath it and Iron IIA material in it; Finkelstein, Herzog, Singer-Avitz and Ussishkin (Tel Aviv 34, 2007) say the walls belong to different structures and the elaborate ones are Hellenistic; Faust (ZDPV 126, 2010) dates it to Iron I and says 'the building was not the palace built for King David by the Phoenicians.'
  • Relation to the Stepped Stone Structure: Mazar and Faust treat LSS and SSS as one complex; Finkelstein et al. (2007) separate the SSS into at least two phases.
  • No radiocarbon date: the 2024 PNAS study that re-dated Iron Age Jerusalem (Regev et al.) did not sample the LSS, so its date still rests on pottery alone.

Since 2024

  • 2024-05Regev et al. (PNAS 121) published a high-resolution 14C chronology for Iron Age Jerusalem: occupation in the 12th-10th c. in other City of David areas; Spring Tower and Giv'ati Building 100 dated to the 9th c. The Large Stone Structure was not sampled, so its date is unchanged. [5]

The Find

  • DateDisputed. Mazar: built in the mid-10th c. BCE (Iron IIA) on top of Iron I pottery; Faust (2010): Iron I; Finkelstein et al. (2007): not one building, main walls Hellenistic.
  • How datedPottery beneath and within the walls (Mazar 2006, 2009). No radiocarbon date for the LSS itself was found; the 2024 City of David radiocarbon study (Regev et al., PNAS) sampled other areas.
  • Discovered2005-2007, Eilat Mazar (Shalem Center / Hebrew University), Northern summit of the City of David ridge, near Kathleen Kenyon's Area H, above the Stepped Stone Structure, Jerusalem
  • Now heldIn situ, City of David, Jerusalem
  • MaterialWalls of large, mostly undressed stones
  • DimensionsWall widths 6-8 ft (c. 1.8-2.4 m) per Mazar (BAR 2006); excavated area over 3,000 sq ft

Biblical Passages

  • 2 Sam 5:11Hiram of Tyre sent cedar, carpenters and masons, 'and they built David a house.' Mazar identifies the LSS with this house on location and date; the building carries no inscription naming its owner.
  • 2 Sam 5:17David 'went down to the stronghold' when the Philistines came. Mazar reads this as the palace lying above/outside the fortress; this is her reading.
  • Jer 37:3; 38:1Name Jehucal son of Shelemiah, a court official under Zedekiah; a bulla with the same name and patronym came from Mazar's excavation (late Iron Age use of the area).
