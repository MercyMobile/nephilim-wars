Radiocarbon Chronology of Iron Age Jerusalem (Regev et al., PNAS 2024)

103 radiocarbon dates from the City of David and the Giv'ati Parking Lot · published April 2024

Overview

Until this study, the absolute dates for Iron Age Jerusalem rested on pottery typology, stratigraphy and the biblical text. Regev and colleagues took single charred items (mostly seeds) from tightly controlled contexts in five excavation areas and dated 103 of them. They also dated 100 individual tree rings from calendar-dated Irish oak to test the calibration curve across the 'Hallstatt plateau' (c. 800-400 BCE), where radiocarbon normally loses resolution.

The paper reports four results. Occupation in the 12th-10th centuries BCE is shown by 18 dates, which the authors call 'widespread occupation of yet undetermined character'. There was extensive building and westward expansion in the 9th century BCE, including Building 100 in the Giv'ati Parking Lot and the Spring Tower area. An earthquake destruction is dated to c. 766-750 BCE, which the authors link to Amos 1:1. Fortifications that had been credited to Hezekiah are reassigned to the later years of Uzziah.

The study also found that measured radiocarbon ages depart from the calibration curve around 730-710 BCE and around 586 BCE. The authors present this as a correction others must apply when dating late Iron Age sites in the region.

What the evidence shows

  • 103 archaeological radiocarbon measurements plus 100 single-ring measurements from dated Irish oak (PNAS 2024).
  • 18 of the 103 dates (almost 20%) fall in the 12th-10th centuries BCE. Only three of them (RTD10780, 9598, 9585) come from contexts with clearly associated early Iron Age pottery; the rest are charred remains in building materials.
  • A twig in Room 17049 (Area U), 'perhaps representing roofing material', dated to the 10th century BCE (RTD11182).
  • Dates from beneath the Spring Tower at the Gihon Spring span the 10th-9th centuries BCE.
  • A 9th-century BCE date was obtained for construction in Area U (Room 19040) and for the start of Building 100 in the Giv'ati Parking Lot, which the authors say stayed in use until 586 BCE.
  • Earthquake horizon: combined 2507 ± 8 uncal BP, calibrated c. 766-750 BCE.
  • In Area 70-south, two of three OSL (luminescence) dates were older than the radiocarbon dates from the same spots, with non-overlapping ranges.
  • Neither the Stepped Stone Structure nor the Large Stone Structure was sampled; the paper does not mention them.
  • The paper names neither David nor Solomon; the biblical references it makes are Amos 1:1 (earthquake) and 2 Kgs 15-16 (Uzziah / Syro-Ephraimite war).

Open questions

  • What kind of 10th-century Jerusalem? Regev et al. (PNAS 2024) show 12th-10th century occupation but call it 'of yet undetermined character', because only 3 of the 18 early dates have diagnostic pottery. Y. Garfinkel (JEMAHS 12/4, 2024) sets these dates beside Khirbet Qeiyafa (c. 30 dates, 1000-970 BCE) and the Gezer six-chambered gate dates (Webster et al. 2023) and reads them as a flourishing early kingdom of Judah; he cites Regev et al. for monumental building in 9th-century Jerusalem. The Jerusalem dates alone do not decide whether the 10th-century settlement was a capital.
  • Who built the 8th-century fortifications? Regev et al. (2024) date the Mid-Slope fortification to after the c. 760 BCE earthquake and credit it to Uzziah in the Syro-Ephraimite war period. The attribution they revise credited these works to Hezekiah in the late 8th century (their ref. 59); readers who keep Hezekiah usually cite 2 Chr 32:5 and Isa 22:10, but in Regev et al.'s reading the dated sequence puts construction soon after the c. 760 BCE earthquake, earlier than Hezekiah. Vukosavovic, Shalev, Roth, Chalaf and Uziel (Tel Aviv, July 2026, 'In the Wake of the Amos Quake') return to the mid-8th-century fortifications; that article's argument has not been reviewed for this entry.
  • Does radiocarbon agree with luminescence? In Area 70-south, two of three OSL dates came out older than radiocarbon from the same spots (PNAS 2024 SI Figs. S39, S41). The paper reports this and leaves it unresolved.
  • I. Finkelstein circulated a response in May 2024 ('Observations on the article Regev et al.', academia.edu). The page was not accessible for this entry, so its arguments are not stated here.

Corrected from the earlier edition of this page
  • Headlines that the study 'proves David and Solomon's kingdom': the paper names neither king. It reports 12th-10th century occupation 'of yet undetermined character' and says only 3 of 18 early dates have diagnostic pottery.
  • Claims that the study dated the Stepped Stone Structure or the Large Stone Structure ('David's palace'): neither was sampled or mentioned.
  • Claims that Hezekiah's wall was re-dated to Uzziah as a fact: the date (after the c. 760 BCE earthquake) is measured; the attribution to Uzziah is the authors' reading of that date.
  • Claims that radiocarbon 'cannot date the Iron Age II' because of the Hallstatt plateau: the study reached decadal resolution in parts of the plateau by wiggle-matching sequences and checking against 100 dated tree rings.

  • DateSamples span the 12th century BCE to the Babylonian destruction of 586 BCE; published online 29 April 2024 (PNAS 121/19)
  • How dated103 AMS radiocarbon measurements on short-lived single items (about two-thirds seeds) from stratified contexts, measured at the Weizmann Institute (lab codes RTD...). Biblical Passages

  • 2 Sam 5:6-9; 1 Kgs 3-11Radiocarbon shows people living and building on the Southeastern Ridge in the 12th-10th centuries BCE, the time the Bible gives for the Jebusite city, David's capture and Solomon's reign. The dates show occupation. They do not show who ruled, how large the town was, or any particular building the Bible describes.
  • Amos 1:1; Zech 14:5The authors link a destruction layer dated c. 766-750 BCE (2507 ± 8 BP) to 'the earthquake' by which Amos dates his book. That link is their reading.
  • 2 Kgs 15-16; 2 Chr 26:9The authors credit the post-earthquake Mid-Slope fortification to Uzziah. 2 Chr 26:9 does say Uzziah built towers in Jerusalem and fortified them. The date is the paper's evidence; the name Uzziah is its reading.
  • 2 Kgs 25:8-10Building 100 and other structures end in the conflagration the paper dates to 586 BCE, which is also the anchor for the second calibration offset.
