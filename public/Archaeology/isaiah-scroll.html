Isaiah Scroll (Great Isaiah Scroll)

Complete parchment scroll of Isaiah from Qumran Cave 1 · 2nd century BCE

Overview

1QIsa-a is one of the seven scrolls found in Qumran Cave 1 in 1947. It contains the whole Book of Isaiah, chapters 1-66, in 54 columns written on parchment, 7.34 m long. The Israel Museum lists it as HU 95.57/27, property of the Government of Israel, displayed in the Shrine of the Book.

Its text is 'generally in agreement with the Masoretic or traditional version codified in medieval codices, such as the Aleppo Codex, but it contains many variant readings, alternative spellings, scribal errors, and corrections' (Israel Museum record). Its spelling is very full (many vowel letters), and in the second half the orthography and morphology differ from the first.

There is a physical break in the middle: a three-line blank at the bottom of column 27 and a sheet join between columns 27 and 28. Popovic, Dhali and Schomaker (2021) found by computer analysis of letter shapes that two main scribes wrote the two halves, with the switch at columns 27-29.

What the text says (summary)

Contains the entire Book of Isaiah (chs. 1-66) in 54 columns; the text follows the same book and order as the medieval Masoretic codices, with many variant readings, fuller spellings, scribal errors and corrections (Israel Museum record).No public-domain translation of the scroll's own variant text has been located for this entry

What the evidence shows

  • 54 columns, all 66 chapters of Isaiah (Israel Museum record).
  • Length 734 cm; height 22-25 cm per museum, c. 26 cm average per Popovic et al. 2021.
  • Radiocarbon: 2128 +/- 38 BP (ETH-6651) and 2141 +/- 32 BP (AA-14984), both from column XXXIX.
  • Three-line blank at the foot of column 27 and a sheet join between columns 27 and 28.
  • Orthography and morphology change in the second half; blank spaces left in the second half (Popovic et al. 2021).
  • Handwriting analysis: two main scribes, transition at columns 27-29 (Popovic et al. 2021).
  • Many variant readings, spelling differences, errors and corrections relative to the medieval Masoretic codices (Israel Museum record).

Open questions

  • One scribe or two: older palaeographic debate asked whether differences between the halves are normal variation of one hand; Popovic, Dhali and Schomaker (PLOS ONE 16, 2021) report statistically significant writer difference with a phase transition in columns 27-29 and conclude two main scribes who closely mirrored one another's style.
  • Absolute date: Jull et al. (Radiocarbon 37, 1995) give a 2-sigma radiocarbon range whose larger probability block is 250-103 BC but which also allows 356-291 BC (24%); the palaeographic date (Cross: c. 150-125 BCE) sits inside the later block. Popovic et al. (PLOS ONE 20, 2025) find their style model dates both halves to the same period, 180-100 BCE, i.e. neither half is significantly later.
  • How close to the Masoretic Text: the Israel Museum record describes the text as 'generally in agreement' with the medieval Masoretic codices 'but' with 'many variant readings'. The critical edition (Ulrich & Flint, DJD XXXII, 2010) lists the variants; whether they are 'minor' is an evaluation, not a measurement, and is left to the variant lists.

Since 2024

  • 2025-06Popovic et al. publish the 'Enoch' model trained on 24 radiocarbon-dated scrolls; it dates both scribal halves of 1QIsa-a to the same period, 180-100 BCE, and finds scroll dates generally older than traditional palaeographic estimates. [4]

The Find

  • Date2nd century BCE: radiocarbon 335-122 BCE (calibrated, 1 sigma); palaeography c. 150-125 BCE; AI style model 180-100 BCE
  • How datedTwo independent AMS radiocarbon measurements on the same area (column XXXIX): 2128 +/- 38 BP (ETH-6651, Bonani et al. 1991/92) and 2141 +/- 32 BP (AA-14984, Jull et al. 1995); Jull et al. give the calibrated range 335-122 BC (1 sigma), 2 sigma 356-291 BC (24%) / 250-103 BC (76%). Palaeographic date cited by Jull et al. from F. M. Cross: 'c. 150-125 B.C.E.' The Israel Museum record says 'ca. 125 BCE'. The 2025 'Enoch' model (Popovic et al.) dates both halves 180-100 BCE.
  • Discovered1947, Among the first seven scrolls found by Bedouin in Qumran Cave 1 (finders' names not confirmed in the sources cited here), Qumran Cave 1, NW shore of the Dead Sea
  • Now heldIsrael Museum, Jerusalem (Shrine of the Book), accession no. HU 95.57/27, Government of Israel
  • MaterialParchment, sheets sewn together (a sheet join between columns 27 and 28 is noted by Popovic et al. 2021)
  • DimensionsL 734 cm; H 22-25 cm (Israel Museum record); Popovic et al. 2021 give 7.34 m, averaging 26 cm high
  • ScriptJewish square script of the Hasmonaean type
  • LanguageHebrew

Biblical Passages

  • Isaiah 1-66The scroll is a copy of the whole book in one scroll, including both chs. 1-39 and 40-66 continuously; it is direct manuscript evidence that the book circulated in its 66-chapter form by the 2nd century BCE.
  • Luke 4:16-20Jesus is handed 'the scroll (biblion) of the prophet Isaiah' in the Nazareth synagogue and unrolls it to Isa 61. The Great Isaiah Scroll shows what a complete Isaiah scroll of the period physically was: one continuous parchment roll.
  • Acts 8:28-35The Ethiopian official reads Isaiah 53 aloud from a scroll; same book, same format.
  • Matt 3:3; Mark 1:2-3; John 1:23The Gospels quote Isa 40:3 for John the Baptist; the scroll contains that verse in a pre-Christian copy.
