Nabonidus Cylinders from Ur (naming Belshazzar)

Clay foundation cylinders of Nabonidus, king of Babylon, with a prayer for 'Belshazzar, the eldest son' · 555-539 BCE

Overview

In 1854 J. E. Taylor, British vice-consul at Basra, excavated at Tell el-Muqayyar, the site of Ur, and recovered inscribed foundation cylinders of Nabonidus, the last native king of Babylon. The text records Nabonidus's restoration of the temple-tower (ziggurat) of the moon-god Sin and closes with a prayer to Sin.

In the prayer Nabonidus asks for long life for himself and then prays for 'Belshazzar, the firstborn son, proceeding from my loins'. The cuneiform writes his name dEN-LUGAL-URU3, Bel-sharra-usur. Four exemplars of the same text are in the British Museum and are edited as Nabonidus 32 in The Royal Inscriptions of the Neo-Babylonian Empire, vol. 2 (Weiershäuser and Novotny 2020).

The cylinders, with the Nabonidus Chronicle's notices that 'the son of the king' was in Akkad while Nabonidus stayed at Tema, establish Belshazzar as Nabonidus's eldest son and a figure of state in Babylon.

In its own words

As for Belshazzar, the firstborn son, proceeding from my loins, place in his heart fear of thy great divinity; let him not turn to sinning; let him be satisfied with fulness of life!Nabonidus's prayer to Sin, trans. G. A. Barton, Archaeology and the Bible (Philadelphia, 1916), Part II ch. XVIII, p. 382

What the evidence shows

  • Four exemplars of one text (RINBE 2, Nabonidus 32) in the British Museum: BM 91125, 91126, 91127, 91128; provenance Ur (CDLI records).
  • Col. ii lines 24-26 of exemplar 1 (CDLI transliteration): u3 sza2 {disz}{d}en-lugal-uru3 / DUMU resz-tu-u2 / s,i-it lib3-bi-ia, 'and of Bel-sharra-usur, the eldest son, offspring of my heart'.
  • The same prayer asks Sin to keep Nabonidus himself from sin and grant him long days (col. ii 19-23).
  • The Nabonidus Chronicle (as translated by Barton 1916, p. 382) records for Nabonidus's 9th year that the king was at Tema while 'the son of the king, the princes, and soldiers were in Akkad'; Barton notes similar entries for other years.
  • Barton (1916, p. 384) reports a contract from Erech, dated in Nabonidus's 12th year, sworn by 'the oath of Nabuna'id, King of Babylon, and of Belshazzar, the king's son' (from Pinches).

Open questions

  • Was Belshazzar 'king'? Daniel 5:1 calls him king. The Ur cylinders call him 'eldest son' and the Chronicle calls him 'the son of the king'. Barton (1916, p. 384) notes that an oath sworn jointly by Nabonidus and Belshazzar shows him 'but slightly lower in rank and power than the king himself'. Dan 5:7, 16, 29 offer 'third ruler in the kingdom', which fits a second-in-rank prince. No text yet seen calls Belshazzar šarru (king).
  • 'Nebuchadnezzar his father' (Dan 5:2, 11, 18): the cylinder calls Belshazzar the son of Nabonidus. Barton (1916, p. 382) notes that Nabonidus was not of Nebuchadnezzar's line. Some take 'father' as 'ancestor' or 'predecessor', or propose descent through his mother; no cuneiform text seen here documents Belshazzar's mother.

The Find

  • DateReign of Nabonidus (555-539 BCE; Barton 1916 gives 555-538)
  • How datedThe text is in Nabonidus's own name as king of Babylon and records his rebuilding of the ziggurat of the moon-god Sin at Ur. It carries no regnal-year date; the reign is fixed by Babylonian chronicles and king lists.
  • Discovered1854, J. E. Taylor, British vice-consul at Basra, working under H. C. Rawlinson (Barton 1916, p. 44), Tell el-Muqayyar (Ur), temple tower of Sin
  • Now heldBritish Museum, London: four exemplars, BM 91125, BM 91126, BM 91127, BM 91128 (CDLI: RINBE 2, Nabonidus 32, ex. 1-4)
  • MaterialBaked clay cylinders
  • ScriptNeo-Babylonian cuneiform
  • LanguageAkkadian (Standard Babylonian)

Biblical Passages

  • Dan 5:1-30; 7:1; 8:1Daniel names Belshazzar as ruler in Babylon on the night it fell. The cylinder independently confirms a Belshazzar (Bel-sharra-usur) as Nabonidus's eldest son, from Nabonidus's own inscription.
  • Dan 5:7, 16, 29The offer to make Daniel 'third ruler in the kingdom' fits a regime in which Nabonidus was king and Belshazzar his son held power in Babylon. Reading 'third' that way is an interpretation.
  • Dan 5:2, 11, 18The cylinder names Belshazzar's father as Nabonidus, not Nebuchadnezzar. How to reconcile this with Daniel is an open question (see questions).
