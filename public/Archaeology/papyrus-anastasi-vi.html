Papyrus Anastasi VI: the Shasu of Edom at the Border Fortress

Hieratic model letter reporting that Shasu herdsmen of Edom were let past a Delta border fortress to the pools of Pithom · late 19th Dynasty, end of the 13th century BCE

Overview

Papyrus Anastasi VI is one of the Late-Egyptian Miscellanies, rolls on which scribes copied letters, reports and praise texts. J. P. Allen describes this piece as a model letter, one of four scribal exercises on the roll, and J. A. Wilson describes the group as letters that served as models for schoolboys.

In the passage numbered lines 51–61 the scribe Inena writes to the treasury scribe Qagabu. He reports that the Shasu clans of Edom have been allowed past the Fortress of Merneptah in Tjeku and on to the pools of Per-Atum (Pithom) in Tjeku, so that they and their herds may stay alive, and he mentions a list of the other days on which the fortress was passed.

K. A. Kitchen notes that the name Edom appears here for the first time. The passage is also a plain account of how Egypt ran its eastern Delta border at the end of the 13th century BCE: a named fort, named water, a people identified by homeland, and permission recorded in writing.

What the text says (summary)

The writer reports that the Shasu clans of Edom have been let through the fortress named for Merneptah in Tjeku and on to the pools of Per-Atum, so that they and their animals may live.Summary, not a quotation. After the translations of J. A. Wilson (ANET, p. 259), J. P. Allen (The Context of Scripture 3.5) and D. E. Fleming (2020), lines 51–61

What the evidence shows

  • Manuscript: British Museum EA 10245; an incomplete dating formula with the name of Seti II in line 1 (Thesaurus Linguae Aegyptiae record).
  • Published in facsimile in Select Papyri in the Hieratic Character (1844), pls. CXXII–CXXVII; hieroglyphic transcription in Gardiner, Late-Egyptian Miscellanies (1937), pp. 72–78a; translation and commentary in Caminos, Late-Egyptian Miscellanies (1954), pp. 279–300 (Thesaurus Linguae Aegyptiae record).
  • Sender and addressee: the scribe Inena to the treasury scribe Qagabu, whose name is partly lost (Allen; Wilson).
  • The people: 'the tribes (mhwt) of the Shasu of Edom' (Fleming 2020, citing Giveon's Document 37).
  • The places: the Fortress of Merneptah-Hotephirmaat in Tjeku and the pools of Pithom of Merneptah in Tjeku (Fleming 2020; Allen).
  • The stated purpose: to keep the Shasu and their herds alive (Allen; Wilson; Fleming).
  • The date line: Year 8, read by Allen as the third epagomenal day, the Birth of Seth, and by Wilson as the '5 [intercalary] days, [the Birth of] Seth'.
  • Kitchen (1992): 'the term Edom appears for the first time' in this report.

Open questions

  • Whose Year 8? The manuscript was written under Seti II, but Allen judges that the year in the letter is probably Merneptah's. Kitchen dates the report to Year 8 of Merneptah, c. 1206 BCE. A passage in the Biblical Archaeology Society library, attributed to M. Bietak on a compilation page, dates it to Year 5 of Seti II. Either way it belongs to the last years of the 13th century BCE.
  • A real dispatch or a school text? Allen and Wilson both class it as a model letter. A model letter may copy a real report, and its place names and procedure had to be credible to the scribes who used it, but the papyrus cannot show that this crossing took place on the day named.
  • Is this Israel? No. The Shasu here are named for Edom. The Merneptah Stele, from the same reign, names Israel as a people already in Canaan. The report shows a neighbouring herding people being admitted to the Delta, not Israel entering or leaving it.
  • Tjeku and Succoth. Kitchen renders the fort as the fort at Succoth, equating Egyptian Tjeku with the Succoth of Exodus 12:37, and Per-Atum with Pithom. The papyrus gives the Egyptian names only; the equations are philological.

The Find

  • DateManuscript: reign of Seti II, whose name stands in its first line. The frontier report copied in it is dated 'Year 8', which J. P. Allen takes as probably that of Seti's predecessor Merneptah; K. A. Kitchen gives Year 8 of Merneptah, c. 1206 BCE
  • How datedThe royal name in line 1 of the manuscript (Thesaurus Linguae Aegyptiae record); the regnal year written in the letter; the fortress and the pools in the letter carry Merneptah's name, so the report is not earlier than his reign.
  • DiscoveredBought from the collection of Giovanni Anastasi (the British Museum records its companion papyri Anastasi III and V as acquired in 1839); facsimile published 1844, Giovanni Anastasi (collector); first facsimile in the British Museum's Select Papyri in the Hieratic Character, Findspot not recorded; probably Saqqara, to judge from what is known of the Anastasi collection (Thesaurus Linguae Aegyptiae record)
  • Now heldBritish Museum, London, EA 10245
  • MaterialPapyrus, ink
  • ScriptHieratic
  • LanguageLate Egyptian

Biblical Passages

  • Genesis 46:31–47:6Herdsmen from the east ask leave to bring their flocks into the eastern Delta to survive, and an official grants it. The papyrus records the same administrative act for a different people at a later date.
  • Exodus 1:11Pithom. The pools in the report are 'of Per-Atum', the Egyptian name behind Pithom, and they lie in Tjeku.
  • Exodus 12:37; 13:20Succoth, the first stage out of Egypt. Kitchen equates it with Tjeku, the district of the border fortress. The papyrus shows that district fortified and its crossings recorded by day.
  • Genesis 36:1–9; Numbers 20:14–21Edom as a people with a land of its own before Israel's monarchy. An Egyptian scribe names Edom as a homeland of herding clans by the end of the 13th century BCE.
