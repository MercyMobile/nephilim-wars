Mari Tablets (Royal Archives of Mari)

Old Babylonian palace archive from Tell Hariri on the Middle Euphrates, Syria · early 18th century BCE (middle chronology)

Overview

Tell Hariri on the Euphrates was identified as ancient Mari by André Parrot's French expedition, whose preliminary reports appeared in the journal Syria from 1935. The royal palace yielded an archive of letters, legal and administrative documents from the Old Babylonian period, published in the series Archives royales de Mari (ARM).

The letters are the main value of the archive: correspondence between the kings of Mari (especially Zimri-Lim), their officials, governors and other rulers, including reports on tribal groups and on messages from prophets and ecstatics delivered in the name of gods. An English translation of a large selection is W. Heimpel, Letters to the King of Mari (2003).

Biblical links drawn from Mari fall into three kinds: (1) prophecy - letters reporting oracles to the king, edited in ARM 26 and compared with biblical prophets by A. Malamat (1998); (2) tribal society - the pastoral tribes of Zimri-Lim's world studied by D. Fleming (2004); (3) places - Mari letters mention Hazor in Canaan (Malamat 1998, chs. on Mari and Hazor).

What the evidence shows

  • Excavation began with Parrot's first campaign, winter 1933-34 (Parrot, Syria 16, 1935, 1-28 and 117-140); second campaign winter 1934-35 (Syria 17, 1936, 1-31).
  • The archive's letters include reports of prophetic messages: edited in ARM 26 (Malamat 1998 ch. 'New Light from Mari (ARM XXVI) on Biblical Prophecy').
  • Hazor appears in Mari documents, including trade relations (Malamat 1998, 'Hazor Once Again in New Mari Documents'; 'Mari and Hazor: Trade Relations in the Old Babylonian Period').
  • Zimri-Lim's kingdom included organised pastoral tribes; Fleming (2004) devotes ch. 1 ('The Tribal World of Zimri-Lim', pp. 24-103) to them.

Open questions

  • How close are Mari prophecy and biblical prophecy? A. Malamat (Mari and the Bible, Brill 1998) argues for real parallels (e.g. 'A Mari Prophecy and Nathan's Dynastic Oracle', pp. 106-121; 'Episodes Involving Samuel and Saul and the Prophetic Texts from Mari', pp. 102-105). The evidence is the text of the ARM 26 letters; the parallels are his reading.
  • Personal and place names compared with Genesis (Laban, Nahor, the 'Benjaminite' tribes, the Hapiru): the old entry asserts these as findings. No primary edition cited here confirms or refutes each; they remain unverified here and should be checked in the ARM volumes before being printed as facts.
  • Absolute date: 'mid-18th century BCE' depends on the middle chronology; Malamat (1998, 'Mari and Hazor: The Implication for the Middle Bronze Age Chronology', pp. 51-55) uses the Mari-Hazor synchronism in that argument.

The Find

  • DateOld Babylonian period; the palace archive belongs mainly to the kings Yasmah-Addu and Zimri-Lim, ending with the city's fall to Hammurabi of Babylon (18th century BCE on the middle chronology)
  • How datedThe letters and administrative texts carry year-names and royal names (Zimri-Lim etc.) and synchronise with Hammurabi of Babylon; absolute dates follow whichever Mesopotamian chronology is used. This reconstruction is standard in the publications cited (e.g. Fleming 2004, ch. 'The Tribal World of Zimri-Lim') but was not re-read in the original reports during this pass.
  • DiscoveredSite identified 1933-1934 (first campaign, winter 1933-34); palace and archives excavated in the following seasons (second campaign 1934-35 onward), French expedition directed by André Parrot, Royal palace at Tell Hariri (ancient Mari), Middle Euphrates, eastern Syria near the Iraqi border
  • Now heldSyria and France (Louvre) hold Mari material; the present repository of the archive tablets is not confirmed from a primary source
  • MaterialClay tablets
  • ScriptCuneiform
  • LanguageAkkadian (Old Babylonian dialect with West Semitic / 'Amorite' vocabulary and names)

Biblical Passages

  • 1 Samuel 9-10; 2 Samuel 7; Jeremiah; EzekielMari letters report prophets speaking to a king in a god's name, with messages checked and sent on by officials - a documented prophetic institution a millennium before the biblical prophets. The texts do not mention Israel.
  • Joshua 11:1-13; Judges 4:2Hazor appears in Mari documents as a significant Canaanite city in trade contact with Mari, consistent with Joshua's description of Hazor as 'the head of all those kingdoms' - an independent attestation of Hazor's standing, not of Joshua's events.
  • Genesis 11-31Mari shows pastoral tribes moving between towns and grazing land in upper Mesopotamia/Syria in the Old Babylonian period. That is a setting like the patriarchal narratives'; it names no patriarch.
