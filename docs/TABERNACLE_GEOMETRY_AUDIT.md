# Tabernacle scene geometry audit

## Governing rule

Exodus is the controlling construction source. The Septuagint is checked for
variants. Josephus, Philo, and rabbinic literature may clarify a silence, but
they do not override an explicit Exodus measurement. Temple descriptions are
not projected backward onto the wilderness Tabernacle.

The Temple Scroll, other Dead Sea Scroll material, the Apocrypha, Jubilees,
Jasher, and 1 Enoch supply no independent measurable geometry for the Mosaic
Tabernacle. Their silence is part of the audit; visionary or later Temple
architecture is not used as construction data.

## Source conflicts kept as alternatives

- Josephus gives seven entrance pillars in one passage; Exodus gives five.
  The scene uses five.
- Josephus preserves a 50 x 20-cubit court account; Exodus gives 100 x 50.
  The scene uses Exodus.
- Josephus gives 1/6-cubit board depth. Rabbinic traditions give a one-cubit
  base, either constant or tapering toward the top. The current view selects
  Josephus; the rabbinic profiles belong in alternative geometry modes.
- Rabbi Jose's ten-cubit altar tradition conflicts with the plain
  5 x 5 x 3-cubit Exodus reading. The current view uses Exodus.
- Josephus gives a triangular use for the eleventh goat-hair curtain. The
  current view follows the Exodus/Rabbi Judah half-front, half-rear reading.

## Unresolved and not to be presented as measured

- metre length of the cubit;
- court-post diameter, socket footprint, cord diameter, and peg placement;
- entrance-screen permeability, areal mass, and fastening clearances;
- pillar spacing where only the number of pillars is stated;
- thickness of textiles, gold overlay, bronze overlay, hides, and vessel walls;
- laver dimensions;
- carrying-pole lengths and diameters;
- exact forms of the veil cherubim;
- exact dimensions and animal identity of the upper hide coverings.

The thin textile volume in the renderer exists only to make edges visible. It
must not be exported as a biblical thickness or as a sealed CFD wall.
