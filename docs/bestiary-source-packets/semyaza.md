# Semyaza / Shemihazah — Watchers Source Packet

## Editorial judgment

Semyaza is firmly attested in the Book of Watchers as leader of the two hundred who descend on Hermon. The card's basic outline is sound, but “bound them by oath to corrupt humanity” overstates the speech: the Watchers propose taking wives and begetting children; Semyaza fears they will abandon the deed and leave him alone to bear the penalty; they answer by swearing together under mutual imprecations. His instruction in enchantments and root-cutting is separately explicit in 1 Enoch 8:3.

## Name boundary

- Forms vary across the textual tradition: Semjaza/Semyaza, Shemihazah, and related spellings.
- Aramaic fragments support a form commonly reconstructed as Šemîḥazah. Proposed meanings such as “the Name has seen” are uncertain and should not be asserted as a settled translation.
- The Greek *grēgoroi* and later “Grigori” label are not the wording of the Aramaic name and are unnecessary on the card.

## 1 Enoch 6–16

- 6:1–6: angels, “children of heaven,” desire human daughters. Semyaza, their leader, fears the others will not complete the deed and that he alone will pay for the great sin. All two hundred swear together and bind themselves by mutual imprecations on Mount Hermon; the mountain is etymologically linked in the narrative to their binding oath.
- 7: each takes a wife; collectively they teach charms, enchantments, root cutting, and plants. Their wives bear giants whose violence consumes the earth.
- 8:3 assigns Semyaza enchantments and root cuttings specifically.
- 9:7 calls him the one given authority to rule his associates; the heavenly intercessors accuse the group of defilement and revealing sins.
- 10:11–13: Michael is commanded to bind Semyaza and his associates after they witness their sons destroy one another, holding them in earth's valleys for seventy generations until final judgment and the abyss of fire.
- 12–14: the condemned Watchers ask Enoch to write their petition. Enoch finds them gathered, weeping with covered faces; their petition is refused, and they are told that they will not regain heaven.

Primary local witness: `docs/scribe-texts/bib/boe/boe009.md` through `boe019.md` (R. H. Charles public-domain translation). The later Manichaean *Book of Giants* tradition preserves Šhmyz’d as father of Ohya and Ahya, but this is not the Qumran Aramaic *Book of Giants* itself and is used only as a later corroborating branch, not as the controlling witness.

## Genesis and Book of Giants boundary

- Genesis 6:1–4 supplies sons of God, human women, and Nephilim, but names neither Semyaza nor Hermon and does not narrate the oath or forbidden teachings. The card must say “expanded in 1 Enoch,” not “implied name.”
- Semyaza's direct role in the surviving Qumran *Book of Giants* fragments is less complete than his Book of Watchers dossier. Later Manichaean fragments make Šhmyz’d father of Ohya and Ahya; that genealogy should remain text-layered.
- The Apkallu comparison is modern comparative interpretation, not eyewitness testimony, and is excluded from the primary-facing card.

## Artwork verdict

Replace generic fallen-angel armor with the oath at Hermon's summit: Semyaza stands at the center of a vast ring of humanlike heavenly beings in ancient robes, visibly hesitant as the others extend hands and swear together under mutual imprecation. The high limestone summit, cold wind, snow traces, and descending dusk establish Hermon. Their number should be suggested by ranks vanishing around the ridge rather than sacrificed to literal counting.

No anatomy is supplied in this passage. Required exclusions: black feather wings, horns, armor, weapons, glowing magic circle, occult runes, giants already present, or a lone commander coercing unwilling followers. Shared Ancient Witness encaustic/egg-tempera style.
