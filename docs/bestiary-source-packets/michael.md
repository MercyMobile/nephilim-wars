# Michael — source packet before dossier or artwork

Status: research pass only. No replacement artwork is approved or generated from this packet.

## Editorial rule

The witnesses are kept in chronological and textual layers. A trait in a later apocalypse is not silently projected backward into Daniel. A scholarly identification is not presented as a name written in a damaged scroll. Silence is evidence against decorative certainty, not permission for generic fantasy art.

## Name and Hebrew evidence

- Masoretic form: מִיכָאֵל (*mîkāʾēl*).
- BDB parses the name as “Who is like God?”: `/home/cisco/hebrew-lexicon/BrownDriverBriggs.xml`, entry `m.bu.ab`.
- Strong H4317 derives it from מִי (“who?”), the comparative particle, and אֵל (“God”): `/home/cisco/hebrew-lexicon/HebrewStrong.xml`.
- The name is a rhetorical question, not the dossier statement “one who is like God.”

## Layer 1 — Daniel

Primary passages: Daniel 10:13, 10:21, 12:1. Consulted across the local Hebrew/biblical collection, including `docs/scribe-texts/bib/tan`, `kjv`, `asv`, and `dar`.

What the witness actually supplies:

- Michael is “one of the chief princes,” not the only prince.
- He comes to assist the delayed heavenly messenger against the prince of Persia.
- He is “your prince,” addressing Daniel’s people.
- He is “the great prince who stands over/for the sons of your people.”
- At the unparalleled time of distress he arises or stands, and Daniel’s people who are written in the book escape.

What Daniel does **not** supply:

- No wings, halo, armor, color, weapon, hair, age, or facial description.
- The brilliant linen-clad figure in Daniel 10 is not named Michael. Its physical description must not be transferred to Michael as though the text says so.
- Daniel does not call Michael “archangel,” “commander of every angel,” or “psychopomp.”

Visual consequence: Daniel supports protection, high rank, territorial/covenantal association, and participation in heavenly conflict. It does not support a portrait.

## Layer 2 — 1 Enoch

Primary local edition: R. H. Charles translation in `docs/scribe-texts/bib/boe/`.

Passage record:

- 1 Enoch 9 (`boe012.md`): Michael, Uriel, Raphael, and Gabriel look down, witness bloodshed, hear the dead, and bring the earth’s case before the Most High.
- 1 Enoch 10:11–15 (`boe013.md`): Michael is commanded to bind Semyaza and his associates for seventy generations after they witness their sons destroy one another; the work belongs to cleansing the earth.
- 1 Enoch 20:5 (`boe023.md`): Michael is one of the holy angels who watch, set over “the best part of mankind” and, in Charles’s text, “over chaos.” The wording and textual history need to remain attached to this edition rather than normalized away.
- 1 Enoch 24:6 (`boe027.md`): Michael is the holy and honored angel who interprets the fragrant tree and mountain vision to Enoch.
- 1 Enoch 40:1–9 (`boe043.md`): Michael is one of four presences around the Lord of Spirits and is called merciful and long-suffering. The four presences are voices before the throne; Michael is not reduced to a battlefield role.
- 1 Enoch 54:6 (`boe057.md`): Michael, Gabriel, Raphael, and Phanuel seize the hosts of Azazel and cast them into the burning furnace at judgment.
- 1 Enoch 60:4–6 (`boe063.md`): Michael sends another holy angel to raise the collapsed visionary and explains mercy, punishment, and judgment.
- 1 Enoch 67–68 (`boe070.md`, `boe071.md`): Michael explains judgment and trembles with a softened heart at the severity of the sentence against the angels.
- 1 Enoch 69:13–25 (`boe072.md`): a chief asks Michael for the hidden divine name connected with the powerful oath; the oath is placed in Michael’s hand.
- 1 Enoch 71 (`boe074.md`): Michael appears among the principal angels in the heavenly assembly and accompanies the Head of Days.

Composite character supplied by this layer:

- Watcher from heaven, petitioner for human victims, binder of rebel Watchers, interpreter of mysteries, guardian of the elect portion of humanity, custodian of an oath, executor of judgment.
- Explicitly merciful, patient, emotionally affected by judgment, and concerned with human suffering.
- No physical anatomy is described in these Michael passages.

Visual consequence: an exclusively martial image is textually incomplete. Mercy, witness, intercession, interpretation, restraint, and grief are as important as force.

## Layer 3 — Dead Sea Scrolls

### 4Q529 — Words of Michael

Primary manuscript record: [Leon Levy Dead Sea Scrolls Digital Library, 4Q529](https://www.deadseascrolls.org.il/explore-the-archive/manuscript/4Q529-1?locale=en_US), with DJD 31 publication metadata.

The fragmentary Aramaic composition opens as words of a book spoken by Michael to the angels. Preserved/reconstructed material includes fiery troops or hosts, nine mountains arranged by directions, Gabriel, a vision, and a heavenly book in which the Lord of the age has written. Lacunae are extensive.

Visual consequence: fire-hosts, directional mountains, Gabriel, and the opened heavenly record belong to a genuine Michaelic vision scene, but damaged lines must not be filled with invented narrative certainty.

### 1QM — War Scroll

The War Scroll supplies the cosmic war of the Sons of Light against Belial and heavenly assistance to the earthly host. Some interpreters identify its Prince of Light with Michael. The surviving passage should **not** be cited as though “Michael” is plainly written wherever that identification is used.

Visual consequence: 1QM can support the larger two-level war setting after the identification is labeled as interpretive. It cannot alone authorize a named Michael portrait.

## Layer 4 — Jude and Revelation

- Jude 9 calls Michael “the archangel” and remembers his dispute with the devil concerning Moses’ body. Michael does not pronounce an abusive judgment from himself; he appeals to the Lord’s rebuke.
- Revelation 12:7–9 presents Michael and “his angels” fighting the dragon and its angels until the accuser is cast down.

Visual consequence:

- Jude emphasizes delegated authority and restraint, not swagger or self-derived power.
- Revelation establishes command of an angelic host and combat against a dragon, but gives no physical description of Michael. The dragon’s anatomy is described; Michael’s is not.

## Layer 5 — Testament of Abraham

Primary text consulted in both recensions: [Version I](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Ante-Nicene_Fathers/Volume_IX/The_Testament_of_Abraham/The_Testament_of_Abraham/Version_I) and [Version II](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Ante-Nicene_Fathers/Volume_IX/The_Testament_of_Abraham/The_Testament_of_Abraham/Version_II).

This later Jewish apocalypse finally gives material appearance and behavior:

- Michael is God’s chief-captain and messenger concerning Abraham’s death.
- In the long recension Abraham sees him approaching like a very comely warrior, bright as the sun, youthful, and more beautiful than ordinary men.
- He is humanlike enough to be welcomed as a traveler and offered an animal to ride, which he refuses; he walks with Abraham.
- The narrative calls him incorporeal, yet he accepts hospitality and has his feet washed.
- He weeps with Abraham and Isaac; his tears become precious stones.
- Sarah recognizes him as one of the three visitors once entertained by Abraham.
- He handles a cloud of light and a cherubic chariot for Abraham’s tour of the world.
- At Abraham’s death he receives the soul in divinely woven linen, accompanied by many angels.

Visual consequence: this witness authorizes a radiant youthful warrior-traveler without wings, a tender and tearful psychopomp, precious-stone tears, plain travel on foot, cloud-light, cherubic conveyance, and sacred linen. These are late-layer traits and must be labeled accordingly.

## Layer 6 — later magical and Solomonic material

Local examples occur throughout `docs/scribe-texts/grim/`, including *The Magus* (`grim/magus/ma247.md`), where Michael is assigned to Sunday/the Sun and conjured as the day’s chief ruler. Other grimoire strata assign colors, directions, planets, seals, and divine names.

Use rule:

- This material may fill a functional blank only when earlier witnesses are silent and the card is explicitly representing that later textual layer.
- Planetary gold, solar insignia, ritual seals, and ceremonial weaponry must never be smuggled into a Daniel/Enoch/Qumran image as timeless attributes.

## Conflicts and unresolved choices

1. **No portrait vs. late portrait:** Daniel, Enoch, Jude, Revelation, and 4Q529 leave Michael physically undescribed. The *Testament of Abraham* supplies a radiant youthful warrior form. Artwork must declare which layer it depicts.
2. **Warrior vs. merciful presence:** Revelation and Daniel foreground conflict; Enoch and the *Testament of Abraham* foreground mercy, grief, interpretation, hospitality, and care for souls. A complete dossier needs both.
3. **Named vs. identified:** 4Q529 names Michael. The War Scroll’s Prince of Light identification is interpretive and must remain marked.
4. **Embodied vs. incorporeal:** the *Testament of Abraham* calls Michael incorporeal while narrating feet, tears, walking, eating-context hospitality, and a warriorlike appearance. The artwork should preserve the apparition paradox rather than solve it with ordinary flesh or generic wings.
