# Lilith in 4Q510 — Songs of the Sage Source Packet

## Editorial judgment

4Q510 fragment 1 does not describe a brood of childbirth demons. It names singular **Lilith** (לילית) inside a catalogue of hostile spirits terrified and driven back by the Maskil's proclamation of God's glorious splendor. The surviving lines provide function and company, but no anatomy, biography, sexual role, or attack on mothers and infants.

## Primary manuscript witness: 4Q510 fragment 1

- The speaker identifies himself as **the Maskil**—the instructor/sage—and proclaims the majesty of God's radiance.
- His stated purpose is to frighten and terrify hostile spirits.
- The damaged catalogue includes spirits of destroying/ravaging angels, spirits of the bastards, demons, **Lilith**, howlers, desert creatures, and beings that strike without warning.
- These assailants lead the spirit of understanding astray and desolate the heart during the present dominion of wickedness and the appointed humiliations of the Sons of Light.
- The text frames that evil interval as bounded rather than eternal.

The direct transcription reads לילית, singular. This packet follows the Hebrew transcription published from M. Baillet, *Qumrân Grotte 4.III* (DJD VII), and García Martínez–Tigchelaar, *Dead Sea Scrolls Study Edition*. Qumran-Digital's manuscript transcription likewise reconstructs `[שד אים] לילי[ת]` where 4Q511 supplies parallel material.

## Textual boundaries

- “Lilitu,” “plural wind-demons,” and “threat to childbearing” are not what this fragment says.
- The fragment does not call Lilith a queen, Adam's wife, mother of demons, succubus, seductress, or killer of infants.
- Mesopotamian comparanda may illuminate the older name-family, but cannot substitute a lilītu dossier for this Qumran witness.
- “Apotropaic liturgical song” is the safest genre description. Scholars discuss magic, exorcism, and communal liturgy, but the text's own mechanism is proclamation of divine splendor—not a narrated binding, amulet, or use of secret names.
- The nearby “spirits of the bastards” can be read within the Qumran/Enochic world of malign spirits, but the line does not identify Lilith as one of those spirits or explain her origin.

## Artwork verdict

Show the textual action rather than inventing Lilith's body: a desert-community Maskil in a lamplit cave or rock-cut gathering place proclaims a hymn as a restrained, radiant glory presses an encircling company of hostile presences back into darkness. Within that company, Lilith is one singular elusive female-associated night presence beside indistinct demons, an uncanny howler, and jackal/desert forms. She must not dominate the scene.

Required exclusions: childbirth, infant, nursery, Adam or Eden, multiple female “Lilitu,” crown, throne, seduction, sexualized anatomy, wings, serpent tail, magical circle, pentagram, pseudo-Hebrew, or an explicit bodily design unsupported by the fragment. Shared Ancient Witness encaustic/egg-tempera style.
