# Azrael / the Angel of Death — Rabbinic Witness Source Packet

## Editorial judgment

The ancient Jewish witnesses describe an unnamed **Angel of Death**; they do not identify him as Azrael. The card may retain “Azrael” as its familiar catalog key, but its visible dossier must lead with *malʾakh ha-mavet* and explicitly mark Azrael as a later name. The supposed compassionate psychopomp and body covered in one eye and tongue for every person are not established by the controlling sources.

## Name boundary

- Hebrew title: מלאך המוות, *malʾakh ha-mavet*, “Angel of Death.”
- “Azrael” (Arabic ʿAzrāʾīl/ʿIzrāʾīl) is not found in the Hebrew Bible, Talmud, Qur’an, or sound early hadith as the angel's proper name. Qur’an 32:11 likewise says only “the Angel of Death.”
- Hebrew עזריאל is normally *Azriel*, “God is my help,” and occurs as a human name; writing it as though the Talmud names the death angel is misleading.

## Hebrew Bible

- Exodus 12:23 speaks of “the destroyer” (*ha-mashḥit*) being prevented from entering the blood-marked houses. It supplies neither the name Azrael nor the later deathbed anatomy.
- 2 Samuel 24:16 / 1 Chronicles 21:15–16 depicts YHWH's angel stretching a hand over Jerusalem in plague; Chronicles gives the messenger a drawn sword. Again, no proper name is supplied.

## Babylonian Talmud

- *Avodah Zarah* 20b: when a sick person is dying, the Angel of Death stands above the head with drawn sword and a drop of poison at its tip. At the sight, the person opens the mouth; the drop enters, producing death, pallor, and corruption. This is the strongest visual witness.
- *Bava Batra* 16a: Resh Lakish identifies Satan, the evil inclination, and the Angel of Death as one sequence of tempter, accuser, and taker of life. This is one rabbinic teaching, not a license to collapse every biblical satan figure into a single later biography.
- *Shabbat* 89a: the Angel of Death gives Moses the secret that incense checks the plague, inferred from Aaron standing between dead and living.
- *Sukkah* 53a: the Angel of Death tells Solomon that the two scribes he seeks are destined to die at Luz; Solomon's attempt to send them away brings them to the appointed place.

## Artwork verdict

Replace the romantic hooded psychopomp with the deathbed scene of *Avodah Zarah* 20b: a sick person lies in a modest late-antique Jewish interior; above the head stands an austere humanlike messenger holding a drawn sword, with one visible dark drop suspended from its tip. The dying person's terrified gaze and opened mouth must convey the text without gore.

No wings or special anatomy are specified. Required exclusions: scythe, skeleton, black European monk's robe, comforting embrace, soul carried as a glowing child, ledger of every living name, body covered in eyes and tongues, and labeling the figure by the name Azrael inside the scene. Shared Ancient Witness encaustic/egg-tempera style.
