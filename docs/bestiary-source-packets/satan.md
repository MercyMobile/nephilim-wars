# The Satan — Hebrew Bible and Life of Adam and Eve Source Packet

## Editorial judgment

This card must not flatten every adversarial figure into one timeless biography. In Job and Zechariah, *ha-satan* is “the adversary/accuser,” a member of the heavenly court who acts only within YHWH's permission. The *Life of Adam and Eve* supplies a later first-person fall story: the devil claims he refused Michael's command to bow before Adam, exalted himself, and was expelled. Revelation supplies still another apocalyptic dragon tradition. “Sammael” is not named in these controlling passages and must not be placed in the heading as a synonym.

## Primary witnesses

- Job 1–2: the satan comes among the sons of God, challenges Job's disinterested piety, receives strictly bounded permission, and strikes Job's possessions and body but must spare his life.
- Zechariah 3:1–2: the satan stands at Joshua the high priest's right hand to accuse him and is rebuked by YHWH.
- 1 Chronicles 21:1 uses *satan* without the article as the one who incites David to number Israel, while 2 Samuel 24:1 assigns the incitement to YHWH's anger—an important textual tension, not grounds for inventing a complete biography.
- Greek *Life of Adam and Eve* 12–16: the devil tells Adam that Michael commanded the angels to worship the image of God in Adam. He refused to bow to a younger being, said he would set his seat above heaven's stars, and was banished with the angels under him. This is the direct source for the pride-and-refusal story on this card.
- Revelation 12 identifies the dragon as the ancient serpent, Devil, and Satan, the deceiver and accuser cast down after war with Michael; this apocalyptic image is later than Job and must not be projected backward into Job's court scene.

## Boundaries

- Isaiah 14 addresses the king of Babylon; its taunt must not be listed as a direct Satan biography.
- Sammael/Samael belongs to later Jewish traditions and is not automatically identical with Job's satan, Adam and Eve's devil, or Revelation's dragon.
- “Father of Lies” and “ruler of this world” are Johannine titles, but should not be silently inserted into the earlier Hebrew scene.
- The serpent in Genesis is not named Satan there. The identification is explicit only in later witnesses such as Revelation.

## Artwork verdict

Replace the horned demon prince with Job's courtroom witness: an austere humanlike accuser standing among the sons of God before overwhelming unfigured divine radiance, speaking and gesturing toward the earthly Job visible far below. He should look like a severe court officer, not a rival god. No wings or anatomy are given.

Required exclusions: horns, goat legs, red skin, bat wings, pitchfork, crown of hell, Isaiah's Babylonian king imagery, Sammael labels, Eden's serpent, or the Revelation dragon within this Job scene. Shared encaustic/egg-tempera Ancient Witness style.
