# Kokabiel — Constellation Instruction Source Packet

## Primary witness

- **1 Enoch 6:7:** Kokabiel/Kokabiel appears among the chiefs of the two hundred Watchers under the Hermon oath. The name transparently contains the Semitic word for star (*kokab/kôkāb*) and the divine element *-el*.
- **1 Enoch 8:3:** the teaching catalogue assigns him the constellations—the ordered groupings or signs of the stars—beside Baraqel's astrologers and other Watchers' celestial disclosures.
- The compact witness says what knowledge was disclosed, not that Kokabiel created, owns, moves, or embodies the stars.

## Textual boundary

No early passage provides his appearance, armor, weapon, seal, planetary throne, number of attendant spirits, or individual combat. Later angelological and magical systems must not be used to turn “Star of God” into a cosmic superhero. The distinction from Baraqel is visual and textual: Kokabiel teaches recognition and ordering of star groups; Baraqel's card depicts correlating wandering heavenly signs.

## Artwork verdict

Show a memory lesson under the open sky. Kokabiel stands with ancient pupils around upright wooden sighting poles and a shallow water bowl reflecting the stars. He traces one constellation's shape only by moving his empty hand from star to star in the air—no glowing lines appear—while pupils memorize the pattern with knotted counting cords. The actual sky and reflected sky dominate, and his otherness remains a restrained rim light.

Required exclusions: drawn zodiac wheel, glowing constellation lines, horoscope glyphs, star crown, stars in hands, cosmic armor, wings, telescope, astrolabe, magic circle, readable diagram, modern astronomical notation. Shared Ancient Witness encaustic/egg-tempera style.
