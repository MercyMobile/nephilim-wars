# Uriel — Ancient Witness Source Packet

## Editorial judgment

The early witnesses do not make Uriel the named guardian of Eden, nor do they give him Genesis's revolving flaming sword. In 1 Enoch he is the seer's exacting guide through cosmic geography: he warns Noah, explains the prison of transgressing stars and angels, identifies the valley of judgment, records the names and laws of the stars, and teaches the measured courses of sun and moon. In 2 Esdras he answers Ezra's protest over Israel and the world's injustice, repeatedly exposing the limits of human comprehension while also comforting and physically restoring the seer.

## Name and boundary

- Hebrew form: אוּרִיאֵל (*Uri'el*), conventionally "God is my light" or "light/flame of God."
- The Hebrew Bible uses Uriel as a human personal name (for example 1 Chronicles 6:24; 15:5, 11; 2 Chronicles 13:2), not as the name of Eden's cherub.
- Genesis 3:24 names cherubim and the flame of a turning sword but no individual angel. Identifying Uriel there is later reception and cannot be listed as biblical attestation.

## 1 Enoch: warning, judgment, and cosmic measure

### Appeal and flood warning (1 Enoch 9–10)

- Uriel joins Michael, Raphael, and Gabriel in seeing the bloodshed and lawlessness produced by the Watchers' corruption and bringing the dead's cry before the Most High.
- God sends Uriel to Noah: reveal the approaching destruction, tell him to hide himself, and instruct him so that his seed may be preserved.

### Guide of the forbidden places (1 Enoch 18–21, 27)

- Uriel explains the place where the angels who joined women await judgment and where their wives become sirens.
- He is chief over the seven transgressing stars bound like burning mountains in a chaotic place.
- He leads Enoch beside an abyss of descending columns of fire and identifies it as the prison of the angels.
- He identifies the accursed valley reserved for those who speak against God's glory.

### Office in the sevenfold list (1 Enoch 20:2)

R. H. Charles reads Uriel as the holy angel "over the world and over Tartarus." Richard Laurence's older translation reads "clamour and terror." The disagreement reflects an unstable textual/translation history; the card may confidently retain Uriel's demonstrated world-and-prison jurisdiction from the surrounding journey, but must disclose the variant rather than treating one English gloss as uncontested anatomy or title.

### Recorder and guide of the luminaries (1 Enoch 33; 72–82)

- Uriel shows Enoch the eastern portals of the stars, their names, courses, positions, times, months, laws, and companies; he also writes the account for him.
- The Astronomical Book calls Uriel guide/leader of the luminaries. He explains the portals, windows, chariots, winds, sun, moon, intercalary days, seasons, and the exact 364-day year.
- 1 Enoch 82:7–8 says the Lord subjected the host of heaven to Uriel and gave him power over night and day so that sun, moon, and stars give light.
- His "light" is therefore best represented by measured celestial order, not an invented handheld flame.

## 2 Esdras / 4 Ezra 4–10

- The sent angel names himself Uriel and answers Ezra's challenge about the way of the Most High.
- Uriel tests him with three impossibilities: weigh fire, measure wind, or recall yesterday. The point is disciplined epistemic boundary, not secret knowledge without limit.
- He explains that the age follows its divinely measured course; the righteous dead also asked "how long," and Uriel answered that the number must be fulfilled.
- He orders Ezra's fast and continues interpreting visions across the dialogue.
- When Ezra collapses "as one that had been dead" before the transformed Zion-woman vision, Uriel comes, looks upon him, takes him by the right hand, comforts him, and sets him on his feet (10:28–31).
- No wings, sword, book, flame, halo, or clothing description is supplied.

## Later Adam literature

The *Life of Adam and Eve* has Michael and Uriel bring linen garments, bury Adam and Abel in Paradise, and teach Seth and Eve burial practice. This gives Uriel a funerary service within Paradise, not guardianship of Eden's gate and not possession of the flaming sword.

## Solomonic and magical boundary

Later magical tables assign Uriel conflicting directions, elements, planets, colors, tools, and seals. These do not fill a genuine silence: 1 Enoch supplies an extensive office, and 2 Esdras supplies a human-facing encounter. They are excluded from the early portrait.

## Artwork verdict

Replace the generic flame-and-scroll archangel with the Astronomical Book encounter: an unwinged, humanlike Uriel beside Enoch at the eastern portals, physically indicating and recording the ordered emergence of stars while Enoch writes. Twelve monumental openings may be suggested as six eastern and six distant western correspondences, but the composition should privilege comprehensible measured order over fantasy machinery.

Required restraints:

- no wings, halo, flaming sword, handheld flame, armor, crown, or Solomonic seal;
- Uriel and Enoch use plausible ancient writing boards/tablets or scroll materials without modern instruments;
- stars, portals, sun/moon courses, and written reckoning establish 1 Enoch 33 and 72–82;
- cosmic geometry should look witnessed and numinous, not like a modern astronomy diagram;
- shared Ancient Witness Bestiary encaustic/egg-tempera narrative realism.
