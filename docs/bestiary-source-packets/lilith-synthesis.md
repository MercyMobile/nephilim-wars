# Lilith — Three-Witness Textual Map

## Editorial judgment

“Lilith” in the bestiary must function as an index, not as a fourth synthetic biography. Three witnesses now have dedicated cards and must remain visibly distinct:

1. **Isaiah 34:14:** singular feminine Lilith settles and finds rest in ruined Edom amid a gathered wilderness ecology.
2. **4Q510 fragment 1:** singular Lilith is named among hostile spirits terrified by the Maskil's proclamation of divine splendor.
3. **Alphabet of Ben Sira:** a much later medieval narrative makes Lilith Adam's first wife, recounts her departure by the Ineffable Name, and places three angels' oath-confrontation beside the sea.

## What cannot be fused

- Isaiah supplies neither Adam nor an attack on children.
- 4Q510 supplies neither plural “Lilitu” nor childbirth victims, sexuality, or a biography.
- Ben Sira supplies the Adam narrative and infant-threatening terms, but belongs to a medieval literary layer and cannot be projected backward into Isaiah or Qumran.
- Mesopotamian *lilītu* comparanda are relevant to the history of the name-family but do not turn the three Jewish texts into one continuous eyewitness dossier.
- “Rebellious feminine archetype,” demon queen, succubus, and modern empowerment/repression readings are reception history, not shared ancient testimony.

## Artwork verdict

The index art should make separation unmistakable through a three-panel ancient visual concordance: ruined Edom and a resting elusive night presence; the Qumran Maskil's radiance driving back a mixed spirit company; the Ben Sira sea confrontation with Lilith and exactly three angels. Repeat no fixed anatomy across panels. The image is a map of witnesses, not a portrait of one timeless demoness.

Required exclusions: glamour portrait, crown, throne, sexuality, serpent tail, modern gothic imagery, readable or pseudo-Hebrew, infant violence, or visual fusion of all three scenes into one biography. Shared Ancient Witness encaustic/egg-tempera style.
