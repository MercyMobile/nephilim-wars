# Gilgamesh and Ḥobabish — Book of Giants Name-Witness Packet

## Editorial judgment

The Aramaic Book of Giants unmistakably preserves the names Gilgamesh (גלגמיס/גלגמיש) and Ḥobabish (חובבש and damaged parallels), but its surviving passages are too broken to transfer the complete Babylonian Epic into their Qumran biographies. This card records two named giants in an Enochic assembly under judgment. It does not claim that the Qumran Gilgamesh is explicitly king of Uruk, two-thirds divine, Semyaza's son, or the slayer/companion of Qumran Ḥobabish.

## Qumran primary witness

- **4Q530 2 ii + 6–12:** a giant assembly and speech sequence survives in damaged form. Ohyah reports what Gilgamesh said to him; Ḥobabish then roars/raises his voice, and language of judgment follows. Reconstructions of speakers and line order vary.
- **4Q531 fragment 17/22d:** the spelling גלגמיש preserves Gilgamesh's name in another damaged witness.
- **4Q203 fragment 3:** חובבש preserves Ḥobabish's name among fragmentary narrative lines.
- The wider Book of Giants recounts ominous dreams, a gathering of giants, Mahaway's journey to Enoch, and an oracle of judgment. The texts name Ohyah, Hahyah, Mahaway, Gilgamesh, and Ḥobabish, but not every named figure can be assigned every preserved action.

## Babylonian namesake boundary

- The Standard Babylonian Epic's Gilgamesh is king of Uruk, son of Ninsun, exceptionally divine by descent, oppressor and builder, companion of Enkidu, killer of Humbaba and the Bull of Heaven, mourner, flood-story hearer, and failed seeker of bodily immortality.
- Humbaba is Enlil's terrifying guardian of the Cedar Forest. Gilgamesh and Enkidu kill him; the Epic does not present “Gilgamesh and Humbaba” as fellow giants receiving Enoch's oracle.
- The Qumran spellings strongly evoke these famous Mesopotamian names and show literary reception. Identity and motif-borrowing may be argued, but missing Qumran biography cannot be silently reconstructed from the Epic.
- “Right of first night” is not a secure literal description of the Epic's damaged oppression lines and is excluded.
- The meanings of Gilgamesh's and Ḥobabish's names are uncertain; “Exposed Ancestor” should not be asserted as translation.

## Artwork verdict

Show a fragmentary Enochic assembly, not Uruk and not the Cedar Forest combat. In a vast antediluvian stone hall open to storm, several gigantic humanlike figures gather in dread around broken tablets bearing only abstract marks. One giant—Gilgamesh—is speaking urgently to Ohyah; another massive, wild-haired giant—Ḥobabish—throws back his head in a terrible roar. Above them, a dream-sign of uprooted trees and a descending flood-shadow foretells judgment. Their exact parentage and anatomy remain unstated; scale comes from architecture and ordinary vessels.

Required exclusions: crown of Uruk, ziggurat identification, Semyaza as father, two-thirds-divine emblem, Gilgamesh fighting or befriending Ḥobabish, Cedar Forest, Enkidu, right-of-first-night scene, horned monsters, readable cuneiform/Hebrew, modern fantasy armor. Shared Ancient Witness encaustic/egg-tempera style.
