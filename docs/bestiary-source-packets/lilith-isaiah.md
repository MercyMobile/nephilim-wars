# Lilith in Isaiah 34:14 — Edom Desolation Source Packet

## Editorial judgment

Isaiah 34:14 is the Hebrew Bible's sole occurrence of לִילִית (*lîlît*). The verse gives no personal history and does not connect its inhabitant of ruined Edom with Adam, infants, amulets, or a demon queen. Yet translating it as a mere ordinary animal also erases the Hebrew term's ancient-night-being resonance. The image should preserve the poem's deliberate borderland: an uncanny female-associated night presence at rest amid a full ecology of desolation.

## Masoretic witness: Isaiah 34:9–17

- Edom's streams become pitch, its dust sulfur, and its land burning pitch; smoke rises as the land lies waste (34:9–10).
- Birds possess it, YHWH stretches over it the line of *tohu* and stones/plummet of *bohu*, and thorns, nettles, and thistles overtake palaces and fortresses (34:11–13).
- Desert creatures meet, jackals/wild creatures answer one another, and a goat-being cries to its fellow. “There also Lilith shall rest/settle, and find for herself a resting place” (34:14).
- A snake nests and hatches; birds of prey gather with mates. YHWH's mouth commands and spirit gathers the entire company, allotting the land to them (34:15–17).

## Versional evidence and uncertainty

- The Septuagint does not transliterate Lilith; its best-known Greek textual tradition renders the creature with *onokentauros* language. Later translations variously use night monster, night hag, screech owl, or Lilith.
- The immediate grammar is singular and feminine: she finds for herself a place of rest. “Nameless in narrative” should not be confused with “not a name or kind-term at all.”
- A Mesopotamian relationship is plausible, but Isaiah does not reproduce a complete Akkadian lilītu dossier. No wind, childbirth attack, sexuality, wings, talons, or specific anatomy appears in the verse.
- The date and compositional history of Isaiah 34 are scholarly questions, not eyewitness claims, and need not be asserted on the card.

## Artwork verdict

Replace any glamorous solitary demoness with Isaiah's whole judged landscape: moonlit ruined Edomite fortifications consumed by thorn and nettle; jackals, ostrich-like birds, a goat-being, snake nest, raven/raptor silhouettes, and at the protected threshold of a collapsed chamber one elusive humanlike female night presence settling into rest. She is one inhabitant among the gathered creatures, not their ruler.

Required exclusions: Adam, Eden, infants, amulet, crown, throne, sexuality, serpent tail, bat wings, talons, red skin, modern gothic costume, solitary character portrait, or a claim to exact anatomy. Shared Ancient Witness encaustic/egg-tempera style.
