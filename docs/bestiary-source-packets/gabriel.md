# Gabriel — Ancient Witness Source Packet

## Editorial judgment

Gabriel's dossier must begin with Daniel, the earliest surviving text to name him, and not with the later artistic convention of a winged annunciator. Daniel repeatedly calls him "the man Gabriel" or describes him as having the appearance of a man. His work is interpretation: he approaches a terrified seer, touches him, sets him upright, and gives understanding. Luke preserves the same personal identity and delegated office: Gabriel stands in God's presence and is sent to speak, first beside the incense altar and then inside Mary's dwelling. Neither witness supplies wings, halo, trumpet, lily, armor, hair color, or a color of clothing.

The Enochic corpus broadens the office sharply. Gabriel joins the appeal over human bloodshed, receives the command to turn the Watchers' children against one another, is placed over Paradise, serpents, cherubim, and "all the powers," and participates in the fiery punishment of Azazel's hosts. Later rabbinic texts remember him as an advocate, marker of judgment, master of fire, and destroying angel. Those traditions belong in the dossier, but must be dated and must not overwrite Daniel's witnessed form.

## Name and language

- Hebrew: גַּבְרִיאֵל (*Gavri'el*).
- Hebrew Strong H1403 derives the name from גֶּבֶר / גָּבַר and אֵל and glosses it "man of God." The first element can carry the sense of man, mighty man, or strength; consequently "mighty one/man of God" is safer than the card's former unqualified "God is my strength."

## Earliest named witness: Daniel 8–9

### Daniel 8:15–19

- Daniel sees one standing before him "as the appearance of a man."
- A man's voice from between the banks of the Ulai commands: "Gabriel, make this man to understand the vision."
- Gabriel comes near. Daniel is frightened and falls on his face.
- When Daniel sinks into a stupor with his face to the ground, Gabriel touches him and sets him upright.
- Gabriel interprets the ram-and-goat vision as concerning the appointed end.

This is the strongest early visual control: manlike appearance, approach, speech, touch, and the restoration of a collapsed human witness. The text supplies no wings or weapons.

### Daniel 9:20–23

- During Daniel's prayer and confession, "the man Gabriel" from the former vision reaches him about the time of the evening offering.
- The ASV renders the difficult Hebrew phrase מֻעָף בִּיעָף as "being caused to fly swiftly." The phrase concerns Gabriel's arrival; it does not describe wings.
- Gabriel touches Daniel, instructs him, and says he has come to give wisdom and understanding because Daniel is greatly beloved.

Primary-text copy consulted: `docs/scribe-texts/bib/asv/dan.md`. Hebrew witnesses consulted: `docs/scribe-texts/bib/tan/dan008.md` and `dan009.md`.

### Boundary at Daniel 10

Daniel 10 contains a radiant, linen-clad manlike figure and later mentions Michael, but never names Gabriel. That figure's body like beryl, lightning face, fiery eyes, bronze limbs, and multitude-like voice therefore cannot be assigned to Gabriel. Similarity of function or modern illustration is not identification.

## Luke 1: the one who stands in God's presence

### Zechariah at the incense altar (Luke 1:11–20)

- An angel stands on the right side of the altar of incense; Zechariah is troubled and fear falls upon him.
- The angel announces John's birth and vocation.
- He identifies himself: "I am Gabriel, that stand in the presence of God; and I was sent to speak unto thee."
- Because Zechariah does not believe his words, Gabriel declares him unable to speak until their fulfillment.

### Mary at Nazareth (Luke 1:26–38)

- Gabriel is sent by God to Nazareth.
- He enters where Mary is and speaks the annunciation concerning Jesus.
- Mary is troubled by the greeting, not by any described monstrous anatomy; Gabriel tells her not to fear.
- Again the text supplies no wings, halo, trumpet, lily, armor, or visual anatomy.

Primary-text copy consulted: `docs/scribe-texts/bib/asv/luk.md`.

## 1 Enoch: power, guardianship, and judgment

### 1 Enoch 9

Michael, Uriel, Raphael, and Gabriel look down, see the earth filled with blood and lawlessness, hear the dead crying at heaven's gates, and bring the human cause before the Most High. Gabriel is therefore a witness and petitioner before he becomes an executor.

### 1 Enoch 10:9–10

God commands Gabriel to proceed against the bastards, reprobates, and children of the Watchers. He is to send them against one another so that they destroy each other in battle, and he is forbidden to grant their fathers' request for their long life. This is Gabriel's clearest Enochic martial act; he does not merely deliver news.

### 1 Enoch 20:7

In the list of holy angels who watch, Gabriel is "over Paradise and the serpents and the Cherubim." This is an assigned jurisdiction, not a portrait of Gabriel as serpent or cherub.

### 1 Enoch 40:3–9

Four presences praise before the Lord of Spirits. The third voice prays and intercedes for earth's inhabitants; the interpreting angel then names the third presence Gabriel and says he is set over "all the powers." The sequential identification strongly associates Gabriel with that third voice, but the dossier keeps the wording cautious because the identification follows the four speeches rather than interrupting each one.

### 1 Enoch 54:6 and 71:7–13

- Gabriel, Michael, Raphael, and Phanuel seize Azazel's hosts and cast them into a burning furnace at the great judgment.
- Gabriel is among the four who enter and leave the fiery/crystalline heavenly house with innumerable holy angels and the Head of Days.
- The white garments and snow-bright faces nearby in chapter 71 describe a collective heavenly company, not an individualized Gabriel portrait.

Local primary-text translation consulted: R. H. Charles, `docs/scribe-texts/bib/boe/boe012.md`, `boe013.md`, `boe023.md`, `boe043.md`, `boe057.md`, and `boe074.md`. The older Enoch translation in `docs/scribe-texts/bib/bep/` was checked for variant wording; unstable names and readings are not silently harmonized.

## Dead Sea Scroll witness

### 4Q529, Words of Michael

The very fragmentary Aramaic composition called *Words of Michael* preserves Gabriel's name in a damaged visionary context involving fire and mountains. It is evidence that Gabriel belonged to the same Second Temple angelic horizon as Michael, but its broken syntax cannot sustain a detailed office or appearance. No visual feature is taken from it.

- Manuscript: 4Q529, fragment 1.
- Primary manuscript images: Leon Levy Dead Sea Scrolls Digital Library, `https://www.deadseascrolls.org.il/explore-the-archive/manuscript/4Q529-1?locale=en_US`.
- Rule applied: damaged clauses are acknowledged rather than completed from modern paraphrase.

## Later rabbinic reception

These witnesses preserve ancient Jewish reception but are later than Daniel, Luke, and much of 1 Enoch. Their identifications are not projected backward into unnamed biblical angels.

- **Shabbat 55a:** Gabriel is told to mark the righteous with ink and the wicked with blood in an interpretation of Ezekiel 9 (`docs/scribe-texts/jud/t01/t0113.md`).
- **Pesachim 118a–b:** Gabriel calls himself master/prince of fire and cools the furnace from within while intensifying it without for Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah; the biblical Daniel 3 messenger remains unnamed (`docs/scribe-texts/jud/t03/psc14.md`).
- **Sanhedrin traditions:** Gabriel appears as advocate for Israel and as executor against Sennacherib's army, with divergent accounts of hand, finger, breath, song, or primordial sickle (`docs/scribe-texts/jud/t08/t0809.md`; `t0814.md`). The divergence is part of the evidence and forbids one confident weapon portrait.
- Other midrashic notices place Gabriel at the right among Abraham's three visitors or as an attendant at Adam and Eve's wedding. These are reception identifications, not explicit details in Genesis.

## Solomonic and magical gap-fillers

The *Key of Solomon*, *The Magus*, and related later magical compilations assign Gabriel such correspondences as west, Monday, Moon, water, colors, perfumes, and seals. The systems vary and postdate the early witnesses by many centuries. Because the early record already supplies Gabriel's roles and a manlike apparition, these correspondences are not needed to fill an actual gap and are excluded from the core card and artwork.

## Artwork verdict

Replace any generic winged herald portrait. The most defensible scene is Daniel 8 at the Ulai: Gabriel in an unwinged, humanlike manifestation approaches and raises the fallen Daniel by touch while the ram-and-goat vision recedes behind them. This depicts the earliest named visual witness and Gabriel's central action—making a human understand—without importing the unnamed splendor of Daniel 10 or later church iconography.

Required restraints:

- no visible wings, halo, trumpet, lily, armor, sword, planetary sigil, or Solomonic seal;
- Gabriel must read as a manlike but unsettling heavenly envoy, not a soft decorative court angel;
- Daniel is face-down or being set upright by Gabriel's touch;
- the Ulai river and the ram/goat vision establish the witnessed scene;
- radiance may be restrained and environmental, but Daniel 10's beryl body, lightning face, fire eyes, and bronze limbs must not be copied;
- maintain the shared Ancient Witness Bestiary medium: historically grounded narrative realism with encaustic/egg-tempera pigment character, tactile ancient materials, and restrained sacred strangeness.
