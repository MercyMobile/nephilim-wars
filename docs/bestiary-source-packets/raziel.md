# Raziel — Sefer Raziel and the Adam Legend Source Packet

## Editorial judgment

Raziel has no biblical, Dead Sea Scrolls, Apocryphal, or early Pseudepigraphal dossier. His card belongs openly to medieval Jewish mystical literature. The received *Sefer Raziel ha-Malakh* is a composite compilation, probably assembled in the thirteenth century from earlier mystical and magical materials. Its opening Adam legend can govern the card, but it must not be backdated into Genesis or presented as an eyewitness text from antiquity.

## Name boundary

- Hebrew: רזיאל, conventionally parsed from *raz* (רז, "secret" or "mystery") and *El* (אל, "God"): "Mystery/Secret of God."
- The work itself explains functional angel names and describes Raziel as the one who transmitted mysteries (*raz*) to Adam.
- Genesis names neither Raziel nor a book revealed to Adam.

## Sefer Raziel / the Book of Raziel tradition

- After expulsion, Adam prays for knowledge of what will befall him and later generations.
- On the third day, beside the river flowing from Paradise and in the heat of day, Raziel appears carrying a book. He says Adam's prayer was heard and that he was commissioned to teach him pure words and deep understanding.
- Raziel reads aloud. Adam falls down in fear; the angel tells him to rise, take the book, learn from it, and teach those worthy of its knowledge.
- When Adam takes it, flame rises beside the river and Raziel ascends in it. The book concerns future calamity and abundance, times and seasons, angelic invocation, and mysteries accessible only in purity and reverence.

The narrative is preserved in the *Sefer Raziel* tradition and retold from it in Louis Ginzberg's *Legends of the Jews*, local witness `docs/scribe-texts/jud/loj/loj104.md` under “The Book of Raziel.” The direct functional-name statement is quoted from *Sefer Raziel* in `docs/scribe-texts/jud/jms/jms19.md`.

## Date and composition

- *Sefer Raziel* is not a unitary book authored in primordial time. The received compilation is generally placed in the thirteenth century and contains earlier Geonic mystical material: local source history `docs/scribe-texts/jud/jms/jms39.md`.
- A local survey associates the present form of *Sode Razaya* and the Raziel book with Eleazar of Worms and older midrashic materials: `docs/scribe-texts/bib/coj/coj003.md`.
- “Stands beside the throne recording every divine secret” is not established by the controlling Adam narrative and is excluded. The Zohar is not needed to fill this scene.
- Crazy Alchemist is useful for discovery only and supplies no controlling claim here. The storybook supplies no earlier witness that overrides the medieval textual boundary.

## Artwork verdict

Replace the generic celestial occultist with the narrative's actual encounter: exiled Adam beside the river from Eden in harsh midday heat; Raziel approaches as a messenger carrying an inscribed codex or bound leaves, reads from it, and extends it toward Adam as Adam recoils or kneels in fear. A sudden column of flame at the riverbank anticipates the angel's ascent.

No physical anatomy is supplied, so the figure should remain restrained and humanlike rather than acquire invented many-eyed or armored anatomy. Required exclusions: throne room, cosmic library, modern occult glyphs, Kabbalistic Tree of Life, Metatron cube, planets orbiting the body, ceremonial-magic costume, or any implication that Genesis depicts the event. Shared Ancient Witness encaustic/egg-tempera style; medieval manuscript material culture should be visible without making the scene look modern.
