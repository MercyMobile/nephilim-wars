# Asmodeus — Tobit and Later Witnesses Source Packet

## Editorial judgment

The card's controlling scene is Tobit 3, 6, and 8. Tobit calls Asmodeus an evil spirit, reports seven bridegrooms killed before consummation, and narrates his expulsion by fish-organ smoke followed by Raphael's pursuit and binding in Egypt. It does **not** call him a king, give him anatomy, or explain the murders as generic “lust and rage.” Later Solomonic and Talmudic texts receive the name into different stories and must remain labeled layers.

## Primary witness: Tobit

- **Tobit 3:7–8:** Sarah, daughter of Raguel in Ecbatana, is falsely taunted because Asmodeus the evil spirit killed seven husbands before they lay with her.
- **Tobit 3:16–17:** God hears the prayers of Tobit and Sarah and sends Raphael to heal both households, give Sarah to Tobias, and bind Asmodeus.
- **Tobit 6:4–8, 13–17:** Raphael, traveling under the name Azariah, has Tobias preserve a fish's heart, liver, and gall. Smoke from heart and liver drives a troubling evil spirit away. Tobias reports that the spirit loves Sarah and harms only men who approach her; this is character speech, not an omniscient explanation.
- **Tobit 8:1–4:** In the marriage chamber Tobias burns the heart and liver on incense ashes. The spirit smells it and flees to the far/upper parts of Egypt; Raphael pursues and binds him. Tobias and Sarah then rise and pray.

Greek textual recensions differ in detail and wording, but the narrative spine is stable: seven dead bridegrooms, Raphael's instruction, fish-organ smoke, flight, and restraint. ## Later labeled witnesses

- **Testament of Solomon 22–24:** Asmodeus tells Solomon that he plots against newlyweds, separates couples by calamities, wastes virgins' beauty, estranges hearts, and drives married men toward others and murderous acts. He names Raphael as his frustrating angel and fish liver/gall smoke as his repellent. Solomon binds and puts him to labor. This expands Tobit's marriage-disruptor tradition; it is not evidence for Tobit's visual anatomy.
- **Babylonian Talmud, Gittin 68a–b:** Ashmedai is “king of the demons” in the shamir narrative. Benaiah traps him with wine and restrains him by a chain bearing the divine Name; the story develops into a contest with Solomon. This title and episode belong to the Talmudic Ashmedai layer, not the Book of Tobit.
- Later grimoires give Asmoday ranks, seals, composite bodies, mounts, and legions. Those are excluded from this ancient-source card and should only appear in a separately sourced grimoire entry if ever needed.

## Name boundary

Tobit transmits Greek Ἀσμοδαῖος; rabbinic Aramaic/Hebrew uses אשמדאי. A connection with Iranian *aēšma daēva* is widely proposed and helps explain later “wrath” glosses, but Tobit itself offers no etymology. “Destroyer” should not be presented as its direct translation.

## Artwork verdict

Show Tobit 8's witnessed action. In a Median/Jewish marriage chamber, Tobias kneels at a small brazier placing the fish heart and liver onto incense ash while Sarah stands behind him in prayer. The smoke and warm sacred light force one anatomically unresolved dark spirit through the doorway toward a distant desert horizon; Raphael, still in a traveler's plain robe but revealed through restrained radiance and modest wings, moves in pursuit. The horror is broken, and prayer—not erotic spectacle—is the human center.

Required exclusions: demon king regalia, throne, three heads, bull/ram/goose chimera, dragon mount, grimoire seal, lustful tableau, attack on Sarah, dead bodies, gore, horns, red skin, modern occult symbols, readable or pseudo-writing. Shared Ancient Witness encaustic/egg-tempera style.
