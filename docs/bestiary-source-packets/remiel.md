# Remiel / Ramiel — Ancient Witness Source Packet

## Editorial judgment

The secure early witnesses give two related offices: 1 Enoch lists Remiel over those who rise, while 2 Baruch names Ramiel as the angel over true visions who interprets Baruch's alternating dark- and bright-water vision of history. The old card wrongly assigns Uriel's answer in 2 Esdras 4:36 to Remiel and turns the terse resurrection office into an unattested scene of guiding individual souls.

## 1 Enoch 20:8

In the list of seven holy angels who watch, Remiel is the one "whom God set over those who rise." The line supports delegated oversight connected with rising/resurrection. It supplies no trumpet, graveyard, individual soul-guidance, clothing, wings, or anatomy.

Primary witness: `docs/scribe-texts/bib/boe/boe023.md`.

## 2 Baruch 53–76

Baruch sees a great cloud ascending from the sea, filled with white and black waters. It rains twelve successive alternating dark and bright waters, followed by blacker waters and then lightning that illuminates and heals the earth. In 55:3, the angel Ramiel, "who presides over true visions," is sent to interpret it. Ramiel explains the waters as successive epochs of corruption and righteousness from Adam through the flood, Abraham, Moses, the judges, David and Solomon, Hezekiah, Josiah, and the final tribulation and anointed age. The angel's office is interpretation of truthful historical apocalypse, not generic fortune-telling.

Primary public-domain translation checked: R. H. Charles, *The Apocalypse of Baruch*, chapter 55 onward, available at `https://www.pseudepigrapha.com/pseudepigrapha/2Baruch.html`. The local library's *Biblical Antiquities* apparatus independently preserves the comparison "Ramiel who presides over true visions" at `docs/scribe-texts/bib/bap/bap11.md`.

## 2 Esdras correction

The angel answering Ezra and the waiting righteous in 2 Esdras 4 is explicitly Uriel (4:1, 36). It is not Remiel. The old citation and claim are removed rather than harmonized through the later Jeremiel name.

Source: `docs/scribe-texts/bib/apo/es2004.md`.

## Name boundary

Remiel, Ramiel, Ramael, and Jeremiel are often associated in later lists, but the equivalence is not uniform. "Thunder of God" is not secure enough to control the portrait; Ramiel's actual 2 Baruch action and Remiel's Enochic office take priority. Biblical Jeremiel in Jeremiah 36:26 is a human royal son, not this angel.

## Later boundary

No Solomonic tool, color, planet, seal, or direction is necessary. Crazy Alchemist was used as discovery only; the storybook supplies no controlling Remiel witness.

## Artwork verdict

Replace the generic resurrection angel with the directly witnessed 2 Baruch interpretation: humanlike Ramiel beside Baruch as the sea-cloud releases distinct black and white waters across a symbolic landscape, with the final lightning bringing light and healing. The scene is visionary rather than meteorological realism. Humanlike anatomy remains a conservative inference because the text does not describe Ramiel's body.

Required restraints: no trumpet, graveyard, floating souls, wings, halo, armor, sword, thunderbolt weapon, seal, or planetary emblem; alternating waters and Baruch's attentive reception must be clear; shared encaustic/egg-tempera Ancient Witness style.
