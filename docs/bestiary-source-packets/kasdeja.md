# Kasdeja — Malignant Strokes Source Packet

## Primary witness: 1 Enoch 69:12

Kasdeja is the fifth figure in the later disclosure list. The transmitted passage charges him with showing humanity:

- the wicked smitings/blows of spirits and demons;
- the smiting of the embryo in the womb so that it passes away;
- a damaged or disputed phrase often rendered a smiting of the soul;
- the bites of the serpent;
- strokes that occur through noontide heat, associated with a named “son of the serpent” in the Ethiopic tradition.

The local primary translation is `docs/scribe-texts/bib/boe/boe072.md`; its punctuation and bracketed uncertainty should not be erased. This is a catalogue of disclosed harms, not a narrated attack performed before an eyewitness.

## Textual boundary

The passage does not give Kasdeja serpent anatomy, make him the serpent of Eden, describe an abortion rite, or provide a complete pharmacology. “Teacher” means revealer of malignant workings; it does not establish whether each injury is a spell, poison, demon, disease, or ancient explanatory category. No Solomonic rank, seal, weapon, face, or battle belongs here.

## Artwork verdict

Use a grim demonstration without depicting a victim. In a scorched noon courtyard, a plain humanlike Watcher shows alarmed pupils three linked signs of harm on a stone work surface: a live desert viper safely held under a forked branch, a clay human figurine cracked at its middle, and a wilted plant under brutal sunlight. At the edge, an empty shaded pallet and water jar suggest unseen sufferers. Thin oppressive shadow presences gather at the wall, embodying “strokes of spirits” without anatomy.

Required exclusions: pregnant body, fetus, miscarriage scene, blood, gore, attack in progress, serpent-man body, Eden, magic circle, sigils, readable spell, medicine-man costume, grimoire, fantasy armor, weapon. Shared Ancient Witness encaustic/egg-tempera style.
