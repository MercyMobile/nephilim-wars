# Prince of Persia — Twenty-One Days of Resistance

## Primary witness: Daniel 10

- Daniel mourns and fasts for three weeks before seeing a radiant man beside the Tigris (10:2–6).
- The messenger says Daniel's words were heard from the first day, but “the prince of the kingdom of Persia” withstood him for twenty-one days (10:12–13).
- Michael, “one of the chief princes,” came to help. The messenger then reaches Daniel to explain what will happen to his people (10:14).
- He expects to return and fight Persia's prince; afterward the prince of Greece will come. Michael is called “your prince” (10:20–21).

The report attests a supra-human conflict corresponding to earthly kingdoms, but gives Persia's prince no name, face, species, weapon, speech, genealogy, or origin. “Territorial prince” is a useful classification, not Daniel's complete ontology. The text does not identify him with a Persian king, deity, demon name, or Satan.

Primary local witness: `docs/scribe-texts/bib/asv/dan.md`, checked against Darby, WEB, KJV, and the local Hebrew/Aramaic lexical corpus.

## Artwork verdict

Show the reported resistance rather than a portrait. Daniel kneels weakened beside the Tigris as the radiant linen-clad messenger finally reaches him. In a translucent storm-memory above the river, that messenger is shown pressed for passage at a narrow mountain threshold by one immense anatomically unresolved shadow associated only through distant Persian landscape and architecture; Michael arrives as clean light and a second plain angelic figure to open the way. No one wears Persian royal regalia.

Required exclusions: named demon, Persian king or crown, Achaemenid royal portrait, Zoroastrian deity, horns, red skin, dark fantasy armor, specific weapon, territorial map, “21” text or symbols, modern Iran, readable writing. Shared Ancient Witness encaustic/egg-tempera style.
