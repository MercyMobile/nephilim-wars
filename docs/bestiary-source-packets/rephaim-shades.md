# Rephaim — The Shades Source Packet

## Two biblical usages must remain distinct

The consonantal/plural term רְפָאִים names living ancient peoples in Genesis 14–15 and Deuteronomy 2–3, including the remnant represented by Og of Bashan. Elsewhere the same written term denotes inhabitants of the dead realm. This card is only the latter dossier; it does not claim that every shade is a dead giant or that every living Rephaite becomes a special ghost-class.

## Hebrew shades witnesses

- **Job 26:5–6:** the Rephaim/shades writhe or tremble beneath the waters and their inhabitants; Sheol lies naked before God and Abaddon has no covering.
- **Psalm 88:10:** the sufferer asks whether God works wonders for the dead and whether the Rephaim rise to praise him—the expected answer within the lament is no.
- **Proverbs 2:18; 9:18; 21:16:** forbidden paths descend to the Rephaim; Folly's guests are in Sheol's depths; one who abandons understanding rests in their assembly.
- **Isaiah 14:9–11:** Sheol stirs the Rephaim to greet the fallen Babylonian tyrant, raising former leaders and kings from their seats; their speech mocks his reduction to their weakness.
- **Isaiah 26:14, 19:** oppressive dead Rephaim do not rise and their memory is destroyed, sharply contrasted with “your dead” who live, awake, and sing from the dust.

The shades retain identity, memory, assembly, and—in Isaiah's taunt—speech, yet are diminished and unable to praise or return by their own power. They are not active necromancers, spectral soldiers, or free-roaming predators.

## Ugaritic comparative boundary

The fragmentary Ugaritic *rpʾum* texts KTU 1.20–1.22 portray honored figures traveling by chariot toward a multi-day feast; divine/heroic terminology and royal-ancestral interpretations are debated. KTU 1.161 summons ancestral royal names in ritual. These are valuable West Semitic comparanda, but their vigorous feast-travelers must not simply replace the Bible's powerless Sheol shades. The fragments' lacunae prohibit a single confident ontology.

## Artwork verdict

Use Isaiah 14's eyewitness-like underworld taunt. Beneath a dark ceiling that faintly suggests deep waters, an ancient rock-cut Sheol hall stirs. Diminished translucent former kings rise weakly from low stone seats to greet a newly fallen proud ruler descending into their level. They retain robes and diadem-shaped remnants as memory, but no one glows with power; dust, moth-eaten cloth, and silence dominate. No corpse horror.

Required exclusions: zombies, skeleton army, necromancer, weapons, combat, giant bodies, free-flying ghosts, hellfire, demons torturing souls, glowing magic, active resurrection, Ugaritic banquet fused into Isaiah, modern cemetery. Shared Ancient Witness encaustic/egg-tempera style.
