# Penemue — Ink, Paper, Bitter and Sweet Source Packet

## Primary witness: 1 Enoch 69:8–11

- Penemue teaches humanity “the bitter and the sweet” and all the secrets of their wisdom.
- He instructs humans in writing with ink and paper.
- The narrator immediately interprets this disclosure as a means by which many sin and falsely secure their word with pen and ink. The passage is a polemic about revealed knowledge and its corrupting use, not a neutral invention legend.
- This later Enochic list is distinct from the Hermon chief roster of 1 Enoch 6–8; the text does not explicitly place Penemue among the twenty chiefs of ten.

Primary translation checked locally at `docs/scribe-texts/bib/boe/boe072.md`, with variant English wording at `bib/bep/bep05.md`.

## Textual boundary

“Paper” renders the transmitted writing material in the ancient translation tradition; artwork should use a plausible prepared sheet or parchment/papyrus-like surface without claiming a specific technology. The text does not give Penemue a library, book of spells, quill, seal, armor, weapon, or bodily form. It condemns dangerous disclosure; it does not establish the historical origin of literacy.

## Artwork verdict

In lamplight, a tall plain humanlike Watcher teaches adults to make black ink and draw the first controlled strokes on an otherwise blank prepared sheet. A second station holds two plain bowls—one with bitter herbs, one with honey or dates—linking the paired lesson without turning it into alchemy. The pupil's marks must remain non-linguistic practice strokes, while in the background another human begins using a marked tally to deceive a neighbor, embodying the narrator's warning.

Required exclusions: readable alphabet, Hebrew or cuneiform, book-filled library, spellbook, magic sigils, feather quill, printing press, modern paper stack, benevolent schoolmaster mood, cosmic armor, wings, weapon. Shared Ancient Witness encaustic/egg-tempera style.
