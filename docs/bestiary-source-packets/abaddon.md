# Abaddon / Apollyon — Destruction and the Abyssal King

## Hebrew wisdom layer

- Job 26:6 places **Abaddon** uncovered before God beside Sheol.
- Job 28:22 makes Abaddon and Death testify that wisdom has only been heard of.
- Proverbs 15:11 and 27:20 pair Sheol and Abaddon as realms or powers of destruction open before God and never satisfied.

These passages do not describe a personal angel. The Hebrew noun אֲבַדּוֹן means destruction or the place of destruction.

## Revelation 9:1–11

- A fallen star receives the key to the abyss and opens it; the text does not explicitly identify this star with Abaddon.
- Smoke rises and an extraordinary locust host emerges. It may torment—but not kill—for five months and must spare those bearing God's seal.
- The locust description combines battle horses, crown-like gold, human faces, women's hair, lions' teeth, iron-like breastplates, roaring wings, and scorpion-like tails.
- The host has as king **the angel of the abyss**, named Abaddon in Hebrew and Apollyon in Greek.

The elaborate body belongs to the locusts. Revelation gives their king no face, armor, wings, weapon, genealogy, or statement that he is fallen.

## Artwork verdict

Show the abyssal host and its king without transferring the host's anatomy. At the mouth of a vast smoking rock shaft, an anatomically unresolved, plain-robed angelic silhouette stands as sober commander. Below and around him surge the composite locust beings exactly as Revelation describes, but they do not attack people. A distant protected human company remains untouched beyond the smoke.

Required exclusions: locust anatomy on Abaddon, horned demon, grim reaper, scythe, crown or armor on the king, conflation with the key-bearing star, dead bodies, gore, hell throne, grimoire seal, readable text. Shared Ancient Witness encaustic/egg-tempera style.
